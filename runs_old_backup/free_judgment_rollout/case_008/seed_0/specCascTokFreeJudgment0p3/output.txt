\[
\log _x(y^x)=10,\qquad \log _y(x^{4y})=10 .
\]

---

### 1.  Translate the logarithms

\[
\log _x(y^x)=\frac{\ln (y^x)}{\ln x}=10
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
y^x=x^{10}. \tag{1}
\]

\[
\log _y(x^{4y})=\frac{\ln (x^{4y})}{\ln y}=10
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x^{4y}=y^{10}. \tag{2}
\]

---

### 2.  Express the logarithms in terms of \(A=\ln x,\; B=\ln y\)

From (1)

\[
xB=10A \quad\Longrightarrow\quad B=\frac{10A}{x}. \tag{3}
\]

From (2)

\[
4yA=10B \quad\Longrightarrow\quad B=\frac{2yA}{5}. \tag{4}
\]

Because \(A>0\) (since \(x>1\)), equate (3) and (4):

\[
\frac{10A}{x}=\frac{2yA}{5}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{10}{x}=\frac{2y}{5}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
xy=25. \tag{5}
\]

So **any** pair \((x,y)\) that meets the original equations must satisfy \(xy=25\).

---

### 3.  Verify that \(xy=25\) is sufficient

With \(xy=25\) we have \(y=25/x\).  
Insert this into (1):

\[
\left(\frac{25}{x}\right)^x = x^{10}.
\]

Taking natural logs:

\[
x\ln\!\left(\frac{25}{x}\right)=10\ln x
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x\ln 25 = (x+10)\ln x .
\]

This equation indeed has a solution \(x>1\) (e.g. \(x\approx1.535\)), and then  
\(y=25/x \approx 16.29\).  These values satisfy both (1) and (2), confirming that
\(xy=25\) works.

Thus the product \(xy\) is determined solely by the given conditions.
