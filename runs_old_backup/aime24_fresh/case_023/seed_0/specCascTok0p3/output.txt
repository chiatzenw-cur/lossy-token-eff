The unique mode is \(9\).

If \(9\) appeared only once, it could not be the mode.  
Thus \(9\) must appear exactly twice.

Since the median is the average of the two middle terms, it must be an integer that is not in the list.  
Hence the two middle terms must be distinct integers of the same parity, and the median must lie strictly between them.

If one of the \(9\)’s were among the two middle terms, that median would be \(9\) (which is in the list), contradicting the requirement.  
Therefore the two 9’s must occupy the last two positions:
\[
a_3=9,\qquad a_4=9.
\]

The remaining two numbers, \(a_1\) and \(a_2\), must satisfy

\[
a_1+a_2 + 9+9 = 30\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a_1+a_2 = 12,
\]
with \(a_1\le a_2<9\).

For the median \(\dfrac{a_2+9}{2}\) to be an integer, \(a_2\) must be odd.  
Also \(a_1\le a_2\) and \(a_1+a_2=12\).
