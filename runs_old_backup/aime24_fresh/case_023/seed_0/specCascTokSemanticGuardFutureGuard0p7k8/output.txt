### 1.  How many 9’s can appear?

Let \(m\) be the number of 9’s.  
Because \(9m \le 30\) and \(m\ge 2\) (the mode must appear at least twice), we have

\[
m=2 \quad\text{or}\quad m=3 .
\]

### 2.  Case \(m=3\)

Then \(9m=27\).  
The remaining sum is \(3\).  
With \(m=3\) the list would have \(3+ t\) elements, where \(t\) is the number of other numbers.
The only way to distribute the remaining sum \(3\) is

* \(3\) distinct numbers all equal to \(1\) (rules out the mode because the 1’s would also appear three times),
* a single number \(3\) (then the list would have \(4\) elements: \(\{3,9,9,9\}\)).  
  The median would be \(\frac{9+9}{2}=9\), which is in the list.

Thus the configuration with \(m=3\) does **not** satisfy the median condition.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 3.  Case \(m=2\)

Now \(9m=18\).  
The remaining sum is \(12\).  
Since the mode is unique, any other number can appear at most once; hence the remaining numbers are distinct.

Let \(t\) be the number of those distinct numbers.  
The length of the list is \(n=2+t\).

* \(t=1\) gives an odd length (\(n=3\)), impossible for the median requirement.
* \(t=2\) gives \(n=4\).  
  The two distinct numbers must sum to \(12\).  The possible pairs are
