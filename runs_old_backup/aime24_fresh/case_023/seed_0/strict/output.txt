### 1.  Length \(n=4\)

Let the four integers (in non‑decreasing order) be  

\[
a_{1}\le a_{2}\le a_{3}\le a_{4},\qquad a_{i}\in\mathbb Z^+,
\]

and let the unique mode be \(9\).  
Thus \(9\) must occur twice (otherwise a single \(9\) cannot dominate the mode).  
Let the two remaining numbers be \(x\) and \(y\) with \(x\ne y\), \(x,y\ne 9\).

The sum of the numbers is \(30\):

\[
x+y+9+9=30 \Longrightarrow x+y=12 .
\]

The median is the average of the two middle terms

\[
m=\frac{a_{2}+a_{3}}{2}=\frac{\min(x,y)+\max(x,y)}{2}
     =\frac{x+y}{2}=6 .
\]

But \(m\) must **not** be an element of the list, and it must be an integer.  
Hence \(m=6\) cannot be one of \(x,y\). Moreover, \(x\) and \(y\) must be
distinct positive integers summing to \(12\); the unordered pairs are  

\[
(1,11),\; (2,10),\; (3,9),\; (4,8),\; (5,7).
\]

The pair \((5,7)\) works because \(6\notin\{5,7\}\); all other pairs either
contain \(9\) (which would tie the mode) or produce a median that equals one
of the numbers in the list.

Thus the only possible list of length \(4\) is

\[
\boxed{5,\;7,\;9,\;9}.
\]

---

### 2.  Length \(n>4\)

If \(n\ge 6\), let \(t\) be the multiplicity of \(9\).  
Because \(9\) has to be the unique mode, \(t\ge 2\).  
The remaining \(n-t\) numbers sum to \(30-9t\).

- For \(n=6,\;t=2\) the remaining four distinct numbers would have to sum to
\(12\), but no 4 distinct positive integers add to \(12\) with the two
middle terms having the same parity (needed for an integral median).

- For \(t\ge 3\) the remaining sum \(30-9t\) is too small to be expressed
as a sum of distinct positive integers (the minimum for \(t=3\) would be
\(1+2+3+4+5+6=21>12\)).  

Thus no list of length \(6\) or more satisfies all the conditions.

---

5,\;7,\;9,\;9 .
\]

\[
5^2 + 7^2 + 9^2 + 9^2 = 25 + 49 + 81 + 81 = 236 .
