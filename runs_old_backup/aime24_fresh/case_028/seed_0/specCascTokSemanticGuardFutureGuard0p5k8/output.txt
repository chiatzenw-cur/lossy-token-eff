N_b &= 1000a+100+10c+d,\\
N_c &= 1000a+100b+10+d,\\
N_d &= 1000a+100b+10c+1.
\end{aligned}
\]

Since all are divisible by \(7\), their pairwise differences are also multiples of \(7\).

Using \(1000\equiv -1,\;100\equiv 2,\;10\equiv 3 \pmod 7\) gives the following congruences:

\[
\begin{aligned}
a+2b &\equiv 3 \pmod 7,\\
a+3c &\equiv 4 \pmod 7,\\
a+d &\equiv 2 \pmod 7,\\
3c-2b &\equiv 1 \pmod 7,\\
d-2b &\equiv 6 \pmod 7,\\
d-3c &\equiv 5 \pmod 7.
\end{aligned}
\]

These are all consistent; they reduce to

\[
a\equiv 3-2b,\qquad c\equiv 5+3b,\qquad d\equiv 2-a\pmod 7 .
\]

---

### 2.  Enforcing one of the numbers to be divisible by \(7\)

Take \(N_a\) (replace the thousands digit with \(1\)):

\[
N_a=1000+100b+10c+d .
\]

Modulo \(7\):

\[
N_a \equiv -1 + 2b + 3c + d \pmod 7 .
\]

Using \(3c\equiv 1+2b\) and \(d\equiv 2-a\) we obtain

\[
N_a \equiv -1 + 2b + 1+2b + d \equiv 4b + d \pmod 7 .
\]

From \(d-2b\equiv 6\) we have \(d\equiv 6+2b\).  
Hence

\[
4b+d \equiv 4b + (6+2b) \equiv 6b+6 \equiv 0 \pmod 7 .
\]

Thus \(6b \equiv 1 \pmod 7\), whose inverse of \(6\) modulo \(7\) is \(6\).  
Therefore

\[
b \equiv 6 \pmod 7 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad b = 6 .
\]

---

### 3.  Determining the other digits

With \(b=6\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
a &\equiv 3-2b \equiv 3-12 \equiv 5 \pmod 7 \;\Rightarrow\; a=5,\\
c &\equiv 5+3b \equiv 5+18 \equiv 2 \pmod 7 \;\Rightarrow\; c=2 \text{ or } 9,\\
d &\equiv 2-a \equiv 2-5 \equiv 4 \pmod 7 \;\Rightarrow\; d=4 .
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence the possible numbers are \(5624\) and \(5694\).  
The larger one is

\[
N = 5694 .
\]

Verification:  
\(1694, 5194, 5614, 5691\) are all divisible by \(7\).

---

### 4.  Compute \(Q+R\)

Divide \(N\) by \(1000\):

\[
Q=\bigl\lfloor\frac{5694}{1000}\bigr\rfloor = 5,\qquad
R=5694-5\cdot1000 = 694 .
\]

Thus

\[
Q+R = 5+694 = 699 .
\]
