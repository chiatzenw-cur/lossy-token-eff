\[
\binom{10}{4}=210
\]

possible outcomes.  
For a fixed set of Jen’s 4 numbers, the number of draws that match exactly \(r\) of her numbers is

\[
\binom{4}{r}\binom{6}{4-r}.
\]

Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
r=0 &: \binom{4}{0}\binom{6}{4}=15,\\
r=1 &: \binom{4}{1}\binom{6}{3}=80,\\
r=2 &: \binom{4}{2}\binom{6}{2}=90,\\
r=3 &: \binom{4}{3}\binom{6}{1}=24,\\
r=4 &: \binom{4}{4}\binom{6}{0}=1.
\end{aligned}
\]

The probability of winning a prize (at least two matches) is

\[
P(\text{prize})=\frac{90+24+1}{210}=\frac{115}{210}.
\]

The probability of winning the grand prize (all four match) is

\[
P(\text{grand})=\frac{1}{210}.
\]

Thus

\[
P(\text{grand}\mid\text{prize})=\frac{P(\text{grand})}{P(\text{prize})}
=\frac{1/210}{115/210}=\frac{1}{115}.
\]

In lowest terms \( \frac{m}{n}=\frac{1}{115}\), so \(m+n=1+115=116\).
