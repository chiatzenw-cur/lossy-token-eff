Let Jen’s chosen set be fixed.  
We count the number of ways the lottery’s 4‑number draw can intersect her set.

For a fixed intersection size \(k\) (\(k=0,1,2,3,4\)):

\[
\text{ways}=\binom{4}{k}\binom{6}{4-k},
\]

because we choose \(k\) of her 4 numbers and fill the remaining \(4-k\) slots with numbers from the other 6.

| \(k\) | \(\binom{4}{k}\binom{6}{4-k}\) |
|-------|------------------------------|
| 0 | \(\binom{4}{0}\binom{6}{4}=1\cdot15=15\) |
| 1 | \(\binom{4}{1}\binom{6}{3}=4\cdot20=80\) |
| 2 | \(\binom{4}{2}\binom{6}{2}=6\cdot15=90\) |
| 3 | \(\binom{4}{3}\binom{6}{1}=4\cdot6=24\) |
| 4 | \(\binom{4}{4}\binom{6}{0}=1\cdot1=1\) |

The total number of possible draws is \(\binom{10}{4}=210\).

**Grand prize** \(k=4\): \(1\) favorable draw, so

\[
P(G)=\frac{1}{210}.
\]

**Prize** \(k\ge 2\): \(90+24+1=115\) favorable draws, so

\[
P(P)=\frac{115}{210}.
\]

Hence

\[
P(G\mid P)=\frac{P(G)}{P(P)}=\frac{\frac{1}{210}}{\frac{115}{210}}
=\frac{1}{115}.
\]

In lowest terms the fraction is \(\frac{1}{115}\).
