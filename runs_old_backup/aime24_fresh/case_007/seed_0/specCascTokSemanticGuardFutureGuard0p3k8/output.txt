\[

ab+bc+ca = 27 &\text{(surface area)}\\[2pt]

\end{cases}
\]

The smallest sphere that can contain the box has its centre at the centre of the box.
The distance from the centre to any vertex is  

\[
\frac12\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}},
\]

so the radius of the circumscribing sphere is  

\[
r_{\text{box}}=\frac12\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}} .
\]

For all boxes in \(\mathcal{B}\) we need a sphere that can contain each of them, so we must
maximise \(r_{\text{box}}\) over all admissible \(a,b,c\).  
Thus we maximise

\[
S=a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}.
\]

---

### 1.  Express \(S\) in terms of the elementary symmetric sums

Let  

\[
s_{1}=a+b+c,\qquad s_{2}=ab+bc+ca=27,\qquad s_{3}=abc=23 .
\]

Then  

\[
S=(a+b+c)^{2}-2(ab+bc+ca)=s_{1}^{2}-2s_{2}=s_{1}^{2}-54 .
\]

Therefore maximizing \(S\) is equivalent to maximizing \(s_{1}\).

---

### 2.  Maximising \(s_{1}\)

The Lagrange-multiplier equations  
\[
\begin{cases}
2a+\lambda_{1}bc+\lambda_{2}(b+c)=0\\
2b+\lambda_{1}ac+\lambda_{2}(a+c)=0\\
2c+\lambda_{1}ab+\lambda_{2}(a+b)=0
\end{cases}
\]
lead to the condition that either all three variables are equal
or two of them are equal.  
(If all three were distinct, the equations would force the three
variables to be equal, which is impossible because \(abc=23\neq27\).)

Hence, at an extremum two side lengths must be equal.  
Let \(b=c=x\) and \(a=y\).  
The constraints become

\[
\begin{cases}
y\,x^{2}=23\\
y\,x+ x^{2}=27
\end{cases}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x^{3}-27x+46=0 .
\]

The cubic factors as  

\[
(x-2)(x^{2}+2x-23)=0 .
\]

Thus

\[
x=2,\; y=\frac{23}{x^{2}}=\frac{23}{4}=5.75
\]
or
\[
x=-1+2\sqrt6\approx3.898,\; y=\frac{23}{x^{2}}\approx1.513 .
\]

Compute \(s_{1}=y+2x\) and \(S\):

* For \(x=2,\;y=5.75\):

\[
s_{1}=5.75+4=9.75,\qquad
S=2^{2}+2^{2}+5.75^{2}=41.0625 .
\]

* For \(x\approx3.898,\;y\approx1.513\):

\[
s_{1}\approx9.309,\qquad
S\approx32.676 .
\]

Thus the maximum is attained at \(a=b=2,\;c=5.75\).
