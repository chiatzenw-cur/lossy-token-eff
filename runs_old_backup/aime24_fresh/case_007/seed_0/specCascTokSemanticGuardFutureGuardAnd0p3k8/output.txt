\[
\begin{cases}
ab+bc+ca=27\\[2mm]
abc=23
\end{cases}
\]

For a box, the smallest sphere that contains it has radius  

\[
r_{\text{box}}=\frac12\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}}
\]

Hence the radius that works for **all** boxes of \(\mathcal{B}\) is  

\[
r=\max_{\mathcal{B}}\frac12\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}}
\]

so we need to maximise \(S=a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}\).

---

### 1.  Express \(S\) in terms of \(s_1=a+b+c\)

\[
S=a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}=s_1^{2}-2(ab+bc+ca)=s_1^{2}-54
\]

Thus maximizing \(S\) is equivalent to maximizing \(s_1\).

---

### 2.  Find the possible maxima of \(s_1\)

Let \(s_1=a+b+c\).  
The numbers \(a,b,c\) are roots of

\[
t^{3}-s_1t^{2}+27t-23=0
\]

All roots must be positive.  
The extremes of \(s_1\) occur when the cubic has a multiple root, i.e. when two side‑lengths are equal.

Assume \(a=b=x\) and \(c=y\).  
From the constraints:

\[
x^{2}+2xy=27,\qquad x^{2}y=23
\]

Solve for \(y=\dfrac{23}{x^{2}}\) and substitute into the first equation:

\[
x^{2}+\,\frac{46}{x}=27\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x^{3}-27x+46=0
\]

The cubic factors as \((x-2)(x^{2}+2x-23)=0\).

Thus

\[
x=2\quad\text{or}\quad x=-1+2\sqrt6
\]

Corresponding \(y\) values:

* \(x=2\): \(y=\dfrac{23}{4}\)
* \(x=-1+2\sqrt6\): \(y=\dfrac{25+4\sqrt6}{23}\)

Now compute \(s_1\):

* For \(x=2,\;y=\dfrac{23}{4}\):
  \[
  s_1=2x+y=4+\frac{23}{4}=\frac{39}{4}\;(=9.75)
  \]
* For \(x=-1+2\sqrt6,\;y=\dfrac{25+4\sqrt6}{23}\):
  \[
  s_1=2x+y\approx9.31
  \]

Hence the maximum is attained at \(x=2,\;y=\dfrac{23}{4}\).

### 3.  Compute the maximal sphere radius
