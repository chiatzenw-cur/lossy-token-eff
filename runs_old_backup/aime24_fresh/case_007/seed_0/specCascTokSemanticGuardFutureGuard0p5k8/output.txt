### 2.  Radius of the circumscribing sphere  

For a rectangular box the smallest sphere that contains it is the sphere
with centre at the centre of the box; its radius is

\[
r=\sqrt{\Bigl(\frac{a}{2}\Bigr)^2+\Bigl(\frac{b}{2}\Bigr)^2+\Bigl(\frac{c}{2}\Bigr)^2}
   =\frac{\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}}{2}.
\]

Hence

\[
r^2=\frac{a^2+b^2+c^2}{4}
    =\frac{s_1^2-2s_2}{4}
    =\frac{s_1^2-54}{4}.
\]

So we must maximise \(s_1^2\) (equivalently \(s_1\)) subject to the
two constraints.

### 3.  Maximising \(s_1\)

Because the problem is symmetric in \(a,b,c\), the extremum occurs when
two of the sides are equal.  
Let \(b=c=x\) and let \(a=y\).  
Then

\[
\begin{cases}
y\,x^2=23,\\[1mm]
2yx+x^2=27 .
\end{cases}
\]

From the first equation \(y=\dfrac{23}{x^2}\).
Insert this into the second:

\[
2\frac{23}{x^2}\,x+x^2=27
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\frac{46}{x}+x^2=27
\;\Longrightarrow\;
x^3-27x+46=0 .
\]

The cubic factors as \((x-2)(x^2+2x-23)=0\), giving the positive root

\[
x=2,\qquad\text{and therefore } y=\frac{23}{2^2}=5.75 .
\]

Thus the side lengths are \((a,b,c)=(5.75,\,2,\,2)\).
