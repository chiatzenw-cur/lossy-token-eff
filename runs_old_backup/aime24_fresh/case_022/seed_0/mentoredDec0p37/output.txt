### 1.  Inscribed rectangles in a circle  
A rectangle inscribed in a circle has the following property:

* its diagonals are equal and bisect each other, and
* the intersection of the diagonals is the centre of the circle.

Hence each diagonal of an inscribed rectangle is a diameter of the circle.
Consequently opposite vertices of a rectangle are diametrically opposite on the
circumcircle.

Thus a rectangle whose vertices are among the 12 vertices of the dodecagon is
completely determined by choosing two distinct diameters: choose two pairs of
opposite vertices.

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2.  How many choices of two diameters?  

The dodecagon has \(6\) pairs of opposite vertices:
\[
(0,6),\;(1,7),\;(2,8),\;(3,9),\;(4,10),\;(5,11).
\]

Pick any two of these \(6\) diameters.  
The four corresponding vertices form a rectangle, and any rectangle formed by
vertices of the dodecagon arises in this way.

Number of ways:
\[
\binom{6}{2}=15.
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 3.  Verification that the sides are dodecagon sides or diagonals  

For any choice of two diameters, the sides of the resulting quadrilateral are
either an edge of the dodecagon (vertices that differ by \(1\)) or a diagonal
(between two non‑adjacent vertices).  
Hence every side lies on a side or a diagonal of the dodecagon, satisfying the
problem’s condition.
