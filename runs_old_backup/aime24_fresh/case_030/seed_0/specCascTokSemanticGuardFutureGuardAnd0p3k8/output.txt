### 1.  What does the maximality condition mean?

If a cell \((i,j)\) is empty, a chip could be added there only if it could be placed without breaking the
two “uniformity” rules:

* the colour of the new chip must equal the colour of all chips already in row \(i\) (or be the first chip if row \(i\) is empty);
* the colour of the new chip must equal the colour of all chips already in column \(j\) (or be the first chip if column \(j\) is empty).

Hence for an empty cell \((i,j)\) we **must** have

\[
R_i\neq C_j .
\]

Otherwise we could put a chip of colour \(R_i=C_j\) and both conditions would stay true.  
Consequently **every empty cell lies at the intersection of a row and a column that are coloured differently**.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.  Every row and column must contain at least one chip

Assume a row \(i\) had no chips.  
Take any column \(j\).  
If column \(j\) also had no chips, putting a chip of either colour in \((i,j)\) would satisfy both rules, contradicting maximality.  
If column \(j\) had chips, we could put a chip of the colour of column \(j\) in \((i,j)\) and again violate maximality.  
Thus no row can be empty.  
The same reasoning applied to columns shows that **every column must also contain at least one chip**.
