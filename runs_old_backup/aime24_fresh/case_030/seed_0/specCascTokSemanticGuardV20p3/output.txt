### 1.  What does “maximal” mean?

If an empty cell could be filled with a white chip **and** still keep the
condition that all chips in its row and column have the same colour,
then the placement would not be maximal.
Hence for every empty cell at least one of the two colours would be
impossible.  
This is only possible when

* every row is not empty, and
* every column is not empty.

Indeed, if a row were empty while some column already contained a chip,
then the intersection of that row and that column would be fillable
with a chip of the column’s colour.  
Thus an empty row cannot coexist with a coloured column, and vice‑versa.
Therefore **every row and every column must contain at least one chip**.

### 2.  Determining the pattern

Let  

* \(a\) = number of **white** rows  \((0\le a\le 5)\)
* \(b\) = number of **white** columns  \((0\le b\le 5)\).

Because all rows and columns are non‑empty, the opposite colour must
also appear in at least one row/column whenever the other colour appears
in a row/column.  
Thus the only admissible pairs \((a,b)\) are

\[
(a,b)=(0,0),\;(5,5),\; \text{or}\; 1\le a\le 4,\;1\le b\le 4.
