\[
p^{2}\mid n^{4}+1\qquad (n\in \mathbb Z^+)
\]

means that \(-1\) is a fourth power modulo \(p^{2}\).
For an odd prime \(p\) the multiplicative group \((\mathbb Z/p^{2}\mathbb Z)^{\times}\) is cyclic of order  
\(\varphi(p^{2}) = p(p-1)\).

If \(-1\) is a fourth power modulo \(p^{2}\), then there is an element \(x\) with

\[
x^{4}\equiv -1 \pmod{p^{2}} .
\]

Squaring gives \(x^{8}\equiv 1\), hence \(x\) has order \(8\).  
Thus the group must contain an element of order \(8\); equivalently

\[
8\mid p(p-1).
\]

Since \(p\) is prime, \(p\) cannot be a multiple of \(8\); therefore

\[
p-1\equiv 0\pmod{8}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p\equiv1\pmod{8}.
\]

The smallest prime with this property is

\[
p=17.
\]

### 1.  Finding the least \(m\) with \(m^{4}+1\equiv 0\pmod{17^{2}}\)

We first solve the congruence modulo \(17\):

\[
m^{4}\equiv-1\equiv 16\pmod{17}.
\]

Checking the residues \(0,1,\dots ,16\) gives

\[
m\equiv 2,\,8,\,9,\,15\pmod{17}.
\]

For each such residue \(a\) we lift the solution to modulo \(17^{2}\) using
Hensel’s lemma.  

Let \(m=a+17k\) with \(k\in\{0,\dots ,16\}\).  
Expanding \((a+17k)^{4}\) modulo \(17^{2}\) gives

\[
(a+17k)^{4}\equiv a^{4}+68a^{3}k \pmod{17^{2}} .
\]

Hence we need

\[
a^{4}+68a^{3}k\equiv 288\pmod{289}\quad(\text{since }-1\equiv 288).
\]

Writing \(68=4\cdot17\) and dividing by \(17\) yields

\[
4a^{3}k\equiv\frac{288-a^{4}}{17}\pmod{17}.
\]

Evaluating for each \(a\):

| \(a\) | \(a^{4}\) | \(\dfrac{288-a^{4}}{17}\) | \(4a^{3}\pmod{17}\) | \(k\) (mod 17) |
|-------|-----------|---------------------------|---------------------|----------------|
| 2     | 16        | 16                        | 15                  | 9 |
| 8     | 50        | 14                        |  8                  | 6 |
| 9     | 203       | 5                         |  9                  |10 |
| 15    | 50        | 14                        |  2                  | 7 |

Thus the four solutions modulo \(289\) are

\[
m = 2+17\cdot9 = 155,\qquad
m = 8+17\cdot6 = 110,\qquad
m = 9+17\cdot10 = 179,\qquad
m = 15+17\cdot7 = 134 .
\]

The smallest positive integer among them is \(m=110\).
