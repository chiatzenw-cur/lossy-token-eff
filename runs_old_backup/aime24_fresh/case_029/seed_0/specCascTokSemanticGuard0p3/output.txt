with \(C=(R\cos\phi,R\sin\phi,0)\) a point on the circle of the torus centre.  
For tangency the radius vectors \(P-C\) and \(P-O\) must be collinear, giving

\[
\tan\theta=-\frac{d}{R}.
\]

Hence  

\[
\cos\theta=\frac{R}{\sqrt{R^{2}+d^{2}}},\qquad
\sin\theta=-\frac{d}{\sqrt{R^{2}+d^{2}}}.
\]

The \(z\)-coordinate of the tangency point is  

\[
z= r\sin\theta=-\frac{rd}{\sqrt{R^{2}+d^{2}}}\;.
\]

For a torus–sphere tangency we require the distance from the sphere centre to the
tangent point to equal the sphere radius:

\[
\sqrt{R^{2}+d^{2}}\pm r = 11 .
\]

This gives two possibilities

\[
\sqrt{R^{2}+d^{2}} = 8 \quad\text{or}\quad 14 .
\]

Thus

\[
d^{2}=28\quad\text{or}\quad 160 ,
\]

corresponding to the two circles of tangency.

The radius of the circle of tangency on the torus is

\[
\rho=\sqrt{\,r^{2}-z^{2}\,}
      =\sqrt{\,r^{2}-\Bigl(\frac{rd}{\sqrt{R^{2}+d^{2}}}\Bigr)^{2}\,}
      =\sqrt{\,r^{2}\Bigl(1-\frac{d^{2}}{R^{2}+d^{2}}\Bigr)} .
\]

For \(d^{2}=28\) (the “outer” tangency) we have

\[
\rho_{\text{outer}}
=\sqrt{\,9-\frac{9\cdot 28}{64}\,}
=\sqrt{\frac{81}{16}}
=\frac{9}{4}.
\]

For \(d^{2}=160\) (the “inner” tangency) we have

\[
\rho_{\text{inner}}
=\sqrt{\,9-\frac{9\cdot 160}{196}\,}
=\sqrt{\frac{81}{49}}
=\frac{9}{7}.
\]

Hence

\[
r_i=\frac{9}{4},\qquad r_o=\frac{9}{7},
\]

and

\[
r_i-r_o=\frac{9}{4}-\frac{9}{7}
        =\frac{9(7-4)}{28}
        =\frac{27}{28}.
\]

Thus \(m=27\) and \(n=28\) are relatively prime, and

\[
m+n=27+28=55.
\]
