\log_x(y^x)=10 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y^x=x^{10}\tag{1}
\]
\[
\log_y(x^{4y})=10 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x^{4y}=y^{10}\tag{2}
\]

---

### 1.  Express the logarithms in natural logs

Let  

\[
a=\ln x,\qquad b=\ln y .
\]

From (1)

\[
x\ln y = 10\ln x \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x\,b = 10a \tag{3}
\]

From (2)

\[
4y\ln x = 10\ln y \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 4y\,a = 10b \tag{4}
\]

---

### 2.  Eliminate \(b\) to relate \(x\) and \(y\)

Divide (3) by (4):

\[
\frac{x\,b}{4y\,a}= \frac{10a}{10b}\;\Longrightarrow\;
\frac{xb}{4ya}= \frac{a}{b}
\]

Cross‑multiplying gives

\[
b^2 = 4\,a\,x\,y \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{b}{a}= \frac{4\,x\,y}{b^2}
\]

But it is easier to compute directly from (3) and (4):

\[
\frac{10a}{x}=b,\qquad \frac{10b}{4y}=a
\]

Replace \(b\) from the first into the second:

\[
a=\frac{10}{4y}\left(\frac{10a}{x}\right)
\;\Longrightarrow\;
1=\frac{25}{xy}\;\Longrightarrow\;
xy=25.
\]

Thus the product \(xy\) is fixed; no further calculation is necessary.

---

### 3.  Verify that a solution exists

With \(xy=25\) we have \(y=\dfrac{25}{x}\).
Substituting into \(y^x=x^{10}\):

\[
\left(\frac{25}{x}\right)^x=x^{10}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
25^x=x^{10+x}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
x\ln 25=(x+10)\ln x ,
\]

which has a unique solution \(x>1\) (approximately \(x\approx1.538\)).
The corresponding \(y=\dfrac{25}{x}\approx16.25\) satisfies both original equations.

Hence the real numbers \(x>1,\;y>1\) exist and

\[
\boxed{25}
