\[
A=(u,v),\qquad B=(p,q),\qquad C=(-u,-v),\qquad D=(-p,-q)
\]

so that the diagonals intersect at the origin.  
All four points lie on the hyperbola  

\[
\frac{x^2}{20}-\frac{y^2}{24}=1 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad 
\frac{u^2}{20}-\frac{v^2}{24}=1,\qquad 
\frac{p^2}{20}-\frac{q^2}{24}=1 .
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 1.  Side‑length condition

For a rhombus the sides have equal length.  
Equality of the lengths of \(AB\) and \(BC\) gives

\[
(p-u)^2+(q-v)^2=(p+u)^2+(q+v)^2
\]

which simplifies to  

\[
pu+qv=0. \tag{1}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.  Parametrisation of the hyperbola

Write the hyperbola in hyperbolic‑parameter form

\[
x=\sqrt{20}\cosh t,\qquad y=\sqrt{24}\sinh t .
\]

Hence

\[
A=(\sqrt{20}\cosh t_1,\;\sqrt{24}\sinh t_1),\qquad
B=(\sqrt{20}\cosh t_2,\;\sqrt{24}\sinh t_2).
\]

Condition (1) becomes  

\[
20\cosh t_1\cosh t_2+24\sinh t_1\sinh t_2=0
\]
\[
\Longrightarrow\qquad
\tanh t_1\,\tanh t_2=-\frac{5}{6}. \tag{2}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 3.  Expression for \(BD^2\)

Since \(D=(-p,-q)\),

\[
BD^2=(p-(-p))^2+(q-(-q))^2=4(p^2+q^2).
\]

Using the parametrisation,

\[
p^2+q^2=20\cosh^2t_2+24\sinh^2t_2
      =20+44\sinh^2t_2.
\]

Therefore  

\[
BD^2=80+176\sinh^2t_2. \tag{3}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 4.  Minimising \(BD^2\)

From (2) we have  

\[
|\tanh t_2|>\frac{5}{6}\qquad(\text{because }|\tanh t_1|<1).
\]

Denote \(w=\tanh^2t_2\;(w>0)\).  
Since \(\tanh^2 t_2=\dfrac{\sinh^2t_2}{\sinh^2t_2+1}\),

\[
\sinh^2t_2=\frac{w}{1-w}\quad\text{and}\quad
w=\frac{25}{36}+ \varepsilon,\qquad \varepsilon>0.
\]

Thus

\[
\sinh^2t_2=\frac{25/36+\varepsilon}{1-25/36-\varepsilon}
          =\frac{25}{11}+ \delta(\varepsilon),
\]
with \(\delta(\varepsilon)\to 0^{+}\) as \(\varepsilon\to 0^{+}\).

Substituting this into (3),

\[
BD^2=80+176\!\left(\frac{25}{11}+\delta\right)
    =480+176\,\delta.
\]

Hence \(BD^2\) can be made arbitrarily close to \(480\) from above, but
never less than \(480\).  
Therefore the greatest real number that is **less than** every value of
\(BD^2\) is

\[
