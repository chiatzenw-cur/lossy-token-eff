Let's craft explanation.<|end|><|start|>assistant<|channel|>final<|message|>Let  

* \(H\) – a horizontal step (one unit to the right)  
* \(V\) – a vertical step (one unit upward).

Because a path yields the point \((8,8)\) it must have

\[
8\ H\text{-steps},\qquad 8\ V\text{-steps}
\]

so the total length of the path is \(16\) unit moves.

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 1.  What does “change direction exactly four times’’ mean?

A “change direction’’ occurs whenever a step of direction \(H\) is
followed by a step of direction \(V\) **or** when a step of direction \(V\) is
followed by a step of direction \(H\).  
Hence “four changes” means that the 16 symbols of the path have exactly

\[
4 \equiv \text{number of transitions } = \text{(# of blocks)}-1 .
\]

This gives a pattern of **5 blocks**,

and 5 blocks can contain 3 blocks of each of the two directions
(2 or 3, depending on the orientation of the first block).  
Thus there are 2 possible patterns:

```
1. H V H V H   (3 H–blocks and 2 V–blocks)
2. V H V H V   (3 V–blocks and 2 H–blocks)
```

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2.  Counting the first pattern (start with H)

For \(M\) layers of \(H\) we need the number of ways to split the 8
horizontal steps into \(3\) positive integers, and for \(N\) vertical
segments we need the number of ways to split the 8 vertical steps into
\(2\) positive integers.

\[
\begin{aligned}
chess\(4 \text{ H components}\; \} &=\binom{8-1}{3-1}=\binom{7}{2}=21,\\[2mm]
chess\(V\text{ components}\; ) &=\binom{8-1}{2-1}=\binom{7}{1}=7 .
\end{aligned}
\]

Each choice of the 3 H‑block lengths and the 2 V‑block lengths gives a
unique path, and together they give:

\[
21\times 7=147\;\text{paths of the first pattern}.
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 3.  Counting the second pattern (start with V)

The calculation is identical; now the 3 V‑blocks are divided into
positive integers and the 2 H‑blocks are positive integers.  
Thus the mirrors of the first pattern give
another \(147\) paths.

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 4.  Total number of paths

\[
\boxed{\,147+147=294\,}
\]

which is the number of 16‑step lattice paths from the lower‑left to
the upper‑right corner that differ in direction exactly four times in an 8 × 8 grid.
