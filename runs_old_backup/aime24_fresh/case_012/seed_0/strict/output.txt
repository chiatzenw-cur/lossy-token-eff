A path from the lower left \((0,0)\) to the upper right \((8,8)\) must contain exactly  
\(8\) right moves and \(8\) up moves, for a total of \(16\) steps.

---

### 1.  What does “change direction exactly four times’’ mean?

Changing direction every time the move switches from \(R\) to \(U\) or \(U\) to \(R\).  
Four changes give five consecutive blocks of identical moves.  
So the path has the form  

\[
\underbrace{R^{a_1}}_{\text{block 1}}\,
\underbrace{U^{a_2}}_{\text{block 2}}\,
\underbrace{R^{a_3}}_{\text{block 3}}\,
\underbrace{U^{a_4}}_{\text{block 4}}\,
\underbrace{R^{a_5}}_{\text{block 5}}
\]

or the same pattern with the roles of \(R\) and \(U\) exchanged.  
Every block length \(a_i\) must be a positive integer.

---

### 2.  Counting the block lengths

**Pattern 1** (The path starts with a right move)

The \(R\)-segments are \(a_1,a_3,a_5\) and must sum to 8:  

\[
a_1+a_3+a_5 = 8,\qquad a_i>0
\]

The number of positive integer solutions (compositions) is  

\[
\binom{8-1}{3-1}= \binom{7}{2}=21 .
\]

The \(U\)-segments are \(a_2,a_4\) and must sum to 8:  

\[
a_2+a_4 = 8,\qquad a_i>0
\]

giving  

\[
\binom{8-1}{2-1}= \binom{7}{1}=7
\]

solutions.

Hence for pattern 1 the number of admissible sequences is  

\[
21 \times 7 = 147 .
\]

**Pattern 2** (The path starts with an up move)

The situation is symmetric: the three \(U\)-segments must sum to 8 and the two
\(R\)-segments must sum to 8, giving the same counts, \(21 \times 7 =147\).

---

### 3.  Total number of paths

\[
\text{Total} = 147 + 147 = 294 .
\]

So there are  

\[
\boxed{294}
\]

paths of length 16 from \((0,0)\) to \((8,8)\) that change direction exactly four times.
