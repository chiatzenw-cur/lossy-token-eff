\log_x(y^x)=10\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad y^x=x^{10}\tag{1}
\]
\[
\log_y(x^{4y})=10\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad x^{4y}=y^{10}\tag{2}
\]

---

### 1.  Express the equations in logarithms

Take natural logarithms of (1) and (2):

\[
x\ln y = 10\ln x \tag{3}
\]
\[
4y\ln x = 10\ln y \tag{4}
\]

Both \(x\) and \(y\) are \(>1\), so \(\ln x>0\) and \(\ln y>0\).

---

### 2.  Eliminate the logarithms

From (3) and (4) express \(\ln y\):

\[
\ln y = \frac{10\ln x}{x}\qquad\text{and}\qquad 
\ln y = \frac{4y\ln x}{10}
\]

Set them equal:

\[
\frac{10\ln x}{x} = \frac{4y\ln x}{10}
\]

Because \(\ln x>0\), we can cancel it:

\[
\frac{10}{x} = \frac{4y}{10}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
y = \frac{25}{x}
\]

---

### 3.  Find \(xy\)

Multiplying by \(x\) gives

\[
xy = 25.
\]

---

### 4.  Verification

With \(y=\frac{25}{x}\),

\[
y^x = \left(\frac{25}{x}\right)^x = \frac{25^x}{x^x}
                               = \frac{(5^2)^x}{x^x}
                               = \frac{5^{2x}}{x^x}
\]

and

\[
x^{10} = \frac{5^{2x}}{x^x}.
\]

Thus \(y^x = x^{10}\) holds, and similarly one checks \(x^{4y}=y^{10}\) holds, confirming the solution.

Hence the product of the two numbers is

\[
\boxed{25}.
\]
