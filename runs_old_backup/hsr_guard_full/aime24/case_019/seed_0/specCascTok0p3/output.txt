\(X^{2/3}+Y^{2/3}=1\); for all other points on \(AB\) the inequality is
strict, hence those points lie on some other segment of \(\mathcal F\).

Set

\[
\left(\frac12(1-t)\right)^{2/3}+\left(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}t\right)^{2/3}=1 .
\]

Because \(\left(\frac12\right)^{2/3}=\frac{1}{2^{2/3}}\) and
\(\left(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right)^{2/3}=\frac{3^{1/3}}{2^{2/3}}\), multiply by
\(2^{2/3}\) to obtain

\[
(1-t)^{2/3}+3^{1/3}t^{2/3}=2^{2/3}.
\]

Let \(t=\frac34\).  Then  

\[
1-t=\frac14,\qquad t=\frac34 .
\]

Compute

\[
(1-t)^{2/3}=\left(\frac14\right)^{2/3}=\frac14,\qquad
3^{1/3}t^{2/3}=3^{1/3}\left(\frac34\right)^{2/3}
=3^{1/3}\frac{3^{2/3}}{4}=\frac{3}{4}.
\]

Thus the left–hand side equals \(\frac14+\frac34=1\), which equals
\(2^{2/3}\). Hence \(t=\frac34\) satisfies the boundary equation.
Because the boundary is reached only once on \(AB\), this point is
unique.

Hence  

\[
C=\left(\frac12(1-\tfrac34),\;\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\cdot \tfrac34\right)
=\left(\frac18,\;\frac{3\sqrt3}{8}\right).
\]

=\frac{28}{64}
=\frac{7}{16}.
\]

Thus \(p=7\) and \(q=16\).
