\[
\binom{10}{4}=210 .

---

### 1.  Probability of the grand prize  

The grand prize occurs only when the lottery draw equals Jen’s set.  
There is exactly one such subset, so

\[
P(\text{grand})=\frac{1}{210}.
\]

---

### 2.  Probability of a prize (at least two matches)

For a draw to give Jen a prize it must match her set in \(k\) elements where \(k=2,3,4\).

Number of draws with exactly \(k\) matches:

\[
\binom{4}{k}\binom{6}{4-k}
\]

because we choose \(k\) of her 4 numbers and the remaining \(4-k\) numbers from the other 6.

\[
\begin{aligned}
k=2 &: \binom{4}{2}\binom{6}{2}=6\cdot15=90,\\[2pt]
k=3 &: \binom{4}{3}\binom{6}{1}=4\cdot6=24,\\[2pt]
k=4 &: \binom{4}{4}\binom{6}{0}=1\cdot1=1.
\end{aligned}
\]

Total draws giving a prize:

\[
90+24+1=115.
\]

Hence

\[
P(\text{prize})=\frac{115}{210}=\frac{23}{42}.
\]

---

### 3.  Conditional probability of the grand prize

\[
P(\text{grand}\mid\text{prize})=
\frac{P(\text{grand})}{P(\text{prize})}
=\frac{\frac{1}{210}}{\frac{115}{210}}
=\frac{1}{115}.
\]

Thus the required probability is \(\displaystyle \frac{1}{115}\).
