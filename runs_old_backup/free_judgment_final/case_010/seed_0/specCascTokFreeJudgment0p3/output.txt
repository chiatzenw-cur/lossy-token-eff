For a random draw to give her a prize we need the intersection of the draw with her set to have size at least \(2\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### Number of draws giving a grand prize

If the draw contains all four of her numbers, the intersection size is \(4\).  
There is exactly

\[
1=\binom{4}{4}\binom{6}{0}
\]

such draw.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### Number of draws giving a prize (size \(\ge 2\))

We count the draws with intersection size \(2,3,\) or \(4\).

*Intersection size \(2\):*  
Choose \(2\) of her numbers and \(2\) from the remaining \(6\) numbers:

\[
\binom{4}{2}\binom{6}{2}=6\cdot 15=90 .
\]

*Intersection size \(3\):*  
Choose \(3\) of her numbers and \(1\) from the remaining \(6\):

\[
\binom{4}{3}\binom{6}{1}=4\cdot 6=24 .
\]

*Intersection size \(4\):* (grand prize)  
\[
1 .
\]

Total draws that give a prize:

\[
90+24+1=115 .
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### Probability of a grand prize given a prize

\[
P(\text{grand prize}\mid \text{prize})=
\frac{\text{# draws with intersection 4}}
{\text{# draws with intersection }\ge 2}
=\frac{1}{115}.
\]

The fraction \(\frac{1}{115}\) is already in lowest terms, so \(m=1\) and \(n=115\).
