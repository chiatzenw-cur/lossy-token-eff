\log_{x}(y^x)=10 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y^x=x^{10}\tag{1}
\]
\[
\log_{y}(x^{4y})=10 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x^{4y}=y^{10}\tag{2}
\]

Let \(a=\ln x\) and \(b=\ln y\).  
Then \(x=e^{a}\) and \(y=e^{b}\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### Convert the two equations

Equation (1):
\[
y^x=x^{10}
\Longrightarrow (e^{b})^{e^{a}}=(e^{a})^{10}
\Longrightarrow e^{\,b\,e^{a}}=e^{\,10a}
\Longrightarrow b\,e^{a}=10a. \tag{3}
\]

Equation (2):
\[
x^{4y}=y^{10}
\Longrightarrow (e^{a})^{4e^{b}}=(e^{b})^{10}
\Longrightarrow e^{\,4a\,e^{b}}=e^{\,10b}
\Longrightarrow 4a\,e^{b}=10b. \tag{4}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### Solve for \(a\) and \(b\)

From (3): \(b = \dfrac{10a}{e^{a}}\).

Insert this into (4):

\[
4a\,e^{b}
=10b
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
4a\,e^{\,\frac{10a}{e^{a}}}=10\left(\frac{10a}{e^{a}}\right).
\]

Simplify:

\[
4a\,e^{\,\frac{10a}{e^{a}}}= \frac{100a}{e^{a}}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
e^{\,\frac{10a}{e^{a}}}= \frac{25}{e^{a}}.
\]

Taking natural logs:

\[
\frac{10a}{e^{a}} = \ln\!\left(\frac{25}{e^{a}}\right)
= \ln 25 - a.
\]

Thus

\[
\frac{10a}{e^{a}} = \ln 25 - a
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{10a}{e^{a}} + a = \ln 25.
\]

Factor \(a\):

\[
a\!\left(\frac{10}{e^{a}} + 1\right)=\ln 25.
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### Recognize the solution

Note that \(a=2\) satisfies the equation:

\[
\frac{10}{e^{2}}+1=\frac{10}{7.389}+1\approx 2.353
\quad\text{and}\quad
2\times 2.353\approx 4.706\approx\ln 25 \;(=3.218).
\]

But let's solve the system directly by noticing a simpler approach.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### Direct algebraic manipulation

Take logarithms of (1) and (2) with base \(e\):

\[
x\ln y = 10\ln x \qquad\text{(from (1))} \tag{5}
\]
\[
4y\ln x = 10\ln y \qquad\text{(from (2))} \tag{6}
\]

Rewrite (5) and (6) as

\[
\frac{\ln y}{\ln x} = \frac{10}{x} \tag{7}
\]
\[
\frac{\ln y}{\ln x} = \frac{4y}{10} = \frac{2y}{5} \tag{8}
\]

Equating the right‑hand sides:

\[
\frac{10}{x} = \frac{2y}{5}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
50 = 2xy
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
xy = 25. \tag{9}
\]

Thus the product \(xy\) is \(25\).
