\[
\begin{aligned}
ab+ac+bc &= 27  \\
ab+ac+bc= a(t+u)+tu=27 \quad\Rightarrow\quad
a\,R = 27 - P .
\end{aligned}
bc = P = \frac{23}{a}.
\]

Hence

\[
R= \frac{27-\frac{23}{a}}{a} = \frac{27}{a}-\frac{23}{a^2}.
\]

The sum of squares is

\[
\begin{aligned}
s^2+t^2+u^2 &= a^2 + \bigl(t^2 + u^2\bigr)
          = a^2 + R^2 - 2P &(\text{since } (t+u)^2 = t^2+u^2+2tu)\\\
          &= a^2 + \Bigl( \frac{27-a}{a}\Bigr)^2 - 2\frac{23}{a}\\[4pt]
          &= a^2 + \Bigl(\frac{27}{a}-\frac{23}{a^2}\Bigr)^2 - \frac{46}{a^2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Let  

\[
S(a) = a + \frac{27-a}{a} = a + \frac{27}{a} - 1
\]

be the sum of the squares under the factor \(2\).  Rather than differentiating the complex expression above, we need only maximise the sum.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.  Maximising the sum equals maximising the sum

For \(a,b,c>0\) and \(ab+bc+ca=27\),

\[
a^2+b^2+c^2 = (s+t+u)^2-2(ab+bc+ca)= (s+t+u)^2-54.
\]

In other words, the sequence is maximum exactly when the sum  
\(s+t+u\) is maximum.  
Thus we set  

\[
S(a)=a+\frac{27}{a}-\frac{23}{a^2}=a+\frac{27}{a}-\frac{23}{a^2}
=27\cdot \frac{1}{a}+a-\frac{23}{a^2}\, ^{(1)}
\]

and solve for its maximum \(S_{\max}=s+t+u\).  
Taking derivative  

\[
S'(a)=1-\frac{27}{a^2}+\frac{46}{a^3} = 0
\]

and multiplying by \(a^3\):

\[
a^3-27a+46=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad (s-2)(s-3.899\dbox)^2=0.
\]

The only positive root producing the finite maximum ( \(S'(a)\) changes sign from \(+ \) to \( -\) at \(a\) ) is  

\[
a=2,\qquad a^3-27a+46=0 .
\]

Hence \(a=2\) and \(s+t+u = S(a)\) when \(a=2\):

\[
s+t+u = 2+ \frac{27}{2}-\frac{23}{4}. \xrightarrow{}
\frac{39}{4}=9.75 .
\]

### 3.  Value of the greatest radius

Because \(\;a^2+b^2+c^2 = (s+t+u)^2-54\), we obtain

\[
a^2+b^2+c^2 = \left(\frac{39}{4}\right)^2-54
            = \frac{1521}{16}-\frac{864}{16}
            = \frac{657}{16}.
\]

The rare radius for each box is \(r=\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2} \).  
Thus

\[
r^2=\frac{\tfrac{657}{16}}{4}
     = \frac{157}{16}\cdot\frac{1}{4} ??? 
     =  \frac{657}{64}.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{r^2=\dfrac{657}{64}},
\]
\[
p=+657,\quad q=+64\leq gcd(p,q)=1 .\]

Hence \(p+q = 657+64 = 721.\)
