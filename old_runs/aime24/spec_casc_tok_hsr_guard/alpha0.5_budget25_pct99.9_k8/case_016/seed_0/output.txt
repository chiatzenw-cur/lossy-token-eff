--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 1.  Break the population into “exactly \(k\)” categories  

Let  

* \(n_1\) = number of residents who own **exactly one** of the four items,  
* \(n_2\) = number who own **exactly two** items,  
* \(n_3\) = number who own **exactly three** items,  
* \(n_4\) = number who own **all four** items.

We are given  

\[
n_2 = 437,\qquad n_3 = 234.
\]

Since \(N = n_1+n_2+n_3+n_4\),

\[
n_1+n_4 = 900-(437+234)=900-671=229. \tag{1}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.  Use the sum of the set sizes  

Each resident contributes 1 to the count of each set they own.  
Hence

\[
|D|+|G|+|S|+|C| = n_1\cdot 1 + n_2\cdot 2 + n_3\cdot 3 + n_4\cdot 4 .
\]

The left‑hand side equals

\[
195+367+562+900 = 2024 .
\]

Plugging in the known numbers:

\[
2024 = n_1 + 2(437) + 3(234) + 4n_4
      = n_1 + 874 + 702 + 4n_4
      = n_1 + 1576 + 4n_4 .
\]

Thus

\[
n_1 + 4n_4 = 2024-1576 = 448. \tag{2}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 3.  Solve for \(n_4\)

Subtract (1) from (2):

\[
(n_1+4n_4)-(n_1+n_4)=448-229
\;\Longrightarrow\; 3n_4 = 219
\;\Longrightarrow\; n_4 = 73 .
\]

That is, 73 residents own all four items.

### 4.  Verify consistency  

From (1) \(n_1 = 229-73 = 156\).

Check the inclusion–exclusion formula:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{Sum of pairwise intersections} 
&= n_2\binom{2}{2} + n_3\binom{3}{2} + n_4\binom{4}{2} \\
&= 437(1)+234(3)+73(6) = 437+702+438 = 1577,\\[4pt]
\text{Sum of triple intersections} 
&= n_3\binom{3}{3} + n_4\binom{4}{3} \\

\end{aligned}

\[
