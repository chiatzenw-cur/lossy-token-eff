\[
\log_x(y^x)=10 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y^x=x^{10}
\tag{1}
\]

and  

\[
\log_y(x^{4y})=10 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x^{4y}=y^{10}
\tag{2}
\]

take logarithms (any base) on both sides of (1) and (2):

\[
x\log y = 10\log x \qquad\text{and}\qquad 4y\log x = 10\log y .
\]

Let  

\[
A=\log x,\qquad B=\log y .
\]

Then

\[
x B = 10A ,\qquad 4y A = 10B .
\]

Solve the two equations for \(y\) in terms of \(x\):

\[
B=\frac{10A}{x},\qquad B=\frac{2yA}{5}.
\]

Equating the two expressions for \(B\) (and \(A\neq0\) because \(x>1\)) gives  

\[
\frac{10}{x}=\frac{2y}{5}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y=\frac{25}{x}.
\tag{3}
\]

Thus \(xy=25\).  
(Indeed, substituting \(y=25/x\) into (1) yields \( (25/x)^x=x^{10}\), which is satisfied by the same \(x\) that also satisfies (2).)
