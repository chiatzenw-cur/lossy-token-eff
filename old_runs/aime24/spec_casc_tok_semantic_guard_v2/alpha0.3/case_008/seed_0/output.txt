\[
\log_x(y^x)=10,\qquad \log_y(x^{4y})=10
\]

we may write the equations in exponential form:

\[
y^x = x^{10}, \qquad x^{4y}=y^{10}.
\]

Take natural logarithms:

\[
x\ln y = 10\ln x,\qquad 4y\ln x = 10\ln y.
\]

Let \(a=\ln x\) and \(b=\ln y\).  
Then \(x=e^a,\; y=e^b\) and the equations become

\[
e^a\,b = 10a,\qquad 4e^b\,a = 10b.
\]

From the first equation \(b = \dfrac{10a}{e^a}\).  
From the second equation \(a = \dfrac{10b}{4e^b}= \dfrac{5}{2}\dfrac{b}{e^b}\).

Substituting the expression for \(a\) into the expression for \(b\):

\[
b = \frac{10}{e^a}\left(\frac{5}{2}\frac{b}{e^b}\right)
     = \frac{25\,b}{e^{a+b}}.
\]

Since \(b>0\), cancel \(b\):

\[
1 = \frac{25}{e^{a+b}}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad e^{a+b}=25.
\]

But \(a+b=\ln x +\ln y = \ln(xy)\), so

\[
xy = 25.
\]

\boxed{25}
