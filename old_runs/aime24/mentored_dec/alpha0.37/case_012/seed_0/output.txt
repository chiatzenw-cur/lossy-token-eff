--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 1.   Direction patterns

The segments must alternate between \(R\) and \(U\).  
There are two possible start directions:

1. \(R\;U\;R\;U\;R\)  
2. \(U\;R\;U\;R\;U\)

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.   Counting the lengths

Each pattern must contain exactly 8 steps of each direction.

---

#### Pattern 1: \(R\,U\,R\,U\,R\)

Let  

\[
\begin{aligned}
r_1,r_3,r_5 &>0 \quad(\text{R segments}),\\
u_2,u_4 &>0 \quad(\text{U segments}).
\end{aligned}
\]

The constraints are  

\[
r_1+r_3+r_5 =8 , \qquad u_2+u_4 = 8 .
\]

* The number of positive integer solutions of \(r_1+r_3+r_5 = 8\) is   
\(\displaystyle \binom{8-1}{3-1}= \binom{7}{2}=21\).

* The number of positive integer solutions of \(u_2+u_4 = 8\) is  
\(\displaystyle \binom{8-1}{2-1}= \binom{7}{1}=7\).

Hence the number of paths for this pattern is  
\(21 \times 7 = 147\).

---

#### Pattern 2: \(U\,R\,U\,R\,U\)

Now let  

\[
\begin{aligned}
u_1,u_3,u_5 &>0 \quad(\text{U segments}),\\
r_2,r_4 &>0 \quad(\text{R segments}).
\end{aligned}
\]

The constraints are  

\[
u_1+u_3+u_5 =8 ,\qquad r_2+r_4 = 8 .
\]

The numbers of solutions are the same as above:
\(21\) for the U‑segments and \(7\) for the R‑segments, giving again  
\(21 \times 7 = 147\) paths.

Adding the two patterns,

147 + 147 = 294 .
