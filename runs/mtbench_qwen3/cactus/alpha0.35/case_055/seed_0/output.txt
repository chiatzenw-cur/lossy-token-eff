**What is a Language Model?**  
A language model is an artificial intelligence system that learns how humans use language. It can understand, generate, and respond to text, like writing emails, answering questions, or creating stories. Think of it as a "digital brain" that studies vast amounts of text to figure out how words connect and what they mean in different contexts.

**Is it trained using labeled or unlabeled data?**  
Language models are trained on **unlabeled data**—this means they learn from huge amounts of text without needing specific " labels" (like answers to questions). Instead, they analyze patterns in how people write and speak to understand grammar, vocabulary, and meaning. For example, they might study millions of books, articles, and conversations to figure out how to complete sentences or generate coherent responses.  

**Why unlabeled data?**  
They don’t need pre-labeled answers because their goal is to learn **general patterns in language**, not to solve specific tasks. Just like a child learns to speak through listening to conversations, not by being told "this is the correct answer," the model learns from the natural flow of real-world text.  

**Simpler Example:**  
Imagine you’re teaching a child how to write. You give them many books to read and let them absorb stories, grammar, and styles. They don’t need strict instructions like "this is a noun adipiscing," but instead learn communication by understanding how words and sentences work in everyday use.
