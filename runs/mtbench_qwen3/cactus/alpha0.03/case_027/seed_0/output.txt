Photosynthesis occurs in two main stages: the **light-dependent reactions** and the **Calvin cycle (light-independent reactions)**. These stages take place in different parts of the chloroplast and have distinct inputs and outputs.

---

### **1. Light-Dependent Reactions**  
**Location**: Thylakoid membranes (within the chloroplast).  
**Inputs**:  
- **Light energy** (sunlight)  
- **Water** (purchase, H₂O)  
- **Chlorophyll** (to absorb light)  
- **ADP** and **NADP⁺** (to create ATP and NADPH)  
**Outputs**:  
- **Oxygen (O₂)** (from splitting water)  
- **Adenosine triphosphate (ATP)**  
- **NADPH** (a high-energy molecule)  
**Process**:  
- Light energy is absorbed by chlorophyll in photosystems (PSII and PSI).  
- Water is split into oxygen (released as a byproduct), protons (H⁺), and electrons.  
- The electrons and protons are used to produce ATP and NADPH via the electron transport chain.  

---

### **2. Calvin Cycle (Dark Reactions)**  
**Location**: Stroma (fluid-filled space inside the chloroplast).  
**Inputs**:  
- **Carbon dioxide (CO₂)**  
- **ATP** (from light reactions)  
- **NADPH** (from light reactions)  
**Outputs**:  
- **Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆)** (and other sugars)  
- **ADP** and **NADP⁺** (as ATP and NADPH are used up)  
**Process**:  
- CO₂ is fixed into a 5-carbon molecule (RuBP) via the enzyme **RuBisCO**.  
- The energy from ATP and NADPH is used to convert the fixed carbon into glucose and other organic molecules.  
- ADP and NADP⁺ are recycled back to the thylakoid for ATP and NADPH production.  

---

### **Summary Table**  

| **Stage**           | **Location**       | **Inputs**                  | **Outputs**                |  
|---------------------|--------------------|-----------------------------|----------------------------|  
| **Light-Dependent** | Thylakoid membranes | Light, H₂O, ADP, NADP⁺       | O₂, ATP, NADPH              |  
| **Calvin Cycle**    | Stroma             | CO₂, ATP, NADPH              | Glucose, ADP, NADP⁺         |  

This process ultimately converts sunlight, water, and CO₂ into glucose (energy storage) and oxygen (released into the atmosphere).
