The **central dogma of molecular biology** is a foundational concept that describes the flow of genetic information within biological systems. It was formulated by **Francis Crick** in 1957 and is often summarized as the direction of information transfer from **DNA to RNA to protein**. This framework outlines the fundamental processes by which genetic sequences are converted into functional molecules.

### Key Concepts:
1. **DNA to RNA (Transcription)**:  
   - DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) carries the genetic instructions for the synthesis of mRNA (messenger RNA) through a process called **transcription**. The enzyme **RNA polymerase** reads the DNA template and synthesizes a complementary RNA strand.  
   - **Function**: mRNA acts as a temporary copy of the DNA instructions, which is then transported to the ribosome for protein synthesis.

2. **RNA to Protein (Translation)**:  
   - The mRNA is decoded by ribosomes during **translation** to produce proteins. Transfer RNA (tRNA) molecules bring amino acids to the ribosome, matching the codons (three-nucleotide sequences) on the mRNA.  
   - **Function**: Proteins are the functional units of the cell, performing most biological roles.

### Exceptions and Clarifications:
- **Reverse Transcription**:  
  Some viruses, such as retroviruses (e.g., HIV), use **reverse transcriptase** to convert RNA into DNA. This process (RNA → DNA) diverges from the central dogma but is noted as an exception.  
  - **Note**: Crick explicitly stated that such exceptions did not contradict the central dogma, which remains about the **unidirectional (from DNA to protein) flow** in most organisms.

### Processes Involved:
- **Transcription** (DNA → RNA)  
- **Translation** (RNA → Protein)  
- **DNA Replication** (DNA → DNA): While not part of the central dogma itself, DNA replication ensures the faithful copying of genetic material during cell division, supporting the continuity of information flow.

### Who Named It:
- **Francis Crick**, a key figure in the discovery of DNA's double-helix structure (alongside James Watson and Rosalind Franklin), coined the term "central dogma" in a 1957 paper titled *"Central Dogma of Molecular Biology."*  

### Summary:
The central dogma establishes the core mechanism of genetic information flow: **DNA (genetic blueprint) → mRNA (messenger) → Protein (functional product)**. Other flows (e.g., reverse transcription) are exceptions and are part of specialized biological processes. Karl Crick's formulation remains a cornerstone of molecular biology.
