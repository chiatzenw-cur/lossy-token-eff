**Balanced Chemical Equation:**

$$
\text{CaCO}_3 (s) + 2\text{HCl} (aq) \rightarrow \text{CaCl}_2 (aq) + \text{CO}_2 (g) + \text{H}_2\text{O} (l)
$$

**Type of Reaction:**  
This is a **double displacement (or metathesis) reaction**. It also involves **acid-carbonate reactions**, where the acid (HCl) reacts with the carbonate ion (CO₃²⁻) from calcium carbonate, producing a salt (calcium chloride), water, and carbon dioxide gas.

**Observations Indicating the Reaction:**  
1. **Bubbling/effervescence:** The release of carbon dioxide gas (CO₂) is evident as bubbles form in the solution.  
2. **Dissolution of solid calcium carbonate:** The white solid CaCO₃ gradually dissolves, indicating the reaction is occurring.  
3. **Possible temperature change:** The reaction might be slightly exothermic, causing a warm sensation.  
4. **Formation of a clear solution:** Harsh calcium chloride (CaCl₂) is soluble in water, so the solution becomes clear as the reaction progresses.  

**Explanation:**  
Calcium carbonate (CaCO₃) reacts with hydrochloric acid (HCl) to produce aqueous calcium chloride (CaCl₂), carbon dioxide gas (CO₂), and water (H₂O). The balanced equation shows that two moles of HCl are required to react with one mole of calcium carbonate. The reaction is a classic example of an acid-base reaction, where the H⁺ ions from HCl neutralize the CO₃²⁻ ions to release CO₂ and H₂O. The dissolution of the solid and the steady release of gas are key physical indicators of the reaction.
