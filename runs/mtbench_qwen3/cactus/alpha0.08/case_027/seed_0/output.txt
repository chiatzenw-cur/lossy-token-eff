Photosynthesis is a vital process that occurs in the chloroplasts of plant cells, converting light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose. It is divided into two main stages:

---

### **1. Light-Dependent Reactions**  
- **Location:** Thylakoid membranes of the chloroplast  
- **Inputs:**  
  - Light energy (sunlight)  
  - Water (H₂O)  
  - Adenosine diphosphate (ADP) and inorganic phosphate (Pi)  
- **Outputs:**  
  - Adenosine triphosphate (ATP)  
  - Nyadophosphate (NADPH)  
  - Oxygen (O₂) as a byproduct  
- **Process:**  
  - Light energy is absorbed by chlorophyll and other pigments in the thylakoid membrane.  
  - Water molecules are split (photolysis), releasing oxygen (O₂), protons (H⁺), and electrons.  
  - The absorbed light energy drives the synthesis of ATP (via chemiosmosis) and NADPH (via the electron transport chain). 

---

### **2. Calvin Cycle (Light-Independent Reactions)**  
- **Location:** Stroma of the chloroplast  
- **Inputs:**  
  - Carbon dioxide (CO₂)  
  - ATP (from the light reactions)  
  - NADPH (from the light reactions)  
- **Outputs:**  
  - Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) or other sugar molecules  
  - Adenosine diphosphate (ADP) and inorganic phosphate (Pi) (used to regenerate ATP)  
- **Process:**  
  - CO₂ is fixed into a five-carbon sugar (RuBP) using the enzyme RuBisCO, forming a six-carbon compound that splits into two three-carbon molecules (3-PGA).  
  - ATP and NADPH provide energy and reducing power to convert these molecules into glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (G3P), which can be used to synthesize glucose.  
  - The cycle regenerates RuBP and consumes ATP and NADPH, decreasing their levels. 

---

### **Summary of Inputs and Outputs**
- **Light-Dependent Reactions:**  
  - **Inputs:** Light, H₂O, ADP + Pi  
  - **Outputs:** ATP, NADPH, O₂  
- **Calvin Cycle:**  
  - **Inputs:** CO₂, ATP, NADPH  
  - **Outputs:** Glucose (or G3P), ADP + Pi  

These stages work together to convert light energy into chemical energy (e.g., glucose) and release oxygen as a byproduct, which is critical for life on Earth.
