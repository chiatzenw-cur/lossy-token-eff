**Lesson Plan: The Opium Wars through Drama and Theater Techniques**  
**Grade Level:** 9–10  
**Duration:** 3 class periods (45 minutes each)  
**Topic:** The Opium Wars (1839–1842 and 1856–1860) between China and Britain  
**Learning Objectives:**  
- Understand the causes, key events, and consequences of the Opium Wars.  
- Analyze perspectives of Chinese and British stakeholders.  
- Use drama, mime, and theater techniques to explore historical events and empathy.  
- Develop critical thinking and collaborative skills through creative expression.  

---

### **Day 1: Exploring the Context & Introducing Drama Techniques**  
**Materials:**  
- Short video on the Opium Wars (e.g., BBC/History Channel clip).  
- Primary source excerpts (e.g., Lin Zexu’s letters, British trade records).  
- Visual aids (maps, images of key locations: Canton, Hong Kong, Humen).  
- Optional: mime props (e.g., claymation "opium pipes," symbolic objects).  

**Activities:**  
1. **Introduction (10 minutes):**  
   - Begin with a 5-minute video overview of the Opium Wars.  
   - Discuss: *What caused the conflict? What were the stakes?*  

2. **Primary Source Analysis (10 minutes):**  
   - Students read excerpts from Chinese and British documents (e.g., Lin Zexu’s demand to destroy opium, British trade reports).  
   - Ask: *How do these sources reflect differing motivations?*  

3. **Drama Warm-Up: Mime & Symbolism (15 minutes):**  
   - Practice mime to depict scenes:  
     - A British opium trader smuggling opium into China.  
     - Chinese officials destroying opium at Humen.  
     - The economic and social impact of opium addiction (use props for visual storytelling).  
   - Students work in pairs to create 2–3 symbolic mime scenes, then share with the class.  

4. **Class Discussion & Reflection (10 minutes):**  
   - How did mime help convey the tension between economic gain and human cost?  
   - What biases might exist in historical records?  

**Homework/Prep for Day 2:**  
- Students research a historical figure (e.g., Lin Zexu, Queen Victoria, Lord Elgin) or event (e.g., Treaty of Nanking, Burning of the Summer Palace) and write a 1-page persona summary.  

---

### **Day 2: Role-Play & Skit Creation**  
**Materials:**  
- Role-play cards (e.g., "British Opium Trader," "Qing Official," "Opium Addict").  
- Art supplies (for creating simple props or posters).  
- Timer for group work.  

**Activities:**  
1. **Role-Play Brainstorming (10 minutes):**  
   - Groups of 4–5 students are assigned perspectives (e.g., British traders, Chinese peasants, diplomats).  
   - Using their persona summaries, they debate a scenario: *Should opium trade be restricted?*  

2. **Skit Creation (20 minutes):**  
   - Groups craft a 5-minute skit dramatizing a key event (e.g., Lin Zexu’s confiscation of opium, the Battle of Humen).  
   - Encourage use of dialogue, gestures, and props to represent historical events.  
   - Example: A group might portray the 1839 Opium Destruction, with one student as Lin Zexu and others as traders or soldiers.  

3. **Peer Feedback (5 minutes):**  
   - Groups present their skits to the class.  
   - Class discusses: *How did the skit highlight power dynamics or cultural conflicts?*  

**Homework/Prep for Day 3:**  
- Refine skit based on feedback.  

---

### **Day 3: Performance & Reflection**  
**Materials:**  
- Stage setup (e.g., classroom space with zones for scenes).  
- Reflection worksheets.  

**Activities:**  
1. **Skit Performances (20 minutes):**  
   - Groups perform their skits. Encourage creative use of space, voice, and expression.  
   - Include a dramatic reading of a primary source (e.g., a Qing decree or a British manifesto).  

2. **Class Discussion & Analysis (15 minutes):**  
   - Discuss the outcomes of the wars: Treaty of Nanking, Hong Kong cession, forced open ports.  
   - Ask: *How did the skits reveal the human cost of colonialism?*  

3. **Reflection & Debrief (10 minutes):**  
   - Students write or discuss:  
     - *What did you learn about empathy through role-play?*  
     - *How might history be different if the Opium Wars had ended differently?*  
   - Optional: Share a short "theater of history" reflection (e.g., a poem or visual art inspired by the skits).  

**Assessment:**  
- Participation in activities, skit creativity, and reflection quality.  
- Rubric: Include criteria for historical accuracy, collaboration, and use of drama techniques.  

---

**Differentiation & Extensions:**  
- **For advanced learners:** Research lesser-known figures (e.g., Chinese rebels, Indian soldiers in the British army).  
- **For struggling learners:** Use visual aids and scripted dialogue to scaffold skit creation.  
- **For artistic learners:** Encourage creative props or music to enhance performances.  

**Standards Alignment:**  
- **CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.RH.9-10.7:** Analyze primary and secondary sources.  
- **CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.SL.9-10.5:** Evaluate speakers’ perspectives and evidence.  
- **CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.W.9-10.7:** Conduct research and present findings through creative formats.  

This lesson plan blends historical analysis with creative expression, helping students connect emotionally and intellectually to the Opium Wars. 🌏🎭
