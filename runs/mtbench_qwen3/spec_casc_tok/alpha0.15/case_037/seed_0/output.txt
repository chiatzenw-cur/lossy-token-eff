1. **Oranges cost more than apples.**

     $$ O > A $$

   - This can be represented as:  
     $$ O < B $$

3. **Bananas cost more than apples and bananas cost more than oranges.**

     $$ B > A \text{ and } B > O $$

---

- $ O > A $ (from Statement 1)
- $ O < B $ (from Statement 2)

$$
B > O > A \Rightarrow B > A
$$

This means that **bananas cost more than apples** as well.

Now, we examine the conclusion:

- **Bananas cost more than apples** → $ B > A $ (already established)
- **Bananas cost more than oranges** → $ B > O $ (given by Statement 2)

Both components of the third statement are therefore **supported** by the first two.

---

Because both components of the third statement follow logically and necessarily from the truth of the first two, the third statement is **true**.
