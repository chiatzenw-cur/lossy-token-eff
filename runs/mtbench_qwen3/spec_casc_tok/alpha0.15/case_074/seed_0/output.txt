**Balanced Chemical Equation:**  
$$ \text{CaCO}_3 (s) + 2\text{HCl} (aq) \rightarrow \text{CaCl}_2 (aq) + \text{CO}_2 (g) + \text{H}_2\text{O} (l) $$

**Type of Reaction:**  
This is an **acid-carbonate reaction**, which falls under the broader category of **neutralization reactions** (since the carbonate acts as a base). It can also be classified as a **double displacement reaction** due to the exchange of ions between the reactants.

**Process Description:**  
When solid calcium carbonate (CaCO₃) reacts with hydrochloric acid (HCl), the acid donates protons (H⁺) to the carbonate ion (CO₃²⁻). This forms carbonic acid (H₂CO₃), which is unstable and immediately decomposes into carbon dioxide gas (CO₂) and water (H₂O). Simultaneously, the calcium ions (Ca²⁺) from the carbonate combine with chloride ions (Cl⁻) from the acid to form aqueous calcium chloride (CaCl₂).

**Observations:**  
1. **Effervescence (Bubbling):** The release of carbon dioxide gas causes visible bubbles in the reaction mixture.  
2. **Dissolution of Solid:** The solid calcium carbonate gradually dissolves as the reaction proceeds.  
3. **Temperature Change:** The reaction is exothermic, so the solution may warm up.  
4. **Color Change:** If the solution is initially colored, it might lose the color as the carbonate is consumed, but calcium chloride is colorless.  

**Key Points:**  
- The reaction is a classic example of an acid-carbonate interaction.  
- The balanced equation ensures the conservation of mass and charge.  
- Observations such as bubbling and dissolution are strong indicators of the reaction's progress.
