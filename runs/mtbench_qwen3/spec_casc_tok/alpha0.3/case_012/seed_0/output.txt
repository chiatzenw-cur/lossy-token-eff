**Lesson Plan: Integrating Drama and Theater Techniques into the Study of the Opium Wars**  
**Grade Level:** 9–10  
**Duration:** 3 Class Periods (45 minutes each)  
**Topic:** Opium Wars (1839–1842 and 1856–1860)  
**Objective:** Students will analyze the causes, key events, and consequences of the Opium Wars through drama, mime, and role-play, fostering empathy, critical thinking, and historical understanding.  

---

### **Day 1: The Opium Trade and Its Consequences**  
**Objective:** Understand the role of the opium trade in sparking conflict between China and Britain.  

**Materials:**  
- Projector for historical images (e.g., opium trade routes, Qing Dynasty maps).  
- Optional: props for mime (boxes, traditional clothing).  

**Activities:**  
1. **Warm-Up (10 minutes):**  
   - Quick discussion: “How do trade routes impact a country’s economy and politics?”  
   - Show images of 19th-century trade routes and ask students to hypothesize about potential conflicts.  

2. **Lecture (15 minutes):**  
   - Explain the British East India Company’s role in the opium trade, the economic motivations, and the social impact of opium addiction in China.  
   - Introduce Lin Zexu’s role in banning opium and the resulting tensions.  

3. **Mime Activity (20 minutes):**  
   - Divide students into small groups. Each group creates a short mime performance depicting:  
     - The opium trade (smuggling, shipping, and selling).  
     - The effects of opium addiction (physical and societal).  
     - Chinese authorities’ attempts to ban opium (e.g., confiscation, enforcement).  
   - Encourage creative use of gestures, expressions, and spatial movement.  

4. **Discussion (5 minutes):**  
   - Ask: “How did the opium trade create a cycle of conflict?” Highlight the economic and human costs.  

**Assessment:** Observational feedback on mime creativity and participation.  
**Homework:** Research the lives of two key figures (e.g., Lin Zexu, Queen Victoria) and prepare a 1-minute role-play monologue.  

---

### **Day 2: The First Opium War (1839–1842)**  
**Objective:** Explore the causes, events, and outcomes of the First Opium War.  

**Materials:**  
- Role-play scripts (optional) for key scenes.  
- Historical documents (e.g., excerpts from Lin Zexu’s letters, British naval strategies).  

**Activities:**  
1. **Warm-Up (10 minutes):**  
   - Quick review of Day 1: “What were the key tensions between China and Britain before the war?”  

2. **Lecture (15 minutes):**  
   - Overview of the First Opium War: Lin Zexu’s actions, British naval response, key battles (e.g., Battle of Humen), and the Treaty of Nanking.  

3. **Role-Play Activity (20 minutes):**  
   - Divide students into groups: Chinese officials, British traders, and the British government.  
   - Script a dramatic scene: Lin Zexu confronts British traders, demanding opium be destroyed. Simulate the debate between enforcing bans and protecting trade interests.  
   - Encourage students to use historical documents to justify their roles.  

4. **Analysis (5 minutes):**  
   - Discuss: “What were the unintended consequences of the war?” Focus on China’s loss of sovereignty and economic exploitation.  

**Assessment:** Evaluate participation in role-play and understanding of historical causes.  
**Homework:** Write a reflection on the Treaty of Nanking’s impact on China’s economy and sovereignty.  

---

### **Day 3: The Second Opium War and the Aftermath**  
**Objective:** Analyze the Second Opium War and its long-term effects on China.  

**Materials:**  
- Maps showing Treaty of Tientsin’s territorial changes.  
- Props for mime (e.g., flags, documents).  

**Activities:**  
1. **Warm-Up (10 minutes):**  
   - Review Day 2: “Why did the Second Opium War occur?”  

2. **Lecture (15 minutes):**  
   - Explain the Second Opium War’s causes (British-French aggression over the Taiping Rebellion), key events (e.g., burning of the Summer Palace), and the Treaty of Tientsin.  

3. **Mime Activity (20 minutes):**  
   - Students create a group mime performance depicting:  
     - The aftermath of the treaties (foreign control over China’s ports, missionary activity).  
     - The loss of Chinese autonomy (e.g., foreign troops, unequal trade agreements).  
     - Contrast pre-war and post-war China (use of space and movement to show change).  

4. **Reflection (5 minutes):**  
   - Group discussion: “How did the Opium Wars reshape China’s role in global trade and politics?”  

**Assessment:** Evaluate mime creativity and depth of reflection.  
**Homework:** Create a visual timeline of the Opium Wars and their consequences.  

---

### **Cross-Curricular Integration and Differentiation**  
- **Drama Techniques:** Use improvisation, character development, and spatial storytelling.  
- **Mime:** For visual learners, focus on non-verbal communication to depict historical events.  
- **Adaptations:** Provide script outlines for role-play, and allow verbal descriptions for students with physical limitations.  

### **Assessment Strategies**  
- **Formative:** Participation in activities, peer feedback, and reflection writing.  
- **Summative:** Final visual timeline or a short essay analyzing the Opium Wars’ historical significance.  

### **Standards Alignment**  
- **Common Core:** CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.RH.9-10.2 (analyze historical events) and CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.RH.9-10.6 (evaluate historical sources).  
- **NGSS:** Interdisciplinary focus on cause-and-effect in global history.  

This lesson plan combines active engagement with historical analysis, helping students connect emotionally and intellectually to the Opium Wars through performance.
