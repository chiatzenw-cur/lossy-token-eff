**Lesson Plan: Opium Wars Through Drama and Theater Techniques**  
**Grade Level:** 9-10  
**Duration:** 3 class periods (45 minutes each)  
**Topic:** The Opium Wars between China and Britain  

---

### **Day 1: Introduction to the Opium Wars and Mime Activity**  
**Objective:** Students will understand the historical context and key events of the Opium Wars through a visual, non-verbal mime activity.  

**Activities:**  
1. **Opening Discussion (10 min):**  
   - Briefly introduce the Opium Wars (1839–1842, 1856–1860).  
   - Ask students: *What do you know about opium? How might it have affected trade and society?*  

2. **Mime Activity: "The Opium Trade" (20 min):**  
   - Divide students into small groups. Assign each group a role:  
     - **British Traders:** Act out the smuggling of opium into China.  
     - **Chinese Officials:** Depict efforts to ban opium (e.g., Commissioner Lin Zexu’s actions).  
     - **Chinese Citizens:** Show the impact of opium addiction on families and society.  
   - Students use mime to convey events without dialogue (e.g., opium being packed, soldiers burning stockpiles, villagers struggling with addiction).  
   - Groups perform their scenes for the class.  

3. **Wrap-Up (15 min):**  
   - Discuss the visual and emotional impact of the mime.  
   - Introduce the concept of "historical empathy" and encourage students to think about perspectives.  

---

### **Day 2: Role-Play and Perspective Analysis**  
**Objective:** Students will analyze the causes and consequences of the Opium Wars through role-play and debate.  

**Activities:**  
1. **Historical Context Review (10 min):**  
   - Quick recap of the Opium Wars: trade imbalances, British interests, and the Qing Dynasty’s resistance.  
   - Show a short video clip (1–2 minutes) of key events (e.g., the Opium Wars, Treaty of Nanking).  

2. **Role-Play Activity: "The Opium Debate" (25 min):**  
   - Assign roles:  
     - **British Merchants:** Argue for the right to trade opium.  
     - **Qing Officials:** Defend China’s ban on opium.  
     - **Foreign Diplomats (e.g., U.S. or France):** Highlight global implications.  
     - **Chinese Citizens:** Share personal experiences of addiction.  
   - Students prepare 1–2 key points in their role.  
   - Conduct a mock debate or forum with students taking turns presenting their positions. Encourage use of dramatic gestures, tone, and eye contact to emphasize arguments.  

3. **Reflection (10 min):**  
   - Discuss the complexity of the conflict. Ask: *Who benefits? Who suffers? How might history be interpreted differently?*  

---

### **Day 3: Creative Performance and Reflection**  
**Objective:** Students will create a collaborative play or skit to dramatize the Opium Wars and reflect on its historical significance.  

**Activities:**  
1. **Script Workshop (15 min):**  
   - In groups, students brainstorm a 5–7 minute skit. Must include:  
     - Key events (e.g., Lin Zexu’s destruction of opium, the Battle of Tianjin, the Treaty of Nanking).  
     - Multiple perspectives (e.g., a Chinese soldier, a British trader, a commoner).  
     - Use of theater techniques (e.g., monologues, tableau, mime).  

2. **Rehearsal and Performance (20 min):**  
   - Groups rehearse and perform their skits. Encourage creative use of props, costuming, and dramatic techniques.  
   - Allow 5 minutes for each group to perform.  

3. **Class Discussion and Assessment (10 min):**  
   - After performances, ask: *How did acting help you understand the Opium Wars? What surprised you?*  
   - Distribute a short quiz (5 questions) or reflection worksheet (e.g., *What did you learn about power dynamics?*).  
   - Optional: Share performances with a different class or grade level.  

---

**Materials Needed:**  
- Video clips (optional)  
- Props (e.g., paper opium, maps, costumes)  
- Worksheets for reflection and assessment  
- Timer for activities  

**Assessment:**  
- Participation in mime and role-play activities (formative).  
- Quiz/reflection worksheet (summative).  
- Creativity and historical accuracy in skits (formative).  

**Differentiation:**  
- Provide simplified scripts for struggling students.  
- Offer advanced challenges for students who want to explore deeper (e.g., analyzing primary sources like the Treaty of Nanking).  

**Extension Ideas:**  
- Compare the Opium Wars to modern conflicts over trade or drug policy.  
- Invite a drama teacher to assist with technical aspects of the skits.  

This plan combines active learning, empathy-building, and creativity to deepen students' understanding of the Opium Wars. 🌏🎭
