Photosynthesis is a critical process that converts light energy into chemical energy, primarily occurring in the chloroplasts of plant cells. It consists of two main stages, each with distinct locations, inputs, and outputs:

---

### **1. Light-Dependent Reactions**  
- **Location:** Thylakoid membranes of the chloroplast.  
- **Inputs:**  
  - **Light energy** (absorbed by chlorophyll and other pigments).  
  - **Water (H₂O)**.  
- **Outputs:**  
  - **Adenosine triphosphate (ATP)**.  
  - **Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH)**.  
  - **Oxygen (O₂)** (released as a byproduct when water is split).  

**Key Process:**  
The light-dependent reactions use light energy to split water molecules, generating electrons, protons (H⁺), and oxygen. The electrons are transferred through the electron transport chain, creating a proton gradient that drives ATP synthesis (via chemiosmosis). NADPH is formed as electrons reduce NADP⁺.

---

### **2. Calvin Cycle (Light-Independent Reactions)**  
- **Location:** Stroma of the chloroplast.  
- **Inputs:**  
  - **Carbon dioxide (CO₂)**.  
  - **ATP** (produced in the light-dependent reactions).  
  - **NADPH** (produced in the light-dependent reactions).  
- **Outputs:**  
  - **Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆)** (and other sugars, such as glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate, G3P).  

**Key Process:**  
The Calvin cycle uses ATP and NADPH to fix CO₂ into organic molecules. Through a series of enzymatic reactions, CO₂ is incorporated into a 5-carbon molecule (ribulose bisphosphate, RuBP) to form 3-phosphoglycerate (3-PGA), which is then converted into G3P. For every 3 CO₂ molecules fixed, the cycle produces one glucose molecule (via 6 turns of the cycle) and regenerates RuBP to continue the process.

---

### **Summary**  
- **Light-Dependent Reactions (Thylakoid):**  
  - **Inputs:** Light, water.  
  - **Outputs:** ATP, NADPH, oxygen.  
- **Calvin Cycle (Stroma):**  
  - **Inputs:** CO₂, ATP, NADPH.  
  - **Outputs:** Glucose (and other sugars).  

These stages work together to convert light energy and carbon dioxide into glucose, sustaining life on Earth.
