**Superposition in Quantum Physics**  
In quantum physics, **superposition** is a fundamental principle that allows a quantum system to exist in multiple states simultaneously until measured. This is mathematically represented by a **wave function**, which is a linear combination of all possible states. For example, a quantum bit (qubit) can be in a superposition of the basis states $ |0\rangle $ and $ |1\rangle $, written as:  
$$
|\psi\rangle = \alpha|0\rangle + \beta|1\rangle,
$$  
where $ \alpha $ and $ \beta $ are complex coefficients representing the probabilities of measuring the system in each state. Until observed, the system does not "choose" a definite state—instead, it exists in a probabilistic combination of all possibilities.

---

**Quantum Entanglement**  
**Quantum entanglement** describes a phenomenon where two or more particles become correlated in such a way that the state of one particle cannot be described independently of the others, even when separated by large distances. This correlation persists regardless of the spatial separation between entangled particles. A classic example is the **Bell state** for two qubits:  
$$
|\Phi^+\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|0\rangle|0\rangle + |1\rangle|1\rangle),
$$  
which is a superposition of the combined states $ |0\rangle|0\rangle $ and $ |1\rangle|1\rangle $. When one qubit is measured, the state of the other is instantaneously determined, despite the distance between them. This non-local correlation defies classical intuition and underpins many quantum technologies.

---

**Connection Between Superposition and Entanglement**  
1. **Superposition as a Building Block**:  
   Entanglement arises when the combined state of multiple particles is a **superposition of their individual states**. For instance, the Bell state above is a superposition of the two particles' states, creating a dependency between them. Thus, entanglement is a type of superposition involving composite systems.  

2. **Measurement and Collapse**:  
   When a measurement is made on an entangled system, the superposition collapses, and the states of the entangled particles are correlated. For example, measuring one qubit in the Bell state $ |\Phi^+\rangle $ instantaneously determines the state of the other, even if they are light-years apart. This highlights how superposition (the general principle) and entanglement (the specific correlation) interact during measurement.  

3. **Distinct but Related Concepts**:  
   - **Superposition** applies to a single particle or system, allowing it to exist in multiple states.  
   - **Entanglement** involves multiple particles sharing a superposition of states, making their individual states dependent on each other.  
   While superposition is a prerequisite for entanglement, not all superpositions are entangled. Entanglement introduces a deeper layer of correlation that cannot be explained by classical superposition alone.

**Implications and Applications**  
- **Superposition**: Enables phenomena like quantum computing, where qubits process multiple possibilities simultaneously.  
- **Entanglement**: Is crucial for quantum teleportation, quantum cryptography, and quantum networks.  
- **Interplay**: The relationship between superposition and entanglement is central to quantum mechanics, illustrating how quantum systems defy classical explanations and open new avenues for technology and fundamental science.
