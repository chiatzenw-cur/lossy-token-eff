### **Step 1: Clarify the Conflict (What’s the Core Issue?)**
Ask yourself:  
- What *specific* issue is causing tension? (e.g., financial stress, parenting disagreements, feeling misunderstood, or unresolved past hurts)  
- Is it a recurring problem, or a one-time disagreement?  
- Are there underlying emotional needs or unmet expectations?  

*Example:* If the conflict is about household responsibilities, it might stem from feelings of resentment, lack of appreciation, or differing values about work-life balance.  

---

### **Step 2: Foster Safe Communication**  
Conflicts often escalate when either partner feels unheard or attacked. Use these techniques to create space for dialogue:  
1. **Active Listening**:  
   - When your spouse speaks, give them your full attention (eye contact, nodding, paraphrasing what they said).  
   - Avoid interrupting or planning your rebuttal. Focus on understanding their feelings *and* their perspective.  
   - Example: *“It sounds like you’re feeling overwhelmed when I don’t help with chores. Is that right?”*  

2. **“I” Statements vs. “You” Statements**:  
   - Frame your concerns without blaming.  
   - Example: *“I feel hurt when we don’t talk about our plans for the weekend”* instead of *“You never listen to me.”*  

3. **Pause and Reflect**:  
   - If emotions run high, agree to take a short break (5–10 minutes) to cool down. Return with a calm mindset.  

---

### **Step 3: Explore Each Other’s Perspectives**  
Ask open-ended questions to uncover deeper needs:  
- *“What does this situation mean for you?”*  
- *“How do you feel when this happens?”*  
- *“What would make this feel better for you?”*  

Sometimes, conflicts arise from misunderstandings or unspoken assumptions. This step helps bridge the gap by fostering empathy.  

---

### **Step 4: Identify Shared Goals**  
Even in conflict, couples often share underlying values (e.g., love, respect, partnership). Reconnect with these:  
- *“We both want to feel supported, right?”*  
- *“Let’s figure out how we can solve this together.”*  

Focusing on shared goals can shift the conversation from “winning” to “collaborating.”  

---

### **Step 5: Find Compromises or Solutions**  
Brainstorm options that respect both perspectives:  
- *“What if we try [idea A] for a week and see how it works?”*  
- *“Can we agree to [specific action] to address this?”*  

If resolution feels stuck, consider:  
- **Setting boundaries** (e.g., “We’ll not bring up this topic during meals”).  
- **Seeking external support** (e.g., couples therapy, a mediator, or a trusted friend).  

---

### **Step 6: Rebuild Connection Post-Conflict**  
After resolving the issue, take time to reconnect:  
- Share appreciation for each other’s efforts.  
- Engage in a low-stakes activity (e.g., a walk, a shared hobby, or a simple “I love you” message).  
- Reaffirm your commitment to the relationship.
