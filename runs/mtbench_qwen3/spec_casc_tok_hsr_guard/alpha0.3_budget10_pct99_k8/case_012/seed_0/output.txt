**Lesson Plan: The Opium Wars – A Dramatic Exploration of History**  
**Grade Level:** 9–10  
**Duration:** 3 class periods (45 minutes each)  
**Subject:** History  
**Topics:** Causes, events, and consequences of the Opium Wars (1839–1842 and 1856–1860)  

---

### **Learning Objectives:**  
By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:  
1. Identify the causes, key events, and consequences of the Opium Wars.  
2. Analyze the perspectives of stakeholders (Chinese officials, British traders, imperial powers).  
3. Apply drama and mime techniques to deepen historical understanding and empathy.  
4. Collaborate in creative problem-solving and performative expression.  

---

### **Materials Needed:**  
- Printed primary source documents (e.g., extracts from Lin Zexu’s letter to Queen Victoria, treaties, or British parliamentary debates).  
- Simple props (e.g., paper opium pipes, maps, flags, or symbolic items).  
- Costume accessories (optional, e.g., hats, robes, or paper "helmets").  
- Sound effects (e.g., war drums, seagulls, or opium trade music).  
- Whiteboard or chart paper for brainstorming.  
- Reflection worksheets.  

---

### **Day 1: The First Opium War – Role-Play and Reenactment**  
**Focus:** Causes and key events of the First Opium War (1839–1842).  

**Activities:**  
1. **Introduction (10 minutes):**  
   - Brief lecture on the Qing Dynasty’s trade imbalance with Britain, the opium trade, and Lin Zexu’s role.  
   - Show a short video clip (if available) or images of 19th-century China and British ships.  

2. **Drama Warm-Up (10 minutes):**  
   - Students act out scenes using mime to depict:  
     - The flow of opium from India to China.  
     - The physical and social effects of opium addiction in China.  

3. **Role-Play Activity (20 minutes):**  
   - Divide students into groups:  
     - **Chinese Officials** (Lin Zexu, Qing court members).  
     - **British Traders** (opium merchants, diplomats).  
     - **Neutral Observers** (scholars, common citizens).  
   - Using primary source excerpts, students perform a reenactment of the **1839 confiscation of opium** in Humen.  
   - Encourage use of props, gestures, and vocal tones to convey tension.  

4. **Class Discussion (5 minutes):**  
   - Debrief: How did each group view the conflict? What were the motivations?  

**Assessment:** Participation and accuracy in role-play.  

---

### **Day 2: The Aftermath – Mime and Debate**  
**Focus:** Consequences of the Treaty of Nanking (1842) and the Second Opium War (1856–1860).  

**Activities:**  
1. **Warm-Up (10 minutes):**  
   - Quick quiz on Day 1 content.  
   - Students mimic the **economic and political changes** in China post-Treaty of Nanking (e.g., opening of ports, unequal treaties).  

2. **Mime Activity (15 minutes):**  
   - Students work in pairs to mime the **spread of opium** and the **British military expansion**.  
   - Example: One student represents Britain’s "gunboat diplomacy," while another mimics China’s loss of sovereignty.  

3. **Debate: "Was the Opium War justified?"** (15 minutes):  
   - Divide the class into three sides:  
     - **British Advocates**: Economic interests, "open door" policies.  
     - **Chinese Defenders**: National sovereignty, moral outrage.  
     - **Neutral Historians**: Analyze the long-term impacts (e.g., weakening of Qing China, rise of imperialism).  
   - Students present arguments using historical evidence.  

4. **Reflection (5 minutes):**  
   - Students write briefly on how the drama and debate changed their understanding of the war.  

**Assessment:** Quality of debate arguments and creativity in mime.  

---

### **Day 3: Creative Performance and Reflection**  
**Focus:** Synthesizing understanding through performance and critical analysis.  

**Activities:**  
1. **Group Performance Planning (10 minutes):**  
   - Students form small groups to create a **10-minute performance** (drama or mime) on a specific aspect of the Opium Wars:  
     - Example: The Taiping Rebellion’s connection to the wars, or the role of Indian opium growers.  
   - Provide guidance on story structure, character development, and historical accuracy.  

2. **Performances (20 minutes):**  
   - Groups perform their pieces. Encourage creative use of props, music, and body language.  

3. **Class Discussion & Analysis (10 minutes):**  
   - After each performance, discuss:  
     - What historical perspective was highlighted?  
     - How did performance deepen understanding of the event?  

4. **Final Reflection (5 minutes):**  
   - Students complete a worksheet:  
     - "How did the Opium Wars shape modern China and global trade?"  
     - "What role does theater play in preserving history?"  

**Assessment:** Creativity, historical accuracy, and depth of reflection.  

---

### **Extension Ideas:**  
- **Creative Writing:** Students write a diary entry from the perspective of a British merchant or a Chinese citizen.  
- **Art Integration:** Draw or paint scenes from the Opium Wars to accompany their performances.  

**Standards Alignment:**  
- CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.RH.9-10.1 (Cite evidence), RH.9-10.6 (analyze multiple perspectives), WHST.9-10.7 (conduct research projects).  

This lesson plan combines immersive role-play, visual storytelling, and critical analysis to help students grasp the complexity of the Opium Wars while developing performance skills.
