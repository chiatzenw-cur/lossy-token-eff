**What is a Language Model?**  
A language model is like a smart assistant that helps computers understand and create human language. Think of it as a tool that learns how words are used in different contexts—like how people speak, write, or even tell stories. For example, if you ask it a question or give it a starting phrase, it can generate a response or complete the text in a way that feels natural and logical.

**Labeled vs. Unlabeled Data:**  
Now, the key question: Is it trained using labeled or unlabeled data?  

- **Labeled data** means the training examples have clear "correct answers" (like a teacher marking a test).  
- **Unlabeled data** is just raw text without any predefined answers.  

Language models are **trained on unlabeled data**. They learn by reading vast amounts of text (like books, articles, and websites) and figuring out patterns, grammar, and meaning on their own. It's like studying how people write by reading many books, without being told *what* the right answer is—just how language works in practice.

**Why Unlabeled Data?**  
Because language is all about context and patterns, not specific "right" or "wrong" answers. For instance, a model learns that "the cat sat on the mat" is a common sentence structure, or that "I am going to the store" often follows "I need to buy groceries." It doesn’t require someone to explicitly point out "this is correct" to learn these patterns.  

In short, a language model is like a digital student who reads a lot of books to understand how to write, but doesn’t need a teacher to grade its work. 💡
