Photosynthesis consists of two main stages, each occurring in distinct locations within the chloroplast and involving specific inputs and outputs. Here's a structured outline:

---

### **1. Light-Dependent Reactions**  
**Location:** Thylakoid membranes  
**Purpose:** Capture light energy to produce ATP and NADPH, which are used in the Calvin cycle.  

**Inputs:**  
- **Light energy** (sunlight)  
- **Water (H₂O)**  
- **ADP** and **inorganic phosphate (Pi)** (for ATP synthesis)  

**Outputs:**  
- **ATP** (adenosine triphosphate)  
- **NADPH** (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate)  
- **Oxygen (O₂)** (a byproduct of water splitting)  

**Key Process:**  
- Light energy excites electrons in chlorophyll, initiating the electron transport chain.  
- Water is split into oxygen (O₂), protons (H⁺), and electrons (photolysis).  
- The energy from the electron transport chain drives ATP synthesis (chemiosmosis) and reduces NADP⁺ to NADPH.  

---

### **2. Calvin Cycle (Light-Independent Reactions)**  
**Location:** Stroma of the chloroplast  
**Purpose:** Use ATP and NADPH from the light reactions to fix carbon dioxide (CO₂) into glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆).  

**Inputs:**  
- **Carbon dioxide (CO₂)**  
- **ATP** (from light reactions)  
- **NADPH** (from light reactions)  

**Outputs:**  
- **Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆)** (or G3P, a three-carbon sugar)  
- From ATP and NADPH:  
  - **ADP** and inorganic phosphate (Pi) (regenerated for reuse)  
  - **NADP⁺** (regenerated for reuse in light reactions)  

**Key Process:**  
- CO₂ is fixed into a five-carbon sugar (RuBP) via the enzyme RuBisCO, forming a six-carbon compound that splits into two three-carbon molecules (3-PGA).  
- ATP and NADPH provide energy and reducing power to convert 3-PGA into G3P.  
- Some G3-Phosphate molecules are used to regenerate RuBP (to sustain the cycle), while others are used to synthesize glucose and other carbohydrates.  

---

### **Overall Summary**  
- **Light-Dependent Reactions (Thylakoid):** Converts light to chemical energy (ATP/NADPH) and releases oxygen.  
- **Calvin Cycle (Stroma):** Uses ATP/NADPH to fix CO₂ into glucose, completing the conversion of light energy into stored chemical energy.  

This division ensures efficient energy capture and utilization in photosynthesis.
