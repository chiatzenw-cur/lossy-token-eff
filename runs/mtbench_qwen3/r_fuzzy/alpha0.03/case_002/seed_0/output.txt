**The Old Abandoned House**  

The old abandoned house at the end of the street held a secret that no one had ever discovered—until Mara stepped through its creaking front door.  

She hadn’t meant to go there. Not really. But when the power went out that Thursday night, her brother, Elias, had muttered, *“That house at the end of the street… it’s been waiting for someone to wake it up.”* Mara had laughed it off, but the words stuck to her like cobwebs. She couldn’t shake the feeling that the house *knew* she was there, its silence too heavy, its shadows too long.  

The street was empty, save for the gnarled oak tree that loomed like a sentry over the crumbling structure. Vines had climbed the walls, and the windows were shattered, their frames warped by time. Mara’s flashlight cut through the darkness as she approached, her breath visible in the cold air. The door groaned open on its own, as if the house were inviting her in.  

Inside, the air reeked of damp wood and something older—something *alive*. She stepped over broken floorboards, her boots echoing in the hollow space. The house was bigger than it looked, its rooms stretching into the unknown. In the parlor, a dusty mirror reflected her face, but when she turned, her reflection was smiling, its eyes hollow and glowing faintly. She gasped, stumbling back, and the mirror shattered, revealing a hidden staircase descending into the earth.  

Elias had never mentioned a staircase.  

The steps were narrow, slick with moss, and descended into what felt like another world. At the bottom, a door stood ajar, its iron handle rusted but intact. Beyond it was a room untouched by time—a library, its shelves lined with books bound in leather that seemed to pulse faint pulse faintly. In the corner, a single chair faced a desk, and on the desk lay a journal.  

Mara’s hands trembled as she opened it. The pages were yellowed, the ink faded, revealing entries scrawled in a desperate hand:  

*“The house is not just a house. It is a vessel. The children who live here… they are not *our* children. They are *its*.”*

Mara’s breath hitched. She stumbled back, but the door slammed shut behind her. The walls began to hum, a low, resonant sound that vibrated in her bones. Shadows slithered across the floor, pooling into shapes that moved of their own accord.

But the street was no longer empty.
