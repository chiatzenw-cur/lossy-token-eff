### 3. **Communication Style**
   - **Indirectness**: Avoid direct "no." Instead, say **"It would be difficult"** or **"We need to consider more."**  
   - **Formality**: Use **polite language (keigo)**. Avoid slang or casual speech.  
   - **Silence as a signal**: Silence can indicate agreement, not disapproval. Avoid interrupting or rushing conversations.

### 4. **Hierarchy and Respect**
   - **Titles and last names**: Address people with **titles** (e.g., Mr., Ms., or company position) and **last names** unless explicitly invited to use first names.  
   - **Respect for superiors**: Wait for higher-ups to speak, and avoid challenging their authority openly.  
   - **Seating arrangements**: In meetings, the **highest-ranking person** sits at the head, and seating order reflects hierarchy.

### 5. **Gift-Giving Customs**
   - **Gifts show respect**: Bring a **gift** (e.g., alcohol, sweets, or a small gift basket) on first meetings or to thank someone.  
   - **Avoid certain items**:  
     - **Bottled water** (if given to a superior, it may imply they are not good at their job).  
     - **Alcohol** (unless the recipient drinks it).  
     - **Items with animal motifs** (seen as disrespectful).  
   - **Presentation**: Wrap gifts in **neutral colors** (avoid black or white, which are associated with mourning). Enclose in a **paper gift holder**.

### 6. **Dress Code**
   - **Formal, conservative attire**: Business suits, ties (for men), and closed-toe shoes are standard.  
   - **Avoid casual clothes**: Jeans, tank tops, or revealing clothing are generally inappropriate, even in casual settings.  
   - **For women**: Business suits or conservative dresses are preferred; avoid overly short or tight outfits.

### 7. **Meeting Etiquette**
   - **Prepare thoroughly**: Arrive with **research** and clear ideas. Silence and listening are valued.  
   - **Do not speak out of turn**: Wait for your turn to speak, and avoid pushing your opinions too forcefully.  
   - **Consensus-driven decisions**: Aim for **group harmony** rather than individual dominance.  
   - **After meetings**: Stay for informal chats (**gyaku**) to build relationships.

### 8. **Dining Etiquette**
   - **Chopstick rules**:  
     - **Never stick chopsticks upright in a bowl** (associated with funerary rites).  
     - **Avoid holding chopsticks in the wrong hand** (left hand is seen as unclean).  
     - **Don’t pass food with chopsticks**; use a tray or plate.  
   - **Respect others’ eating pace**: Avoid finishing your food immediately.  
   - **Toast etiquette**: Raise your glass **after** the host does, and avoid emptying your glass in one draught.

### 9. **Avoiding Taboos**
   - **No personal discussions**: Avoid topics like **politics, religion, or personal finances** unless the other person initiates.  
   - **Cell phones**: Silence or turn off your phone during meetings, and avoid checking it.  
   - **Public displays of emotion**: Suppress anger, frustration, or impatience; maintain calm and composed behavior.
