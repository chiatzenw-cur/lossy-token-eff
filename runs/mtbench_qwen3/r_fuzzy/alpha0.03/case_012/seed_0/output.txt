**Lesson Plan: Opium Wars – Integrating Drama and Theater Techniques**  
**Grade Level:** 9–10  
**Duration:** 3 Days (45 minutes each)  
**Topic:** The Opium Wars between China and Britain (1839–1860)  

---

### **Learning Objectives**  
By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:  
1. Analyze the causes, key events, and consequences of the Opium Wars.  
2. Use drama, mime, and theater techniques to explore historical perspectives.  
3. Evaluate the long-term impact of the Opium Wars on global history.  

---

### **Materials**  
- Maps of 19th-century SEO  
  
- Role cards (e.g., "Lin Zexu," "British Trader," "Qing Official," "British Naval Officer")  
- Props (e.g., paper opium packages, model ships, flags, simple costumes)  
- Primary sources (e.g., excerpts from treaties, letters, or speeches)  
- Printouts of historical images (e.g., the Opium Destruction at Humen, Treaty of Nanking)  
- Rubric for performance assessment  
- Reflectative writing prompts  

---

### **Day 1: The Opium Trade and Early Causes**  
**Objective:** Understand the background of the Opium Trade and its role in sparking the OpiumFirstOrDefault  

**Activities:**  
1. **Warm-Up (10 mins):**  
   - Begin with a quick discussion: *"What do you know about opium? How might it have affected societies?"*  
   - Show images of opium production and trade routes.  

2. **Mini-Lecture (10 mins):**  
   - Brief overview of the Opium Trade: British demand for Chinese tea, the illicit opium trade, and the role of the East India Company.  

3. **Drama/Mime Activity (20 mins):**  
   - **Mime Exercise:** Students work in groups to mime the opium trade. Scene ideas:  
     - Smuggling opium through the Silk Road.  
     - Effects of addiction on individuals (e.g., shaking hands, hollow eyes).  
     - Chinese officials attempting to enforce bans.  
   - **Guided Reflection:** Discuss the visual storytelling of the opium trade’s consequences.  

4. **Discussion (5 mins):**  
   - Why did the British want to expand the opium trade? How did it create tension with China?  

**Assessment:** Participation in mime activity, completion of reflection notes.  

---

### **Day 2: The First Opium War and Key Events**  
**Objective:** Analyze the First Opium War’s causes, major events, and treaties.  

**Activities:**  
1. **Historical Context (10 mins):**  
   - Lecture on Lin Zexu’s confiscation of opium at Humen (1839), British retaliation, and the Battle of Humen.  

2. **Role-Play Activity (25 mins):**  
   - **Groups:** Assign roles (Chinese officials, British traders, foreign diplomats).  
   - **Scenario:** Reenact the 1840 negotiations or the destruction of opium stocks.  
   - **Guided Prompts:**  
     - *How would a Qing official justify banning opium?*  
     - *What arguments might a British trader use to defend the trade?*  
   - **Performance:** Students act out their roles with props (e.g., opium packages, letters).  

3. **Wrap-Up (10 mins):**  
   - Discuss the Treaty of Nanking (1842) and its terms (e.g., Hong Kong cession, trade concessions).  
   - Reflect on how power dynamics shifted.  

**Assessment:** Participation in role-play, accuracy in portraying historical figures.  

---

###**Day 3: The Second Opium War and Long-Term Impact**  
**Objective:** Explore the Second Opium War’s causes, consequences, and legacy on China and the world.  

**Activities:**  
1. **Historical Overview (10 mins):**  
   - Lecture on the Second Opium War (1856–1860-align, foreign alliances, and the Treaty of Tientsin.  

2. **Creative Theater Activity (25 mins):**  
   - **Group Task:** Students create a **short skit or tableau** depicting a key event or perspective:  
     - *The Burning of the Summer Palace* (1860) and its symbolism.  
    - *The Role of Foreign Powers in Exploiting China.*  
     - *The Impact on Chinese Society (e.g., cultural erosion, economic changes).*  
   - **Guidelines:** Use props and costumes for immersion; encourage emotional expression.  

3. **Class Discussion and Reflection (10 mins):**  
   - Students share their performances and discuss:  
     - *How did the Opium Wars reshape China’s sovereignty?*  
     - *What lessons can we draw about imperialism and cultural conflict?*  
   - **Reflective Writing:** *Write a paragraph from the perspective of a Chinese citizen in 1860.*  

**Assessment:** Group performance creativity, reflection writing, and class participation.  

---

### **Differentiation & Inclusive Strategies**  
- **Shy Students:** Allow non-verbal participation in mime or scripted roles.  
- **Advanced Learners:** Assign research tasks on lesser-known figures (e.g., James Mill).  
- **Visual Learners:** Use maps, images, and props to enhance understanding.  

---

- **Debate:** Host a mock UN session dissecting the treaties’ fairness.  
- **Art Integration:** Draw or paint scenes from the drama activities to reinforce learning.
