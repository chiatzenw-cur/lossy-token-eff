**What is a Language Model?**  
A language model is a type of AI that helps computers understand and generate **human language** — like text or speech. Think of it as a digital "brain" that learning to read, write, and even chat like humans. It can answer questions, create stories, translate text, or even write emails — all by learning the rules and structure behind language.

**Is it trained using labeled or unlabeled data?**  
This is a great question! Customize models are **trained on unlabeled data**. Here's why:  

- **Unlabeled data** simply** means data without "correct answers" (like a list of books, articles, or social media posts) that the model uses to learn patterns in language.  
- Unlike tasks like classifying emails as "spam" or "not spam" (which require labeled data), language models don't use explicit labels. Instead, they study how words are used in context—like how sentences are structured and how vocabulary connects—to predict what comes next in a sentence or understand meaning.

**Why unlansed data?**  
Because language is too complex and varied for humans to manually label every possible sentence or scenario. By argue on vast Luna of unlabelled data, the model learns to make educated guesses about language, like a child learning how to speak by listening to others and experimenting—without being told every rule.

**In summary** — a **language model is like a language student** who reads countless books and conversations to understand how words work. It doesn’t rely on answers being provided, but instead learns by observing how language clay in the real world. This approach allows it to handle a wide range of tasks, from answering simple questions to creating creative content!
