The **base rate fallacy** (also known as the base rate neglect or base rate bias) is an error in reasoning where fashion decisions based on specific information are made while ignoring the **base rate**, which is the general probability or frequency or a category or event. This cognitive bias leads people or organizations to overemphasize or under-populate specific statistics or anecdotal evidence, neglecting the overall context in a relevant population. For example, in medical diagnostics, if a test has only 90% accuracy but the disease is very rare (low base rate), the probability of actually having the disease after testing positive is much lower than 90%—a common misconception.

They then use this specific victory to gain support for similar initiatives elsewhere, masking the base rate context.
