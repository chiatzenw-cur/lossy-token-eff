**Lesson Plan: The Opium Wars – A Dramatic Exploration**  
**Grade Level:** 9–10  

### **Learning Objectives**  
1. Students will understand the causes, key events, and consequences of the Opium Wars (1820s1940s).  
2. Students will analyze historical perspectives by engaging in role-play and dramatic interpretation.  
3. Students will develop creativity and critical thinking through mime and performance.  
4. Students will reflect on the impact of imperialism and its long-term relevance.  

---

### **Materials Needed**  
- Printed handouts on the Opium Wars, British East India Company, and Qing Dynasty.  
- Simple costumes (optional: paper hats, fabric for flags, etc.).  
- Background music for performances (optional).  
- Large poster paper, markers, or digital tools for group work.  
- Video clips (e.g., BBC documentary snippets, historical reenactments).  
- Rubric for performance and reflection.  

---

### **Day 1: Understanding the Opium Wars Through Role- Defining Perspectives**  
**Duration:** 45 minutes  
**Focus:** Context, key events, and historical perspectives**  

#### **1. Warm-Up (15 minutes)**  
- ** activity:** Begin with a 5-minute discussion: *“What do you know about the Opium Wars?”*  
- **Video Clip (10 minutes):** Show a 5-minute BBC clip on the Opium Wars (or a teacher-created summary).  
- **Guiding Questions:**  
  - How did trade imbalances affect Sino-British relations?  
  - What role did the British East India Company play?  

#### **2. Role-Play Activity: The 1839 Negotiation (20 minutes)**  
- **Groups:** Assign roles (Lin Zex measure, British traders, Qing officials, Chinese merchants, British diplomats).  
- **Script:** Provide a simplified dialogue based on the 1839 opium confiscation and subsequent tensions. Students act out the scene using props (e.g., opium pipes, letters, maps).   
-**Teacher Prompt:** Encourage students to express the motivations and conflicts ( remind them of historical accuracy).  

#### **3. Class Discussion (15 minutes)**  
- Debrief the role-play: *“What challenges did each side face with the opium trade? How do these actions reflect larger issues like imperialism or economic exploitation?”*  
- Introduce the concept of "unilateral actions" and the lead-up to the First Opium Wars.
