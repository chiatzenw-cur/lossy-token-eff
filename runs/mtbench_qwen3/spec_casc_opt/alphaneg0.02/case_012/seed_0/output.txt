**Lesson Plan: **  
**Title: "Voices of the Opium Wars: A Dramatic Exploration of History"**  
**Grade Level: 9-10**  
**Duration: 3 class periods (45 minutes each)**  
**Topic: Opium Wars between China and Britain**

---

### **Learning Objectives**  
1. Understand the causes, key events, and consequences of the Opium Wars (1839–1842 and 1856–1860).  
2. Analyze perspectives from different stakeholders (British, Chinese, global).  
3. Develop drama skills (role-play, mime, tableau) to interpret historical events.  
4. Reflect on the ethical and social implications of imperialism and trade imbalances.  

---

### **Materials Needed**  
- Primary sources and excerpts (e.g., Emperor Daoguang’s edict, British parliamentary debates, Lin Zexu’s letters).  
- Printed role-play cards, scenario guides, and script templates.  
- Art supplies for creative reflection (optional).  
- Video clips or images (e.g., "The Opium Wars" documentary by BBC, visuals of opium dens or trade routes).  
- Rubric for performance and reflection.  

---

### **Day 1: Role-Play - Perspectives and Consequences**  
**Focus:** Causes and key events of the Opium Wars.  

#### **1. Introduction (100 min)**  
- **Hook (5 min):** Show a short video clip on the Opium Wars (e.g., BBC’s "The Opium Wars"). Ask questions:  
  - *What do you know about the Opium Wars?*  
  - *How might British and Chinese leaders view the conflict differently?*  

- **Mini-Lecture (10 min):** Brief overview of the background (19th-century trade imbalances, British opium trade, Lin Zexu’s actions, and the First Opium War).  

#### **2. Group Role-Play Activity (20 min)**  
- **Task:** Split students into groups representing key stakeholders (British merchants, Chinese officials, British naval officers, opium traders, Chinese peasants).  
- **Scenario:** Groups discuss a pivotal event (e.g., Lin Zexu’s destruction of opium stocks, the Treaty of Nanking).  
- **Guidance:** Provide role-play cards with outline key points (e.g., British merchants: “We need to balance trade; opium is essential.” Chinese officials: “Opium trade harms society; we must stop it.”).  

#### **3. Script Writing & Performance (10 min)**  
- Students draft a 15-minute scene (focus on one event) and perform it. Example: A debate between Lin Zexu and a British naval officer.  
- **Teacher Support:** Assist groups with historical accuracy and dialogue.  

#### **4. Reflection (5 min)**  
- **Discussion:** What did you learn about the conflict from different perspectives? How did these roles shape the outcome?  

**Assessment:** Participation in role-play, accuracy in historical context, and collaboration.  

---

### **Day 2: Mime & Tableau - Visualizing the Impact**  
**Focus:** Social and economic consequences of the Opium Wars.  

#### **1. Introduction (10 min)**  
- **Review:** Quick recap of Day 1 events.  
- **New Angle:** Discuss the human cost of opium addiction and poverty.  

#### **2. Mime Activity: Opium Addiction (15 min)**  
- **Task:** Students acting out scenes of opium addiction using mime to depict:  
  - Balance between opium use and daily life.  
  - The despair of addiction and the trade’s exploitation.  
- **Guidelines:** Encourage ghostly expressions, while avoiding graphic content.  

#### **3. Tableau Vivant: Trade and Power (15 min)**  
- **Task:** Create a still image to represent a key moment (e.g., the burning of opium stocks, British ships enforcing treaties).  
- **Group Work:** Students pose as characters as "trade routes," "British naval ships," or "Chinese villagers affected by the war."  
- **Teacher Support:** Provide prompts (e.g., “Show the contrast between the opium trade and the suffering it causes”).  

#### **4. Critical Thinking & Discussion (5 min)**  
- **Prompt:** How might the visual and physical portrayal of the Opium Wars change your understanding of its impacts?  

**Assessment:** Creativity in mime/tableau, ability to convey historical themes visually, and group collaboration.  

---

### **Day 3: Integration & Reflection & Final Presentations**  
**Focus:** Synthesize knowledge through performance and reflection.  

#### **1. Performance Review (10 min)**  
- **Student Presentations:** Groups present their Day 1 role-play and Day 2 tableau.  
- **Peer Feedback:** Students give brief, constructive feedback (e.g., “How did the performance make you feel about the conflict?”).  

#### **2. Group Discussion (15 min)**  
- **Debate:** “Was the Opium War an inevitable consequence of trade or a result of British imperialism?”  
- **Guided Questions:**  
  - *Who benefited from the Opium Wars?*  
  - *How did the treaties change China’s sovereignty?*  

#### **3. Creative Reflection (10 min)**  
- **Task:** Students create a short visual (e.g., drawing, collage, or written reflection) to symbolize the legacy of the Opium Wars.  
- **Example: “A single opium pipe represents both addiction and economic exploitation.”  

#### **4. Closing (5 min)**  
- **Reflection Sharing:** Volunteers share their artwork or reflections.  
- **Teacher Wrap-Up:** Emphasize the importance of empathy, understanding negotiation, and the consequences of diplomacy.  

**Assessment:**  
- **Performance Rubric:** Creativity, historical accuracy, and collaboration.  
- **Reflection Tasks:** Depth of analysis and connection to historical events.  

---

### **Differentiation & Extensions**  
- **For Advanced Learners:** Research primary sources and integrate them into role-play or tableau.  
- **For Struggling Learners:** Provide pre-written dialogue and visual aids for mime/tableau.  
- ** student Choice:** Allow students to choose between performing or creating art for the final reflection.
