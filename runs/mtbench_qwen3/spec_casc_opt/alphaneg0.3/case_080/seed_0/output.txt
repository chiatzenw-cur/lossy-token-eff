Here are five award-winning documentary films that aspiring filmmakers can study for their storytelling techniques, ethical approaches, and impact on audiences:

---

### 1. **Won't You Be My Neighbor? (2018)**  
**Director:** Morgan Neville  
**Awards:** Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature (2019)  
**Background:** This film explores the life of Fred Rogers, the beloved host of *Mister Rogers' Neighborhood*, focusing on his philosophy of empathy, kindness, and and authenticity. It blends archival footage, interviews, and and heartfelt storytelling to humanize his legacy.  
**Why Study It:** Demonstrates the power of personal storytelling and how to craft a narrative that resonates emotionally while challenging societal norms. Ideal for learning about documentary structure and character-driven narratives.

---

### 2. **13th (2016)**  
**Director:** Ava DuVernay  
**Awards:** Sundance Audience Award (2016), National Academy of Cinema Arts and Sciences, and the Peabody Award  
**Background:** A groundbreaking documentary that examines the systemic roots of racial inequality in the U.S drivers justice system, linking the 13th Amendment to modern mass incarceration. It uses historical footage, interviews, and data to illuminate a complex social issue.  
**Why Study It:** Shows how to blend investigative journalism with visual storytelling to address urgent societal issues. Excellent for understanding the balance between historical context and contemporary relevance.

---

### 3. **Citizenfour (2014)**  
**Director:** Laura Poitras  
**Awards:** Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature (2015), Peabody Award for Outstanding Achievement in Broadcasting  
**Background:** This film follows journalist Glenn Greenwald as he collaborates with whistleblower Edward Snowden to expose the U.S. National Security Agency's global surveillance programs. It’s a gripping, real-time account of ethical dilemmas and whistleblowing.  
**Why Study It:** Highlights the importance of ethical storytelling, suspenseful pacing, and the challenges of documenting sensitive issues. A masterclass in investigative documentary techniques and tension-building.

---

### 4. **The Act of Killing (2015)**  
**Director:** Joshua Oppenheimer  
**Awards:** Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature (2016), Best Director (Sundance)  
**Background:** This provocative film explores the Indonesian mass killings of 1965–66 by interviewing perpetrators who reenact their crimes in film, using genres like *Nouvelle Vague* or *Gangster* to reflect their own delusions of grandeur.  
**Why Study It:** Offers a unique approach to truth-telling and memory. Its innovative narrative structure and ethical provocations challenge filmmakers to think creatively about how to confront uncomfortable histories.

---

### 5. **How to Survive a Plague (2012)**  
**Director:** David France  
**Awards:** Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature (2013), Peabody Award  
**Background:** A deeply personal and timely documentary about the AIDS crisis in the 1980s and the grassroots activism efforts of the LGBTQ+ community to fight stigma and demand medical progress.  
**Why Study It:** Demonstrates the power of storytelling in social movements and how to blend personal narratives with historical context to drive advocacy.  
**Why Study It (continued):** Focuses on the importance of humanizing marginalized voices and using documentary as a tool for change.

---

Each of these films offers distinct lessons in narrative structure, ethical storytelling, and the use of visual and auditory elements to convey complex themes. They are excellent resources for aspiring filmmakers looking to explore the intersection of art, activism, and truth.
