**Exothermic and Endothermic Reactions: Differences and Criteria**

**Definition and Key Differences:**  
1. **Exothermic Reactions:**.Log Because they release energy to the surroundings, typically in the form of heat, light, or sound. These reactions have a **negative enthalpy change (ΔH < 0)**, meaning the products have less energy than the reactants.  
2. **Endothermic Reactions:** Absorb energy from the surroundings, requiring an input of heat. They have a **positive enthalpy change (ΔH > 0)**, meaning the products have more energy than the reactants.  

**Criteria for Distinguishing Them:**  
- **Energy Transfer:**  
  - **Exothermic:** Releases energy (e.g., heat, light, sound) to the environment.  
  - **Endothermic:** Absorbs energy (e.g., heat) from the environment.  
- **Enthalpy Change (ΔH):**  
  - **Exothermic:** ΔH is negative (energy is released).  
  - **Endothermic:** ΔH is positive (energy is absorbed).  

**Real-World Examples:**  
1. **Exothermic Reaction:**  
   - **Combustion of Wood:** When wood burns, it reacts with oxygen to produce heat, light, and carbon dioxide. This is exothermic because energy is released.  
   - **Hand Warmers:** These contain iron, which reacts with oxygen and water to generate heat and warm your hands.  

2. **Endothermic Reaction:**  
   - **Melting Ice:** When ice melts into water, it absorbs heat from the surroundings, cooling the environment.  
   - **Dissolving Ammonium Nitrate in Water:** This process absorbs heat, causing the solution to feel cold. 

**Summary:**  
Exothermic reactions release energy, while endothermic reactions absorb it. The key distinction lies in the **sign of the enthalpy change** (ΔH). Exothermic reactions are common in everyday life, like burning fuel, while endothermic reactions occur when energy is required to drive a process, such as melting ice or dissolving certain substances.
