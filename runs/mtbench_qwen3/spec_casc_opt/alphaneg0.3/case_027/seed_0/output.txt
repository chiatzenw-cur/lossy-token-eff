Photosynthesis is divided into two main stages: the **light-dependent reactions** and the **Calvin cycle (light-independent reactions)**. Here's a structured outline of each stage, including their locations within the chloroplast and their inputs/outputs:

---

### **1. Light-Dependent Reactions**  
- **Location**: **Thylakoid membranes** (specifically, the grana structures of the chloroplast).  
- **Inputs**:  
  - **Light energy** (photons energy).  
  - **Water (H₂O)**.  
- **Outputs**:  
  - **Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP)**.  
  - **Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide Phosphate (NADPH)**.  
  - **Oxygen (O₂)** as a byproduct (from the photolysis of water).  

**Process Summary**:  
Light energy is absorbed by chlorophyll and other pigments, driving the splitting of water molecules (photolysis) to release oxygen, protons, and electrons. These electrons flow through an electron transport chain, generating ATP and NADPH. These molecules are essential for the next stage.

---

### **2. Calvin Cycle (Light-Independent Reactions)**  
- **Location**: **Stroma** of the chloroplast.  
- **Inputs**:  
  - **ATP** (from light reactions).  
  - **NADPH** (from light reactions).  
  - **Carbon dioxide (CO₂)**.  
- **Outputs**:  
  - **Glyceraldehyde-3-Phosphate (G3P)** (a 3-carbon sugar used to synthesize glucose and other carbohydrates).  
  - **Regeneration of RuBP (Ribulose Bisphosphate)**, the starting molecule of the cycle, using some ATP.  

**Process Summary**:  
The Calvin cycle uses ATP and NADPH to fix CO₂ into organic molecules from the atmosphere. Through a series of enzymatic reactions, CO₂ is incorporated into RuBP, ultimately producing G3P. Some G3P molecules are used to form glucose, while others are recycled to regenerate RuBP, maintaining the cycle.

---

### **Key Take\core**:  
- **Light-Dependent Reactions**: Convert light energy into chemical energy (ATP and NADPH) and produce oxygen.  
- **Calvin Cycle**: Uses ATP and NADPH to synthesize glucose from CO₂, relying on the stroma's enzymes and environment.  

These stages work together to transform light energy into stored chemical energy in the form of glucose, sustaining life on Earth.
