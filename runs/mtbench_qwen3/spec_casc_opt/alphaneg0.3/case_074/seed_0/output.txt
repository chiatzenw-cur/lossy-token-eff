The reaction between solid calcium carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$) and hydrochloric acid ($\text{HCl}$) is a **neutralization reaction**, where an acid reacts with a carbonate?"

### **Balanced Chemical Equation:**
$$
\text{CaCO}_3(s) + 2\text{HCl}(aq) \rightarrow \text{CaCl}_2(aq) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l) + \text{CO}_2(g)
$$

### **Type of Reaction:**
This reaction is classified as a **neutralization reaction** (specifically, an acid-carbonate reaction). It can also be considered a **double displacement reaction** because the calcium ion ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$) from the carbonate swaps places with the hydrogen ion ($\text{H}^{+}$) from the acid. However, the primary classification is neutralization due to the formation of water and a salt.

### **Observations:**
1. **Effervescence (Bubbling):** Carbon dioxide gas ($\text{CO}_2$) is produced, which appears as bubbles in the solution.
2. **Dissolution of Solid:** The solid calcium carbonate gradually dissolves as the reaction proceeds.
3. **Temperature Change (Optional):** The reaction may be slightly exothermic, causing the solution to warm up, though this is not always visually obvious.

### **Key Points:**
- **Stoichiometry:** The balanced equation requires 2 moles of HCl to react with 1 mole of $\text{CaCO}_3$.
- **Products:** Calcium chloride ($\text{CaCl}_2$), water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$), and carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) are formed.
- **Relevance:** This reaction is commonly used in laboratories to produce carbon dioxide gas and is also the basis for the process of neutralizing stomach acid in antacids.
