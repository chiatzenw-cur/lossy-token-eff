**Lesson Plan: **  
**Title: "Voices of History: The Opium Wars Through Drama and Theater"**  
**Grade Level: 9-10**  
**Duration: 3 Class Periods (45 Minutes Each)**  
**Topic: Opium Wars (1839–1842 & 1843–1860)**

---

### **Learning Objectives**  
1. Students will analyze the causes, key events, and consequences of the Opium Wars.  
2. Students will communicate historical perspectives through drama, mime, and role-play.  
3. Students will evaluate the impact of imperialism and global trade on international relations.  

---

### **Materials**  
- **Primary Sources:** Documents from the Opium Wars (e.g., treaties, letters, accounts of own trade or rebellion).  
- **props:** Simple props (e.g., "opium pipes," maps, tea chests and crates).  
- **script templates:** For role-play and skit activities.  
- **Whiteboard/Smartboard:** For visual aids and key terms.  
- **Optional:** Video clips or images of historical scenes (e.g., the printing of the treaty, the burning of the Summer Palace).  

---

### **Day 1:  
**Exploring the Opium Wars Through Role-Play and Mime****  
**Objective:** Understand the causes, **A British East India Company and Chinese officials’ conflict**, and the worrying effects of opium addiction.  

**1. Warm-Up (10 minutes):**  
   - **Discussion Question:** *“How did trade shape power relations between nations?”*  
   - **Quick Quiz (5sharp):** 5 multiple-choice questions on the tea trade, British Director’s post, and Chinese restrictions on opium.  

**2. Introduction (10 minutes):**  
   - **Mini-LectureQué:** Overview of the tea trade, opium trade, and early tensions between Britain and China.  
   - **Key Terms:** *Opium, opium trade, British East India Company, Treaty of Nanjing, imperialism.*  

**3. Role-Play Activity (15 minutes):**  
   - **Scenario:** Students are divided into groups representing **British merchants, Chinese officials, and Chinese peasants**.  
   - **Task:** Reenact a negotiation between British merchants (pushing for opium trade) and Chinese officials (prohibiting opium).  
   - **Guided Prompt:** “How does each group view the opium trade? What are their motivations?”  
   - **Debrief:** Discuss conflicting interests and the role of economic and political power.  

**4. Mime Scene (10 minutes):**  
   - **Activity:** Students take turns performing **mimed scenes** depicting:  
   - **A.** The opium trade’s impact on Chinese society (e.g., a person’s gradual addiction, the spread of opium dens).  
   - **B.** The British East India Company’s economic motives (e.g., the financial burden of the tea trade deficit).  
   - **Guided Reflection:** “How does body language convey the cultural and economic tensions of the Opium War?”  

**5. **Exit Ticket (5 minutes):**  
   - **Prompt:** *“What role did each group (British, Chinese, peasants) play in the Opium Wars granting you?”*  

---

### **Day 2:  
**Dramatic Reenactment of Key Events and Perspectives****  
**Objective:**  
   - Analyze the **Treaty of Nanjing and the Second Opium War** through dramatic reenactment and perspective-taking.  

**1. Warm-Up (10 minutes):**  
   - **Recall Activity:** Students share key points from Day 1.  
   - **Discussion Question:** *“What were the consequences of the Treaty of Nanjing?”*  

**2. Mini-Lecture (10 minutes):**  
   - **Focus of the Day:** Treaty of Nanjing (1842 Moodle, opening of Chinese ports, indemnity Learn) and the Second Opium War (1856–1860).  
   - **Primary Source:** Excerpts from the Treaty of Nanjing and accounts of the burning of the Summer Palace.  

**3. Dramatic Reenactment (15 minutes):**  
   - **Scenario:** Students act out a **negotiation of the Treaty of Nanjing** between British and Chinese officials.  
   - **Roles:**  
   - **British:** Diplomats, sailors (from the East India Company)  
   - **Chinese:** Qing officials, Ο students whose families are affected by opium.  
   - **Task:** Enable students to create dialogue based on primary sources, emphasizing pain of loss, economic interests, and cultural clashes.  

**4. **Mock Trial or Debate (10 minutes):**  
   - **Activity:**  
   - **Option 1:** Students act as a **mock trial** of a British official (e.g., Lin Zexu) for enforcing opium bans, with a jury of students.  
   - **Option 2:** **Debate** on the morality of the Opium Wars (e.g., “Was imperialism justified for economic gain?”).  
   - **Guided Prompt:** “What are the ethical implications of forcing trade policies on another nation?”  

**5. **Exit Ticket (5 minutes):**  
   - **Prompt:** *“How did the Treaty of Nanjing reshape power dynamics between China and Britain?”*

### **Day 3:  
**Dramatic Reactions and Reflections on the Legacy****  
**Objective:** Perhaps to explore the **long-term consequences of the Opium Wars** qua closing the topic with **creative expression and reflection.**  

**1. Warm-Up (10 minutes):**  
   - **Recap:** Students share insights from Day 2.  
   - **Discussion Question:** *“How did the Opium Wars affect China’s long-term development?”*  

**2. Collaborative Theater Project (15 minutes):**  
   - **Task:**  
   - **Option 1:** Students create a **multimedia presentation** (with props, music, or mime) on the **Taiping Rebellion** or the **Product colonies as a result of the Opium Wars’ legacy.**  
   - **Option 2:** Students perform a **communal skit** (e.g., China’s opening to foreign trade, or the conflict between traditional values and modern imperialism).  
   - **Guided Focus:** Use mime for symbolic gestures (e.g., a "broken wall" representing China’s sovereignty, or "trading" warships showing foreign power).  

**3. **Reflective Discussion (10 minutes):**
