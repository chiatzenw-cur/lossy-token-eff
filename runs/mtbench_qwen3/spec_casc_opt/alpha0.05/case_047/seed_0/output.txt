**Exothermic vs. Endothermic reactions**

1. ****Definition and Energy Transfer:  
   - **Exothermic reactions** release energy (typically heat) to the surroundings. The system (the reacting substances) loses energy, and the surroundings gain it.  
   - **Endothermic reactions** absorb energy (typically heat) from the surroundings. The system gains energy, and the surroundings lose it.

2. ****Enthalpy Change (ΔH):  
   - **Exothermic**: ΔH is negative (ΔH < 0), indicating energy is released.  
   - **Endothermic**: ΔH is positive (ΔH > 0), indicating energy is absorbed.

3. ****Temperature Change:  
   - **Exothermic**: The surroundings (e.g., the environment) may feel hotter due to heat release.  
   - **Endothermic**: The surroundings (e.g., the environment) may feel cooler due to heat absorption.

**Real-World Example:**  
- **Exothermic Reaction**: **Combustion of wood**.  
  When wood burns, it reacts with oxygen (O₂) to produce carbon dioxide (CO2) and water (H2O), releasing heat and light energy. This is an exothermic reaction because it releases energy.  
  - **Chemical equation**:  
    $C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2 \rightarrow 6CO2 + 6H2O + \text{for energy}$  
    (Note: This is a simplified example, often associated with burning wood.)

- **Endothermic Reaction**: **Melting of ice**.  
  When ice melts (at standard atmospheric pressure), it absorbs heat from the surroundings, causing the ice to cool and the environment to feel colder.  
  - **Chemical equation**:  
    $H2O_{(s)} \rightarrow H2O_{(l)} + \text{absorption of heat}$  

**Criteria for Distinguishing:**  
1. **Sign of ΔH**:  
   - **Negative** (ΔH) for exothermic; **Positive** (ΔH) for endothermic.  
2. **Energy Transfer**:  
   - **Release of energy** (heat, light, or sound) for exothermic; **Absorbtion of energy** (heat, light, or mechanical) for endothermic.  
3. **Temperature of the Surroundings**:  
   - **Increase in temperature** (hotter) for exothermic; **Decrease in temperature** (colder) for endothermic.  

**Key Concept Recap**:  
   - **Exothermic** reactions release energy, resulting in an overall decrease in the system's energy of the system.  
   - **Endothermic** reactions absorb energy, resulting in an overall increase in the system's energy.  

**Conclusion**:  
   - **Exothermic reactions** are common in daily life, such as combustion, and are often associated with heat release.  
   - **Endothermic reactions** are observed in processes like ice melting and are associated with heat absorption.  
   - **The distinction between them is primarily based on the direction of energy flow in the system, along with the change in enthalpy and Reporting of the surrounding temperature.**
