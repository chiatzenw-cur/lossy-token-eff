The central dogma of molecular biology is a foundational concept that describes the flow of genetic information within a cell. It outlines the unidirectional direction, from DNA to RNA to protein, as the primary pathway for transmitting genetic information. Here's a detailed breakdown:

### **Processes Involved**
1. **Transcription**:  
   - **DNA → mRNA**:  
   - **Description**: DNA is transcribed into messenger RNA (mRNA) by the enzyme **RNA polymerase**. This process occurs in the nucleus and involves the synthesis of an RNA strand complementary to a DNA template.  
   - **Products**: mRNA carries the genetic code from DNA to the ribosome for protein synthesis.

2. **Translation**:  
   - **mRNA → Protein**:  
   - **Description**: The mRNA is translated into a protein by the ribosome, which uses the genetic code to assemble amino acids in the correct sequence. Transfer RNA (tRNA) delivers amino acids to the ribosome, while ribosome RNA (rRNA) forms the structural core of the ribosome.  
   - **Products**: A polypeptide chain (protein) is synthesized based on the mRNA sequence.

- **Directionality**: The central dogma emphasizes that information flows from DNA to RNA to protein in a **one-way direction**, without the reverse (e.g., pairing with DNA or protein Spend to DNA).
