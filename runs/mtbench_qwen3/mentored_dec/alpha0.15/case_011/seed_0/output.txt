### 2. **Bridge Type Selection**

### 3. **Seismic Design Features**
   - **Seismic Isolation**: Install base isolators (e.g., rubber bearings) between the superstructure and substructure to reduce ground motion transfer. This decouples the bridge from seismic waves, allowing it to sway without failure.
   - **Energy Dissipation Systems**: Integrate fluid viscous dampers or tuned mass dampers to absorb and dissipate seismic energy, preventing resonance and excessive vibrations.
   - **Ductile Materials and Detailing**: Use high-strength, ductile materials (e.g., high-performance concrete, steel with yield points) and design joints to allow controlled deformation without brittle failure.

### 4. **Foundations and Soil Stabilization**
   - **Deep Foundations**: Employ pile foundations extending into bedrock to avoid liquefaction and ensure stability. If liquefaction is a risk, stabilize the soil through grouting, soil nailing, or dynamic compaction.
   - **Substructure Redundancy**: Design piers and abutments with redundancy to prevent localized failures from propagating through the structure.

### 5. **Structural Redundancy and Flexibility**
   - **Redundant Load Paths**: Ensure the bridge has multiple load paths so that failure in one component does not compromise the entire structure.
   - **Flexible Joints**: Incorporate expansion joints and seismic-resistant connectors to accommodate movement without damage, especially in the deck and support systems.

### 6. **Compliance with Seismic Codes**
   - **Adherence to Standards**: Follow rigorous codes like ASCE 7, ISO 2394, or local seismic design regulations. Utilize PBSD to tailor the design to the specific seismic risk and expected performance level (e.g., life safety, operational continuity).

   - **Disaster Response Integration**: Collaborate with local authorities to ensure the bridge can be quickly inspected and repaired after seismic events.
