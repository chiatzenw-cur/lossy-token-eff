Photosynthesis consists of **two main stages**, each with distinct locations within the chloroplast and specific inputs and outputs:

---

### **1. Light-Dependent Reactions**  
- **Location**: **Thylakoid membranes** of the chloroplast.  
- **Inputs**:  
  - **Light energy** (captured by chlorophyll and other pigments).  
  - **Water (H₂O)**.  
  - **ADP** and **inorganic phosphate (Pi)** (used to synthesize ATP).  
- **Outputs**:  
  - **ATP** (adenosine triphosphate).  
  - **NADPH** (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate).  
  - **Oxygen (O₂)** (released as a byproduct of water splitting).  

**Key Process**: Light energy is absorbed and used to split water molecules, generating electrons, protons (H⁺), and oxygen. These electrons drive the production of ATP and NADPH through the electron transport chain, which are later used in the Calvin cycle.

---

### **2. Calvin Cycle (Light-Independent Reactions)**  
- **Location**: **Stroma** of the chloroplast.  
- **Inputs**:  
  - **ATP** and **NADPH** (from the light-dependent reactions).  
  - **Carbon dioxide (CO₂)**.  
  - **Ribulose bisphosphate (RuBP)** (a five-carbon sugar).  
- **Outputs**:  
  - **Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (G3P)** (a three-carbon sugar, used to build glucose and other carbohydrates).  
  - **Regenerated RuBP** (to sustain the cycle).  

**Key Process**: The enzyme **RuBisCO** catalyzes the fixation of CO₂ into RuBP, forming an unstable six-carbon compound that splits into two molecules of G3P. ATP and NADPH provide energy and reducing power to convert G3P into glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) and regenerate RuBP.

---

**Summary**:  
- **Light-Dependent Reactions**: Convert light energy into chemical energy (ATP and NADPH) and release oxygen.  
- **Calvin Cycle**: Uses ATP and NADPH to fix CO₂ into sugars, forming the basis of glucose production.  

These stages work synergistically to transform light energy and carbon dioxide into glucose, sustaining life on Earth.
