To solve the inequality:

$$
|x + 5| < 10
$$

we first interpret the absolute value. The expression $ |x + 5| < 10 $ means that the distance between $ x + 5 $ and zero is less than 10. This translates to the compound inequality:

$$
-10 < x + 5 < 10
$$

Subtracting 5 from all parts of the inequality to solve for $ x $:

$$
-10 - 5 < x < 10 - 5 \Rightarrow -15 < x < 5
$$

So, the solution set consists of all real numbers between $-15$ and $5$, **not including** the endpoints. Now, since the question asks for **how many integers** lie in this interval, we need to count all integers $ x $ such that:

$$
-15 < x < 5
$$

---

### Counting the Integers

To count the integers strictly between $-15$ and $5$, we can consider all integers from $-14$ to $4$, inclusive. That is:

$$
x \in \{-14, -13, -12, \ldots, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4\}
$$

We can calculate how many such integers there are using the formula for counting integers in a range from $ a $ to $ b $ (inclusive):

$$
\text{Number of integers} = b - a + 1
$$

In this case, $ a = -14 $, $ b = 4 $, so:

$$
4 - (-14) + 1 = 4 + 14 + 1 = 19
$$
