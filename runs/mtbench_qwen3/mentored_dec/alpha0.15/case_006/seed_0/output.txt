---

### Step 1: Total Number of Outcomes

When rolling two dice, the first die can show any of the six numbers from 1 to 6, and the second die can also show any of the six numbers from 1 to 6. Therefore, the **total number of possible outcomes** is:

$$
6 \times 6 = 36
$$

---

### Step 2: Understand the Desired Outcome

We are looking for the probability that the **sum of the numbers on both dice is at least 3**, meaning the possible outcomes we want are those where the sum is:

$$
3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, \text{or } 12
$$

That is, we exclude the outcome where the sum is **strictly less than 3**. Since the smallest total you can get from two dice is **2**, and the only way to get a sum of 2 is when both dice show **1**, the complement event is just:

- Rolling a **sum of 2**

---

### Step 3: Count the Complement Event

As calculated earlier, **only one outcome** yields a total of 2:

$$
(1,1)
$$

Therefore, the number of outcomes that are **not** favored (i.e., lead to sum less than 3) is just **1**.

---

### Step 4: Calculate the Desired Probability

Using the complement rule:

$$
P(\text{sum} \geq 3) = 1 - P(\text{sum} = 2)
$$

$$
P(\text{sum} = 2) = \frac{1}{36}
$$

$$
P(\text{sum} \geq 3) = 1 - \frac{1}{36} = \frac{35}{36}
$$

---

### Final Answer

$$
\boxed{\dfrac{35}{36}}
$$

This is the probability that you roll a total number that is **at least 3** when rolling two dice.
