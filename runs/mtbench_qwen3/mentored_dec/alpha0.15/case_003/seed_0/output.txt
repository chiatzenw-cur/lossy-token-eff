Once you share more context, I can tailor strategies to your unique situation. For now, here are some general principles to consider:  

### 1. **Pause and Reflect**  
   - Before rehashing the conflict, take a moment to acknowledge your emotions and ask: *What is my core need here?* (e.g., feeling respected, secure, understood).  
   - Ask yourself: *Am I reacting to the issue or to my feelings about it?*  

### 2. **Practice Active Listening**  
   - When your spouse speaks, focus on understanding their perspective rather than preparing your rebuttal.  
   - Use phrases like:  
     - *"I hear you feel [emotion] when [specific situation]. Can you help me understand why that matters to you?"*  
     - *"It sounds like you’re [summarizing their point]. Is that right?"*  

### 3. **Use "I" Statements**  
   - Replace accusatory language with personal feelings. For example:  
     - Instead of: *"You never listen to me!"*  
     - Say: *"I feel unheard when our conversations don’t include my thoughts."*  

### 4. **Identify Shared Goals**  
   - Ask: *What do we both want to achieve here?* (e.g., happiness, partnership, solving a problem).  
   - Focus on collaboration rather than "winning" the argument.  

### 5. **Create a Safe Space**  
   - Choose a calm time and place to talk. Avoid bringing up conflicts during high-stress moments (e.g., after a long day).  
   - Agree on a "no-name-calling" rule to prevent escalation.  

### 6. **Embrace Compromise**  
   - Brainstorm solutions that partially satisfy both of you. For example:  
     - *"What if we try [idea] for a week and see how it works?"*  
   - Be open to adjusting your expectations.  

### 7. **Rebuild Trust (if applicable)**  
   - If trust is damaged, commit to consistent, small actions to demonstrate reliability.  
   - Acknowledge past pain and express willingness to grow together.  

### 8. **Seek Outside Support**  
   - If the conflict persists, consider couples counseling or workshops. A neutral third party can help reframe dynamics.
