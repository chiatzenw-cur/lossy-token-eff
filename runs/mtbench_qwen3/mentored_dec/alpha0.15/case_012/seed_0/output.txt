**Lesson Plan: Exploring the Opium Wars Through Drama and Theater Techniques**  
**Grade Level:** 9–10  
**Duration:** 3 Class Periods (45 minutes each)  
**Topic:** The Opium Wars between China and Britain  

---

### **Learning Objectives**  
By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:  
1. Analyze the causes and consequences of the Opium Wars (1839–1842 and 1856–1860).  
2. Examine the perspectives of key stakeholders (Qing Dynasty, British traders, opium dealers, etc.).  
3. Use drama, mime, and theater techniques to deepen historical understanding and empathy.  
4. Collaborate in creative performances while practicing critical thinking and communication.  

---

### **Materials Needed**  
- Printed primary source excerpts (e.g., Qing edicts, British letters, Treaty of Nanjing terms).  
- Props: Opium vials, tea chests, maps, costumes (optional).  
- Art supplies for creating visual aids (e.g., posters for treaty terms).  
- Timer for activities.  
- Rubric for performance and reflection.  

---

### **Day 1: Role-Play – Perspectives on the Opium Trade**  
**Warm-Up (10 minutes):**  
- **Discussion:** “Why would a country allow the import of a drug that harms its citizens? How might this cause conflict?”  
- **Goal:** Activate prior knowledge about trade and imperialism.  

**Mini-Lesson (15 minutes):**  
- Overview of the Opium Wars:  
  - Background on the Qing Dynasty’s trade imbalance with Britain.  
  - Role of the British East India Company in the opium trade.  
  - Lin Zexu’s campaign to ban opium and the British response.  

**Drama Activity: Role-Play Negotiation (20 minutes):**  
- **Setup:** Divide students into groups representing key stakeholders (e.g., Chinese officials, British traders, opium dealers, Qing Emperor, ordinary citizens).  
- **Task:** Students role-play a negotiation over opium trade policies. Use primary sources to guide dialogue.  
  - Example: “British traders argue for free trade; Chinese officials demand restrictions.”  
- **Guided Questions:**  
  - What were each group’s priorities?  
  - How did cultural and economic values influence their decisions?  

**Wrap-Up (5 minutes):**  
- Discuss the complexity of the conflict and its human cost.  

**Homework:** Research one stakeholder group (e.g., opium addicts, British merchants) and prepare a short summary.  

---

### **Day 2: Mime and Theater – Visualizing the Opium Crisis**  
**Warm-Up (10 minutes):**  
- Quick review of the role-play. Ask students to share key takeaways.  

**Mini-Lesson (15 minutes):**  
- Timeline of the Opium Wars:  
  - First Opium War (1839–1842): Lin Zexu’s confiscation, British naval invasion, Treaty of Nanjing.  
  - Second Opium War (1856–1860): Foreign intervention, Taiping Rebellion, Treaty of Tientsin.  

**Mime Activity: “The Opium Trade” (20 minutes):**  
- **Task:** In groups, students create a 5-minute mime performance depicting the opium trade’s spread, addiction, and Qing resistance.  
- **Tips:** Use props (e.g., opium vials, tea chests) and body language to convey:  
  - Smuggling opium into China.  
  - The physical and social effects of addiction.  
  - Lin Zexu’s efforts to ban the trade.  
- **Debrief:** Students share their mime scenes and discuss the emotional impact of the crisis.  

**Theater Activity: Treaty of Nanjing Role-Play (5 minutes):**  
- Students reenact the signing of the Treaty of Nanjing, focusing on its terms (e.g., Hong Kong cession, trade concessions). Use primary source excerpts for accuracy.  

**Wrap-Up (5 minutes):**  
- How did the treaty reshape China’s relationship with Britain?  

---

### **Day 3: Reflection and Creation – Theater as History**  
**Warm-Up (10 minutes):**  
- Debrief Day 2: “How did mime and role-play help you understand the Opium Wars?”  

**Mini-Lesson (15 minutes):**  
- Long-term consequences:  
  - Unequal treaties, Western influence, and China’s modernization.  
  - Debate: Were the Opium Wars justified?  

**Theater Activity: Student-Designed Skit (20 minutes):**  
- **Task:** In groups, students create a 10-minute skit from a different perspective (e.g., a Chinese citizen, a British soldier, an opium addict).  
- **Guidelines:**  
  - Use historical accuracy and empathy.  
  - Incorporate theater techniques (e.g., monologues, dialogue, symbolism).  
- **Examples:**  
  - A scene showing the impact of the Opium Wars on families.  
  - A British merchant’s justification for trade policies.  

**Group Presentations (5 minutes):**  
- Students perform their skits. Peers provide feedback using a rubric (focus: historical accuracy, creativity, clarity).  

**Wrap-Up (5 minutes):**  
- **Reflective Writing:** “How did acting out historical events deepen your understanding of the Opium Wars?”  
- **Class Discussion:** How can theater help us analyze complex historical events?  

---

### **Assessment**  
- **Formative:** Participation in role-plays, mime, and skits; peer feedback.  
- **Summative:** Reflective writing assignment (200–300 words) and rubric for skit performance (assesses creativity, historical accuracy, and collaboration).  

---

### **Differentiation**  
- **ELL Students:** Provide sentence starters for role-play and skit writing.  
- **Advanced Learners:** Research lesser-known figures (e.g., Admiral Sir James Hope, Chinese resistance leaders).  
- **Visual Learners:** Encourage use of props and visual storytelling in mime.  

---

### **Extension Ideas**  
- Analyze modern-day parallels (e.g., drug policies, trade wars).  
- Compare the Opium Wars to other imperialist conflicts (e.g., Spanish colonization, Scramble for Africa).  

This lesson plan combines historical analysis with creative expression, fostering a deeper connection to the past through empathy and collaboration.
