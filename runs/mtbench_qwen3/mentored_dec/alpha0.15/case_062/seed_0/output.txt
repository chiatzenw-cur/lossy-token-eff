To determine the probability that a randomly selected student from the school likes neither blue nor green, we'll use the principle of **inclusion-exclusion** for probability.

---

### Step 1: Understand the Given Information

We are told:

- **P(Blue)** = 58% (Probability that a student likes blue)
- **P(Green)** = 45% (Probability that a student likes green)
- **P(Blue ∩ Green)** = 22% (Probability that a student likes both colors)

We are asked for the **probability that a student likes neither color**, which can be expressed as:

$$
P(\text{neither Blue nor Green}) = 1 - P(\text{Blue ∪ Green})
$$

---

### Step 2: Use the Inclusion-Exclusion Principle

The probability of liking either Blue **or** Green (or both) is given by:

$$
P(\text{Blue ∪ Green}) = P(\text{Blue}) + P(\text{Green}) - P(\text{Blue ∩ Green})
$$

Substituting the values:

$$
P(\text{Blue ∪ Green}) = 58\% + 45\% - 22\% = 81\%
$$

---

### Step 3: Calculate the Complement

Now, subtract this from 100% to find the probability of liking **neither** color:

$$
P(\text{neither Blue nor Green}) = 1 - 0.81 = 0.19
$$

---

### Final Answer

$$
\boxed{0.19}
$$

So, the probability that a randomly picked student likes neither blue nor green is **0.19** or **19%**.
