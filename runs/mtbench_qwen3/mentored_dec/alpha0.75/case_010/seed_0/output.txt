**Superposition** in quantum physics refers to the principle that a quantum system can exist in multiple states simultaneously until measured. This is mathematically represented as a linear combination of possible states, often visualized through a wave function. For example, a qubit in a quantum computer can be in a superposition of both |0⟩ and |1⟩ states. Upon measurement, the wave function collapses to a specific state, with probabilities determined by the square of the amplitude of the superposition components.

**Quantum entanglement** occurs when two or more particles become interconnected such that the state of one particle instantly correlates with the state of the other, regardless of the distance separating them. This phenomenon defies classical intuition and was famously termed "spooky action at a distance" by Einstein. However, entanglement does not enable faster-than-light communication because the outcomes of measurements are inherently probabilistic, and no information is transmitted through the correlation itself.

**Relationship between Superposition and Entanglement**:
- **Mathematical Basis**: Entangled states are a form of superposition involving multiple particles. For instance, if two particles are entangled in a singlet state, their combined state can be described as a superposition of pairs like |↑↓⟩ and |↓↑⟩. The individual states of the particles are not well-defined until measured.
- **Correlation vs. Individual States**: While superposition applies to the state of a single quantum system, entanglement involves the non-classical correlation between multiple systems. The entire system is in a superposed state, and the measurement of one particle determines the state of the other or other entangled particles, illustrating the interconnectedness of entangled systems.
- **Collapse Upon Measurement**: Both concepts involve the collapse of the quantum state upon measurement. In superposition, the act of measurement resolves the system into a definite state. In entanglement, measuring one particle collapses the combined state, revealing the correlated state of the others, even at distant locations.

**Key Distinctions and Implications**:
- **Scope**: Superposition is a fundamental property of individual particles, while entanglement is a phenomenon that arises from the interaction of multiple quantum systems.
- **Experiment and Technology**: Superposition underpins technologies like quantum computing and quantum cryptography, while entanglement enables applications such as quantum teleportation and secure communication protocols.
- **Non-Locality and Realism**: Entanglement challenges classical concepts of locality and realism, demonstrating that quantum systems can exhibit correlations that cannot be explained by classical physics. Superposition, in turn, is the foundation of these correlations, as entangled states require the superposition of individual particle states.

In summary, superposition and entanglement are deeply interconnected yet distinct quantum phenomena. Superposition allows a system to exist in multiple states simultaneously, while entanglement creates correlations between particles that persist regardless of their separation. Together, they exemplify the non-intuitive nature of quantum mechanics, leading to groundbreaking applications in technology and fundamental insights into the fabric of reality.
