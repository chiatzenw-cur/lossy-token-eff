**Balanced Chemical Equation:**

The reaction between solid calcium carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$) and hydrochloric acid ($\text{HCl}$) is a **neutralization reaction** and produces aqueous calcium chloride ($\text{CaCl}_2$), carbon dioxide gas ($\text{CO}_2$), and water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$). 

$$
\text{CaCO}_3 (s) + 2\text{HCl} (aq) \rightarrow \text{CaCl}_2 (aq) + \text{CO}_2 (g) + \text{H}_2\text{O} (l)
$$

**Type of Reaction:**  
This is an **acid-base (neutralization) reaction** and can also be classified as a **double displacement reaction**. The carbonic acid ($\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3$) formed during the reaction decomposes into water and carbon dioxide. 

**Observations:**  
1. **Bubbling or Fizzing:** The reaction releases carbon dioxide gas, which appears as bubbles.  
2. **Dissolution of Solid:** The solid calcium carbonate gradually dissolves as the reaction progresses.  
3. **Exothermic Heat:** The reaction may produce heat, causing the solution to warm slightly.  
4. **Change in pH:** The solution may become less acidic, though this is not always visually obvious.  

**Key Balancing Steps:**  
- A coefficient of **2** is added to $\text{HCl}$ to balance the chlorine atoms.  
- All other atoms (Ca, C, O, H) are balanced by verifying the stoichiometry.  

**Summary:**  
This chemical reaction exemplifies the interaction between an acid and a carbonate, with clear observable indicators of gas evolution and dissolution. It is a classic acid-base reaction involving the formation of a salt, water, and a volatile gas (carbon dioxide).
