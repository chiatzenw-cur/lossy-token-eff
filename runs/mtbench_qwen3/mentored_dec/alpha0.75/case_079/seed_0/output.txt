Business etiquette in Japan is deeply rooted in tradition, respect, and formality, emphasizing harmony, hierarchy, and meticulous attention to detail. Here's a structured overview of key norms and practices:

### **1. Greeting and Introductions**
- **Bowing**: A bow is a universal greeting. Use the **depth of the bow** to express respect:
  - **Shallower (15°)** for informal or casual meetings.
  - **Deeper (30°–45°)** for formal or respectful situations.
  - **Full bow (50°)** when expressing sincere apology.
- **Handshake**: Not commonly used, except in some modern or international contexts. A bow is more traditional.
- **Titles and Names**: Address individuals with **honorifics** and last names, e.g., *Tanaka-san* (Mr./Ms. Tanaka). Use **first names** only after being given permission or in very informal settings.

### **2. Business Cards (Meishi)**
- **Exchange**: Present and receive **business cards with both hands** as a sign of respect.
- **Handling**: 
  - **Do not look at the card immediately** upon receiving.
  - **Store it with care** in a special holder (like a wallet or on your lap), not just on your desk.
  - **Never write on the card** unless it involves a personal note (e.g., a birthday or anniversary).
- **Presentation**: When giving the card, **face the recipient directly** and use both hands. Avoid using a pen to hand it over.

### **3. Meetings and Punctuality**
- **Punctuality**: Arrive **exactly on time** (5–10 minutes early is acceptable), as being late is considered **disrespectful**.
- **Respect Hierarchy**:  
  - The **most senior member** often leads the meeting; junior employees should avoid interrupting.
  - **Do not rely on phones or email** to update colleagues; use face-to-face communication.
- **Keep Meetings**: Aim to conclude by the scheduled time. Do not expect meetings to extend indefinitely unless agreed upon.

### **4. Communication and Politeness**
- **Indirect Communication**: Avoid direct "no" responses. Use phrases like *"It may be difficult" (no hodo dekinai)* or *"I regret to say that..."* to express disagreement.
- **Silence**: **Silence is not rudeness**; it can signal listening or reflection. Avoid interrupting.
- **Honorifics**: Use **appropriate honorifics** (e.g., *sama*, *desu*, *masu*) when speaking to show respect, especially in formal contexts.
- **Document Sharing**: Receive **detailed documents** (e.g., books, brochures) in advance, as they may indicate a long discussion or deeper engagement.

### **5. Dining Etiquette**
- **Start and Finish**: 
  - **Wait for the host to start the meal** before eating.
  - Do not leave the table early; take time to finish the meal.
- **Tea Ceremonies**: In formal settings, tea is brewed with care. Wait for the host to pour before drinking and never refill your own cup.
- **Food Respect**: 
  - **Avoid taking food directly from others' plates**.
  - **Share dishes** but don't overindulge; leave some for others.

### **6. Gifts and Hospitality**
- **Gift-Giving**: Generously offer gifts to demonstrate appreciation (e.g., **gift baskets** or **bento boxes**).
- **Avoid Unlucky Items**: 
  - **Clocks** (associated with "time to die").
  - **Sharp objects** (include knives or scissors).
  - **Red-colored gifts** (considered bad luck for formal occasions).
- **Sealed Envelopes**: For monetary gifts, use **sealed envelopes** (e.g., *otoshidama* for New Year).

### **7. Dress Code**
- **Formal Attire**: 
  - **Men**: Suit, tie, and jacket (black or light blue is common).
  - **Women**: Business suits (avoid short skirts, spaghetti straps, or revealing clothing). 
- **Silence on Environments**: **No loud noises or distracting items** (e.g., phones, gaming devices) during meetings.

### **8. Hierarchy and Respect**
- **Hierarchy Matters**: Follow the **100 percent rule**—ensure everyone's wishes are acknowledged, even if it means compromising your point.
- **Avoid Boasting**: Understate your achievements; let others praise you. Focus on contributing to the group rather than individual recognition.

### **9. Follow-Up and Relationships**
- **No Rush**: Business is often done **after the meeting**. Follow-up gestures (e.g., calling, thanking, or sending a gift) are expected.
- **Building Rapport**: Use time for **small talk** (e.g., discussing weather, hobbies) to build trust. Focus on creating **long-term relationships** (*ninja*).

### **10. Cultural Sensitivity**
- **Avoid Excessive Eye Contact**: While direct eye contact is acceptable in some contexts, it may be perceived as confrontational.
- **Language Caution**: Be mindful of **cultural taboos** (e.g., topics like religion, politics, or sex unless it's a shared interest).

### **Key Takeaway**
In Japan, **respect, humility, and patience** are paramount. Business interactions are often about building trust and mutual respect rather than immediate transactions. Adhering to these norms ensures a positive, harmonious experience, especially in a culture where **"wa" (harmony)** and **"ma" (space)** are critical to social and business dynamics. Always observe and adapt to the local culture, as even small gestures can make a significant impact.
