The **central dogma of molecular biology** is a foundational principle that describes the direction of genetic information flow in living organisms. It was formulated by **Francis Crick** in 1957 and outlines the process by which genetic information is transmitted from DNA to RNA to protein. Here's a structured breakdown:

---

### **1. Central Dogma Statement**  
The central dogma asserts that **genetic information flows unidirectionally** from **DNA to RNA to protein**. It does not include reverse processes, such as RNA to DNA (as seen in retroviruses like HIV), unless explicitly accounted for. This concept emphasizes that **DNA serves as the blueprint** for all cellular functions through its transcription and translation into proteins.

---

### **2. Key Processes Involved**  
The central dogma consists of three main processes:  
1. **DNA Replication**  
   - **Description:** DNA is copied to produce identical DNA molecules during cell division.  
   - **Purpose:** Ensures that genetic information is faithfully transmitted to daughter cells.  
   - **Location:** Occurs in the nucleus (in eukaryotic cells).  

2. **Transcription**  
   - **Description:** A segment of DNA is transcribed into a complementary RNA molecule (typically messenger RNA, or mRNA).  
   - **Purpose:** Produces RNA that carries genetic instructions from DNA to the ribosome.  
   - **Location:** Occurs in the nucleus (in eukaryotic cells).  

3. **Translation**  
   - **Description:** The mRNA is decoded by ribosomes to synthesize proteins (amino acid sequences).  
   - **Purpose:** Converts the genetic code into functional proteins.  
   - **Location:** Occurs in the cytoplasm (in eukaryotic cells).  

---

### **3. Who Named It?**  
- **Francis Crick** (1916–2004) coined the term **"central dogma"** in a 1957 paper titled *"Central dogma of molecular biology"*, published in the journal *Nature*.  
- Crick emphasized the **unidirectional flow of genetic information** as a core principle, even acknowledging that exceptions (e.g., reverse transcription in retroviruses) exist but are "not part of the central dogma" itself.  

---

### **4. Significance and Exceptions**  
- **Importance:** The central dogma forms the basis of molecular biology and explains how genetic information is stored, transmitted, and expressed.  
- **Exceptions:** Some biological systems, such as retroviruses (e.g., HIV) or certain mobile genetic elements (e.g., retrotransposons), use reverse transcription (RNA to DNA) and thus challenge the strict unidirectional model. However, these exceptions are considered deviations rather than the central mechanism.  

---

### **Summary**  
- **Central Dogma:** DNA → RNA → Protein (unidirectional flow).  
- **Processes:** Replication, transcription, translation.  
- **Proposed By:** Francis Crick (1957).  

This principle remains a cornerstone of molecular biology, despite its simplification of complex biological processes.
