Here are five award-winning documentary films, each with a brief background and insights for aspiring filmmakers to study:

1. **"Earth" (2009)**  
   - **Director**: Yann Arthus-Bertrand  
   - **Awards**: Won the Best Short Film at the Cannes Film Festival (2009), Golden Globe for Best Documentary.  
   - **Background**: This visually stunning documentary uses breathtaking aerial footage to explore the beauty and fragility of our planet. It interweaves global perspectives on environmental issues, emphasizing the need for conservation and community action.  
   - **Lessons**: Study its innovative use of cinematography and how to convey large-scale environmental themes through intimate, accessible storytelling.

2. **"13th" (2016)**  
   - **Director**: Ava DuVernay  
   - **Awards**: Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature (2017), Emmy for Outstanding Documentary.  
   - **Background**: This powerful film examines the legacy of slavery in the U.S. through the lens of the 13th Amendment, tracing the evolution of mass incarceration and systemic racism.  
   - **Lessons**: Learn how to deconstruct historical and social issues, craft a compelling narrative with archival footage, interviews, and data, and balance urgency with accessibility.

3. **"The Act of Killing" (2012)**  
   - **Director**: Joshua Oppenheimer  
   - **Awards**: Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film (2013), Golden Lion at Venice Film Festival (2012).  
   - **Background**: Explore the Indonesian genocide of 1965 through interviews with former perpetrators, who reenact their crimes in styles from Western films to musicals. The film confronts the psychology of mass violence and perpetrators' denial.  
   - **Lessons**: Study its unconventional storytelling, ethical challenges in interviewing violent figures, and the courage to tackle sensitive topics with raw honesty.

4. **"The Fog of War" (2003)**  
   - **Director**: Errol Morris  
   - **Awards**: Three Emmy Awards, Academy Award for Best Documentary (2003).  
   - **Background**: This film interviews former U.S. Secretary of Defense Robert McNamara about his role in the Vietnam War, delving into his regrets and the moral ambiguity of military decisions.  
   - **Lessons**: Analyze Morris's signature interview style, his ability to craft immersive narratives with ambiguous conclusions, and the use of archival material to build historical context.

5. **"Crips" (2017)**  
   - **Director**: Robert Rubenstein  
   - **Awards**: Grand Jury Prize for Best Documentary at Sundance (2017).  
   - **Background**: This film presents a rare, balanced portrayal of the Crips gang, juxtaposing the lived experiences of members and their families with the legacy of systemic violence. It avoids clichés, focusing on personal stories and societal impact.  
   - **Lessons**: Understand how to approach divisive subjects with empathy, use personal narratives to humanize complex issues, and maintain neutrality while depicting contentious realities.

Each of these films offers unique techniques in narrative construction, ethical storytelling, and visual innovation, making them excellent resources for aspiring filmmakers.
