Photosynthesis occurs in two main stages, each taking place in distinct regions of the chloroplast. Here's an outline of these stages, including their locations, inputs, and outputs:

---

### **1. Light-Dependent Reactions**  
**Location:** Thylakoid membranes of the chloroplast.  

**Inputs:**  
- **Light energy** (sunlight)  
- **Water (H₂O)**  
- **ADP + inorganic phosphate (Pi)** (used to synthesize ATP)  

**Outputs:**  
- **ATP** (adenosine triphosphate)  
- **NADPH** (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate)  
- **Oxygen (O₂)** (a byproduct of water splitting)  

**Process:**  
- Light energy is absorbed by chlorophyll and other pigments in photosystems.  
- Water is split into oxygen, protons (H⁺), and electrons (photolysis).  
- Electrons are transferred through an electron transport chain, generating ATP and NADPH via chemiosmosis.  

---

### **2. Calvin Cycle (Light-Independent Reactions)**  
**Location:** Stroma of the chloroplast.  

**Inputs:**  
- **Carbon dioxide (CO₂)**  
- **ATP** (from light reactions)  
- **NADPH** (from light reactions)  

**Outputs:**  
- **Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (G3P)** (a sugar precursor)  
- **Regeneration of RuBP (ribulose bisphosphate)** (the starting molecule for the cycle)  

**Process:**  
- CO₂ is fixed into an organic molecule (RuBP) via the enzyme RuBisCO.  
- ATP and NADPH provide energy and reducing power to convert CO₂ into G3P.  
- Multiple G3P molecules are used to synthesize glucose and other carbohydrates, while some G3P is recycled to regenerate RuBP.  

---

**Summary:**  
- **Light-dependent reactions** occur in the **thylakoid membranes**, producing ATP, NADPH, and oxygen.  
- **Calvin cycle** occurs in the **stroma**, using ATP and NADPH to fix CO₂ into sugars like glucose.  

This process sustains life by converting solar energy into chemical energy stored in glucose, which fuels ecosystems.
