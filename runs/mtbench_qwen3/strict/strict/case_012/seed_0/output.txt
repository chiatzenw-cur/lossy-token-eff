**Lesson Plan: The Opium Wars - A Historical Drama Exploration**  
**Grade Level:** 9-10  
**Duration:** 3 class periods (45 minutes each)  
**Topic:** Opium Wars between China and Britain (1839–1842, 1856–1860)  

---

### **Learning Objectives**  
By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:  
1. Analyze the causes, key events, and consequences of the Opium Wars.  
2. Empathize with diverse perspectives (e.g., Qing officials, British merchants, opium traders).  
3. Use drama and theater techniques to interpret historical events and their impacts.  

---

### **Materials Needed**  
- Primary source excerpts (e.g., letters, treaties, accounts of the wars)  
- Character cards (Qing officials, British traders, opium addicts, diplomats)  
- Props (e.g., opium pipes, maps of China/Asia, treaty documents)  
- Whiteboard/projector for visuals and key terms  
- Optional: Costume accessories (simple hats, uniforms, etc.)  

---

### **Day 1: Introduction & Role-Play (Understanding Causes & Perspectives)**  
**Focus:** Origins of the Opium Wars and key stakeholders.  

#### **Activities**  
1. **Warm-Up (10 minutes):**  
   - **Mime Activity:** Students in pairs act out the process of the opium trade (e.g., opium being smuggled, addiction effects).  
   - **Discussion:** How does this activity reflect the economic and social impacts of the opium trade?  

2. **Mini-Lecture (10 minutes):**  
   - Overview of the 19th-century trade imbalance, British East India Company’s role, and Qing Dynasty’s stance on opium.  
   - Key terms: "Opium Trade," "Treaty of Nanking," "Forced Open-Door Policy."  

3. **Role-Play (20 minutes):**  
   - **Groups:** Divide students into roles (Qing officials, British traders, Chinese peasants, foreign diplomats).  
   - **Scenario:** Reenact a negotiation over the opium trade. Students use primary source excerpts to argue their positions.  
   - **Prompt:** "What are the ethical dilemmas of trade and sovereignty?"  

4. **Wrap-Up (5 minutes):**  
   - Discuss key takeaways and how role-play deepened understanding of conflicting interests.  

**Assessment:** Participation in role-play, written reflection on one perspective (e.g., "What would you prioritize as a Qing official?").  

---

### **Day 2: Theatrical Reenactment of a Key Event**  
**Focus:** Dramatizing the destruction of the opium stockpile at Humen (1839).  

#### **Activities**  
1. **Recap & Context (5 minutes):**  
   - Review Day 1’s role-play and introduce the event at Humen.  

2. **Tableau Activity (15 minutes):**  
   - **Groups:** Create a static tableau depicting the chaos of the opium destruction (e.g., Chinese soldiers, British envoys, opium vials).  
   - **Discussion:** What emotions and tensions are conveyed through body language?  

3. **Theatrical Reenactment (20 minutes):**  
   - **Script:** Provide a simplified script for students to perform the event. Include dialogue from historical accounts.  
   - **Focus:** Highlight the clash between Chinese sovereignty and British imperialism.  
   - **Optional:** Incorporate mime for non-verbal scenes (e.g., spreading of opium, international reactions).  

4. **Analysis (5 minutes):**  
   - Discuss the event’s significance: How did it escalate tensions? What were the long-term consequences?  

**Assessment:** Participation in tableau and reenactment, accuracy in portraying historical events.  

---

### **Day 3: Creative Performance & Reflection**  
**Focus:** Synthesizing knowledge through creative expression and critical reflection.  

#### **Activities**  
1. **Group Planning (10 minutes):**  
   - **Task:** In small groups, students create a 5-minute skit or tableau on the Opium Wars’ impact (e.g., a debate on imperialism, a modern-day reflection on the wars).  
   - **Guidelines:** Incorporate at least one drama technique (e.g., symbolism, improvisation, or metaphor).  

2. **Performances (20 minutes):**  
   - Groups perform their skits/tableaux.  
   - **Audience Questions:** After each performance, classmates ask questions or share insights.  

3. **Reflection & Discussion (10 minutes):**  
   - **Prompt:** "How does the legacy of the Opium Wars affect global trade and diplomacy today?"  
   - **Class Discussion:** Link historical events to modern issues (e.g., cultural imperialism, economic exploitation).  

4. **Wrap-Up (5 minutes):**  
   - Summarize key takeaways. Assign a creative homework task (e.g., write a diary entry from a historical figure’s perspective).  

**Assessment:** Quality of performance, originality in creative expression, and depth of reflection in class discussion.  

---

### **Differentiation Strategies**  
- **For Struggling Learners:** Provide pre-written dialogue, assign specific roles, or pair with peers for support.  
- **For Advanced Learners:** Encourage research on lesser-known figures (e.g., Lin Zexu) or analyze primary sources in depth.  
- **Alternative Formats:** Offer a written reflection option for those uncomfortable with performing.  

---

### **Cross-Curricular Links**  
- **English:** Analyzing historical documents, creative writing.  
- **Art:** Designing props or posters to accompany performances.  
- **Social Studies:** Critical thinking about imperialism and globalization.  

---

### **Extension Ideas**  
- Compare the Opium Wars to modern conflicts over resources or sovereignty.  
- Explore the role of propaganda in shaping public opinion during the wars.  

This lesson plan combines historical analysis with engaging drama activities, fostering empathy and critical thinking while meeting educational standards.
