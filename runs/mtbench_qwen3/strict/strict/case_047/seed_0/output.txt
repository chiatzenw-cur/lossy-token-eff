**Exothermic vs. Endothermic Reactions**  
Exothermic and endothermic reactions are classifications based on how energy is transferred during chemical processes. Here's a clear breakdown:

---

### **1. Definitions and Key Differences**  
- **Exothermic Reactions**:  
  - **Energy Release**: These reactions release energy (typically as heat) to the surroundings.  
  - **Enthalpy Change (ΔH)**: ΔH is **negative** because the system loses energy.  
  - **Temperature Effect**: The surroundings become **warmer**.  

- **Endothermic Reactions**:  
  - **Energy Absorption**: These reactions absorb energy (often as heat) from the surroundings.  
  - **Enthalpy Change (ΔH)**: ΔH is **positive** because the system gains energy.  
  - **Temperature Effect**: The surroundings become **cooler**.  

---

### **2. Criteria to Distinguish Them**  
- **Heat Transfer**:  
  - **Exothermic**: Energy flows **from the system to the surroundings** (e.g., heat released).  
  - **Endothermic**: Energy flows **from the surroundings to the system** (e.g., heat absorbed).  
- **Enthalpy Sign**:  
  - Exothermic: ΔH < 0 (products have less energy than reactants).  
  - Endothermic: ΔH > 0 (products have more energy than reactants).  
- **Observation**:  
  - Exothermic reactions often feel **warm** (e.g., combustion).  
  - Endothermic reactions often feel **cold** (e.g., ice melting).  

---

### **3. Real-World Examples**  
- **Exothermic Example**:  
  - **Combustion of Fuel** (e.g., burning wood or gasoline).  
    - **Explanation**: The oxidation of hydrocarbons releases heat and light, warming the environment.  
    - **ΔH**: Negative (energy is released).  

- **Endothermic Example**:  
  - **Dissolving Ammonium Nitrate in Water** (used in ice packs).  
    - **Explanation**: The process absorbs heat from the surroundings, causing the solution to cool.  
    - **ΔH**: Positive (energy is absorbed).  

---

### **Summary**  
- **Exothermic**: Energy is released; surroundings warm up; ΔH < 0.  
- **Endothermic**: Energy is absorbed; surroundings cool down; ΔH > 0.  
- **Key Criterion**: The **sign of the enthalpy change (ΔH)** determines the type of reaction, alongside the **direction of heat transfer**.  

By observing these energy exchanges and enthalpy changes, you can identify whether a reaction is exothermic or endothermic.
