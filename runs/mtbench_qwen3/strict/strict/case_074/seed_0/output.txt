**Balanced Chemical Equation:**  
The reaction between solid calcium carbonate (CaCO₃) and hydrochloric acid (HCl) is as follows:  
$$
\text{CaCO}_3(s) + 2\text{HCl}(aq) \rightarrow \text{CaCl}_2(aq) + \text{CO}_2(g) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l)
$$

**Type of Reaction:**  
This is an **acid-base neutralization reaction** specifically involving a **carbonate and an acid**, which produces a **salt (calcium chloride), water, and carbon dioxide gas**. It can also be classified as a **double displacement reaction** because the ions from the reactants exchange partners to form new compounds.

**Observations Indicating the Reaction:**  
1. **Effervescence (Bubbling):** The reaction produces carbon dioxide gas (CO₂), which manifests as visible bubbles in the solution.  
2. **Dissolution of Solid:** The solid calcium carbonate (a white powder or chunk) gradually dissolves as the reaction proceeds.  
3. **Temperature Change (Optional):** The reaction may slightly warm the solution, though it is not highly exothermic.  
4. **Lime Water Test:** Passing the gas through lime water (calcium hydroxide solution) would cause it to turn milky due to the formation of calcium carbonate precipitate, confirming the presence of CO₂.  

**Summary:**  
The reaction is driven by the acid (HCl) reacting with the carbonate (CaCO₃) to release CO₂ gas, dissolve the solid, and form calcium chloride in aqueous solution. The primary observable signs are bubbling and the disappearance of the solid.
