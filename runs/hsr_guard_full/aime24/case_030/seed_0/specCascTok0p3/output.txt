### 1.  What a maximal configuration must look like  

If a cell \((i,j)\) is occupied, then the colour of the chip must be the same as the colour of row \(i\) and the colour of column \(j\).  
Hence for every occupied cell  

\[
R_i=C_j .
\]

For an empty cell \((i,j)\) a new chip could be added only if the colour of the row and of the column were the same – then the cell could be filled with that colour and the conditions would still be satisfied.  
Since the position is **maximal**, every empty cell must satisfy

\[
R_i\neq C_j .
\]

Therefore the set of occupied cells is exactly

\[
\{(i,j)\mid R_i=C_j\}.
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.  Rows and columns must contain chips  

Assume a row \(i\) had no chip.  
Then for every column \(j\) the cell \((i,j)\) would be empty, but column \(j\) already has a chip of colour \(C_j\).  
Because \(R_i\neq C_j\) for all \(j\), we could add a chip of colour \(C_j\) to \((i,j)\), contradicting maximality.  
Thus **every row must contain at least one chip**.  
A symmetric argument shows that **every column must contain at least one chip**.

Consequently, for each colour there must be at least one row and one column of that colour; otherwise a row/column would have no chip.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 3.  Counting colour assignments  

Let  
* \(a\) be the number of white rows (\(1\le a\le 4\)),  
* \(b\) be the number of white columns (\(1\le b\le 4\)).

White chips are placed in the \(a\times b\) positions where the row and column are both white, and black chips are placed in the \((5-a)\times(5-b)\) positions where both are black.  
With \(a,b\in\{1,2,3,4\}\) we never exceed the available 25 chips of either colour.

\[

\]

The same count holds for the columns.

Thus the number of colour assignments with both colours present in rows and columns is

\[
30 \times 30 = 900 .
