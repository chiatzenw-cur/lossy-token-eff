The condition states that for each position, replacing the digit by \(1\) gives a number divisible by \(7\).

---

### 1.  Congruence conditions

Using \(1000\equiv 6,\;100\equiv 2,\;10\equiv 3,\;1\equiv 1\pmod 7\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
N_1&=1000+100b+10c+d\equiv 6+2b+3c+d,\\
N_2&=1000a+100+10c+d\equiv 6a+2+3c+d,\\
N_3&=1000a+100b+10+ d\equiv 6a+2b+3+d,\\
N_4&=1000a+100b+10c+1\equiv 6a+2b+3c+1.
\end{aligned}
\]

All four must be \(0\pmod 7\).  
Thus we have

\[
\begin{cases}
2b+3c+d\equiv 1,\\
6a+3c+d\equiv 5,\\
6a+2b+d\equiv 4,\\
6a+2b+3c\equiv 6.
\end{cases}\tag{1}
\]

---

### 2.  Reduce the system

Subtracting the equations pairwise gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
3c-2b &\equiv 1,\\
d-3c &\equiv 5,\\
d-6a &\equiv 2.
\end{aligned}
\]

From these we find

\[
c\equiv 2a+6,\qquad b\equiv 3a+5,\qquad d\equiv 6a+2\pmod 7. \tag{2}
\]

Insert relation (2) into the first equation of (1):

\[
2b+3c+d\equiv 1 \;\Longrightarrow\; 4a+2\equiv 1 \pmod 7
\]
which gives \(4a\equiv 6\pmod 7\), hence \(a\equiv 5\pmod 7\).

Since \(a\) is a single digit, \(a=5\) is the only possible value.

---

### 3.  Find the other digits

For \(a=5\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
c &\equiv 2\cdot5+6\equiv 2\pmod 7,\\
b &\equiv 3\cdot5+5\equiv 6\pmod 7,\\
d &\equiv 6\cdot5+2\equiv 4\pmod 7.
\end{aligned}
\]

Each digit can be the residue itself or the residue plus \(7\) (if still \(\le 9\)):

\[
\begin{aligned}
c&\in\{2,9\},\\
b&=6\quad(\text{no second choice}),\\
d&=4\quad(\text{no second choice}).
\end{aligned}
\]

So the possible numbers are \(5624\) and \(5694\).
