A rectangle whose vertices are all on the circle must have opposite vertices

through the centre.

---

### 1.  Opposite pairs (diameters)

The dodecagon has \(6\) diameters:

\[
(0,6),\;(1,7),\;(2,8),\;(3,9),\;(4,10),\;(5,11).
\]

Any rectangle will have its vertices as the two endpoints of two distinct
diameters.  
If we choose the diameters \((i,i+6)\) and \((j,j+6)\) with \(i\neq j\), the
four vertices

\[
i,\;j,\;i+6,\;j+6
\]

form a rectangle.  
Indeed, the diagonals of this quadrilateral are the two chosen diameters,
hence they are equal and bisect each other at the centre; thus all angles are
\(90^\circ\).  
The sides of the rectangle are the chords \(ij,\;j(i+6),\;(i+6)(j+6),\;(j+6)i\),
each of which is either a side or a diagonal of the dodecagon (any chord
between two vertices is a side if the vertices are adjacent, otherwise a
diagonal).

---

### 2. Counting

We only need to count the unordered pairs of distinct diameters:

\[
\binom{6}{2}=15.
\]

Every such pair yields a unique rectangle, and no other rectangle can be
obtained because a rectangle inscribed in a circle must have opposite
vertices diametrically opposite.

\[
\boxed{15}
