\[
\begin{array}{|c|c|c|}
\hline a & b & c\\
\hline d & e & f\\
\hline
\end{array}
\]

be the digits of the \(2\times3\) grid.  
The two required conditions give

\[
\begin{cases}
100a+10b+c+100d+10e+f=999,\\[2mm]
10a+d+10b+e+10c+f=99 .
\end{cases}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 1.  Re‑express the conditions

Let  

\[
X=a+d,\qquad Y=b+e,\qquad Z=c+f .
\]

Then

\[
100X+10Y+Z=999 \quad\text{(1)}
\]

and

\[
9(a+b+c)+(X+Y+Z)=99 \quad\text{(2)} .
\]

Notice that  
\[
S:=X+Y+Z=a+b+c+d+e+f .
\]

Equation (2) becomes  

\[
9T+S=99, \qquad\text{where }T:=a+b+c .
\]

Thus

\[
S=99-9T. \tag{3}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.  Find the possible \((X,Y,Z)\)

The digits satisfy \(0\le X,Y,Z\le 18\).  
From (1) we see that

\[
100X+10Y+Z=999 .
\]

If \(X\le 8\) the left side is at most \(100\cdot8+10\cdot18+18=940<999\);  
if \(X\ge 10\) the left side is at least \(100\cdot10=1000>999\).  
Hence

\[
X=9 .
\]

With \(X=9\) the equation becomes

\[
10Y+Z=999-900=99 .
\]

The only way to obtain \(99\) with \(0\le Y,Z\le 18\) is

\[
Y=9,\qquad Z=9 .
\]

Thus the *only* triple that satisfies (1) is

\[
(X,Y,Z)=(9,9,9).
\]

So \(S=X+Y+Z=27\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 3.  Determine \(T\)

Using (3):

\[
27 = 99-9T \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 9T = 72 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad T=8.
\]

Hence

\[
a+b+c=8 .
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 4.  Count the digit assignments

From \(X=9\) we have \(a+d=9\).  
Similarly \(b+e=9\) and \(c+f=9\).

For any \(a\in\{0,1,\dots ,9\}\) there is exactly one \(d=9-a\) that is a digit;  
the same holds for the pairs \((b,e)\) and \((c,f)\).

Thus the only restriction is on \(a,b,c\):

\[
a+b+c=8,\qquad a,b,c\in\{0,1,\dots ,9\}.
\]

Since each of \(a,b,c\) is at most \(8\), the upper bounds \(0\le a,b,c\le9\) are automatically satisfied.  
The number of non‑negative integer solutions to \(a+b+c=8\) is given by stars and bars:

\[
\binom{8+3-1}{3-1}=\binom{10}{2}=45 .
\]

For each such triple \((a,b,c)\) the corresponding digits
\(d,e,f\) are uniquely determined as \(9-a,9-b,9-c\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 5.  Result

There are **45** distinct ways to fill the \(2\times3\) grid with digits satisfying both conditions.

\[
\boxed{45}
\]
