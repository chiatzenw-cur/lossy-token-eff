* From the lower‑left corner to the upper‑right corner this requires  
  \(8\) steps to the right (R) and \(8\) out to the top (U) – in total  
  \(8+8=16\) unit moves.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 1.  Direction changes

A *change of direction* occurs when a step changes one of the
two possible directions.
So a path that changes exactly **four** times has 5 consecutive runs of
identical directions:

*run 1 – restart of \(R\) or \(U\)*  
*run 2 – first change*  
*run 3 – second change*  
*run 4 – third change*  
*run 5 – final run*

There are only two options in which the runs can be arranged:

```
  1.  R   U   R   U   U     (3 R‑runs and 2 U‑runs)
  2.  U   R   U   R   U     (3 R‑runs and 2 U‑runs)
```

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.  Splitting into positive parts

For a given arrangement the lengths of the run are positive integers
whose sum is 8 for the horizontal (R) moves and 8 for the vertical (U)
moves.

If we decide the path starts with “\(R\)” it must have  

```
R‑runs (3 of them) : U‑runs (first 3 R‑runs, U‑runs: 2 of them)
```

Number of ways to pack the eight \(R\)’s into \(3 R\)-runs is  

\[
\#\text{ways}=\binom{8-1}{3-1}=\binom{7}{2}=21.
\]

The two \(U\)-runs together must carry 8 upward moves; their lengths are

\[
\#\text{ways}=\binom{8-1}{2-1}= \binom{7}{1}=7.
\]

So for a path starting with \(R\) there are  

\[
21\times7=147
\]

different sequences.

If the start direction is \(U\) the situation is symmetric,
the same counts apply: for a path starting with \(U\)

the number of ways is again 147.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 3.  Total count

Adding the two possibilities gives

\[
147+147 = 294
\]

paths of length 16 that change direction exactly four times.
