Thus answer.<|end|><|start|>assistant<|channel|>final<|message|>**How the reaction occurs**

1. **Acidification of the carbonate** – When you pour dilute hydrochloric acid (HCl) onto solid calcium carbonate (CaCO₃), the proton from HCl instantaneously attacks the carbonate ion (CO₃²⁻).  
2. **Buffering step** – The first proton converts CaCO₃ to calcium chloride (CaCl₂) and a *beryllium* of bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻).  
3. **Release of CO₂** – The remaining proton ion (H⁺) quickly protonates the bicarbonate, driving it to **CO₂(g)** and water.

The net transformation is a direct acid–base/two‑forward reaction that *displaces* CO₂ gas from the solid.

---

**Balanced chemical equation**

\[
\boxed{\text{CaCO}_3(s)+2\,\text{HCl}(aq)\;\Rightarrow\;\text{CaCl}_2(aq)+\text{CO}_2(g)+\text{H}_2\text{O}(l)}
\]

*All atoms and charges are conserved.*

---

**Type of reaction**

- **Acid‑base (hydrolysis) reaction** – the H⁺ from the acid acts as a proton donor, and the carbonate exists as the conjugate base of H₂CO₃.  
- It can also be called an **acid‑carbonate cleavage** or **soda‑lime reaction**.  
- Simultaneously, it is a **double‑displacement** (metathesis) reaction on the ionic level:  

  \[
  \text{H}^+ + \text{CO}_3^{2-}\;\rightarrow\; \text{CO}_2(g)+\text{H}_2\text{O}(l)
  \]

---

**Observations that the reaction is taking place**

| Observable | Explanation |
|------------|-------------|
| **Bubbling / effervescence** | CO₂ gas is produced; the gas escapes in visible bubbles. |
| **Heat removal or release** | The reaction is slightly exothermic; the mixture warms or feels warm. |
| **Tunged color change** | The solution may slightly brighten when the solid dissolves; any traces of impurities (crusts) could color the discharge. |
| **Decrease in acid concentration** | As CaCO₃ is consumed, the acid neutralizes, so the aqueous solution becomes darker or the chemical test (pH etc.) shows a drop in acidity. |
| **Sudden bubble cadence** (if the acid is in excess) | Once all CaCO₃ is incorporated, the fizzing stops; any continued bubbling indicates CaCO₃ still present or excess acid. |

For a typical laboratory demonstration, the most unmistakable cue is the *fizzing*—the phenomenon that’s so characteristic of acid–carbonate reactions that you even get a small “lightning” effect if you do the high‑temperature test so the CO₂ turns into a tiny flame!

---

**In short**

- **Process:** Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl ⇒ NaCl + CO₂ + H₂O  
- **Equation:** CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + CO₂ + H₂O  
- **Reaction type:** Acid‑base (hydrolysis/neutralization) + double displacement  
- **Observations:** Effervescence, heat, disappearance of the solid, and a drop in acidity.
