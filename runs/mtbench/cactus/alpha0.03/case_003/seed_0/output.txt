### You’re not alone – the first step is to turn a sentiment into action.  

Below is a 7‑step framework I use with couples who were once split and are fighting for unity again. Feel free to ask again for personalised details, but I’m confident you’ll can try at least a few of these below.

---

### 1. **Find the “why” behind the conflict**  
**Action:**  
- **Ask each other:** “What happened?” (not *how* they felt).  
- **Week of “Intentions.”** Each partner writes a 1‑page summary of what the other’s main care‑point was in that situation.  
> **Why it matters:** The dialogue often shifts from *“why you did X?”* to *“what were you trying to protect me from?”* – this flips blame into discovery.

---

### 2. **Echo before you speak**  
**Technique – “Stand‑and‑Echo”**  
- One partner says a sentence in one minute.  
- The other repeats (exactly) what they had heard.  
- Confirm: “Did I catch that right?”  
- Only | after the echo, the “speaker” can extend or say “I pause.”  

> **Why it matters:** Active listening and validation reduce the defensive feel and label feelings as *valid* rather than *wrong.*

---

### 3. **Use “I” language to prevent blame**  
- I write:  
  - **I feel pain** → “I feel hurt when we talk about that.”
  - **I value** → “I value our shared calendar time; it means we’re connected.”
  - **I want** → “I wish for…”
  
**Practice:**  
- Turn a 3‑minute **blame** into an **“I” sentence**.  
- Try a quick partner exercise; pen and read aloud.

> **Why it matters:** “I” statements reduce physical defense mechanisms (asking “Why did you do that?” mindset).

---

### 4. **Create a “conversation date” and revisit the agreed ground rules**  
- **Set a timer:** 15‑30 minutes, no phones or external disturbance.  
- **Validate ground rules:**  
  1. “Let’s finish it before we speak.”  
  2. “No interrupt fast‑talk.”  
  3. “Last speaker = 30‑sec ‘adjust’ moment.”  

> **Why it matters:** A scheduled safe space prevents panic‑start loops.  
> **Tip:** End each conversation with a quick *Take‑away* summary – “Together, we decided…”.

---

### 5. **Map dreams & “pain zones”**  
**Tools**  
- **Dream‑Map** – divided: Life‑shelter, Personal growth, Collective values.  
- **Pain‑Zone scroll** – lines for the “hard things” only you *two* can share.

> **Why it matters:** Aligning on joint details keeps you from seeing each other as “others” – they’re fundamentally shared like a shared, evolving concept.

---

### 6. **Reality check: Self‑filter before drill**  
**How to self‑filter before you hear them**  
- Face steps:  
  1. Count to 5.  
  2. Pick a new “compass” phrase: “I am looking for proactive solutions.”  
  3. Re‑translate the cue as a *“How can I get that better?”* rather than *“Why did that happen?”*  

> **Why it matters:** You turn the brain‑switch from “I cannot fix” to “I can help.”  

---

### 7. **Make the bridge tangible**  
| Action | Why it does it | How to do it |
|--------|--------------|--------------|
| **Disability 50‑X** | Gives partners an indicator of health/rebasse. | Use an agreement that: Each partner records 1‑2 “add‑on”, keep a shared watch, keep each one tidy (when delivered). |
| **“Check‑In” snapshot** | Daily beat note contributions | At 6 pm each partner gives a one‑minute “digital note” reaction: “Quick vibes: good/bad?” |
| **“Tend in Light”** | Keeps the distance rending shy | Each partner has a 5‑minute personal toast that acknowledges someone’s light. |

### Quick reframing exercise

> **Write a short note of your partner** each day, outlining one thing you appreciate.  
> **Skip or give 5‑15 minutes**: label new *crack.* Build on each daily note.  

> **Pro tip:** Take a short, 10‑minute “take‑away” after each conversation with an “action list”: • I will bring back a jar‑toast. • I will not repeat the same sentence. • I will ban phone 20 min after dinner.
