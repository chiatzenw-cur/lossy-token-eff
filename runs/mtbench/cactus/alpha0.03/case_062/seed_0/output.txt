* \(B\) = event “likes blue”  \(P(B)=58\%\)  
* \(G\) = event “likes green”  \(P(G)=45\%\)  
* \(B\cap G\) = “likes both”  \(P(B\cap G)=22\%\)

The probability that a student likes **at least one** of the colors is the union \(P(B\cup G)\):

\[
P(B\cup G)=P(B)+P(G)-P(B\cap G)
           =0.58+0.45-0.22
           =0.81\;(\text{or }81\%).
\]

But we want the probability of liking **neither** blue **nor** green:

\[
P(\text{neither}) = 1-P(B\cup G)=1-0.81=0.19.
\]

So there is a **19 %** chance that a randomly chosen student likes neither blue nor green. (In decimal form, \(0.19\) or \(\frac{19}{100}\).)
