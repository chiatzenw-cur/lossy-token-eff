Machine Learning (ML) is the science of building systems that let computers operate automatically by learning patterns from data, rather than having to relearn from scratch each time. Unlike traditional programming, where a programmer specifies every rule, a machine‑learning model processes data, detects regularities, and adapts its internal parameters to achieve a task.

At the heart of ML are **three crucial classes of learning algorithms**:

| Learning Paradigm | What core signal guides the model? | Primary use‑case | Representative algorithms |
|-------------------|-----------------------------------|------------------|---------------------------|

## 1. Supervised Learning

### Concept
A supervised learner receives an **input–output pair** for each training example. The objective is to minimise the discrepancy between the model’s output and the *true* label. The sample space is *not* inherently sparse; the actively observed signals come from the target among many features.

### The “error” relationship
1. **Predict** \( y_{\text{pred}} = f_\theta(x) \) for an input `x`.
2. Compute the **loss** \( L(y_{\text{pred}}, y_{\text{true}}) \) (e.g., mean‑squared error or cross‑entropy).
3. **Back‑propagate** gradients: \( \theta \leftarrow \theta - \alpha \nabla_\theta L \).
4. Repeat over many epochs until convergence.

### Real‑world examples

| Domain | Problem | Typical model | Example |
|--------|---------|--------------|---------|
| **Finance** | Credit scoring | ML‑boosted trees | Lending a‑p is “approved” vs. “denied” |
| **Healthcare** | Hot‑spot prognostics | CNN, RNN | ICU readmission prediction from vital‑sign logs |
| **Media** | Personalized content (e.g., movie ratings) | Matrix factorization, regression | SoftUni’s recommendation engine |
| **Computer vision** | Image classification | ResNet or ResNet‑50 | Dog‑classification → “Dug” vs. “Parking‑Lot” |
| **Speech** | Speech‑to‑text | Acoustic‑to‑text neural nets | Google Voice Search |

### Technical nuances

* **Label type**: stricter (classification) vs. continuous (regression).
* **Early stopping & regularisation** to handle overfitting.
* ** Anchoring**: always calibrate using held‑out true samples for validation.

## 2. Unsupervised Learning

### Concept
No labelled data exists, so the learner rewards the model by **making sense of structural properties** (e.g., dimensionality reduction, group‑filling). The data points alone are assumed to be *explanatory* to such patterns.

### Non‑trivial structures

| Schematic idea | Typical algorithm | What it tells you |
|---------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Facets: approximate “Clusters” | k‑means | People in marketing zones |
| Beauty: training unsupervised coarse latent structure | Auto‑encoders | Reconstruction looks like a “background” |
| Separation: sub‑clustering | Gaussian mixture | PCA to witness cosmetic items |
| Hidden: 7‑dim sample can be hidden | Observations → particle‑filter |

### Practical story

*You have a log‑file of 1 million records containing `<timestamp, user_id, action, screen_width, color, click_score, ...>` where no names exist.*

1. **Clustering**: k‑means merges all customers into 5 groups.  
   *You’d notice a cluster “P1‑72” (High‑search guest).*
2. **Bundle**: PCA shows co‑location of website traffic.  
   * Insight: brand‑cluster features as training data urbanization (high‑speed). * 
3. **Anomaly detection**: Negative‑class unsupervised auto‑encoder identifies unusual patterns.

## 3. Reinforcement Learning

### Concept
A reinforcement agent interacts with a **dynamical state transition environment**. After each step, the agent changes the environment, returns a reward, and get a new state. The resulting **policy** is optimized through a feedback loop to maximise cumulative reward.

### Typical workflow

| Stage | Goal | Sample |
|-------|------|-------|
| **State** – `x_t` | Represent the world | `x_t = [cartoon coordinates, battery position, lighting]` |
| **Action** – `a_t` | The decision we wish to make | ` od += π(x_t)` |
| **Reward** – `r_t` | The environment’s tick | `x_t = r_t` (which is “actively”). |
| **Policy** – `π` | The learning plan | `π(a_t | x_t) (action; v)` parameter. |

### RL algorithm unspecified

- “Reinforcement learning” is macros that involve:

  1. **Policy gradient** – e.g., `∇θ log π(Pao, θ∇)  
   2. **TD‑learning** – discount on noisy states  
   3. **Monte Carlo** – approximate continuous vs. sequential update

### Example stash

|--------|---------|--------------|-----------|
| **Gaming** | Game‑playing (AlphaZero) | MCTS + policy‑gradient | Taking over Chess. |
| **Autonomous** | AI car; self‑driving cars | TD‑Alpha | Risks, 10–20% safe reward windows. |
