**Probability** is simply a mathematical way of talking about how often we expect something to happen.  
Think of a game: you roll a die, your friend turns a coin, or you shuffle a deck of cards.

- **Random experiment** – the process that produces the outcome (a die roll, a coin flip, a card draw)  
- **Outcome** – one particular result of the experiment (e.g., “you get a 3” or “the coin lands heads”)  
- **Sample space** of all possible outcomes (e.g., flip a coin → {Heads, Tails})  
- **Event** – a subset of the sample space (e.g., “the fair coin lands heads”)  

and we can ask: “How fast does the event happen?” That fast “rate” is probability.

## 🌟 Classic Dice Example

**Problem** – “What is the probability of rolling an even number on a fair six‑sided die?”

**Step 1 – List outcomes.**  
A die has 6 faces: {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}.

**Step 2 – Count favorable outcomes.**  
Even numbers: {2, 4, 6} → 3 favorable outcomes.

**Step 3 – Count total outcomes.**  
Total outcomes = 6.

**Step 4 – Compute.**  
\(P_{\text{even}} = \frac{\text{favorable}}{\text{total}} = \frac{3}{6} = 0.5 \) or **50 %**.

**Common “noise”**  
People sometimes confuse “average die” with “average roll.”  
We *remember* that the five outcomes are **identical** to each other (over 6 equally probable events).

## 📈 Going More: “Among those events, combine two events”

Inside a set of two possible events (**A and B**). The probability of *either* event is:

```
P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) – P(A ∩ B)
```

**What it means:**

- A probability of *both* (intersection) is solved by subtracting that once‑applied share to avoid double‑counting.  
- If A and B are **mutually exclusive** (because they cannot occur simultaneously), \(P(A ∩ B) = 0\).
