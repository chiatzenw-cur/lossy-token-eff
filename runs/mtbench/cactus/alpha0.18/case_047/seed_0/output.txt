| Reaction Type | Thermodynamic Signature | What Happens to the System | Typical Energy Flow |
|---------------|------------------------|---------------------------|---------------------|
| **Exothermic** | **ΔH < 0** (negative enthalpy change) | The system **loses** energy to surroundings. | Heat **out** of the reaction mixture. |
| **Endothermic** | **ΔH > 0** (centre additive sign) | The system **gains** energy from the surroundings. | Heat **into** the reaction mixture. |

> *In a nutshell: the system's **enthalpy** drops (exothermic), or raises (endothermic).*

## 2.  Criteria I Used to Distinguish Super‑Secular Mefic

| Criteria | Exothermic | Endothermic |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| **Energy flow** | Heat dissipates into the surroundings (unforced or forced). | Heat is taken in from the surroundings (or supplied externally). |
| **Instantaneous temperature** | Temperature of the reaction mixture increases **well below** or at reaction start (received via external heating). | Temperature of the reaction mixture decreases unless you put extra heat on the system. |
| **Enthalpy change, ΔH** | Negative \(\hbar{H} < 0\). | Positive \(\hbar{H} > 0\). |
| **Temperature of surroundings** | Tendency to **rise** when the system is in thermal isolation. | Tendency to **fall** when the system is in thermal isolation. |
| **Who “holds” the heat** | The system is the *donor* (heat stored in the reaction). | The surroundings act as the *donor* (heat liberated into the system). |

> **Practical**: When we test a reaction, pick a thermometer — if the thermometer *bounces up* or the reaction vessel *feels warmer* as soon as it starts, you’re seeing an exothermic reaction. If it *bounces down* or the vessel *feels cooler*, you’re seeing an endothermic reaction.
