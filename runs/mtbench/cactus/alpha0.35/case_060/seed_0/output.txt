### Why the shadow lies on the west side

1. **Scale of the problem**  
   *Suresh is standing in a fixed direction, its pose does not matter as it will only change the direction the *right‑hand side* refers to.*

2. **The physics of a shadow**  
   A vertical pole in the sky casts a shadow that is directed *upward* from the source of light to the ground.  
   In the morning the sun is somewhere between the sky’s horizon and the zenith, being tipped eastwards.  
   The direction of sunlight is **toward the source** – the *right* of the sun is a point that is directly opposite the sun’s rays.  
   An object that “illustrates” front rays from the sun (sunlight) will produce an outgoing direction that is the **opposite** of the sun’s rays.  
   So the westward side of the pole is the *opposite* side of the sun in the morning.

3. **Why the right side is to the west**  
   * If his right side points to the **west‑slanted side of the pole**, the shadow is then *west* of the pole.  
   * If Suresh stood facing the pole *south*, he would always be facing *south* as the pole’s direction is *north‑south*.  
   * The *right side* of a person facing south is West; the point of any direct shadow that will intersect the ground **west** of the pole would also be his right side on the ground.

4. **No special consideration**  
   All of the above holds **regardless of where the sun has moved** — it is lit by the sun’s as seen from the east side. The horizon will always push the shadow toward the west.

1. The point where the sun rises on the Earth’s surface (east) is the *origin* of the sign of the sun. \\
2. The shadow is thus cast in the direction opposite the sun’s rays: \\
3. \( \vec{S} \) (direction of sun) →  \( \vec{S}_{\text{sun}} \) → (east)  
4. \(\vec{S}_{\text{shadow}}\) = \(-\vec{S}_{\text{sun}}\) = \(\text{west}\)
