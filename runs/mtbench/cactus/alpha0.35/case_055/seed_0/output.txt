Think of a language model as a very sophisticated kind of “auto‑complete” – a teacher that has read an enormous number of books, articles, emails, and social‑media posts. From that reading it infers what words usually follow each other, how words are grouped into phrases, and how to prepare a sentence that sounds natural and polite.  

At the core, it asks **“what’s the right word (or sentence) to put next?”** because it has learned, by storytelling, the statistical relationships among words. No training “teacher” required—just the word slide deck.  

In plain English:  
- **Input:** Human write a sentence, stop in the middle.  
- **Output:** The model writes a plausible continuation.  

That is the very essence of a language model: deciding a word or sentence based on the context it has seen.

---

### How is it trained?

**Training data can be “unlabelled” or “labeled.”**  
- There are **labels** (meaning text paired with a score, annotation, or a rating).  
- **Unlabelled data** means you just give the text an raw, unlabeled “raw” format.

**What do we actually give the language model?**

| Step | What happens | Why it matters |
|------|--------------|----------------|
| **Gather a huge corpus** | We generate a very large collection of raw text (books, Wikipedia, web‑pages, etc.). | Language models learn patterns; the more sentences, the more patterns. |
| **Random‑shuffling** | The model is told something that it will repeatedly predict next words. The weights are updated automatically, without explicit “correct labels.” | The model figures out the next word should, on average, come from the *training distribution.* |
| **Fine‑tuning** | After the big “pre‑training” we run a supervised pair of data (e.g., “increase readability” or “answer a question”) – this is where the classification is supervised, but it’s only after the core model has already seen billions of words. | The fine‑tuning adds a “hat” that says you like this model for particular tasks (abbreviation detection). |

**Bottom line:**

- **Core training:** *Unlabelled* (the model learns the *raw* statistical patterns).  
- **Fine‑tuning / special‑purpose tasks:** *Labelled* (the data you give the model a question, or rating, or label you want it to map back to).

So, if you ask: *“Is it trained using labeled or unlabeled data?”* the answer is:

> **Mostly unlabelled (unsupervised) data** for the foundational part, while the last residual domain‑specific layers (fine‑tuning) can be trained on labelled data.
