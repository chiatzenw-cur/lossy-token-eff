At its core, **machine learning (ML)** is a set of techniques that allow computers to **discover patterns and make predictions or decisions** without being explicitly programmed for every detail.  
- **Data**: It learns from data.  
- **Model**: It builds an internal model that over time becomes better at the task.  
- **Objective**: Produces outputs (images, texts, actions, etc.) that match a target like a function or a set of decision rules.  

Think of ML as an apprentice who watches multiple demonstrations, learns the underlying rules, and eventually can perform afterwards.

## Three Core Paradigms

| Paradigm | What It Does | How It Learns | Typical Domain | Sample Real‑World Example |
|----------|--------------|--------------|----------------|---------------------------|
| **Supervised Learning** | Co‑ordinate specific inputs with desired outputs. | Learns by minimizing the difference between a *prediction* and a *target* (e.g., via a loss function). | Process translation, object detection, sentiment analysis. | *Spam detection*: emails labeled “spam” or “ham” → model learns to tag future emails. |
| **Unsupervised Learning** | Discovers hidden structure or patterns from data that is *unlabeled*. | Uses statistical incentives (e.g., clustering similarity, density estimation) or selects a rough normal representation. | Market segmentation, recommendation, anomaly detection. | *Customer segmentation*: groups shoppers based on purchase history to target different marketing campaigns. |
| **Reinforcement Learning** | Learns policy to optimize a *reward* through interaction with an environment. | Uses **action‑goals‑reward** trade‑offs via keen‑tracking of reward signals. | Robotics, game playing, recommendation systems. | *AlphaGo*: training an agent to play root games by treating each win as a reward. |

## How Do They Differ?

| Step | Supervised | Unsupervised | Reinforcement |
|------|------------|--------------|---------------|
| **Data** | Expert‑validated pairs (x → y). | Structured data only, no labels. | No explicit labels; the environment provides a *reward signal*. |
| **Goal** | Predict *y* for a new *x*. | Produce a useful representation (clusters, embeddings, anomaly detection). | Learn a policy (π) that outputs an action maximizing long‑term reward. |
| **Learning Efficiency** | Strong; often “clean” (labeled data). | Weak; needs extra regularization; may be noisy. | Less expensive to optimize in theory; susceptible to infinite compute. |
| **Evaluation** | Accuracy, precision/recall, MSE, etc. | Task‑agnostic but often needs distance metric, silhouette. | Activation metrics: samples, cost of episodes. |
| **Box‑level Issue** | Bias; may hit “label noise”. | Appearance of learning; may forget the concept, but can find “tension”. | “Exploration vs exploitation” problems. |
