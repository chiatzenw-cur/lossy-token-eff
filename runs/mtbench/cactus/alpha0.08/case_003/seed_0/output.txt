When tensions rise, *our first instinct is to defend* or *attack*.  
Instead, pause for a breath (or a few) and decide:

| What to *do* | Why it helps |
|--------------|--------------|

### 2. **Establish Ground Rules for “Safe” Speaking**

Think of it like a “conversation window” – it allows you both to express without interruption.

| Ground Rule | Script to use |
|--------------|--------------|
| **No interrupting** | “Let’s each finish our thoughts before we discuss.” |
| **Speak in “I”, not “You”** | “I feel hurt when this happens because I…”. |
| **Keep it within 10‑minute blocks** | “Let’s talk about it for 10 minutes, then we’ll take a 5‑minute break if needed.” |
| **Listen for understanding, not the next reply** | “I hear you saying … am I finally hearing that?” |

### 3. **Use the “Listen‑Echo‑Cut” Cycle**

1. **Listen** – Take the other words at face value.  
2. **Echo** – Repeat your partner’s main idea in first‑person language to show you heard them.  
3. **Cut** – If you want to fill in or ask clarifying questions, do so after the echo, not during the listening.

> **“I’m hearing you say that you’re… Because… [pause]**  
> **That’s a good cadence for me to feel heard—valued.”**

### 4. **Identify Core Needs, Not Surface Issues**

Often, a conflict surfaces as a *skill mismatch* (e.g. “The dishes aren’t done,” “The recipes are wrong”), highlight the underlying *common* needs:

| Surface Issue | Typical Hidden Need |
|---------------|--------------------|
| “I need the dishes done” | Feeling respected, calm at home |
| “I’m upset that plans change” | Predictability, transparency |

Both conversations should ask: “What command do you need to be met to feel respected?”

### 6. **Plan for a Checking‑in After a Successful Conflict Resolution**

Give yourself the template for reflection so the growth grows:

1. **What did you do differently?**  
2. **What did you gain from the conversation?**  
3. **What’s still tough?**  

*Quick exercise:* Write the next day or call each other to revisit the summary; it builds trust.
