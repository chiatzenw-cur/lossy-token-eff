### Initial Work‑up (What the Dr. Might Order)

| Test | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| **CBC (Complete Blood Count)** | Detect WBC elevation, anemia, infection. |
| **CMP (Comprehensive Metabolic Panel)** | Electrolytes, liver enzymes, kidney function. |
| **Lipase & Amylase** | Suspected pancreatitis. |
| **Uric Acid** | Gout or kidney stones. |
| **Urinalysis** | Urinary tract infection, hematuria. |
| **Lactate** | Hypoperfusion or sepsis. |
| **Abdominal X‑ray / Ultrasound / CT** | Structural causes (appendix, gallbladder, bowel obstruction). |

**Note:** If you’re already in pain or uncertain, it is safer to obtain at least a **CBC** and **lactate** if you have access to a lab; otherwise, go to urgent care or ER.

### Conventional (Pharmaco‑Therapeutic) Management — If the Diagnosis is Clear

| Condition | First‑line Medications (Examples) | Practical Points |
|-----------|-----------------------------------|------------------|
| **Acute Appendicitis** | Immediate surgical consultation; NSAIDs only after analgesic test to confirm not surgical. | **Avoid** unnecessary opioids until definitive diagnosis. |
| **Cholecystitis** | **NSAIDs** (naproxen 500 mg q8h) for pain, **bile acids** if needed. Antibacterial therapy (e.g., ceftriaxone + metronidazole) if infection suspected. | If gallstones, consider **cimetropium** for motility but not first‑line. |
| **Acute Pancreatitis** | **IV fluids** aggressively; analgesics (acetaminophen ≤ 4 g/day, avoid NSAIDs); anti‑emetics (ondansetron). | **Avoid** opioids if possible—lactic acid, constipation. |
| **Peptic Ulcer** | **Proton‑pump inhibitors** (omeprazole 20 mg BID) for ≥4 wks; **H₂ blockers** (famotidine 20 mg BID) if mild. | Abstain from alcohol, NSAIDs & smoking. |
| **IBS/Functional** | Antispasmodics (dicyclomine 20 mg q6h), **antidepressants** for pain (low dose amitriptyline). | Dietary management (low FODMAP). |
| **Diverticulitis** | **Broad‑spectrum antibiotics** (e.g., amoxicillin‑clavulanate), pain control (acetaminophen). | Bed rest and low‑fiber diet until symptoms subside. |

> **Remark:** For all patients, avoid NSAIDs if there is a suspicion of **peptic ulcer disease** or **renal impairment**, and be particularly cautious with **opioids** when the pain’s etiology remains unclear or if the patient is prone to constipation – common in older adults.

### When to Seek Immediate/“Red‑Flag” Care

| Red‑Flag Symptom | Why Urgent |
|-------------------|------------|
| Severe, sharp pain that wakes you from sleep | Appendicitis, perforation, pancreatitis |
| Fever > 38.5 °C + abdominal guarding | Infection, peritonitis |
| Persistent vomiting | Shock risk, aspiration |
| Blood in stool or vomit | GI bleeding (ulcer, diverticulosis, malignancy) |
| Severe back pain accompanied by abdominal pain, especially in mid‑epigastric region | Pancreatitis, aortic dissection |
| Unexplained weight loss, night sweats | Possible malignancy, chronic infection |
| Inability to pass gas or stool for > 48 h | Possible obstruction |
| Pelvic pain + vaginal bleeding (women) | Ectopic pregnancy, ovarian torsion |

If any of these signs are present, **do not wait**. Call emergency services or go to the nearest ER immediately.
