## 1. Key Components

| Component | Function | Typical Variants | Design Notes |
|-----------|----------|------------------|--------------|
| **Solar Collector** | Harvests solar energy and heats the fluid | • Flat‑plate (FPC)<br>• Evacuated‑tube (ET) **(preferred for cooler climates)**<br>• Concentrating PV‑thermal (rare for 1‑4 kW residential scale) | • 2–8 kW array depending on space and demand<br>• Material: stainless steel or aluminum with glass cover |
| **Heat Transfer Fluid (HTF)** | Transfers thermal energy from collector to tank | • Glycol‑water mix (0‑50 % antifreeze)<br>• Pure water (in warm seasons) | • 35 % glycol for low‑temperature fluctuations |
| **Buffer / Intermittent Storage Tank (S1)** | Stores collector‑heated fluid, provides spill‑over control | • Fluid‑filled tank 300–2000 L | • Works with a heat‑exchanger & balancing valve |
| **Primary Storage Tank (S2)** | Holds thermostatically‑conditioned hot water for use | • 200–500 L insulated (polyethylene/foam) | • Attached directly to plumbing; leg‑arm for inlet |
| **Pump & Motor** | Moves HTF through collectors → S1 → S2 | • Solar‑driven water pump (IP65 sealed) or, if available, a wind/solar hybrid | • Use of three‑phase max‑35 rpm motor filters » reduces noise |
| **Controller / PLC** | Automates pumping, valve actuation, safety limits | • 4‑wire “”Intelligent” controller or PLC with RTU | • Exposes Modbus/TCP, communicates with building‑automation system |
| **Valves** | Isolation & flow control | • Solenoid balancing valve<br>• Three‑way diverter (collector → store or skip)<br>• Backup low‑temp non‑return valve | • Valve positioning ensures no cold‑water back‑flow into collectors |
| **Expansion & Safety** | Protects against over‑pressure & thermal expansion | • Expansive tank (gel or rubber)<br>• Pressure relief valve (0.4 MPa) | • Check valve upstream of tanks ideally |
| **Piping & Insulation** | Transfers fluid between components | • Copper (UGE‑C) or PEX for warmth<br>• Insulation (foam or fiberglass) rated ≤3 °C loss | • Rectilinear routing reduces pressure drop |
| **Backup Heater** | Provides hot water during low‑solar periods | • Gas condensing boiler (BHKW) or an 850 W electric immersion backup | • Automatic switchover programmed in controller |
| **Monitoring & Data Acquisition** | Enables remote diagnostics | • Temperature sensors (NTC), flow meters, pressure transducers<br>• IoT gateway (Wi‑Fi or LTE) | • Provides usage analytics and fault detection |

## 2. Design Considerations

| Category | Question | Suggested Approach |
|----------|----------|--------------------|
| **Site & Climate** | What is the average daily insolation? | Use Meteonorm or local data; pick collectors rated >90 % effective during 1,000 kWh/m² yr. |
| **Roof Geometry & Orientation** | Is the roof suitable for collectors? | Tilt 30–35° southward (N hemisphere). Avoid shading from trees or higher buildings. |
| **Space & Layout** | How many square meters are available? | Map out collector area; allocate buffer tank in a garage or utility room. |
| **System Capacity** |  Typical hot‑water demand per day? | Use IAEA/ASHRAE comfort curve; size collector * 1.3–1.5× demand to cover peak. |
| **Control Strategy** | Do we use 3‑phase (e.g., 230 V/50 Hz) or single‑phase pumping? | Small residential system can use a 3‑phase Step‑down motor and a PWM controller. |
| **Safety & Code Compliance** | What local codes? | Verify with EN 12975 (boiler), EN 38090 (solar DHW), local plumbing codes. |
| **Reliability & Maintenance** | How easy is to access pump and valves? | Place the pump and controller in an accessible utility floor. |
| **Economics** | Payback period? | Perform LCOE (Levelized Cost of Energy) calculation; include tax incentives and rebates. |
| **Redundancy** | What if pumps fail? | Include a manual bypass valve and a sealed backup unit. |
| **Environmental Impact** | Water‑use? | Use a closed‑loop system with minimal grey‑water needs. |

### Quick Reference Checklist

* **Collector mounting** – secure, corrosion‑resistant brackets; tilt adjustment.
* **Pump voltage** – higher starting voltage (≥ 400 V) for quick start; else start‑up surge handling.
* **Heat‑exchanger to S1** – 2‑way welded plate heat‑exchanger, 10 mm gap.
* **Backup control** – SWITCH‑OVER algorithm: if collector temp < 45 °C or outside temp < 10 °C → ACTIVATE backup heater.
* **Water‑purity filter** – 0.2 µm cartridge in collector inlets to limit fouling.
