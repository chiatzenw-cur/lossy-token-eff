*Grade Level:* 9‑10  
*Duration:* 3 class periods (45 min each)  
*Theme:* The Opium Wars (1839‑1842 & 1856‑1860) – China vs. Britain  
*Objective:*  
- Students will analyze the political, economic, and cultural forces that sparked the Opium Wars.  
- Students will employ drama, mime, and basic theatre‑techniques to embody historical actors, settings, and conflicts.  
- Students will assess how performance can illuminate motives, consequences, and perspectives in history.  

---

## Day 1 – 45 min  
### 1. Warm‑up (10 min) – “Body‑Symbol Mapping”  
| Activity | Purpose | Steps |
|----------|---------|-------|
| **Act‑the‑Trade** | Activate kinesthetic memory & a shared vocabulary | 1. Stand in a circle. 2. Teacher says a term (e.g., “opium,” “trade,” “Canton”); all students create one‑body‑movement to represent it. 3. Quick “freeze‑frame” – each student holds the pose. |  

*Takeaway:* Students get comfortable using the body to visualize abstract historical concepts.  

### 2. Contextual Mini‑Lecture (10 min)  
- **Key points:** British economy & imperialism, Chinese self‑contained trade policy, the “Golden Lotus” trade, the 1839–42 Opium War & the Treaty of Nanking, the 1856–60 war & Treaty of Tianjin, long‑term impacts on China.  
- **Resources:** PowerPoint with images of Macartney’s embassy, Tsarists, opium market, treaty signing.  
- **Integration Tip:** After each major point, pause for a 15‑second “mime‑check” – students echo the idea in a physical gesture.

### 3. Primary‑Source Analysis (10 min)  
- Students read brief, annotated excerpts from:  
  1. Lord Macquorn’s diary entry on the opium stash.  
  2. A 19th‑century Chinese observer’s lament.  
  3. The Treaty of Nanking clause on “extraterritoriality.”  
- In small groups, identify *emotions* and *motives* in each text.

### 4. Dramatic Grouping & Role Allocation (15 min)  
| Step | Teacher | Students |
|------|---------|----------|
| **Identify Key Figures** | Present list: Sir Henry Montagu, Lord Macquorn, Kadoshō, Qiu Shizhen, Emperor Daoguang. | Vote or brainstorm which role each student feels best suited for. |
| **Research & Script** | Share resources: digital archives, web pages, library books. | Write a 2‑scene script (max 2 min each) capturing a critical negotiation moment. |
| **Physical Preparation** | Explain use of **“lean‑in frames”** (body posture signaling power/weakness). | Warm‑up: stretching the upper torso, shoulder circles. |

*Homework:* Finish scripts; think of one dramatic gesture (mime) that will represent each character’s emotional state.

---

## Day 2 – 45 min  
### 1. Quick Review & “Sound/Movement Check” (5 min)  
- Students perform the gestures from the previous day for 30 sec.  
- Teacher gives feedback on “physical veracity” (how well the gesture reflects the role).

### 2. “Technique Lab” – Mime & Call‑Blue (20 min)  
| Technique | Description | Execution |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Macro‑Mime** | Large, gestural storytelling. | Students pair up; each pair acts a 1‑minute story of an event (e.g., “Opium in Canton”); no dialogue. |
| **Object‑Free Mime** | Convey objects via invisible representations. | Teacher demonstrates with “white paper” – students alliling the texture/shape of the paper. |
| **Call‑Blue** | Sub‑text utilisation: unheard voices. | Students sit back‑to‑back, speak “to one other.” While their partner narrates what the first says – practise subtle cues. |

*Outcome:* Students can now create vicarious “physical” narratives of events, enhancing empathy.

### 3. Script Refinement (10 min)  
- In original groups, incorporate at least one mime element (from the lab) into the scripts.  
- Teacher circulates, offering questions that push for deeper symbolic choices.

### 4. Rehearsal & Blocking (10 min)  
- Groups rehearse in-place onto a “stage” marked by a chalk circle.  
- Teacher mentors blocking: where are characters? What signals confrontations? Who has eye contact?  
- Peer feedback on “presence” and “stage‑feel.”

---

## Day 3 – 45 min  
### 1. Performance Rotation (15 min)  
- Students form 3‑4 “theatre booths”.  
- Each group performs their 4‑minute skit in turns (including a brief opening “scene” of the setting).  
- **Note:** Each performance must include: a spoken dialogue, a mime sequence, and a stage direction.

### 2. “Drama Evaluator” Peer Review (10 min)  
- Use a simple rubric:  
  1. **Historical Accuracy** (5 pts) – content matches known facts.  
  2. **Dramatic Technique** (5 pts) – effective use of mime/pose, vocal variety.  
  3. **Emotive Impact** (5 pts) – audience visibly engages.  
  4. **Teamwork** (5 pts) – how well did members coordinate?  

Students give each group a score + one strength + one recommendation.

### 3. Reflection & Socratic Seminar (15 min)  
| Prompt | Discussion Points |
|--------|-------------------|
| *“What perspective do we, as 21st‑century observers, carry when portraying these historical figures?”* | Investigate biases, privilege, colonial narratives. |
| *“How can drama help us understand the stakes of treaty negotiation in ways that the text alone cannot?”* | Sensory memory, embodied empathy, active recall. |
| *“If you were a child of 1840s Guangzhou, how would you have interpreted the arrival of opium?”* | Speak in student’s voice. |

Teacher facilitates, encouraging the use of dramatic memories to answer.

### 4. Exit Ticket (5 min)  
Students answer:  
1. One new fact you learned today.  
2. One way the performance changed or reinforced your understanding.  
3. Suggest an alternative modern medium for exploring this history (e.g., VR, graphic novel).

---

## Materials & Tech  
- Projection with PowerPoint & videos (10‑15 s opium market footage).  
- Sound system for short audio clips (excerpts of speeches).  
- 3–4 large whiteboards for blocking notation.  
- Sticky‑notes for improvisation prompts.  

## Assessment & Extension  
- **Continuous**: Observation of engagement, use of drama tools.  
- **Summative**: Final reflection essay (500 words) tying drama to historical analysis + the rubric from Day 3.  
- **Extension**: Compare the Opium Wars dramatization to a modern “global trade negotiation” (e.g., US‑China tech trade talks) through a mini‑play.  

## Safety & Inclusion Notes  
- Conduct a 3‑minute pre‑lesson stretch to prevent injuries during physical enactment.  
- Provide alternative “low‑impact” gestures for students with mobility limitations.  
- Remind students that all portrayals are respectful and historically grounded; avoid caricature or cultural appropriation.
