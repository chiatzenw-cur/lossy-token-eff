## 2. Structural Design – Choosing a Seismically‑Resilient System

| System | Key Seismic Advantages | Typical Layout for 1.8 km |
|--------|-----------------------|---------------------------|
| **Cable‑Stayed (CSR)** | • Light deck, high flexibility → lower inertial forces <br>• Towers can be isolated <br>• Long‑span flexibility reduces mid‑span stresses | • 3–4 towers each 50–70 m tall <br>• 4–5 cable stays per side spanning 300–500 m |
| **Segmented Box‑Girder (SBG)** | • Steel–concrete composite offers ductility <br>• Redundant load paths <br>• Can use prefabricated segments (handled by cranes) | • 6–8 piers, each 30–40 m tall <br>• 6 segmental decks of 250 m |
| **Arch‑Bridge (A‑B)** (open‑spandrel or closed‑spandrel) | • Arch action transfers loads compressively → efficient <br>• Can be constructed with pounders (less on‑site work) | • Few piers (2–3) <br>• 500‑m arch span(s) |

### 2.1 Why Cable‑Stayed?
1. **Reduced Stiffness → Less Earthquake Demand** – Sweet‑spot interaction with ground motion; the deck behaves as a nearly mass‑only system.
2. **Base‑Isolation Capability** – Towers can be anchored with **S‑ or M‑type isolation bearings**.  Coupled with a **tuned mass damper (TMD)** at deck level, the structure can dissipate > 70 % of seismic energy.
3. **Prefabrication & Speed** – Long stays and tower sections can be fabricated off‐site, then lowered or erected with lower‑risk methods.

> **Bottom line:** The cable‑stayed solution is *flexible, lightweight, and highly modular*, perfect for seismic resilience and fast construction.

### 2.2 Core Seismic Design Elements

| Element | Feature | Seismic Benefit |
|---------|---------|------------------|
| **Deck** | *Composite steel‑concrete viaduct* with steel girder fronting | Provides ductile energy dissipation via steel’s “crossover” point; composite action reduces weight. |
| **Tower Foundations** | *Deep pile caps* with **suction grouting** (to mitigate settlements; assures isolation integrity) | Stabilizes tower against ground slide; secures interface for isolation bearings. |
| **Isolation Bearings** | *Hybrid S‑bearings* (spring‑cylinder + steel‑cylinder) at tower base | Allows controlled horizontal and vertical motion; absorbs peak ground displacement. |
| **Cable Stay Tensioning** | *Active tension control* (tensiometers + high‑performance steel) | Maintains cable stiffness during seismic action; reduces temporary overstress. |
| **Damping** | *Tuned Mass Damper* perched on deck or a *viscous damping system* in the stay‑cable assemblies | Lowers post‑quake vibration, protecting structural elements and pedestrians. |
| **Redundancy** | *Multiple stays per tower* (≥ 4 lateral partners) + *outrigger cables* at mid‑span | Ensures failure‑tolerant behavior; if one stay fails, others carry the load.

## 3. Construction Strategy – Low‑Risk, High‑Quality

### 3.1 Prefabrication & Off‑Site Assembly

| Phase | Prefab Element | Construction Benefit |
|-------|----------------|----------------------|
| **3.a. Tower Sections** | 15–20 m tower modules (concrete with embedded post‑tension cables) | Reduced on‑site concrete pours (less vibration on soil). |
| **3.b. Deck Segments** | 12–15 m precast concrete segments (with attached steel masts) | Steel‑lifting reduces crane load; simultaneous deck and stay assembly. |
| **3.c. Stay‑Cable Galvanized Parallel Chains** | 50 m stay‑cable lengths | Avoids on‑site steel welding → fewer weld‑related fire hazards. |

### 3.2 Construction Sequencing & Phasing

| Stage | Activities | Seismic Safety Measure |
|-------|------------|------------------------|
| **Stage 1** | *Pier Foundations* – Sequential pile driving + slab footing | Ensure each pier's footing is fully cured before adjacent pier’s nails; limit long‑span loading during construction. |
| **Stage 2** | *Tower Erection* – Chen‑style or incremental lift | Use **fixed‑point tensioning** to keep tower vertical during lift; maintain isolation bearing alignment. |
| **Stage 3** | *Stay‑Cable Installation* – Actively tensioned via hydraulic rigs | Tension incrementally to meet design limits; avoid abrupt jumps that could generate unwanted forces. |
| **Stage 4** | *Deck Launching* – Push or pull method depending on approach | Launch with controlled speed (≤ 0.5 m/s) to reduce aerodynamic and seismic coupling. |
| **Stage 5** | *Demolition of Temporary Scaffolds* | After toasting and testing of the structure; ensure no residual load on unsupported portions. |

### 3.3 Construction Safety Protocols in a Seismic Context

1. **Real‑time Seismic Monitoring** – Install accelerometers on towers and foundation to detect ground motion as construction progresses.  
2. **Emergency Shutdown Go‑Aways** for cranes and lifting rigs – Immediate power‑off thresholds ~ 0.3 g.  
3. **Temporary Seismic Isolation for Work Platforms** – Use crush‑pads or isolation footings to keep workers safe.  
4. **Post‑Seismic Inspection Checks** – Before heavy equipment returns to a segment, perform visual & ultrasonic inspection of reattached cables, bearings, and deck contact surfaces.

### 3.4 Example of Prefabricated Module Launch

*A 12‑m deck segment with embedded stay‑cable heads will be cast in a dedicated segmental yard.  Once cured, the segment is lifted onto a hydraulic jack, tensioned to exact specifications, and then lowered onto the preceding pier with a gantry crane.  The stay cable attaches to a steel tower and is tensioned simultaneously.  This loop‑oriented workflow simultaneously completes the **stay cable** and **deck panel** in one coordinated lift, dramatically shortening construction time and reducing hazard exposure.*

| **4.2. Cable Tension Audits** | Annual hydraulic re‑tensioning checks using load cells. | Keeps stays within design regime; extends service life. |
| **4.3. Seismic Basis Reassessment** | Every 10‑year review of PSHA data (based on new fault studies). | Ensures the bridge remains compliant with updated seismic standards. |
| **4.4. Maintenance Plan** | Damping medium renewal (viscous damper fluid), PETT coating on steel components, and repointing of concrete. | Minimises corrosion, rust, and plating degradation that could alter stiffness. |
