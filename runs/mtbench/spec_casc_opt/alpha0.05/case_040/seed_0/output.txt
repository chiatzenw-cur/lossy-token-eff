Since \(n\) is divided by \(10\) and leaves an remainder of \(4\),

\[
n \equiv 4 \pmod{10}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad n = 4 + 10k \quad (k\in\mathbb{Z})
\]

Now double this number:

\[
2n = 2(4 + 10k) = 8 + 20k
\]

Now consider the remainder when \(2n\) is divided by \(4\).

\[
8 \equiv 0 \pmod{4},\qquad 20k \equiv 0 \pmod{4}\quad\text{(since \(20k\) is a multiple of 4)}
\]

Thus

\[
2n \equiv 0 \pmod{4}.
\]

So the remainder of \(2n\) when divided by \(4\) is **0**.
