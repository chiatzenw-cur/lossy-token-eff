## 1. The “Pause‑and‑Check” Method (30‑second question)

| Step | How to do it | Example |
|------|--------------|---------|
| **Pause** | Stop and breathe for 3 deep breaths. | “I’m noticing my pulse quickening.” |
| **Identify** | “I feel hurt when I hear being upset.” | “What feels most upsetting to you of the last time?” |
| **Ask** | “Can you share what’s on your mind?” | “How do you feel about the chores you noticed?” |

**Why it works**  
You start by clearing the air before you blame. It signals you’re open to dialogue and not just to win.

---

## 2. The “I‑Statement–You‑Listen” Cycle

| Stage | Language | Purpose |
|-------|----------|---------|
| **I‑Statement** | “I feel ___ when ___.” | Owns the emotion, shows you’re not blaming. |
| **Listen** | “Do you hear that I’m saying…?” | Gives them space to confirm. |
| **Clarify** | “So I’m uncertain whether you believe…?” | Ensures you understood what they want. |

**Example**  
> “I feel hurt when you interrupt me when I’m speaking. I notice I’m frustrated because it feels like I’m not being heard. Did you come to mind that you committed to listening?”

---

## 3. “Three‑Step Conflict Map”

1. **Identify the Core Issue**  
   *List unambiguously* (e.g., “I’m upset about the Sunday meal plan”).  
2. **Explain**  
   *Narrate without blame.*  
   *Example:* “I feel left at the kitchen table when we don’t plan it together.”  
3. **Request**  
   *Give a concrete, actionable ask.*  
   *Example:* “Could we spend five minutes discussing the menu each week?”

> *Tip:* Write it down (even during a text thread). It details both of you now and removes lingering drama.

---

## 4. The “Ethical Listening” Framework (5‑step)

| Step | Action | Why it Helps |
|------|--------|--------------|
| **1️⃣ Acknowledge** | “Paraphrasing, I hear you say X.” | Shows you’re noticing. |
| **2️⃣ Empathize** | “I can see why you’d feel that way.” | Validates feelings. |
| **3️⃣ Clarify** | “I’m not sure I understand… could you mean Y?” | Keeps the conversation respectful. |
| **4️⃣ Reflect** | “You’re bringing up Z, and that’s….” | Addresses all points. |
| **5️⃣ Invite** | “Let’s figure out a solution together.” | Moves conversation forward. |

---

## 5. “Three‑Step Test” to Check Your Approaches

| Question | How to apply  | What to look for |
|----------|---------------|------------------|
| **Does My Partner Feel Heard?** | Ask, “Did anything ever feel wrong?” | Gauge “yes/no” strongly. |
| **Do I Hear Their Needs?** | Partner to summarise your silence. | Mistake? |
| **Do Our Actions Match Words?** | Gradually adjust the actions until they match. | Confirmation of intentions. |

## 6. Handling Emotional Overload (The “Phase‑C” Strategy)

| Phase | What to do | Example |
|-------|------------|---------|
| **C‑1 ✓ tangible needs** | Offer a small, relevant choice. | “I can pick the dinner menu or you can choose the weekend.” |
| **C‑2 ✓ simpler language** | Use “I understand” instead of “You never say X.” | “I understand you’re left out.” |
| **C‑3 ✓ emotional language** | Use “Let’s imagine it as…” | “If you’re still angry, we’re in a loop.” |

## 7. Practical “Switch‑Room” Ideas

| Situation | Quick Moves | Spotlight |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Chore disagreements** | One‑on‑one lists, “Do you want it done before or after.” | Your partnership, not chores. |
| **Finances** | *Announce* a “money‑talk” every 30 days; no surprises. | Honesty + transparency. |
| **Intimacy** | *Floor‑lights* – ask “What did you want to feel closer?” | Re‑connect, not the other’s real move. |
| **Parenting** | Structured “Time‑table” for family duties; each parent gets a turn. | Equality and prevention. |

---

## 8. Where to Seek Professional Help

- **Co‑therapy** – a single therapist seeing both partners.  
- **Marriage counseling** – structured, rated by partner satisfaction.  
- **Mediation** – if you’re comfortable with neutral third‑party sufficiency.  

> “If the same pattern repeats, you’ll want to bring guidance widely.”

## 9. Quick “You” Script to Use

> “I hear you, and I feel excited to solve our issue together. What can we do right now to make the next conversation feel safe?”
