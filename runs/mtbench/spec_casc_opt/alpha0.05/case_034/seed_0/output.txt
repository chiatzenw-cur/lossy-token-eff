## 1. Quick “Red‑Flag” Checklist  
Check for these triggers (any one of them means you should seek urgent help):

| Symptom | Why it matters |
|---------|----------------|
| **Sudden, severe pain** (especially sharp, localized, or “inside-out” pain) | Could indicate appendicitis, pancreatitis, perforated ulcer, etc. |
| **Fever ≥ 38.5 °C (101 °F)** | Infection or inflammation likely. |
| **Rigidity/guarding** (imminent abdominal pain when touched) | Suggests peritoneal irritation. |
| **Intractable vomiting** or bile‑colored vomit | Possible obstruction or severe pancreatitis. |
| **Bloody or black tarry stools** | GI bleeding (e.g., peptic ulcer, esophageal varices). |
| **Shortness of breath or chest pain** | Could be a left‑sided heart failure, pulmonary embolism, or metastatic disease. |
| **Pain during pregnancy or after awakening** | Could be ectopic pregnancy, ovarian cyst, or miscarriage. |
| **History of gallstones** (e.g., sudden biliary pain) | Progressive ischolecystitis. |
| **Signs of low blood pressure (pale, white‑eyed, sweating)** | Sepsis; possible perforation or pancreatitis. |

If any of these red‑flags are present, **call emergency services or go for urgent care immediately.**

## 2. Structured History of Present Illness

1. **Onset & Duration**  
   - *When did it start?* (e.g., 3 h ago, 2 days ago, recent episode)  
   - *What was the pain like?* (sharp, dull, cramping, constant, intermittent)  

2. **Location & Radiation**  
   - *Where exactly?* (upper abdomen, right abdomen, left abdomen, etc.)  
   - *Has it moved or spread?* (e.g., from epigastric to right lower quadrant)

3. **Emotional State**  
   - *Pain scale?* (0‑10)  
   - *Any relief/discomfort?* (ever pain, or does it change with movement or food?)

4. **Associated Physical Symptoms**  
   - *Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, fever, chills, rash, chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting?*  

5. **Recent Fecal/Bowel Appearance**  
   - Sunny‑yellow, black, red.  

6. **Feeding Habits**  
   - Did you ate or drink anything unusual or unusual (e.g., greasy, spicy, cold, high‑fat)?  

7. **Medical History / Medications**  
   - *Chronic conditions:* diabetes, hypertension, pre‑existing GI disease, kidney disease, heart disease.  
   - *Medications:* NSAIDs, anticoagulants, steroids, antacids, PPIs, antibiotics.  
   - *Medications:* any recent new drugs or supplements?  
   - *Recent surgeries:* abdominal, pelvic, or peritoneal.  

8. **Lifestyle and History**  
   - Alcohol intake, smoking, alcohol, drug use.  
   - Medals oriented toward consumption of red meat, oily foods.  
   - Physical activity level.  

9. **Travel & Exposure**  
   - Recent travel, new food, travel, or dietary changes.  

10. **Family History**  
    - GI cancer, inflammatory bowel disease, gallstones, pancreatitis.

## 3. Common Differential Diagnoses

| Group | Typical Features | Why they fit your scenario |
|-------|------------------|---------------------------|
| **A. Gastrointestinal** |  | |
| Appendicitis | Pain starts at right lower quadrant, worsens with movement, fever, vomiting. | Severe abdominal pain; may be associated with fever. |
| Cholecystitis | RUQ pain, nausea, vomiting, after fatty meal. | Intense pain with cold travel. |
| Pancreatitis | Epigastric pain radiates to back, nausea, vomiting, seen in heavy alcohol use or gallstones. | Intense pain; specific associated right pain. |
| Peptic ulcer ulcer | Burning epigastric pain, burning; worsens with meals or blank stomach. | Severe abdominal pain. |
| Gastroenteritis / Food poisoning | Bowel cramping, diarrhea, vomiting, fever. | Red‑flag: vomiting, fever. |

## 5. Immediate “Next Steps”

1. **Do Not Delay**  
   - If you suspect any of the above red‑flags, consider calling emergency services or a walk‑in urgent care.  

2. **If Pain is *Moderate* but *Not Emergency**  
   - **Nasal/Head‑stack**: If you have healthy heart with none of the red‑flags, you might consider:
      - **Hydration** – sip water or oral rehydration solution.  
      - **Diet(s)** – bland diet (plain toast, bananas, rice).  
      - **Over‑the‑counter medications** – acetaminophen, caffeine‑free NSAIDs if no contraindications.  

3. **Pediatric or Elderly**  
   - **Pediatric**: Many infants get abdominal pain from constipation, GERD, etc.  
   - **Elderly**: Chronic conditions (e.g., abdominal aortic aneurysm, dehydration) may present.  

4. **Patient‑Specific**  
   - Take **age** into consideration: Lightning, sudden surgical events often more likely in young adults or those with gynecologic issues; older adults can have higher risk for ischemic heart disease or diverticulitis.  

5. **When to Seek Medical Advice**  
   - **Any** of the following: >2 days of abdominal pain, fever >38 °C, vomiting, tachycardia >120 bpm, blood in stool, not able to tolerate fluids, or any change in mental status.

## 6. “Before You Wait»  
- **Note**: If the pain began at the same time as a *specific event* (trauma, new medication, walk‑in meals, GI exposure), personal notes.  
- **Home Logs**: Keep a diary of the pain, everyday sensations, food and drink intake, and any medications.  

## 7. Summary

| Common Causes | Key Identifying Features | Quick Action |
|---------------|------------------------|--------------|
| Appendicitis | RLQ pain, fever, nausea | Urgent care if severe. |
| Cholecystitis | RUQ pain, after fatty meal | Urgent care if severe. |
| Pancreatitis | Epigastric pain, severe, alcohol, gallstones | Urgent care if severe. |
| Peptic ulcer | Epigastric burning, mild | Urgent care if bleeding or severe. |
| Gastroenteritis | Diarrhea, vomiting, fever | Seek care if fever >38 °C or dehydration. |
| Ovarian torsion | Sudden unilateral pain | Urgent care if severe. |
| Ectopic pregnancy | Pregnant, bleeding, pain | Seek care if ectopic suspected. |
| Kidney stones | Radial ache, hematuria | Urgent care if intense, hematuria. |

> **Bottom line:** Unless you have a known history of something benign (like a known gallstone but you’re not anemic or real‑painful), **do not wait**. If you’re uncertain or the pain is significant (especially with any symptom, fever, or red‑flag), please seek urgent medical help.
