## 1. Start with a “Safe Space” Check‑In

| Step | What to Do | Why It Helps |

## 2. Use “I” Statements and Active Listening

| I‑Statement | Example | How to Practice |
|-------------|---------|-----------------|
| **I feel** | “I feel upset when the laundry pile gets bigger and I feel that I’m not supported.” | Speaking from **your emotion** rather than an accusation. |
| **I need** | “I need someone to help me with the dishes, a small shared time.” | Clarifying concrete needs reduces ambiguity. |
| **I notice** | “I noticed you were angry the other day, and it made me worry.” | Listens to what’s happening—valuing observation, not interpretation. |

### Listening Drill
1. **Speaker**: 2‑3‑minute uninterrupted sharing (“I feel that…”).  
2. **Listener**: 1‑minute reflection: “So you’re saying, *you feel…* and you need… (paraphrase).”  
3. **Check**: “Did I get that right?”  
4. **Switch second**.

Repeat until each of you feels heard. It’s four minutes, but it can add up to a lifetime of ‘understood’ moments.

## 3. Turn Conflicts into “Investments”

* **Reframe** what the conflict means for *you* as an institution: “We’re only here to stay together. And we’ll do this.”

  | Question | Goal |
  |----------|------|
  | What’s the bigger picture? | You’re both committed to building a life, not just the target. |
  | What’s the underlying emotion? | Fear, hurt, insecurity… recognizing it can dissolve hostility. |
  | How will we win together? | Think of an outcome that benefits both, like a mutual compromise. |

* **Example**: Instead of “Why do you never help with the dishes?” and “I never dish!” shift to “It’s hard for me to keep the space tidy when I don’t have one or two help. How can we split the chores in a way that works for both?”

## 4. Open a “Neutral Inquiry” Window

Instead of jumping straight into blame, ask curiously:

1. **Choose a question instead of a statement**  
   *“Could you tell me how you felt when I sent that text?”*  

2. **Invite them to ask something too**  
   *“What would you give me if you could?”*

3. **Brainstorm *solutions* together**  
   Set the clock for 5 minutes to brainstorm (no judgement) and then vote on the best.  

The process: **(1) Identify the problem → (2) Share from *your* perspective → (3) Ask *them* to explain → (4) Co‑create solutions.**
