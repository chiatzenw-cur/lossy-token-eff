## 2.  US Antitrust Landscape  

| Law / Agency | Core Feature | Key Guidance for Modern Tech |
|---|---|---|
| **Sherman Antitrust Act (1890)** | Prohibits “unreasonable restraints of trade” & “monopolization.” | Stresses “consumer‑welfare” test (price exclusion, foreclosure). |
| **Clayton Act (1914)** | Adds clear rules on mergers & specific practices (price‑discrimination, tying). | Provides §2 (merger review) and §3 (interlocking directorates). |
| **Federal Trade Commission Act (1914)** | Creates the **FTC** to investigate and investigate anticompetitive conduct. | Focus on “unfair methods of competition.” |
| **FTC & DOJ Joint Antitrust Division** | Investigates consumers at times; decisions are either **civil** or **criminal**. | Recent emphasis on *technology‑specific rules* (e.g., “data‑set” monopoly conditions). |
| **Supreme Court (2022–2023)** | Recent rulings broaden the shareholder‑concept and privacy‑data concerns. | **Qualifying the “market‑making” function** of tech platforms. |

## 6.  Key Take‑aways  

1. **Antitrust is the invisible “checking” mechanism** that keeps markets from turning into a “winner‑takes‑all” environment.  
2. **US antitrust law remains principle‑centric, anchored in consumer welfare**; China’s focus blends social welfare with strategic protection, especially around data & digital sovereignty.  
3. **China’s recent enforcement bursts (2019‑2024)** show it is willing to punish companies that use their data advantage to close competition (e.g., WeChat Pay).  
4. **Tech firms that cross borders must plan for *dual* compliance** – U.S. FTC/DOJ and China tariffs may clash even if underlying monopolistic practices are similar.  
5. **Global enforcement will gradually converge**: WTO/International trade moves toward “digital‑market‑economics” frameworks; both US and China are testing the boundaries of how monopoly‑like data can be regulated.
