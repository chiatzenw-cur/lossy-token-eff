**Topic**: The Opium Wars (1839–1842, 1856–1860) – China ↔ Britain  
**Grade Level**: 9–10  
**Duration**: 3 consecutive 45‑minute periods (three days)  

---

## Overall Learning Objectives  
By the end of the unit, students will be able to:

| Knowledge | Skills | Attitudes |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| • Explain the economic, political, and cultural factors that led to the Opium Wars. | • Use drama & mime to rehearse the perspectives of historical figures. | • Develop empathy for diverse cultural viewpoints. |
| • Identify the main events of the First and Second Opium Wars and the resulting treaties. | • Create and perform a short, scripted theatrical piece that conveys cause/effect of the wars. | • Understand the long‑term impact of colonialism on China. |
| • Analyze the role of the British, the Chinese, and the role of the international community. | • Critically assess primary source choices (e.g., newspapers, diplomatic cables) and integrate them into performance. | • Appreciate the importance of multiple perspectives in history. |

---

## Essential Questions (for all three days)

1. **Why did the Opium Wars happen?**  
2. **What were the experiences of the main actors (British merchants, Chinese officials, common people)?**  
3. **How did the outcomes of the wars alter China’s domestic and international trajectory?**  

---

## Safety & classroom protocols

* **No trash or unused props** on the stage to avoid tripping hazards.  
* **Respectful language** — no name‑calling.  
* **Movement safety** — students must keep a safe distance from the stage edges.  
* **Prop‑safety** — paper “opium” sticks are flammable; handle with care.

## Day‑by‑Day Breakdown

### **Day 1 – “The Arrival” (45 min)**  

| Time | Activity | Drama Technique | Teacher’s Role | Student Notes |
|------|----------|-----------------|----------------|---------------|
| **0‑5 min** | **Warm‑up**: “The Silent Walk” – each student walks around the room, adopting a posture that might represent a foreign merchant or Chinese merchant. | *Movement & Silence* | Sketch on board what students see. | I’m a trader anticipating contact. |
| **5‑10 min** | **Historical Context**: Mini‑lecture + timeline creation on a large whiteboard. | *Narrative* | Guidelines for the “arrival” scenario. | Need to note the economy of Canton. |
| **10‑15 min** | **Role Assignment**: Hand out index cards with names (e.g., “Lord Macartney”, “Sir William Jardine”, “Prince Daoguang” etc.). | *Word‑on‑Skin* | Clarify roles and responsibilities (Cornell note‑style). | I’m a British diplomat. |
| **15‑20 min** | **Research & Script‑Prep**: In pairs, groups research their character’s motives and write 3‑scene outlines (5‑6 lines each). | *Collaborative Writing* | Provide worksheets + links to primary sources. | Seeking info on opium’s value. |
| **20‑30 min** | **Rehearse “Silent Tableaux”**: Each group creates a 1‑minute silent tableau (body language + limited props) for the “Arrival of the Opium Traders”。 | *Mime* | Walk through for clarity; remind of low‑light movement. | Body gestures: pinky sign, sorrow. |
| **30‑35 min** | **Performance**: 3‑minute silent dramatic tableau per group (time 45 s each). | *Silent Theater* | Cue intro music; organise seating. | 1) Merchant arrival 2) Chinese reaction 3) Idea of commerce. |
| **35‑40 min** | **Reflect & Debrief**: “What Did We See?” – Students write one sentence at the back of their folder. | *Reflective Writing* | Encourage empathy; ask “What did you feel?” | Felt the tension in the air. |
| **40‑45 min** | **Preview of Day 2**: Teaser audio clip of a British diplomatic dispatch. | *Audio‑Tap* | Announce Homework: Prepare a “letter” draft from a merchant. | *Homework*: Write 2‑paragraph letter. |

---

### **Day 2 – “The Negotiations” (45 min)**  

| Time | Activity | Drama Technique | Teacher’s Role | Student Notes |
|------|----------|-----------------|----------------|---------------|
| **0‑5 min** | **Quick Ice‑Breaker**: “Say‑It‑Back” – one student says a word (e.g., “Opium”), the rest repeat it with similar intonation. | *Repetition & Tone* | Set the tempo. | “Across, across…” |
| **5‑10 min** | **Review & Hook**: Play 2‑minute audio clip of a British envoy. | *Audiovisual* | Summarize key take‑aways. | “Who delivered the ultimatum?” |
| **10‑15 min** | **Role Recap**: Students revisit their character’s letter, read aloud, and share overview. | *Role‑Mirror* | Encourage clarity & voice. | Addressing moral conflict. |
| **15‑25 min** | **Negotiation Script**: In pairs, students create a 3‑scene dialogue (British vs Chinese). Emphasis on **conflict** and **non‑verbal cues**. | *Written Dialogue* | Circulate, prompt with questions on stakes. | “If Britain pushes...” |
| **25‑35 min** | **Rehearse & Peer Feedback**: 2‑minute run‑through per group with classmates pointing out *body language* and *tone changes*. | *Peer Review* | Facilitate critique using rubrics. | “Use firmer posture after the ultimatum.” |
| **35‑40 min** | **Performance**: 3‑minute negotiation scenes. | *Theatrical Performance* | Record performances for optional video review. | “Set a bargaining table.” |
| **40‑45 min** | **Reflect & Homework**: Students write a brief note on how the negotiation affected their character’s fate. | *Reflective Writing* | Clarify expectations. | “It made me choose….” |

---

### **Day 3 – “The Aftermath” (45 min)**  

| Time | Activity | Drama Technique | Teacher’s Role | Student Notes |
|------|----------|-----------------|----------------|---------------|
| **0‑5 min** | **Mind‑Map Warm‑Up**: Students write a single word that sums up “Treaty of Nanking” on a sticky note (e.g., “Unequal”). | *Brainstorm* | Collect them to form a world map. | “Unequal.” |
| **5‑10 min** | **Revisiting the Treaty**: Quick Q&A, interactive timeline (SATOR). | *Timeline Ball‑Head* | Provide answer sheet for ACT. | “What concessions?” |
| **10‑15 min** | **Script for ‘Tea‑Party’**: In groups, arrange a 4‑scene short (Mr. Jardine’s tea‑party, Chinese aristocrats, people’s protest, British announcement). | *mini‑script* | Task for storyboard. | “Add 1 Mumon moment.” |
| **15‑25 min** | **Rehearsal – Silent Mime**: 2‑minute silent “Tea‑Party” scene to depict subtle power dynamics. | *Mime‑Only* | Highlight body language; no words. | “Nodding posture. ” |
| **25‑35 min** | **Performance**: 3‑minute “Tea‑Party” + 2 min “Protest” silent segment. | *Combined Mime + Dialogue* | Cue music and lights. | “Stage‑hand to create background.” |
| **35‑40 min** | **Assessment & Feedback**: Peer‑review forms & formal rubric evaluation. | *Formative Assessment* | Tally scores. | “Strong/conflict depiction.” |
| **40‑45 min** | **Wrap‑Up & Reflection**: Structured reflection: “What did I learn about the cost of colonialism? What would I remember across time?” | *Reflective Writing* | Collect and read a few aloud. | “Tragic for China.” |

| Criterion | Exemplary (4) | Proficient (3) | Developing (2) | Needs Improvement (1) |
|-----------|---------------|----------------|----------------|-----------------------|
| **Content Accuracy** | Presents comprehensive facts with accurate detail. | Clear facts, minor inaccuracies. | Factually correct but superficial. | Misrepresentations or major omissions. |
| **Drama Skills** | Masterful use of voice, gestures, and pacing; fully immersive. | Good use of drama, minor stumbles. | Evident attempt but limited comfort. | Minimal drama or audience disengaged. |
| **Teamwork & Collaboration** | All members contribute; supportive environment. | Generally works; few conflicts. | Conflicts affect performance; uneven participation. | Dominator or withdrawal. |
| **Creative Interpretation** | Original, thoughtful reinterpretation of source material. | Clear, original ideas. | Uses basic ideas. | Stuck in script template. |
| **Reflective Insight** | Deep reflection on perspectives, motivations, and lessons. | Thoughtful reflection. | Basic reflection. | No reflection. |

*Rubrics are displayed on the board and used individually on the last day.*
