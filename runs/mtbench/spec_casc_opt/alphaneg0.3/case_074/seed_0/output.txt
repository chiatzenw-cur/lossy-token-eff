### Balanced chemical equation

\[
\boxed{\mathrm{CaCO_3(s) + 2\,HCl(aq) \;\longrightarrow\; CaCl_2(aq) + CO_2(g) + H_2O(l)}}
\]

- **CaCO₃**: solid calcium carbonate (the “pale crystal” that hardens in bottles).  
- **2 HCl**: hydrochloric acid dissolved in water.  
- **CaCl₂(aq)**: aqueous calcium chloride, the salt that stays dissolved.  
- **CO₂(g)**: carbon dioxide gas that bubbles out.  
- **H₂O(l)**: liquid water produced as a by‑product.

### Type of reaction

This is a **acid‑base neutralization (double‑displacement) reaction**.  
- An acid (HCl) reacts with a base (CaCO₃) to produce a salt (CaCl₂) and water, plus an additional gas (CO₂) that comes from the carbonate ion.

### What observers notice

1. **Bubbling or fizzing** – the dissociation of CO₂ gas from the melt.  
2. **Temperature rise** – the reaction releases heat.  
3. **Color change** – the solution changes from cloudy/opaque (if any CaCO₃ remains) to clear once the solution is homogeneous.  
4. **Reduction in acidity** – if you measure the pH, it climbs from acidic (pH < 3) toward neutral (pH ≈ 7).  
5. **Removal of solid residue** – once the fizzing stops, the container shows no signs of solid CaCO₃; the solid has been entirely dissolved, leaving a colorless calcium‑chloride solution.

Other classical observations: in the presence of a CO₂‑indicator (e.g., limewater), you can detect the rising gray‑white cloud that dissolves upon the CO₂’s escape, confirming the gas‑evolution phase of the reaction.
