### 1. What Is Probability?

- **Probability** measures how likely an event is to happen.  
- It’s always a number between **0** (impossible) and **1** (certain).  
- In everyday life you’re already using it:  
  - “Will it rain today?” → 0.4 (40 %)  
  - “Will I have that exact seat on the bus?” → 0.05 (5 %)

---

### 2. Core Vocabulary

| Term | Meaning | Quick Math |
|------|---------|-----------|
| **Outcome** | One possible result of a random trial. | Rolling a die → 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 |
| **Sample Space (S)** | All possible outcomes. | For a die:  {1,2,3,4,5,6} |
| **Event (E)** | A subset of outcomes we care about. | Rolling a “4” → {4} |
| **Induced Probability** | The probability of an event: *P(E)* / *P(S)* | For a fair die: P(4) = 1/6 ≈ 0.1667 |
| **Complement** | “Not E” → All outcomes that aren’t E. | P(not 4) = 1 – P(4) = 5/6 |
| **Independent Events** | Events whose outcomes don’t influence each other. | When you flip a coin, the next flip is independent. |
| **Dependent Events** | Outcomes influence each other. | Drawing from a deck without replacement. |

---

### 3. How to Calculate Probability

1. **Count** the # of ways the event can happen.  
2. **Count** the total # of possible outcomes.  
3. **Divide**:  
   \[
   P(\text{Event}) = \frac{\text{Favorable outcomes}}{\text{All outcomes}}
   \]

**Example 1 – Rolling a die**  
- Event: “Roll a 4”  
- Favorable outcomes: 1 (just the number 4)  
- Total outcomes: 6  
  \[
  \frac{1}{6} \approx 0.1667
  \]

**Example 2 – Picking a card**  
- Event: “Draw a red card”  
- Favorable outcomes: 26 (13 hearts + 13 diamonds)  
- Total outcomes: 52  
  \[
  \frac{26}{52} = \frac{1}{2} = 0.5
  \]

---

### 4. Common Probability Rules

| Rule | What it says | Example |
|------|--------------|---------|
| **Additive Rule** | If two events are **mutually exclusive** (they can’t both happen), add their probabilities. | P(rolling a 4 or a 5) = P(4)+P(5)=1/6+1/6=1/3 |
| **Complement Rule** | P(not E) = 1 – P(E) | P(not 4) = 1 – 1/6 = 5/6 |
| **Multiplication Rule** | For independent events, multiply. | P(Heads on flip 1 **and** Heads on flip 2) = 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/4 |
| **Conditional Probability** | P(A | B) = P(A∩B) / P(B) | If you pick a card, given it's an Ace, probability of it being Red? 2/4 = 1/2 |
| **Bayes’ Theorem (quick version)** | Updates P(B) after seeing A. | If a test is 95 % accurate, find probability that a randomly tested person actually has the disease given a positive result. |

### 6. Step‑by‑Step Practice Problem

**Problem:**  
A bag contains 4 red marbles and 6 blue marbles. How many ways to pick 2 marbles that are *both* red, and what’s the probability?

**Solution:**  
1. **Total ways to pick 2 marbles** (order doesn’t matter):  
   \[
   \binom{10}{2} = \frac{10!}{2!8!} = 45
   \]
2. **Favorable ways (both red)**:  
   \[
   \binom{4}{2} = \frac{4!}{2!2!} = 6
   \]
3. **Probability**:  
   \[
   P(\text{both red}) = \frac{6}{45} = \frac{2}{15} \approx 0.133
   \]

Feel free to try a similar problem with the **blue** marbles or a mix of red and blue.

### 8. Quick Tips

| Tip | Why |
|-----|-----|
| **Draw a picture** | Even a quick diagram helps you see the sample space. |
| **Check independence** | Forgot to consider independence? Your multiplication may be wrong. |
| **Use the complement** | When a direct count of “at least one” is hard, count “none” and subtract from 1. |
| **Remember the “fair” assumption** | If using a 6‑sided die, each face has probability 1/6, but if it’s weighted, you need to adjust. |
