## 1. Preliminary Differential (Intense Abdominal Pain)

| **Condition** | **Key Clinical Notes** | **Urgent Red Flags** |
|---------------|------------------------|----------------------|
| **Acute Appendicitis** | RLQ pain, nausea, low‑grade fever, rebound tenderness. | High fever, worsening pain, vomiting > 48 h, peritonitis. |
| **Cholecystitis / Choledocholithiasis** | RUQ pain, often after fatty meals, fever, jaundice. | Dark urine, polydipsia, generalized guarding. |
| **Pancreatitis** | Epigastric pain radiating to back, often after alcohol or gallstones. | Hemorrhagic shock, acute respiratory distress. |
| **Gastroenteritis / Food Poisoning** | Cramping, diarrhea, vomiting, often following specific food. | Dehydration, electrolyte imbalance. |
| **Peptic Ulcer / Gastritis** | Burning epigastric pain. | Hematemesis, melena, severe bleeding. |
| **Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS)** | Crampy pain relieved by defecation, strict bowel pattern. | No red flags. |
| **Pelvic Inflammatory Disease** | Lower abdominal pain, cervical motion tenderness, pelvic pain. | Fever, tachycardia. |
| **Diverticulitis** | LLQ pain, often with fever, constipation. | Severe pain, perforation signs. |
| **Obstruction** | Crampy pain, vomiting, distension. | Severe distension, absent bowel sounds. |
| **Inflammatory Bowel Disease** | Chronic pain, diarrhea, blood. | Severe anemia, perforation. |
| **Abdominal Wall Pain or Muscular Strain** | Pain worsens with movement or palpation. | High work‑related activity. |

## 2. Immediate Red‑Flag Symptoms Checklist (Call emergency services if any present)

- 📗 Sudden, severe, “sharp” pain radiating to the back or shoulder blade (think pancreatitis).
- 📗 High fever (> 38.5 °C) or chills.
- 📗 Persistent vomiting or inability to keep fluids down.
- 📗 Blood in stool (melena), bright red blood, or coffee‑ground stool.
- 📗 Unexplained weight loss or anemia.
- 📗 Severe abdominal distension or lack of bowel sounds.
- 📗 Altered mental status or confusion.
- 📗 Signs of shock: tachycardia, hypotension, dizziness, sweating.

## 3. Initial Home‑Based Measures (until you see a clinician)

| **Action** | **Benefit** | **Caution** |
|------------|-------------|-------------|
| **Hydration** | Prevent dehydration. | Use clear fluids; avoid alcohol or sugary drinks. |
| **Diet** | *Clear liquid* (water, broth, tea) for 1‑2 h if vomiting. | Avoid heavy, fatty, or spicy foods. |
| **Heat** | Apply warm compress (30 min) to crampy pain. | Don’t use hot if there's an acute inflammatory process. |
| **OTC** | **Acetaminophen** (Tylenol) 500‑1000 mg q6 h PRN for mild pain. | Avoid NSAIDs if ulcer, kidney disease, bleeding risk. |
| **H2 Blockers / PPIs** | Antacids (e.g., famotidine) can relieve gastritis. | Not first‑line for acute severe pain. |
| **Herbal** | *Chamomile tea* for mild abdominal cramps; *peppermint oil* for IBS (after confirming no ulcers). | Verify no other meds interactions. |
| **Rest** | Reduce muscular strain. | Avoid intense activity if possible. |

## 4. When to Seek Professional Care

- **Any red‑flag symptoms** (see above).
- **Pain lasting > 24 h** without clear improvement.
- **Unexplained systemic changes** (fever, chills, cold sweats).
- **Past GI conditions** (e.g., known ulcers, abdominal surgery).
- **Medications** that predispose to ulcer or bleeding.

## 6. Persistent Abdominal Pain – Suggested Diagnostic Work‑Up

| **Test** | **When to Order** | **What It Shows** |
|----------|-------------------|--------------------|
| **Complete Blood Count (CBC)** | Fever, fatigue, anemia | Infection, inflammation, blood loss |
| **Basic Metabolic Panel (BMP)** | Dehydration, electrolyte imbalance | Kidney function, fluid status |
| **Liver Function Tests (LFTs)** | RUQ pain, jaundice | Hepatobiliary disease |
| **Lipase / Amylase** | Epigastric pain, pancreatitis | Pancreatic inflammation |
| **Urinalysis** | Concerning renal colic or infection | Hematuria, infection |
| **Plain Abdominal X‑ray** | Suspected obstruction | Air‑fluid levels, ileus |
| **Abdominal CT scan** | Persistent, severe pain; suspicion of perforation | Detailed organ imaging |
| **Upper‑GI endoscopy** | Epigastric pain, positive bleeding | Peptic ulcer, gastritis |
| **Defecography / Colonoscopy** | Chronic constipation; alarm signs | Structural or mucosal abnormalities |

## 8. Practical Tips for the Patient

1. **Keep a Pain Diary**  
   - Note time of onset, specific location, intensity (0–10 scale), food/services, medication, and any relieving factors.  
   - Helps clinicians pinpoint diagnosis quickly.

2. **Limit Irritants**  
   - Reduce caffeine, alcohol, spicy foods, and high‑fat meals until pain resolves.

3. **Hydration & Electrolytes**  
   - Sip water, oral electrolyte solutions, or coconut water.  
   - Avoid sugary drinks if diuresis.

4. **Gentle Movement**  
   - Light walking can help relieve gas and constipation.  
   - Avoid heavy lifting or twisting if painful.

5. **When to Seek Immediate Care**  
   - Red‑flag symptoms (see bullets above).  
   - Pain unrelieved by home measures after 12‑24 h.  
   - New or worsening symptoms such as fever, vomiting, or changes in urine color.
