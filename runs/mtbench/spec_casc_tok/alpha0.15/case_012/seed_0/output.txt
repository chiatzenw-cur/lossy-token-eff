## Detailed Lesson Script

### Day 1 – 45 min: “Lights, No Sound: The Silent Prelude”

#### 1. Hook (5 min)  
- **Teacher** activates curiosity: “What if you could watch history unfold without words?”  
- Play a 1‑min montage of archival photos (e.g., cholera‑infested Guangzhou, opium dens, British cannon). Soundtrack: low‑key Chinese instrumental blending with distant gunfire.

#### 2. Introduce Drama Technique (5 min)  
- **Teacher** demonstrates a 2‑min silent vignette: a “British merchant” delivering opium to a trembling Chinese farmer.  
- Highlight gestures: hand‑to‑hand, eye‑contact, posture.  
- Students note: “What story is being told? What feels strong/weak?”

#### 3. Mini‑Workshop: “Move the Moment” (10 min)  
- **Pair‑Up**: Students choose a historical figure (e.g., Sir George Canning, Sir Lin Zexu).  
- **Task**: Create a 30‑second mime in which the figure experiences a key moment (e.g., the burning of opium).  
- **Share**: Each pair performs for the class.  

#### 4. Role‑Research & Character Sheet (12 min)  
- **Group Work**: 6‑8 students per group, each assigned a perspective (English diplomat, Chinese official, merchant, sailor, etc.).  
- **Resources**: Handouts of primary sources (e.g., newspaper clippings, treaty excerpts).  
- **Template**: Character header (Name, Age, Role), Motivations, Conflict, “life‑sentence” (one sentence summarizing their view).  

#### 5. Social‑Meta‑Reflection (5 min)  
- **Whole‑Class Discussion**: “What did you feel when you acted your role? What did you learn about non‑verbal communication?”  
- Teacher writes key insights on board.  

#### 6. Exit Ticket (3 min)  
- Students write: “Describe one argument your character would make about the Opium Trade.”  

---

### Day 2 – 45 min: “Script & Stage”

#### 1. Quick Review (3 min)  
- Review previous day’s characters and key events.  

#### 2. Warm‑Up: “Hot‑Seat Podcast” (5 min)  
- Students take turns sitting in a “hot‑seat” while the teacher asks rapid-fire questions about their character’s motivations.  
- Purpose: Think on feet, solidify character voice.  

#### 3. Evidence‑Driven Scriptwriting (20 min)  
- **Teacher**: Provide a “Script Blueprint” (Scene, Setting, Conflict, Evidence Tags, Dialogue).  
- **Groups**: Using evidence from texts, write a 3‑min dialogue (2‑3 characters).  
- **Notes**: Each line must be backed by a source (cite on back of script).  

#### 4. Rehearsal & Coaching (10 min)  
- **Partner Practice**: Students read each other's script, providing margin notes.  
- **Teacher**: Circulate, listening for clarity, pacing, and emotional truth.  

#### 5. Problem‑Solving: “The Cache of Props” (5 min)  
- **Mini‑challenge**: Given limited props (e.g., a book, a flag, a small model), redesign a scene to use them effectively.  
- **Goal**: Encourage creativity and resourcefulness.  

#### 6. Homework (Optional)  
- Students sketch 1‑2 “puppet” silhouettes of their character for a later “silent” scene.  

---

### Day 3 – 45 min: “Showtime & Reflection”

#### 1. Warm‑Up: “Signal & Silence” (5 min)  
- Quick marching, leap, pivot, freeze sequence to bring energy.  

#### 2. Performance (15 min)  
- **Staged**: Each group performs their 3‑min scene (could be two groups per room for 45 min).  
- **Recording**: Teacher records all performances for later critique.  
- **Audience**: Peer feedback cards (focus on historical accuracy, emotional conveyance, clarity of motive).  

#### 3. Debrief (10 min)  
- **Whole‑Class**: After each performance, teacher asks: “What did the audience misunderstand? What did you learn about the source material?”  
- **Discussion**: “How did the drama help you ‘feel’ the conflict?”  

#### 4. Reflection Essay (5 min)  
- **Prompt**: “Imagine you are a 19th‑century Chinese official. Write a 200‑word diary entry reflecting on the Opium War’s impact on your city.”  
- **Goal**: Synthesize content and perspective.  

#### 5. Assessment Rubric (5 min)  
- Show rubric (Performance, Script, Collaboration, Reflection).  
- Clarify expectations for the grading.  

#### 6. Closing (2 min)  
- Teacher thanks students for bravery in embodying historical personas.  
- Tease next unit: “The Legacy of the Opium Wars: Modern China & Global Trade.”

## Cultural Sensitivity & Ethical Considerations

- **Contextualize Colonialism**: Discuss the power imbalance and the moral implications of forced trade.  
- **Avoid Stereotypes**: Encourage research from multiple viewpoints; use primary sources to counter caricatures.  
- **Respect for Chinese Heritage**: Highlight resilience and agency of Chinese actors (e.g., Lin Zexu).  
- **Debrief Openly**: Create a safe space for students to express discomfort or questions about historical injustices.
