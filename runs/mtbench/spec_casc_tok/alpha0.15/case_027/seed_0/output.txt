### 1. Light‑dependent reactions

* **What happens:** Light energy excites electrons in chlorophyll, driving electron transport through the photosystems (PSII and PSI) and the cytochrome b₆f complex. This generates a proton gradient that powers ATP synthase (photophosphorylation) and reduces NADP⁺ to NADPH. Water is split (photolysis) to replace the lost electrons, releasing O₂ as a by‑product.
* **Why it matters:** It produces the energy carriers (ATP, NADPH) and O₂ that are essential for the next stage.

### 2. Calvin cycle (dark reactions)

* **What happens:** CO₂ is fixed into a carbohydrate backbone by the enzyme Rubisco, starting with the formation of 3‑phosphoglycerate (3‑PGA). Through a series of reduction, regeneration, and carboxylation steps, ATP and NADPH are consumed to produce G3P. One G3P exits the cycle to form glucose and other sugars; the rest is used to regenerate ribulose‑1,5‑bisphosphate (RuBP) so the cycle can continue.
* **Why it matters:** It converts inorganic CO₂ into organic molecules that become the primary building blocks of plant biomass and the food source for other organisms.
