### How to Apply These Principles

1. **Read for the Claim**  
   - Look for a thesis sentence or a clear statement of position.  
   - If it’s buried or ambiguous, the argument lacks foundation.

2. **Check the Evidence**  
   - Identify data, examples, or citations.  
   - Verify source credibility (peer‑reviewed, primary, statistical soundness).  

3. **Trace the Reasoning**  
   - Map out the logical flow: Claim → Evidence → Reasoning → Conclusion.  
   - Spot any jumps in logic or use of non‑sequitur reasoning.

4. **Uncover Assumptions**  
   - Ask “what must be true for this argument to hold?”  
   - Evaluate whether the argument acknowledges those assumptions and addresses them.

5. **Consider Counter‑Arguments**  
   - Look for a section where the author acknowledges opposing views.  
   - If absent, consider whether the argument is too one‑sided.

6. **Assess Scope & Context**  
   - Determine whether the argument is universal or limited to a specific context.  
   - Examine whether the conclusion is realistic given the evidence and reasoning.

---

### Quick Evaluation Checklist

- **Claim**: Clear, specific, and testable?  
- **Evidence**: Reliable, relevant, enough?  
- **Reasoning**: Valid, free of fallacies?  
- **Assumptions**: Explicit, justified?  
- **Counter‑arguments**: Acknowledged, refuted?  
- **Scope**: Realistic, context-aware?  

Using these five principles consistently ensures that your analytical evaluations are thorough, defensible, and persuasive.
