### 1. Key Components

| Component | Function | Typical Specs / Variants |
|-----------|----------|--------------------------|
| **Solar Thermal Collector** | Harvests solar energy; hot water created in the collector fluid. | • Flat‑plate collectors (PA‑90) – 4–6 mm glass, aluminum backing<br>• Evacuated‑tube collectors (e.g., Atkinson Domestos) – higher temp, better for colder climates |
| **Collector Loop (Piping & Pump)** | Transports heat‑transfer fluid (HTF) to/from the collector. | • PEX‑g or CPVC (clear, corrosion‑resistant)<br>• Single‑stage un‑vented pump (120 Hz, 1–2 HP) |
| **Heat‑Exchanger (Loop‑to‑Water)** | Transfers heat from HTF to potable water. | • Plate‑type or coil‑type exchanger (aluminum or copper) – ~4–6 in. |
| **Storage Tank** | Holds hot water for instantaneous use. | • 200–300 gal (or 300–400 gal for large families)<br>• 3‑layer insulation (Densiron, mineral wool) |
| **Expansion Tank & Pressure Relief** | Manages pressure changes & protects against over‑pressure. | • 2 psi expansion tank; 150 psi relief valve |
| **Control & Dispatch System** | Monitors temperature, flow, and sunlight; schedules pump operation. | • Thermostat & float valve controller<br>• Solenoid valve & pump timer – 6‑hour delay for “first‑flush” |
| **Backup / Supplementary Unit** | Provides hot water when solar isn’t enough. | • Electric (heat‑pump or resistance) or gas boiler (if available) |
| **Piping & Insulation** | Connects all components and minimizes heat loss. | • ¾‑in. copper OR PEX‑g<br>• Wrap with ρ = 6–8 ft²·°F·h/BTU |
| **Monitoring & Data Logger** | Enables performance tracking, diagnostics, and billing. | • Wireless module (Wi‑Fi, Zigbee) + mobile app |
| **Safety & Compliance** | Meets local codes for water heaters, fire safety, and electrical work. | • Code‑approved fuses, GFCI, and venting (if required) |

### 2. Design Considerations

| Category | What to Think About | Why It Matters |
|----------|---------------------|----------------|
| **Solar Resource** | Average insolation, seasonal swing, cloud cover | Determines collector size & backup need |
| **Roof Orientation & Tilt** | Slope, azimuth, shading | Maximizes direct‑sun exposure |
| **Load Profile** | Peak hot‑water demand (morning, evening, weekend) | Drives tank volume & collector array sizing |
| **Space & Aesthetics** | Roof space, wall space, roof load | Influences collector type (flat‑plate vs. evacuated) |
| **Local Codes & Incentives** | Building codes, safety codes, rebates | Avoid retrofits & benefit from subsidies |
| **Climate** | Cold snaps, humidity, wind | Impacts minimum operating temp, insulation levels |
| **Water Quality** | Hardness, corrosion | Affects exchanger life & maintenance |
| **System Modularity** | Expandability (add more collectors or tank) | Future‑proof for increased demand |
| **Maintenance** | Ease of cleaning, access to components | Keeps efficiency high |
| **Safety** | Low‑temperature pumps, pressure relief, electrical connections | Prevents accidents & equipment damage |
| **Economics** | CAPEX, OPEX, payback period | Drives component selection and financing |

### 4. Quick Reference Installation Checklist

| Item | Checklist |
|------|-----------|
| **Roof prep** | Ensure no ice dam, adequate load rating, mounting hole prep |
| **Collector mounting** | Level, secure, sealants, flashing |
| **Piping** | Proper slope, avoid kinks, check for leaks |
| **Heat‑exchanger** | Correct orientation, secure fixation |
| **Pump & controller** | Verify wiring, GFCI, proper sequencing |
| **Tank** | Proper venting, expansion tank, point‑of‑use valve |
| **Insulation** | Seal all joints, thermal breaks |
| **Safety** | Check relief valve, pressure rating, clearances |
| **Documentation** | Store all drawings, warranties, commissioning log |

### 5. Performance & Maintenance Tips

| Issue | Tip |
|-------|-----|
| **Low harvest day** | Deploy backup (heat‑pump) automatically when solar temp < 70 °F |
| **Scaling** | Use water softener or float‑treatment; inspect exchanger annually |
| **Leak** | Inspect HTF loop every 6 months; seal PEX‑g glands if needed |
| **Pump life** | Replace after 4–5 years; use cartridge pumps for shorter burst |
| **Energy audit** | Re‑evaluate after 1 year to capture real usage patterns |
