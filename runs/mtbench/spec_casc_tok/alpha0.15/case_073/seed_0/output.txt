The central dogma is a concise statement that describes the directional flow of genetic information within a living cell:

> **DNA → RNA → Protein**

In other words, the sequence of nucleotides in DNA is transcribed into a messenger RNA (mRNA) sequence, and that mRNA is translated into a polypeptide chain (protein). The dogma underlines the principle that genetic information travels in one direction—from the storage form in DNA to the functional form in proteins.

---

### Processes Involved

| Step | Process | Key Players | Notes |
|------|---------|-------------|-------|
| **1** | **Transcription** | DNA → RNA polymerase → mRNA | DNA’s double‑helix is read by RNA polymerase, which synthesizes a complementary RNA strand. The process is regulated by promoters, enhancers, and transcription factors. |
| **2** | **RNA Processing** *(eukaryotes)* | Introns → splicing; 5′ cap; poly‑A tail | The primary transcript (pre‑mRNA) is edited to produce mature, export‑ready mRNA. |
| **3** | **Translation** | mRNA → ribosome → tRNA → amino acids | The ribosome reads mRNA codons, matching them with tRNA anticodons, and links amino acids into a polypeptide chain. |
| **4** | **Post‑translational Modifications** | Enzymes, chaperones | Proteins may be cut, folded, phosphorylated, etc., to become functional. |

**Exceptions & Extensions**

- **Reverse Transcription** – RNA → DNA (e.g., retroviruses) shows that the flow can be reversed, but this is an exception, not the rule.
- **Prions** – protein‑only infectious agents that propagate without nucleic acids.
- **Non‑coding RNAs** – participate in regulation but are not translated into proteins.  
- **RNA‑based catalysis** – ribozymes hint at early life stages where RNA performed both informational and catalytic roles.

---

### Who Named It?

The phrase **“central dogma”** was coined by **Francis Crick** in 1958. In his article *“On Protein Synthesis”* (Nature, 1958), Crick formalized the concept, noting that
> “it is a principle that the information which is stored in the DNA travels in one direction and is not returned to the DNA.”

Crick’s formulation clarified the unidirectional flow of genetic information and remains a foundational principle taught in genetics, molecular biology, and biochemistry courses worldwide.

**Key Takeaway**

- **Central Dogma**: DNA → RNA → Protein  
- **Core processes**: Transcription, RNA processing, translation, post‑translational modifications.  
- **Originator**: Francis Crick (1958).
