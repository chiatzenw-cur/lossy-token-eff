> The *central dogma* describes the flow of genetic information within a biological system:  
> **DNA → RNA → Protein**  
> It summarizes how the hereditary information encoded in DNA is used to produce functional proteins that carry out the cell’s work.

---

### 1. The Core Processes

| Step | What Happens | Key Molecules | Main Enzymes / Complexes |
|------|--------------|---------------|--------------------------|
| **DNA → RNA** | **Transcription** – the genetic code in DNA is copied into messenger RNA (mRNA). | *DNA* (template), *mRNA* (product) | RNA polymerase (RNAp), transcription factors, σ‑factor (in bacteria) |
| **RNA → Protein** | **Translation** – the mRNA sequence is decoded to build a polypeptide chain. | *mRNA*, *tRNA*, *ribosome* | Ribosomal subunits (30S/50S in bacteria, 40S/60S in eukaryotes), elongation factors, aminoacyl‑tRNA synthetases |
| **DNA → DNA** | **Replication** – DNA makes a copy of itself before cell division. | *DNA* (template), *new DNA* (product) | DNA polymerase (α, δ, ε, etc.), helicase, primase, ligase |
| **RNA → DNA** | **Reverse transcription** – RNA can be reverse‑transcribed into DNA (retroviruses, some eukaryotes). | *RNA* (template), *DNA* (product) | Reverse transcriptase |
| **RNA → RNA** | **RNA processing** – primary transcripts (pre‑mRNA) are modified to become mature mRNA. | *pre‑mRNA* → *mRNA* | Splicing factors, capping enzymes, polyadenylation enzymes |
| **DNA → Protein** | **Transcriptional regulation** – signals that influence transcription rates. | *DNA*, *transcription factors* | Various regulatory proteins, enhancers, silencers |

> **Note**: The central dogma is a *simplified* picture. Many exceptions and additional layers (e.g., non‑coding RNAs, RNA editing, post‑translational modifications) exist, but the core statement remains the same.

---

### 2. Who Named It?

The phrase **“central dogma”** was coined by **Francis Crick** in 1958, in a *Nature* paper titled *“On the Origin of the Genetic Code”* (actually 1958? it was 1958 in the paper). Crick was a key figure in deciphering the genetic code and, along with James Watson, contributed to the discovery of the double helix structure of DNA.

Crick’s 1958 paper introduced the idea that information flows from *genotype* to *phenotype* in a single direction (DNA → RNA → protein). He later expanded the concept in his 1970 book *“The Central Dogma of Molecular Biology”*.

### 4. Key Take‑away

The **central dogma** is a conceptual framework that describes how genetic information guides the synthesis of proteins. It is built on the fundamental processes of **transcription, translation, DNA replication, and reverse transcription**. **Francis Crick** named this principle in 1958, and it has since become a cornerstone of molecular biology.
