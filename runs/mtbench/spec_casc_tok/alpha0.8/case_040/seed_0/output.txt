Since \(n\) divided by 10 leaves remainder 4, we can write  

\[
n = 10k + 4 \qquad (k \text{ is an integer}).
\]

Now consider twice the number:

\[
2n = 2(10k + 4) = 20k + 8.
\]

We need the remainder when \(2n\) is divided by 4.  
Both terms of \(2n\) are divisible by 4:

- \(20k = 4 \times 5k\)  (exactly divisible by 4),
- \(8 = 4 \times 2\) (exactly divisible by 4).

Thus

\[
2n \equiv 0 \pmod{4}.
\]

So the remainder is **0**.
