## 1. Set the Stage for Safe, Constructive Dialogue

| Step | What to Do | Why It Matters |
|------|------------|----------------|
| **Choose a neutral time** | Pick a moment when both of you are calm and not rushed (e.g., after dinner, before bed). | Reduces defensiveness. |
| **Create a “safe space”** | Agree on a brief rule: no interrupting, no name‑calling, no digging into past grievances. | Both partners feel heard and respected. |
| **Keep the focus on the issue at hand** | Avoid bringing in unrelated past events. | Keeps communication focused and productive. |

---

## 2. Get “I” Statements to Own Your Experience

- **Instead of “You always…,” say “I feel… when….”**  
  Example: “I feel anxious when plans change last minute because I value predictability.”  

### Why it works

- **Reduces blame** – language like “you always” can trigger defensiveness.  
- **Shifts focus to your feelings** – invites empathy from your partner.

---

## 3. Practice Active Listening

| Technique | How to Use It | Effect |
|-----------|---------------|--------|
| **Mirroring** | Repeat back what you heard in your own words. | Shows you’re truly hearing them. |
| **Paraphrasing** | “So you’re saying you felt…?” | Validates their perspective. |
| **Ask clarifying questions** | “What did you mean by that?” | Removes misinterpretations. |

### Quick Active‑Listening Drill
1. **Partner A**: 2‑minute talk about what’s bothering them.  
2. **Partner B**: 1‑minute active listening (mirroring, paraphrasing).  
3. **Swap**.

---

## 4. Identify Underlying Needs & Interests

- **Surface the stated problem** (e.g., “I’m upset you didn’t pick up the kids”).  
- **Probe for deeper needs** (e.g., “I need to feel supported when I’m running late.”).  

### Why it matters
- **Needs, not positions, are the root of conflict**.  
- When both sides speak of their needs, you can find common ground.

---

## 5. Brainstorm Solutions Together

- **Create a “win‑win” list**: each partner writes down possible solutions.  
- **Select one or two that address both sets of needs**.  
- **Agree on a trial period** (e.g., “Let’s try this for one week and revisit.”).

---

## 6. Commit to a Check‑In Routine

- **Schedule a brief, regular check‑in** (weekly, bi‑weekly).  
- **Use a “gratitude + frustration” format**:  
  1. Share one thing you appreciated.  
  2. Share one small friction you’d like to improve.  

This keeps the relationship dynamic and prevents issues from snowballing.

---

## 7. When to Seek Professional Help

- **Persistent patterns of hurt or abuse** (physical, emotional, or psychological).  
- **Repeatedly failing to resolve issues** despite trying.  
- **Significant life changes** (moving, career change, health issues).  

A therapist or couples coach can keep the conversation balanced and offer tools beyond what you can manage yourself.

1. **What was the core emotion for each of you?**  
2. **Did you feel heard or dismissed?**  
3. **What was the action you both could take next?**  
4. **When will you revisit the conversation?**
