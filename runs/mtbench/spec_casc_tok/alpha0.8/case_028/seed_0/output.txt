### 3. Five‑Step Workflow for Designing the System  

> **Goal** – Deliver a fully integrated, cost‑effective, and compliant solar‑water‑heating system that meets occupant demands and regulatory requirements.

#### Step 1 – **Feasibility & Preliminary Analysis**  
1. **Collect Solar Data** – Obtain DNI, DHI, temperature data from local meteorological stations or LiDAR.  
2. **Assess Roof/Building Space** – Measure usable area, shade analysis, structural load capacity.  
3. **Demand Snapshot** – Interview occupants, review monthly hot‑water bills, calculate average daily usage (gal/day).  
4. **Budget & Incentive Review** – Determine project budget, available tax credits, rebates, and payback targets.  
5. **Risk Assessment** – Preliminary check on water‑quality (hardness), local zonal regulations, and fire safety.

#### Step 2 – **Load & Demand Analysis**  
1. **Peak Demand Calculation** – Identify peak hourly hot‑water demand (often 3–5 % of total daily usage).  
2. **Temperature Risk Assessment** – Determine required storage temperature margin (≈ 10–15 °C above ambient).  
3. **Backup Capacity Estimation** – Roughly estimate how many hours of hot water will be required during low‑sun periods.  
4. **Refine Design Parameters** – Use calculations to estimate present‑day thermal load, peak‑flow water temperature, and peak‐hour energy demand.

#### Step 3 – **Sizing & Component Selection**  
1. **Collector Area** –  
   - \( A = \frac{Q_{sys}}{η×\overline{H} } \)  
   - \(Q_{sys}\) = daily hot‑water heat requirement, \(η\) = expected collector efficiency (≈ 0.50–0.65), \(\overline{H}\) = average daily solar irradiance.  
2. **Storage Tank Volume** –  
   - \( V = \frac{Q_{req}}{ΔT×ρ×c_p} \)  
   - Ensure 2–3 % reserve; include expansion tank.  
3. **Heat Exchanger** –  
   - Select a plate‑type exchanger with Q‑capacity ≥ 1.2× peak heat demand.  
4. **Pump Sizing** –  
   - Flow rate = Q‑rate / ΔT; use 3‑stage pump to maintain efficiency.  
5. **Backup Heating** –  
   - Size backup based on worst‑case scenario (e.g., 48 h of no sun).  
6. **Material & Finish** –  
   - Choose corrosion‑resistant materials (COP‑285 for collectors, stainless steel for heat exchangers).  
7. **Compliance Check** –  
   - Verify component compliance with local codes (e.g., IPC, NFPA 54).

#### Step 4 – **Detailed Design & Integration**  
1. **Piping Layout** – Draft a 2‑D schematic: collector → pump → heat exchanger → storage tank → potable line.  
2. **Control Logic** –  
   - Auto‑start pump when collector temp > 30 °C and tank temp < 45 °C.  
   - Shut‑off when tank > 55 °C or collector temp < 17 °C.  
   - Trigger backup when pump cycle < 15 min over 24 h.  
3. **Electrical Design** –  
   - 3‑phase, 120 V or 240 V wiring; provide MCB, GFCI, and fuse rating.  
4. **Safety Devices** –  
   - Install insulated pressure relief valve, temperature‑over‑pressure safety valve, and a thermal cut‑off.  
5. **Documentation** –  
   - Create BOM, draw detailed schematics, and prepare a maintenance plan.  
6. **Coordination** –  
   - Engage with roofing, plumbing, and electrical contractors for integration.

#### Step 5 – **Verification, Commissioning & Handover**  
1. **Simulation** – Run a transient simulation (e.g., TRNSYS, MATLAB) to confirm performance against design assumptions.  
2. **Site Inspection** – Verify collector mounting, tilt, and shading; confirm tank installation and insulation.  
3. **Performance Testing** –  
   - Measure collector temperature, water temperature, flow rate, and energy output.  
   - Verify start/stop logic, backup activation, and safety valve release.  
4. **Commissioning Report** – Document test results, any deviations, and corrective actions.  
5. **Owner Training** – Provide a simple user manual, emergency contacts, and maintenance schedule.  
6. **Final Handover** – Obtain occupancy permit, update building records, and submit to incentive program.
