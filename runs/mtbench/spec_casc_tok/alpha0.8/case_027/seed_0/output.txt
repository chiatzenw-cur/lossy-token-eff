### Light‑dependent reactions (Photophosphorylation)

1. **Photon absorption** – Light excites chlorophyll in photosystem II (PSII), which ejects an electron.  
2. **Water splitting** – PSII splits water, releasing O₂ and providing electrons.  
3. **Electron transport chain** – Electrons travel through plastoquinone, cytochrome b₆f, plastocyanin, and finally to photosystem I (PSI).  
4. **NADP⁺ reduction** – PSI uses the electron energy to reduce NADP⁺ to NADPH.  
5. **ATP synthesis** – The electron flow pumps protons into the thylakoid lumen, creating a proton motive force that drives ATP synthase to make ATP.

### Calvin cycle (Light‑independent reactions)

1. **Carbon fixation** – Ribulose‑1,5‑bisphosphate (RuBP) reacts with CO₂ via Rubisco to form two 3‑phosphoglycerate (3‑PGA) molecules.  
2. **Reduction** – 3‑PGA is phosphorylated by ATP and reduced by NADPH to produce glyceraldehyde‑3‑phosphate (G3P).  
3. **Regeneration** – Some G3P molecules are used to regenerate RuBP using ATP, allowing the cycle to continue.  
4. **Product formation** – The surplus G3P exits the cycle to be used for glucose, starch, cellulose, and other organic molecules.

> **Bottom line:**  
> • Light reactions (thylakoid membranes) convert light + water into ATP + NADPH + O₂.  
> • Calvin cycle (stroma) uses CO₂ + ATP + NADPH to produce G3P (and ultimately sugars), while regenerating RuBP and the cofactors.
