### How to Use Them Together

1. **Locate the Claim**: Start by reading the paragraph or series of paragraphs that the author considers the argument’s “core.”  
2. **Trace the Evidence**: For every point that supports the claim, ask whether its evidence is strong, relevant, and verifiable.  
3. **Check the Flow**: Reread the argument as a logical chain: Claim → Evidence → Reasoning → Conclusion.  
4. **Spot the Counter‑Side**: Even a brief acknowledgement of a counter‑argument should be followed by a rebuttal or alternative evidence.  
5. **Weigh the Context**: See if the author’s choice of evidence, style, and rhetorical tools match the audience and the intended purpose.  

By systematically applying these five principles, you’ll be able to spot *both* the strengths and the weak spots in any argument, and you’ll have a clear, structured approach for critiquing or improving it.
