## 1. Light‑dependent reactions

**Location**
- **Thylakoid membranes** (the ladder‑like stacks called grana) embedded in the chloroplast.

**Key events**

| Step | What happens | Output details |
|------|--------------|----------------|
| **Photon absorption** | Photosystem II (PSII) absorbs light and excites electrons. | Excited electrons |
| **Water splitting (photolysis)** | PSII oxidizes water → O₂ + H⁺ + 2 e⁻. | O₂ released into the atmosphere |
| **Electron transport chain** | Electrons move through PSII → plastoquinone → cytochrome b₆f → plastocyanin → PSI, driving proton pumping (creating a proton gradient). | Proton gradient = ΔpH |
| **ATP synthesis** | Proton flow through ATP synthase (CF₀/CF₁) converts 3 ADP + 3 Pi to ATP. | 3 ATP per 2 e⁻ pair. |
| **NADPH production** | PSI re-excites electrons → NADP⁺ + 2 H⁺ → NADPH. | NADPH + H⁺ (reduced). |

**Primary inputs & outputs** (simplified):

- **Inputs:** Light (H₂O, ADP + Pi, NADP⁺)
- **Outputs:** O₂, ATP, NADPH (plus protons and electrons that feed the next stage).

## 2. Calvin cycle (light‑independent reactions)

**Location**
- **Stroma** – the fluid interior of the chloroplast surrounding the thylakoid membranes.

**Main steps**

| Step | What happens | Key intermediates |
|------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Carbon fixation** | CO₂ + RuBP → 2 3‑phosphoglycerate (3‑PGA) (via Rubisco). | 2 3‑PGA |  
| **Reduction phase** | 3‑PGA + ATP + NADPH → 1‑carboxy‑2‑phosphoglycerate → G3P (as glyceraldehyde‑3‑phosphate). | G3P |
| **Regeneration of RuBP** | 5 G3P + 3 ATP → 3 RuBP + 3 ADP + Pi + 6 ATP… | RuBP again |
| **Synthesis** | G3P can be used to build sucrose, starch, cellulose, or export as glucose. | Glucose/other sugars |

**Primary inputs & outputs**:

- **Inputs:** CO₂ (from atmospheric air), ATP (from light reactions), NADPH (from light reactions).  
- **Outputs:**  
  - **G3P**: a 3‑carbon sugar that is the building block for glucose, starch, cellulose, etc.  
  - **Regenerated ATP & NADPH** so the cycle can continue.  
  - **(Optional) Some of the G3P is used to produce oxygen‑transporting sugars (e.g., sucrose).**

**Overall net reaction of photosynthesis (simplified)**

\[
6\,\text{CO}_2 + 6\,\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{light}}\; C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6\,\text{O}_2
\]

- Light reactions capture light energy and form ATP & NADPH.  
- The Calvin cycle uses those high‑energy carriers to fix CO₂ into sugars and eventually release oxygen.
