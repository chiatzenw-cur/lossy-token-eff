**Purpose** – Deliver domestic hot water (DHW) with 90 %+ solar contribution while meeting the building’s peak load and code requirements.  

Below is a concise, yet comprehensive, overview of the key components, design–considerations, and a five‑step workflow that will guide you from concept to commissioning.

---

### 1. Key Components

| Component | Role | Typical Parameters | Typical Placement |
|-----------|------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **Solar Collector** | Capture solar energy | 60–80 % efficiency, flat‑plate or evacuated tube | Roof or south‑facing accessible slope |
| **Heat Exchanger (Transmitter)** | Transfer heat from collector to storage | 300–600 W m⁻² hot water, 200–300 °C hot water inlet | Inside the collector or just below the collector |
| **Storage Tank (Sky‑Heat / Cold‑Walled)** | Store thermal energy | 80–400 L, two‑storage (hot / cold) tanks or a single glass‑insulated tank | Ground floor, e.g., in a utility closet |
| **Distribution Loop (Piping & Valves)** | Move water to/from the backup | Stainless steel 3–4 mm, insulated | Near the tank and HVAC/plug‑in outlets |
| **Active Pump** | Circulate water for active systems | 100–300 L/min, 200–400 W | Above the collector or fast‑head, fed by low‑current pump |
| **Electric Backup (PV‑in‑grid or Battery)** | Warm water during low‑solar periods | 400–800 W, 25 kWh batteries | Back near the collector or stored in the system |
| **Control & Monitoring System** | Regulate operation & give user data | 4‑wire PLC, SCADA/IoT, mobile app | Interface panel near the tank or building’s HVAC duct |
| **Mechanical Isolation & Safety** | Prevent pump cavitation, ensure safe operation | Pressure relief valve, temperature probe, over‑heat shut‑off | Valve‑manifold near the tank |
| **Ventilation & Sealant** | Keep combustion and seal leakage | Pressure‑rated fittings | Near the storage tank |
| **Winter‑ization & Thermal Diaphragm** | Reduce heat loss during colder months | Mechanical separators, fan or heat‑pathway | Inside the tank or piping |

*Note:* For a residential building, **active** systems (with pumps) usually give the largest solar dis­tribution; **passive** or **gravity‑fed** systems still work if the roof is near the water column and solar resources are abundant.

### 2. Design‑Considerations

| Category | What to Check | Typical Rationale |
|----------|---------------|-------------------|
| **Solar Resource** | Avg. solar irradiance, seasonal variations | Determines collector size & required backup |
| **Load Profile** | Daily peak DHW, peak space‑heating, “hot‑water surge” | Drives tank sizing + high‑temperature collector |
| **Roof Orientation & Tilt** | 30–35° pitch for Southern Hemisphere, ~55–60° for Northern | Maximizes energy capture |
| **Shading & Debris** | Nearby trees, chimneys, fall debris | Direct loss of collector efficiency |
| **Permitting & Code** | Local building codes, electrical codes (e.g., NEC, CBC) | Avoid re‑work |
| **Safety & Certification** | UL/PNE, UL 2200 for solar water heaters, cross‑connection standards | Prevents leaks & contamination |
| **Maintenance & Access** | MEP access, service points | Reduces long‑term cost |
| **Economic Factors** | OPEX, salvage value, incentives, payback period | Business case |
| **Environmental Impact** | GHG reduction, water‑use reduction | Compliance with local sustainability goals |
| **Scalability & Flexibility** | Modular unit additions/backup upgrades | Future‑proofing |

### 3. Five‑Step Workflow

> **Goal** – Efficiently loop from concept to commissioning.

> **Deliverables** – A written design document, a CAD drawing, and a short video demonstration (optional).

| # | Stage | Key Actions | Deliverables | Key Questions |
|---|-------|-------------|--------------|---------------|
| **1** | **Assess Feasibility** | • Survey building data<br>• Determine solar site map (tilt, shade, roof area)<br>• Measure peak DHW requirements (kWh/day)<br>• Evaluate grid‑backing options & local incentives | • Feasibility Report<br>• Data Sheet | *“Do I have enough roof space and the right solar exposure?”* |
| **2** | **Perform Sizing & Modeling** | • Compute collector area: A = (Load / Avg Solar × Desired solar share) / Collector efficiency<br>• Size tank: Volume = 1.5× peak daily demand (for 75 % availability)<br>• Select collector type (flat‑plate vs. evacuated tube)<br>• Software simulation (e.g., EnergyPlus, TRNSYS) | • Specification Sheet for all components<br>• Simulation Results (graphs of solar utilization, efficiencies, over‑heat likelihood) | *“What balance of collector area, tank volume, and backup do I need to hit the target?”* |
| **3** | **Design the System Layout** | • Draw piping schematics (PV‑in‑grid / battery loop)<br>• Integrate pumps & control logic (flow control, pump timing, pump‑cool‑off)<br>• Label safety devices (reliefs, temperature sensors)<br>• Optimize piping length to reduce pressure loss | • Fully‑labeled CAD drawing (collector & tank layout)<br>• Bill of Materials (BOM) | *“Do I have secure routes and sufficient pressure drop?”* |
| **4** | **Procurement & Installation Planning** | • Select suppliers (collector, tanks, energy‑kinetic, command‑line)<br>• Check local code compatibility (CFD, UL, PHI) <br>• Create procurement schedule (ordering, lead time)<br>• Create installation schedule (roof work, plumbing, electrical) | • Purchase Order (PO) documents<br>• Installation checklist | *“Do all units fit within local code, and have the required warranties?”* |
| **5** | **Commissioning & Field‑Testing** | • Verify panel orientation and cleanliness<br>• Test pump integration & programmable logic<br>• Run a two‑day full‑load test (cold, hot, intermediate), record pressure, temperature, flow<br>• Connect monitoring software to the control system<br>• Calibrate: auto‑run strategies (e.g., start pump 30 min before shower)<br>• Document findings & maintenance recommendations | • Commissioning Report (with data logs and analysis)<br>• User‑manual & safety instructions | *“Is the system delivering 90 %+ solar hot water, and are safety checks satisfied?”* |

### 4. Quick Start Checklist

- [ ] **Roof assessment** – no heavy shade, please.
- [ ] **DHW usage chart** – spreadsheets or data‑collector logs.
- [ ] **Back‑flow protection** – check if you need a secondary‑pressure HVAC or a check valve inside the tank.
- [ ] **Permits** – begins with a local permitting office.  
- [ ] **Electrical & pump** – gold‑standard efficiency rated for 240 V (U.S.) or 400 V (EU).
