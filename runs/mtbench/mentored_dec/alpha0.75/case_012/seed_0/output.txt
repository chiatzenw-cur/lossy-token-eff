| Technique | Educational Benefit | Example for Opium Wars |
|-----------|---------------------|-----------------------|
| **Role‑play** | Direct engagement + empathy | “Character of Lin Zexu – He wants to protect the empire.” |
| **Mime** | Emphasizes non‑verbal narrative | “Silex, smoke of chops – the opium scent.” |
| **Tableau** | Visual memory, visual mapping | “Bondage of commission – show the interior tension.” |
| **Improvisation** | Cues student creativity & quick thinking | “One‑minute reaction to the “Treaty of Nanking.” |
| **Narrative Frame** | Students form narrative scaffold | | 

**Differentiation** – give students choice of props, different roles (e.g., “operatic” vs. “non‑character”). Use peer‑graded rubric for performance: addition of a “scenery‑definition” step for ELL (the children working on new words).

## 4. Assessment

| **Criteria** | **KPI** | **Tools** |
|--------------|--------|-----------|
| Accuracy of facts | 1/4 (textual) | Script labels + focused feedback |
| Understanding of impact | 1/4 (essay) | 5‑sentence reflection |
| Performance suit | 1/4 (artistic perception) | Performance rubric (objectivity & imagination) |
| Stage presence & Mime | 1/4 | Teacher observation & peer comments. |

**Rubric** – *Accuracy & Relevance*  
- **4 I – Content** – Fully historically accurate (beyond De 2‑quarrow).  
- **3 – Good** – Comprehends main points.  
- **2 – Partial** – Misinterprets details.  
- **1 – Low** – Missing major points.

**Rubric for Performance** – *Artistic Impact*  
- **4** – Clear, expressive gestures; throttle cast.  
- **3** – Mostly solid; minor pauses.  
- **2** – Follows the script but lacking energy.  
- **1** – Confusing, poorly moved parts.
