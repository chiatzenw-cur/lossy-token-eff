## 6. When to Call a Doctor vs. You’re “OK”

- **Call emergency services** (or go to ED) if **any** of the following is present:
  - Fever > 38 °C (100.4 °F)
  - Persistent vomiting (especially if you can't keep fluids)
  - Blood–tinged stool or visible vomit
  - Severe, escalating pain that doesn’t improve with OTC measures
  - Rigidity, inability to move, or dizzy, fainting
  - Horner’s syndrome (drooping eyelid), severe jaundice, signs of sepsis

- **Call primary care** for:
  - Persistent localized pain (>24 h), especially if associated with red‑flag features but not intense enough to require ED
  - Minor episodes of abdominal pain that are recurrent (e.g., IBS‑type pattern)
  - Need for prescription medication or specialized imaging

- **Home care** (if no red flags, mild discomfort):
  - Follow “Home‑Based Management” list above
  - Keep symptom diary
  - Seek professional evaluation if symptoms persist > 1–2 weeks or worsen.

## 7. What to Bring to the Doctor

- **Patient’s age, sex, occupation, and fitness level**
- **Detailed symptom diary**: date/time, severity (0–10), location, quality, precipitating factors, relieving factors
- **Medication list**: prescription, OTC, supplements, herbal remedies used
- **Recent health changes**: infections, new bedding, travel, vaccinations, surgeries
- **Dietary preferences**: typical meals, any recent changes (e.g., fasting, new diet)
- **Allergies**: food, medication, latex
- **Medical history**: chronic diseases (diabetes, hypertension, gastroesophageal reflux disease), surgeries, family history of GI disorders

## 8. A Quick “Decision Tree” (for your own use)

1. **Intensity ≥ 8/10** + **Sudden onset** → Go to ED (immediate imaging + labs).
2. **Intensity ≥ 7/10** but **Gradual or tractable** → Call primary care (for urgent imaging if needed).
3. **Intensity ≤ 6/10** + **No red flags** → Try at‑home measures for 24–48 h; if no improvement, see doctor.
4. **Associated fever, vomiting, blood, or weakness** → Seek care immediately.
