## The “Elenchus” in Detail – The Standard “Socratic Drill”

1. **A starting point** – we pick a *pivot* from a specific speaker or *public* claim: “All things are *rational* (or *good*).”
2. **Guarding the claim** – either Socrates or the presumed “opinion” would ask how the *ability* or *definition* is evidenced by an everyday example.
3. **Evidence is conflicting** – the learner’s short answer leads to a **conflict** that is easily shown.
4. **Breaking the chain** – The chain collapses as the mind loses ground, falling back to the **chain of truth**.
5. *And by continuing the chain, the *response* returns to a *question* of validity.

## The *Strategic Power* of Socratic Irony  

- **“I am not a teacher, I am a question-hunter.”**  
- Think of the audience: it becomes aware that the *subject* – the functioning of being *educated* – is *in question*.  

During *Apology*, Socrates declares himself “rationalizing” while he is rejecting the idea that people can strut on given knowledge. Irony turns a familiar *practice* into an *experience* in doubt.

## When Socrates Turns the Paradigm

- **Skepticism kills or protects civic autonomy.**  
  By making the elite think of their *expertise* as *no more than an opinion* – Socrates gives the *folk* power to *join* the conversation rather than simply *listen*.
- **Dialectic forbids the “statesman’s stamp.”**  
  Opposing viewpoints are forced into *dialogue* rather than *monologue*.  
  Then the Aristotelian *law of the excluded middle* becomes contested.

### A Quick “Mini‑School” for a Socratic Move  

1. **State the anchor** – *“What, exactly, is justice?”*  
2. **Ask the supporting details** – *“Give me a circumstance where justice is exercised.”*  
3. **Challenge the definition** – *“Could the same example be ‘unjust’ if you looked at the person's cost?”*  
4. **Box the contradictions** – *“You just doubted the very definition, which has a comedic form."*  
5. **Invite new insight** – *“Let me ask you to propose your own alternative definition. What will the universe say, if any?"*

- *The Socratic method is *interrogation*, not instruction.  
- It *replaces* the *derived* authority (the “uncommon”) with *collective reasoning* hammered out by the group.  
- It is an *incentive* to think against the *accepted* reputations.
