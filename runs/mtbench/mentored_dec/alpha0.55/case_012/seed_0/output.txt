**Grade Level:** 9‑10  
**Total Time:** 3 days × 45 min each (135 min total)  
**Focus:** Integrating drama, mime, and theater techniques for 9‑10 year‑old learners exploring the Opium Wars between China and Britain.  

---

### 1. Learning Objectives  
| Objective | K–E‑C (Kalamata‑Edwards‑Clinton) | Assessment |
|-----------|----------------------------------|------------|
| **Historical** | • Understand the causes, key events, and outcomes of the Opium Wars. | • Scene‑based performance shows 3–5 key moments and the roles of Britain, China, the Opium Smugglers, and the Khan (or “Inspector”) of the Governor‑General. |
| **Literacy** | • Read and analyse primary sources (treaties, newspaper clippings). | • Individual reflection essay (300 words) tying personal experience to the moral legacies of the war. |
| **Communication** | • Use oral, visual, and non‑verbal communication to convey character motivations. | • Peer‑review rubric scoring clarity, accuracy, and creativity. |
| **Critical Thinking** | • Evaluate differing perspectives in a socio‑political context. | • Group‑discussion notes, argument visuals. |
| **Collaboration** | • Work in interdisciplinary groups to produce a mini‑theater piece. | • Teacher evaluation of teamwork, contributions, and problem‑solving. |

### 2. Materials & Resources

| Material | Purpose | Notes |
|----------|---------|-------|
| **Primary Sources** (excerpted treaties, opium petitions, diplomatic correspondence) | Visual & verbal cues for character roles | 3‑5 pages per group |
| **Script Templates** | Helps structure scenes | Use a “Mad‑Lib” simple framework for quick writing |
| **Props & Costumes** | Simple cross‑cultural items (silks, navy patches, “exotic’ hats, hatchets, etc.) | Encourage minimalism; avoid reinforcing stereotyping |

### 3. Class‑by‑Class Breakdown

---

#### **Day 1 – “The Opium‑Cash Flow” (45 min)**

| Time | Activity | Teacher Actions | Student Actions | Drama Technique |
|------|----------|-----------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| 0‑5 | **Warm‑Up**: “Tongue‑and‑Tail” & “Stroke the Sky” | Cue a relaxed “zoom in” on the head, instruct the class to move slowly, smile. | Keep calm, stretch. | Hinge on slow movement & body articulation. |
| 6‑12 | **Context Quiz** (3‑question rapid polling) | Quick poll: “Why did Britain export opium? (1) profit, 2) medicine, 3) habit.” | Doodle & guess. | Immediate, physical response. |
| 13‑20 | **Mini‑Lecture**: 3‑minute overview of trade & conflict | Use a slide deck highlighting timelines. | Take notes. | Teacher **asks** & **presents**; student **listens**. |
| 21‑35 | **Group Formation**: 4 groups (British, Chinese, Opium Smugglers, “Observer”) | Prepare short character sheets. | Identify who they are. | “Role‑play” with one quick handshake between groups for “meeting” moment. |
| 36‑45 | **Property & Script Structure** (writing 1‑sentence “Elevator Pitch” for each role) | Provide a 3‑sentence hook. | Draft each role’s hook. | Mutual collaboration – the “pitch” is a mini‑dramatic monologue. |

**Homework:** Students read their assigned source (treaty or manifesto) and write a 2‑sentence “what you are feeling” note (create a stage‑direction).

---

#### **Day 2 – “On Stage: The Clashing Trade” (45 min)**

| Time | Activity | Teacher Actions | Student Actions | Drama Technique |
|------|----------|-----------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| 0‑5 | **Warm‑Up**: “Sliding Hand” – short silent movement with partner to indicate a new point. | Cue __Swipe Mark__ on the floor. | Students pair up. | Silent communication; sets tone. |
| 6‑15 | **Foundations of Mime** – Brainstorming: “How did the Opium Must Move Body (Ship, River, Market)?” | Use a simple mime script that the teacher leads: “The Opium Swing." | Students mimic. | Non‑verbally portrays trade. |
| 16‑30 | **Scene Formation** – 3‑Act block: (1) Customs (19‑21 British) (2) Conflict (12‑26 Chinese) (3) Resolution (Treaty) | Provide a storyboard grid; students place scenes on the floor. | Confine each to 3‑5 min. | “Silent storytelling” with pauses. |
| 31‑38 | **Rehearsal** – 3‑minute run‑through with “phonetic cue” per line | Provide a line pair; teacher counts. | Students rehearse. | “Scripted rehearsal” used to keep timing. |
| 39‑45 | **Class‑wide Debrief** (using the “Debrief Circle”) | Prompt questions: “What did you feel when you saw the Chinese traders? What did the British feel in the crowd?” | Discuss and connect to emotions. | Rehearsal for crowd voice drama. |

**Homework:** Practice monologue (3‑min) in front of a mirror.

#### **Day 3 – “Final Performance & Reflection” (45 min)**

| Time | Activity | Teacher Actions | Student Actions | Drama Technique |
|------|----------|-----------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| 0‑5 | **Final Stretch** – Warm‑up for presentational performance | “Walk a line, then pause.” | Do. | Tension & relaxation. |
| 6‑25 | **Performance** – 4 × 3‑minute scenes (British–Chinese, Opium Traffic, Treaty Negotiations, Closing) | Cue stage lights, set a timer, record the clip. | Perform. | Use of accent, props, and minimal dialogue. |

### 4. Audience & Safety

- **Roles**: Each student participates in a way that ensures active competition: role‑play, mime‐rap, silent observation.  
- **Safety**: No weapons or dangerous props; all props are safe and properly stored.  
- **Inclusion**: Option for “scene–reader” to keep quiet groups or sensory‑overloaded students involved.  
- **Respect**: Emphasize respectful attitudes; the historical setting is not a place to attack but to understand.

## Closing Statement  

With this blended strategy, students will *feel* the volatility of the Opium Wars, *see* how narratives were formalised, and *consciously* reflect on the double‑edges of trade, empire, and resistance. By giving them a role, a movement, and a narrative voice, history becomes a living dialogue rather than a static recitation, and drama becomes the pneuma that breathes life into the past.
