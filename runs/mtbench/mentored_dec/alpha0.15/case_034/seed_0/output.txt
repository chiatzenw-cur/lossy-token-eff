## 3️⃣  Filtering by Age & Lifestyle

### Children / Adolescents  
- **High suspicion for appendicitis**, especially if pain migrates to RLQ.  
- **Dietary indiscretions** (e.g., newly introduced high‑fat food) may trigger gallstone‑like pain.  
- **Infection** (rotavirus, adenovirus) overwhelms with diarrhea/vomiting.

### Adults (18‑60 yr)  
- **Lifestyle factors**: high‑fat diet, alcohol, smoking, NSAID use.  
- **Stress & diet** contribute to IBS or functional dyspepsia.  
- Please check for **chronic diseases** (diabetes → microvascular GI issues, gastroparesis).

### Seniors (>65 yr)  
- **Higher risk of gallstones, peptic ulcer, diverticulitis, and colon cancer**.  
- **Polypharmacy** (NSAIDs, steroids, anticoagulants) can cause GI bleeding or ulceration.  
- **Weiss**: Cachexia, weight loss, subtle pain can mask serious conditions.

## 4️⃣  “First‑Aid” Strategies While You Investigate

| What | Why | How |
|------|-----|-----|
| **Cold/Heat Pad** | Relieves peritoneal muscle spasm | 10–15 min at 10–15 °C (cold) or 40–45 °C (heat) on the referred area. |
| **Hydration** | Prevents dehydration (especially if vomiting) | 500 mL water/Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS) every 15–20 min. |
| **Gentle, Small Meals** | Avoids provoking gastritis or gallstone pain | BRAT (bananas, rice, applesauce, toast) or low‑fat/low‑fiber nutrition. |
| **Peppermint tea** | Antispasmodic effect on smooth muscle | 1 cup, 2–3 x per day; safe for mild pain, but avoid in reflux or gallstone patients. |
| **Ginger root** | Anti‑nausea, lowers gastric acid | 1 g ginger sliced, steeped for 5 min; sip slowly. |
| **Avoid certain triggers** | Stops re‑activation of underlying pathologies | NSAIDs, coffee, alcohol, fermented foods, high‑fat meals. |
| **Monitor vital signs** | Looks for early signs of shock, sepsis | Temperature, pulse, blood pressure, capillary refill. |
| **Consider a PAN‑CT** | For emergency (high suspicion of perforation or obstruction) | Radiology discretion: CT abdomen/pelvis with contrast. |

## 7️⃣  A Practical Mini‑Guideline for “Intense Abdominal Pain”

1. **Triage**  
   - **Ask**: age, onset, location, radiation, associated symptoms.  
   - **Look**: Baseline vitals, signs of peritonitis, toxic appearance.

2. **Stabilize**  
   - Keep them hydrated (if not vomiting massively).  
   - Administer pain control if safe (paracetamol ≤ 4 g).  
   - Reassure and monitor.

3. **Investigate**  
   - **Initial labs**: CBC, CMP, lipase, LFTs, urinalysis.  
   - **Imaging**: Ultrasound (RUQ for gallstones), X‑ray (to rule out obstruction), CT abdomen/pelvis if needed.

4. **Treat**  
   - If diagnosis = **acute appendicitis** → surgical consult.  
   - **Gallstone** → analgesia + gastric protection + plan for ERCP or cholecystectomy.  
   - **Peptic ulcer** → proton‑pump inhibitor + H. pylori eradication.  
   - **Pancreatitis** → fluid resuscitation, NPO, analgesia, consider ERCP.  
   - **IBS** → dietary plan, antispasmodics (e.g., butylscopolamine), low‑dose tricyclics, or 5‑HT3 antagonists.  
   - **IBD flare** → steroids, biologic agents, or other disease‑modifying therapy.  
   - **Functional** → gut‑friendly herbal teas, mindfulness exercise, CBT.

5. **Follow‑up**  
   - schedule within 48–72 h after acute episode.  
   - educate on red‑flag signs, medication adherence, lifestyle modifications.
