When solid calcium carbonate (CaCO₃) is poured into aqueous hydrochloric acid (HCl), the acid dissolves the carbonate, producing calcium ions (Ca²⁺) and carbonate ions (CO₃²⁻). The carbonate ion is protonated by the acid:

1. \( \text{CaCO}_{3(s)} + 2\,\text{HCl}_{(aq)} \longrightarrow \text{Ca}^{2+}_{(aq)} + 2\,\text{Cl}^-_{(aq)} + \text{CO}_3^{2-}_{(aq)} \)

2. The carbonate ion then reacts with protons from HCl to form carbonic acid (H₂CO₃), which immediately decomposes into water and carbon dioxide gas:

   \(\text{CO}_3^{2-}_{(aq)} + 2\,\text{H}^+_{(aq)} \longrightarrow \text{H}_2\text{CO}_3_{(aq)} \; \xrightarrow{\text{decomposition}}\; \text{H}_2\text{O}_{(l)} + \text{CO}_2(g)\)

Combining the two steps gives the overall balanced reaction:

\[
\boxed{\text{CaCO}_3(s) + 2\,\text{HCl}_{(aq)} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{CaCl}_2(aq) + \text{CO}_2(g) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(l)}
\]

**Type of reaction** – *Acid–base (neutralisation) reaction* (more specifically, a classic example of an acid reacting with a carbonate/CO₂‑evolving base).

**Observations that confirm the reaction is occurring**

| Observation | Why it indicates the reaction |
|-------------|--------------------------------|
| **Effervescence / bubbling** | CO₂ gas is being released as the carbonate ion is protonated and carbonic acid decomposes. |
| **Disappearance of white solid** | CaCO₃ dissolves; the precipitate is gone as it turns into soluble CaCl₂. |
| **Solution becomes clear** | CaCl₂ is fully soluble; the solution turns from cloudy (due to suspended CaCO₃) to clear. |
| **Temperature rise** | The acid–carbonate reaction is slightly exothermic; a mild warming of the solution can be felt. |
| **Change in acidity** | The pH of the solution will rise after the carbonate is consumed, observable with a pH test strip or meter. |

**In short:** The solid calcium carbonate dissolves in hydrochloric acid, producing aqueous calcium chloride, releasing carbon dioxide gas (seen as bubbling), and forming water, with the reaction described by the balanced equation above.
