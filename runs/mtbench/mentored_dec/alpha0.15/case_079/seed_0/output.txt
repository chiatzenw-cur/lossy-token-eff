### How to Put It All Together

1. **Prep Before the Meeting**  
   - Have your business cards ready in a proper folder/holder.  
   - Write a brief note in Japanese if you’re sending an email invite—this signals that you respect their language.

2. **Upon Arrival**  
   - Greet everyone with a slight bow, saying “はじめまして” (Hajimemashite) if it’s your first encounter.  
   - Offer your card. Receive theirs with both hands and carefully read.

3. **During the Meeting**  
   - Wait for the senior partner to start speaking.  
   - Speak softly and avoid making a "yes/no" flat statement.  
   - If you have a question, list it after the host’s presentation; use a respectful phrase like “すみません、質問があります” (Sumimasen, shitsumon ga arimasu).

4. **After the Meeting**  
   - Thank the main host (e.g., “本日はお時間をいただきありがとうございます”).  
   - Return to your office, draft a short recap in Japanese, and send it with a polite closing.

5. **Lunch/Dinner Etiquette**  
   - Follow the host’s lead for seating.  
   - When offering a toast, say “乾杯” (Kanpai) and keep the voice low.  
   - Wait for the host’s cue before beginning to eat.

6. **Gifts & Follow‑ups**  
   - Bring a thoughtfully packaged gift if you’re visiting; present it with both hands.  
   - Send a thank‑you card one to two days later, noting how much you appreciated the meeting.

#### Quick “Do’s” vs. “Don’ts”

| Do | Don’t |
|---|---|
| Use both hands to give/receive business cards | Hand cards without addressing the recipient’s name first |
| Send polite thank‑you notes (in Japanese) | Use informal language or sign the note in random text |
| Use a slight bow | Shake hands vigorously or use a firm slap |
| Let the host start: “Welcome” or “Okay, let’s begin” | Take over conversation prematurely |
| Offer a small, boxed gift | Gift packages that hint at funerals (white) or weddings (red) |

### A Few Cultural Skeptical Notes

- **“No” is often missed**: Japanese colleagues may say “I’ll try” or “That’s a possibility,” not “No.”  
- **Group over individual**: A decision may be postponed until all senior members of the group meet.  
- **Hierarchy matters**: Addressing a superior takes precedence over a peer.  
- **Public humility**: Compliment is placed on the group, not on the individual.

By internalizing these norms, you’ll quickly come across as an attentive, respectful partner and’ll find it easier to build long‑lasting business relationships in Japan. Good luck!
