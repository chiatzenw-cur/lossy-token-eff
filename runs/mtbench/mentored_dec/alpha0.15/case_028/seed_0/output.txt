## 2️⃣ Five‑Step Workflow to Design a Residential Solar‑Powered Water‑Heating System

**Step 1 – Data Gathering & Site Assessment**  
1. **Homesize & Load**  
   - Number of occupants, number of bathrooms, appliances.  
   - Estimate daily hot‑water consumption: e.g., 100 L/day per 4‑bedroom house.  
2. **Solar Resource**  
   - Download monthly/hourly solar insolation data for the site (PVWatts, SolarGIS).  
   - Record average daily kWh/m²/day.  
3. **Roof Conditions**  
   - Total usable area, orientation, pitch, shading (trees, neighboring buildings).  
4. **Local Codes & Incentives**  
   - Building codes for solar installations, permits, certification bodies.  
   - Available rebates, tax credits, net‑metering provisions.  

*Deliverable*: A “Site Assessment Report” that lists all requirements and constraints.

**Step 2 – Preliminary Sizing & Performance Estimate**  
1. **Collector Area Calculation**  
   - \( A_{c} = \frac{\text{Required Energy}}{\eta \times H} \)  
   - \( \eta \) = system efficiency (typically 0.5–0.7).  
   - \( H \) = average solar insolation (kWh/m²/day).  
   - Example: 100 L × 5 °C = 5 kWh/ day; with η = 0.6 and H = 5 kWh/m²/day → \( A_c ≈ 1.7 \) m².  
2. **Tank Volume & Outlet Temperatures**  
   - Maintain minimum 55 °C in tank; outlet at 48 °C to meet 50 °C user demand.  
   - Reserve 10–20 % for standby.  
3. **Pump & Capacity**  
   - Flow rate = Hot‑water demand × 2.5 min (typical 4‑min peak).  
   - Use a variable‑speed pump if required for energy efficiency.  
4. **Performance Modelling**  
   - Use software such as **THERM**, **SolarQuote**, or **PV*SOL** to model system performance over a year.  

*Deliverable*: A sizing spreadsheet and performance model screenshots.

**Step 3 – Component Selection & Integration Design**  
1. **Collector Type**  
   - Choose between flat‑plate and evacuated‑tube based on cost, temperature range, and shading tolerance.  
2. **Storage & Heat‑Exchange**  
   - Decide on direct or indirect tank. Direct (no‑glaze) offers simplicity; indirect improves safety.  
3. **Pumping & Control**  
   - Select a commercially available controller with built‑in flow meter, temperature sensor, and low‑temperature cutoff.  
4. **Safety Accessories**  
   - Expansion tank (0.1–0.2 m³) plus PRV set to 6 bar.  
5. **Integration Jacks**  
   - Choose the correct inlet/outlet fittings: “solar” gauge, “no‑glaze” gauge, etc.  
6. **Technical Documentation**  
   - Create a **BOM** (Bill of Materials) and Bill of Quantities.  

*Deliverable*: Detailed component list with datasheets, installation diagrams, and cost estimate.

**Step 4 – Mechanical / Electrical Draft & Permit Preparation**  
1. **Plan Framing**  
   - Sketch collector mounting layout, pipe routing, pump placement on the roof or ground level, and tank location (basement/utility).  
2. **Electrical Wiring**  
   - List the controller, pump, potential inverter (for electric backup). Use compliant wiring (e.g., 4 AWG for 400 W).  
3. **Compliance Checks**  
   - Cross‑check all details against local building and electrical codes.  
4. **Permit Bundle**  
   - Compile the drawing set, device certificates, and a cost summary for submission.  

*Deliverable*: Final Design DGL (Drawings, General Layout) and Permit Installation File.

**Step 5 – Installation, Commissioning, and Validation**  
1. **Installation**  
   - Mount collectors (tilt angle, azimuth), weld / connect HTF piping, secure the tank, install the controller & pump, run the plumbing to the hot‑water line.  
2. **Commissioning Tests**  
   - Leak‑test HTF loop.  
   - Pressure test tank (10‑15 psi above operating).  
   - Run controller for a sunny day: check temperatures, flow, and mis‑sensing alarms.  
3. **Performance Verification**  
   - Compare observed energy output with the earlier model; adjust controller set‑points if needed.  
4. **Documentation & Training**  
   - Provide a user guide, maintenance schedule, and a troubleshooting checklist.  
5. **Post‑Installation Review**  
   - After 30 days, assess real‑world performance, confirm payback period, and adjust marketing if needed.  

*Deliverable*: “Commissioning Report” with test results, a maintenance log, and a user guide.

### Final Thoughts

- **Scalability**: Design the system to be modular so you can add more collectors or a larger tank later.  
- **Passive Options**: If budget‑constrained, consider a thermosyphon (no pump) collector + a passive solar hot‑water hand‑er.  
- **Smart Features**: Incorporate IoT-based monitoring (smart thermostat, remote alerts) so users can check performance on the go.  

This workflow will lead you from conceptual design to a fully commissioned, code‑compliant solar‑water‑heating system that meets the resident’s thermal comfort while delivering noticeable energy savings.
