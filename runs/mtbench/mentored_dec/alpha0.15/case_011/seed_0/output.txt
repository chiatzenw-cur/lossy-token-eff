## 3.  Bridge Typology – Candidate Options

## 4.  Detailed Design Phases

### 4.1.  Geotechnical & Seismic Hazard Assessment

1. **Site Investigation** – Borehole drilling, seismic refraction, SA (spectral acceleration) maps.  
2. **Fault Analysis** – Map active faults, estimate Paleoseismic slip.  
3. **Ground Motion Modelling** – Use regional SSI (Site‑Specific Intensity) curves, consider uplift and torsion.  
4. **Scour Analysis** – Combine hydraulic modeling with seismic uplift scenarios.

### 4.2.  Structural Analysis

1. **Linear‑Elastic Dynamic Analysis** – Finite element model of the full bridge (including soil‑structure interaction).  
2. **Non‑Linear Time‑History Analysis** – Using recorded or simulated earthquakes to assess drift and damping.  
3. **Drift Reduction Measures** – Introduce isolation bearings, expansion joints, and dampers.  

### 4.3.  Materials & Innovations

| Element | Material | Reasoning |
|---------|----------|-----------|
| **Deck** | Fiber‑reinforced polymer (FRP) or steel‑bonded concrete | High toughness, low weight. |
| **Piers** | Precast concrete with polymer‑fiber cable anchoring | Quick installation, uniform characteristics. |
| **Isolation Bearings** | Low‑friction bushing + high‑ductility elastomer | Basic seismic isolation + rotational flexibility. |
| **Fasteners** | Stainless steel with corrosion protection or high‑strength stainless fibers | Atmosphere resistant, maintain ductility. |

## 5.  Construction Approach

### 5.1.  Phasing

| Phase | Objective | Key Practices |
|-------|-----------|---------------|
| **Site Preparation** | Clear riverbed, install cofferdams, protect existing infrastructure. | Use temporary pontoon supports, spread load during real time. |
| **Foundation Works** | Drilled shafts/piles to bedrock; embed ISO & seismic bracing. | Dog‑leg or reinforced scaffold to avoid lateral sliding. |
| **Pier and Pylon Assembly** | Modular, minimal in‑water work. | Use subsea press‑locks + tension‐stage construction. |
| **Deck Segmentation** | Continuous pre‑cast segments; impose isolation bearings. | Use floating cranes or overhead gantry; precise alignment. |
| **Finishing & Inspection** | Full seismic testing (modal analysis, drift monitoring). | Use 3‑D laser scanning, vibration sensors, and embedded structural health monitoring. |

### 5.2.  Construction Safety & Minimizing Seismic Exposure

* **Schedule Construction During Quiescent Periods** – If possible, delay major load‑bearing components until low seismic probability windows.  
* **Use Rapid Components** – Prefabrication reduces on‑site worktime, thus less exposure to potential events.  
* **Real‑time Monitoring** – Deploy seismic sensors on the partially built structure to react quickly if gust.  
* **Redundant Lifting & Support** – Use double‑chained lube and fall arresting systems for heavy gantry cranes.  
* **Emergency Shut‑Down Plans** – Ensure crane/rigging can be safely secured before an event.

## 6.  Risk Management & Lifecycle

1. **Pre‑construction Risk** – Earthquake‑related supply chain disruptions. Mitigate: local fabrication of major components.  
2. **Construction Risk** – Ground motion during building. Mitigate: segmentation, minimize load on partially built spans.  
3. **Operational Risk** – Post‑earthquake earthquake creep and fatigue over 100‑yr life. Mitigate: embed structural health monitoring (SHM).  
4. **Maintenance Risk** – Scour, corrosion, and aging. Mitigate: continuous inspection program, cathodic protection, and flexible joints that can be replaced.
