> “Information flows in the sequence DNA → RNA → Protein.”  
> While the dictum is deceptively simple, it summarizes **how genetic information is stored, read, and used** to build the molecules that make life possible. It also sets the stage for understanding how cells preserve, copy, and transform that information.

---

## 1. The Core Information‑Flow Pathway

| Step | Molecule(s) | Process | Key Enzymes / Complexes |
|------|-------------|---------|------------------------|
| **Replication** | DNA ↔ DNA | Copies genetic material before cell division | DNA Polymerase, helicase, topoisomerase, ligase |
| **Transcription** | DNA → RNA (usually mRNA) | Reads a DNA template to produce a single‑stranded RNA copy | RNA polymerase II (in eukaryotes), RNAP I/III, σ‑factor (prokaryotes), transcription factors, CTP, UTP, GTP, ATP |
| **Translation** | mRNA → Polypeptide (Protein) | Decodes the mRNA codon sequence into a linear chain of amino acids | Ribosome (80S in eukaryotes, 70S in bacteria), tRNA with aminoacyl‑tRNA synthetases, initiation/elongation/termination factors |
| **Post‑Translational Modifications** | Protein (after translation) | Adds functional groups, cleaves peptides, folds into 3‑D structure | Kinases, phosphatases, proteases, glycosyltransferases, chaperones |

The *canonical* Central Dogma, as originally formulated, states that  
**DNA can be copied into DNA (replication), DNA can be transcribed into RNA, and RNA can be translated into protein**.

---

## 2. Beyond the Original Formulation – Modern Extensions

| Extended Process | What It Adds | Example|
|------------------|--------------|--------|
| **Reverse Transcription** | RNA → DNA | HIV reverse transcriptase; retrotransposons |
| **RNA Editing** | Modifications of RNA nucleotides post‑transcription | A‑to‑I editing in human brain; RNA splicing |
| **Non‑Coding RNAs** | RNA molecules that do not code for proteins but regulate gene expression | miRNA, siRNA, lncRNA – influence transcription, mRNA stability, translation |
| **Epigenetics** | Chemical marks on DNA or histones that affect gene expression without changing the DNA sequence | DNA methylation, histone acetylation |

These additions are *compatible* with the central dogma: the flow of information is still unidirectional in the sense that **DNA is the ultimate storage form**; all other changes are downstream events.

---

## 3. Who Named It?

| Person | Year | Source |
|--------|------|--------|
| **Francis Crick** | 1958 | In a 1958 lecture “The Potential for Genetic Regulators” at the University of Cambridge, Crick formalized the *Central Dogma* and coined the phrase. |  

**Crick’s Original Statement**  
> “…the genetic information stored in DNA can be copied into DNA and into RNA, and the information carried by RNA can be used to produce proteins. No other flow of information is possible.”

---

## 4. Why the Central Dogma Matters

1. **Framework for Gene Expression** – From a DNA sequence to functional proteins.
2. **Cutting‑Edge Research** – Understanding viral replication, developing gene therapy, designing synthetic biology circuits.
3. **Philosophical Insight** – It establishes the *irreversibility* of information flow (DNA → RNA → Protein) in modern biology.
