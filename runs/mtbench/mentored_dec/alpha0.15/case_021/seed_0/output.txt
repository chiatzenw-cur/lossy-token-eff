\[
|x+5| < 10,

we remove the absolute value by considering the two cases that arise from the definition of absolute value.

\[
|x+5| < 10 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad -10 < x+5 < 10.
\]

Subtract 5 from every part of the compound inequality:

\[
-10 - 5 \;<\; x \;<\; 10 - 5 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad -15 \;<\; x \;<\; 5.
\]

So \(x\) must lie strictly between \(-15\) and \(5\).

We now count the integer solutions in this interval.

The smallest integer greater than \(-15\) is \(-14\), and the largest integer less than \(5\) is \(4\).  
Thus the integers are

\[
-14,\,-13,\,-12,\dots,\,3,\;4.
\]

This is an arithmetic sequence from \(-14\) to \(4\) with a common difference of 1.  
The number of terms in such a sequence is

\[
(4 - (-14)) + 1 = 18 + 1 = 19.
\]
