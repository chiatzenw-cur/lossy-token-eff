**“Acting Through the Opium Wars: A Drama‑Infused History Experience”**  
*(Three 45‑minute periods – 3 days, Grades 9–10)*  

---

## 1. Overview  

Students will become “time‑traveling actors” who first *research* and *understand* the causes, events, and repercussions of the Opium Wars (1839–1842, 1856–1860). Then they will *transmute* that knowledge into short theatrical scenes (gestural or dialogue‑rich) that capture divergent perspectives (British merchants, Chinese officials, local villagers, soldiers, and modern observers). At the end of the unit, the class will perform a mini‑play for peers, integrating drama, mime, and theatrical storytelling to deepen empathy and historical understanding.

---

## 2. Learning Objectives  

| Domain | Objective |
|--------|----------|
| **Historical Knowledge** | • Identify key causes, milestones, and outcomes of the Opium Wars.<br>• Analyze the differential impact on Britain, China, and the broader world. |
| **Historical Thinking Skills** | • Interpret primary documents (trade agreements, newspapers, diaries).<br>• Make evidence‑based inferences about trade, imperialism, and resistance.<br>• Compare perspectives across cultures. |
| **Drama & Expressive Skills** | • Employ mime, gesture, and spoken language to portray historical characters.<br>• Use stage space, props, and masks to create symbolic meanings. |
| **Collaboration & Communication** | • Work in ensembles to develop a coherent dramatic scene.<br>• Provide constructive feedback during peer reviews. |
| **Critical Reflection** | • Reflect on how performance shapes personal interpretation of history.<br>• Write a reflective paragraph connecting the drama to historical analysis. |

## 5. Lesson Sequence  

Below is a 45‑minute timer for each day. Time is manageable but can be shifted slightly if necessary.

### Day 1 – 45 min: “Discover & Build”  

| Time | Activity | Drama Technique | Notes |
|------|----------|-----------------|-------|
| 0–5 | **Hook** – Show 30‑second clip of a British merchant negotiating opium, with subtle tension. | None | Sparks curiosity. |
| 5–10 | **Partner Warm‑up** – “Freeze, Mirror, Stretch” quick group exercises (students mirror a partner, then freeze). | Physical warm‑up; builds awareness of body shapes. | Optional. |
| 10–15 | **K‑W‑L Chart** – Students fill out: *What do I know* (5 min), *What do I want to know* (5 min). | None | Sets learning goals. |
| 15–20 | **Mini‑Lecture** – 5 min overview: chronology, key figures, and stakes. | Visual prompts. | Keep to ground truths. |
| 20–25 | **Group Formation** – 5 min – split into 3–4 students per “scene group.” | Collaborative. | Ensure mix of r1/2/3. |
| 25–35 | **Contextual Research** – Each group receives primary sources & persona cards. They note key facts & character motivations. | Silent reading; note‑taking. | Teacher circulates. |
| 35–40 | **Draft “Scene Outline”** – Groups decide on key moment they’ll dramatize (e.g., first treaty signing, the “Battle of Peking”, or a village’s reaction). | Brainstorm + index cards. | Use a 5‑minute time‑box. |
| 40–45 | **Homework Assignment** – Students individually bring back an image (from internet or textbook) that represents the main idea of their scene and jot a 2‑sentence emotional response to the image. | None | Prepares for Day 2. |

---

### Day 2 – 45 min: “Conceive & Create”  

| Time | Activity | Drama Technique | Notes |
|------|----------|-----------------|-------|
| 0–5 | **Recap & Washing** – Quick “What did we learn?” and “What image surprised you?” | None | Engages memory. |
| 5–10 | **Mime Warm‑up** – “Shadow Self” (students pair, each acts out an object; partners mirror). | Gesture play. | Helps dynamic listening. |
| 10–15 | **Role Assignment** – Each student chooses their persona card (public vs. private motives). | Decision making. | Reinforce empathy. |
| 15–25 | **Scriptwriting / Mime Planning** – 10 min each group charts their scene on a flip‑chart: <br>– Opening gesture <br>– Key dialogue (short, 2‑3 lines per character) <br>– Ending payoff | Use of index cards, basic script format. | Keep script to 5 min max. |
| 25–35 | **Rehearsal** – Chunked practice: <br>• 5 min “type‑building” (rehearse gestures + lines). <br>• 8 min “run‑through” with minimal feedback from peers. | “Witness” method – students watch their partner’s performance and record one thing they loved and one thing to improve. | Teacher gives quick structural pointers. |
| 35–40 | **Peer Feedback** – 5 min “Critique carousel” – rotate to give/receive quick feedback on clarity & authenticity. | Constructive critique. | Ensure safe environment. |
| 40–45 | **Planning for Performance** – Groups fill a “performance checklist” (costume, prop, space, line delivery). | Organization. | Transition to Day 3. |

---

### Day 3 – 45 min: “Perform & Reflect”  

| Time | Activity | Drama Technique | Notes |
|------|----------|-----------------|-------|
| 0–5 | **Final Warm‑up** – “Soundscape” – each student makes a quick sound that comfort or tension will accompany them during the scene (e.g., a bark, a cough). | Linking emotion & sound. | Sets atmosphere. |
| 5–15 | **Rehearsal Sprint** – Groups do a full run‑through, with teacher note‑taking. | “Staged rehearsal” – emphasise cues. |
| 15–30 | **Performance** – Each group presents a 3‑4 minute scene. After each, 1‑min appreciative reflection by the audience. | Performance art. | Encourage standing ovation style. |
| 30–35 | **Teacher Reflection** – Summarize play’s historical significance; highlight key global impacts. | Speaker lesson. | Use bullet‑points on board. |
| 35–40 | **Post‑Performance Discussion** – “What did you feel? How did gestures alter your perception?” | Socratic questioning. | Journals to be filled later. |
| 40–45 | **Exit Ticket** – “One thing I learned today that I didn’t know before” and “One question I still have.” | Assessment. | Optional digital via Google Forms. |

## 6. Drama Techniques & Artistic Guidance  

| Technique | Purpose | How It’s Used in Our Unit |
|-----------|---------|---------------------------|
| **Mirror Exercise** | Builds body awareness, empathy | Warm‑up (Day 1). |
| **Freeze/Spotlight** | Develops self‑disclosure & character focus | Rehearsals (Day 2). |
| **Shadow Self / Mime** | Visualizes abstract themes (trade, wealth, war) | Warm‑up & scene creation (Day 2). |
| **Masking (Paper Masks)** | Externalises internal motives | Character design before performance (Day 2). |
| **Soundscape** | Creates an aural backdrop influencing mood | Quick warm‑up (Day 3). |
| **Scene Mapping on Flip‑Chart** | Spatial planning for stage use | Used in script planning (Day 2). |

Students are encouraged to *think physically* (e.g., a scholar’s march, a soldier’s hesitation) to make historical concepts tangible.

## 8. Differentiation & Accommodations  

| Need | Strategy |
|------|----------|
| **EAL/D Students** | Voice recording of drama script to listen before performance; use of visual cue cards; partner with a fluent speaker. |
| **Students with Physical Disabilities** | Adapt mask-making to use neuron‑friendly materials; assign roles requiring less movement; create rotation system. |
| **Students needing content support** | Provide scaffolded sheet with bullet‑points; use “Topic Sentence + Evidence” format for script writing. |
| **High‑achieving students** | Pose additional “outside‑the‑box” scenes (e.g., internal monologue of a Chinese courier) or write a short play synopsis and annotate staging. |
| **Students needing extra time** | Allow extended rehearsal time (10 min extra). |
| **Students with anxiety** | Offer script rehearsal alone; provide transformation choices (character, setting, sound). |

## 9. Extensions & Enrichment  

1. **“Time‑Travel Interview”** – Students role‑play as a journalist interviewing a British merchant or a Chinese magistrate.  
2. **Digital Performance** – Record scenes and upload them to a shared class channel or YouTube playlist.  
3. **Listening to Historiographical Debate** – Add a 10‑min debate segment about “Was the Opium Trade morally justified?” integrating the drama performed.  
4. **Cross‑Curricular Art Project** – Students produce a painting or collage summarizing the drama’s emotional core (Book Arts/Art course partnership).

## 10. Follow‑Up & Reflection  

- **Lesson After‑Action Chat** – 10 min discussion with students about what the drama process revealed about primary sources.  
- **Aspirational Essay** – “If I were a legislator after the Opium Wars, what policy would I implement, and why?” | 3‑Paragraph essay. | Builds on knowledge & empathy.
