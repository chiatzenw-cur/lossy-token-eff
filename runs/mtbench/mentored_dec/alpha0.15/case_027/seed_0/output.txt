### 1. Light‑dependent reactions (in the thylakoid membranes)

1. **Photon absorption** – Photosystem II (PSII) absorbs sunlight, exciting electrons in chlorophyll a.  
2. **Water splitting (photolysis)** – PSII drives the oxidation of H₂O → O₂ + 4 H⁺ + 4 e⁻, releasing oxygen to the atmosphere.  
3. **Electron transport chain (ETC)** – Electrons flow from PSII → plastoquinone → cytochrome b6f → plastocyanin → Photosystem I (PSI).  
4. **ATP synthesis** – The flow of protons back across the thylakoid membrane through ATP synthase (chemiosmosis) turns ADP + Pi into ATP.  
5. **NADPH formation** – PSI re‑excites electrons, which are used to reduce NADP⁺ to NADPH.

| **Inputs** | **Outputs** |
|-----------|-------------|
| Light (photons), H₂O, NADP⁺, ADP + Pi | O₂, ATP, NADPH |

---

### 2. Calvin‑Benson cycle (in the stroma)

1. **Carbon fixation** – RuBisCO catalyzes the addition of CO₂ to ribulose‑1,5‑bisphosphate (RuBP), forming 2 molecules of 3‑phosphoglycerate (3‑PGA).  
2. **Reduction** – 3‑PGA is converted to G3P using ATP and NADPH: 3‑PGA → 1‑PGA → G3P.  
3. **Regeneration of RuBP** – Some G3P molecules are used to regenerate RuBP (needs extra ATP), allowing the cycle to continue.  
4. **Sugar synthesis** – The net product, one G3P, can leave the cycle and be used to build glucose, starch, etc.  

| **Inputs** | **Outputs** |
|-----------|-------------|
| CO₂, ATP, NADPH | G3P (tri‑sugar), NADP⁺, ADP + Pi (re‑cycled) |

**Net summary of the Calvin cycle** (per 3 CO₂ molecules):

```
3 CO₂ + 9 ATP + 6 NADPH → 1 G3P + 9 ADP + 9 Pi + 6 NADP+
```

**Why the two stages are distinct**

- **Light‑dependent**: energy‑harvesting, oxygen‑producing, establishes the necessary energy carriers (ATP, NADPH).
- **Calvin**: energy‑consuming, fixes carbon into organic molecules.

Together they convert light energy and atmospheric CO₂ into chemical energy stored in carbohydrates, powering life on Earth.
