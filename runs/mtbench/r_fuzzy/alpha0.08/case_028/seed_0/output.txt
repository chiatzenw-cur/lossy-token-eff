## 1. Key Components of a Residential Solar Water‑Heating System

| Component | Purpose | Typical Specifications |
|-----------|---------|-----------------------|
| **Solar Heat Collector** | Harvests solar energy | Flat‑plate (glass‑covered) or evacuated‑tube collectors, 2–3 m²/m³ of system rating |
| **Heat‑Transfer Fluid (HTF)** | Carries heat from collector to storage | Distilled water, antifreeze (ethylene glycol) or low‑viscosity oils |
| **Storage Tank** | Holds heated water for use | Insulated (6–10 mm steel & 20 mm foam), 80–200 L (size based on demand) |
| **Circulation System** | Moves HTF/ water | Self‑pump (electric or solar‑driven) or gravity‑fed; looped or well‑mixed |
| **Control Apparatus (SCM)** | Automates operation | Temperature sensors, indicating backup heat, iconot, tilting sensor |
| **Backup Heating** | Provides heat when solar insufficient | Gas or electric resistance heater |
| **Pumping & Distribution** | Feeds domestic hot‑water (DHW) demand | Electrically driven pump, thermostatic mixing valves |
| **Safety & Protection** | Safeguards system | Pressure relief valve, temperature relief, anti‑freeze, venting |
| **Monitoring & Data Logger** | Tracks performance | SCADA interface or simple display (weekly monthly data) |

## 3. Five‑Step Workflow to Design the System

### Step 1 – **Site Assessment & Data Collection**
1. **Solar Resource Analysis** – Obtain daily/seasonal insolation data; note shading from trees, buildings, antennas.  
2. **Building Hot‑Use Profile** – Gather domestic hot‑water (DHW) usage data and occupant patterns.  
3. **Site Constraints** – Check roof type, load capacity, pitch, orientation, local codes, required setbacks.

*Deliverable:* “Site Characterization Report” (solar diagram, shading graph, roof condition list).

---

### Step 2 – **Solar Load & Collector Sizing**
1. **Capacity Calculation** – Stack up DHW energy demand; subtract allowable storage capacity (e.g., 50% of total).  
2. **Collector Area** – Use the formula above; round up to nearest standard collector size (e.g., 1.5 m², 3 m²).  
3. **Efficiency Assumption** – Use 0.45–0.55 efficiency for flat‑plate; account for collectors’ orientation offset.

*Deliverable:* “Collector Sizing & Layout Diagram” (collector placement, mounting, spacing).

---

### Step 3 – **Storage & Distribution Design**
1. **Tank Size & Insulation** – Apply 0.6–0.8 L/NHB rule, add 20–30 mm foam insulation.  
2. **Hot‑Water Delivery** – Define pipe sizing, run layout to fixtures, incorporate thermostatic valves.  
3. **Blend with Backup** – Position electric/gas backup heater such that it’s only triggered when solar temp < 1 °C of set‑point.  
4. **Safety** – Install pressure/temperature relief and anti‑freeze valves per ASHRAE/IEC standards.

*Deliverable:* Final “System Schematic” (collector, tank, pump, backup, piping, safety devices).

---

### Step 4 – **Control & Automation Implementation**
1. **SCM Design** – Layout sensors: collector, tank, return, backup. Define logic:  
   - If Tank > 60 °C → standby;  
   - If Tank < 60 °C & Sun ≥ 250 W/kW → Pump;  
   - If Pump fails & Tank < 50 °C → Backup.
2. **Communication** – Choose simple display (LED, LCD) or advanced SCADA (Davis‑Q‑Mark).  
4. **Energy Management** – Consider coupling with residential PV for pump power.  

*Deliverable:* “Control Logic Diagram & Software (or PLC) specification.”

---

### Step 5 – **Installation, Commissioning & Evaluation**
1. **Installation** – Follow manufacturer’s instructions; ensure proper mounting, parabolic surfaces, seal integrity.  
2. **Commissioning** – Run system for 48–72 h; check for leaks, correct flow rates, temperature consistency.  
3. **Performance Logging** – Record Sun hour, modeled vs. actual output; compute performance ratio (PR).  
4. **Iterate** – Adjust controls (e.g., delayed pump start) based on real number of hot‑water draws.  
5. **Documentation** – Produce a final report with maintenance schedule, expected payback, warranties.

*Deliverable:* “Commissioning Manual & Performance Report.”

### Quick Reference  
- **Solar Panel Efficiency**: 0.45–0.55 for flat‑plates; 0.55–0.65 for evacuated tubes.  
- **Annual Payback**: 4–7 years (depends on incentives).  
- **Safety First**: Always include a pressure relief valve set to 2 psi above operating pressure.  
- **Maintenance Tips**: Clean collector glass quarterly; replace HTF filter every 6–12 months.
