If the satellite’s speed **drops below the value needed for a circular loop**, its orbit can no longer remain circular.  
The gravitational pull of the Earth exceeds the (now weaker) centripetal force, so the satellite starts to fall inward.  
After the brief adjustment, the motion is that of an *elliptical orbit* whose apogee (farthest point) is the radius at which the speed was reduced, and whose periapsis (nearest point) is smaller than the original \(r\).

---

### 1.  What happens to the orbital radius?

Let the original circular orbit have radius \(r\) and speed

\[
c = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{r}}\qquad(\mu = GM_{\oplus})
\]

The specific mechanical energy of a circle is \( \varepsilon_c = -\mu/(2r)\).

After a reduction to a speed \(v'<c\), a new specific energy was

\[
\varepsilon' = \frac{v'^2}{2}-\frac{\mu}{r}
            = \varepsilon_c + \frac{v'^2-c^2}{2}
            < \varepsilon_c .
\]

Because the energy is *more negative*, the satellite’s
“effective” gravitational potential is deeper; the orbit shrinks.
For an ellipse the specific energy is

\[
\varepsilon' = -\frac{\mu}{2a},
\]

so the new semi‑major axis is

\[
a = -\frac{\mu}{2\varepsilon'} < r .
\]

Consequently the satellite will travel from the apogee radius \(r\) to a
periapsis radius

\[
r_{\text{p}} = a(1-e)\; <\; a < r ,
\]

with \(e>0\) the eccentricity.  
Thus the **orbital radius decreases** (the satellite will swing inwards and then outwards again, but the average distance from Earth is now smaller).

---

### 2.  What happens to the period of revolution?

Kepler’s second law follows from conservation of angular momentum,
but the *third* law gives the period in terms of the semi‑major axis:

\[
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{a^{3}}{\mu}}.
\]

Because the new semi‑major axis \(a\) satisfies \(a < r\),
the orbital period **shortens**.  
Quantitatively, if the speed is reduced to a fraction \(\beta = v'/c\),

\[
a = r\,\frac{1-\beta^{2}}{2-\beta^{2,\;} } \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{T'}{T}=\left(\frac{a}{r}\right)^{3/2}<1 .
\]

---

### 3.  Summary

| Quantity | Before the speed drop | After the speed decrease |
|----------|-----------------------|---------------------------|
| Orbital shape  | circular | elliptical (apogee at former \(r\)) |
| Semi‑major axis \(a\) | \(r\) | \(a<r\) |
| Periapsis distance | \(r\) | \(r_{\text{p}}<r\) |
| Orbital radius (average) | \(r\) | \(< r\) |
| Period \(T\) | \(2\pi\sqrt{r^{3}/\mu}\) | \(<\) previous period |

The conservation of angular momentum keeps the orbital *shape* but
a loss of kinetic energy reduces the total energy, pulling the
orbit inward and tightening the satellite’s cycle. The satellite therefore
enters a smaller–radius, shorter‑period elliptical orbit.
