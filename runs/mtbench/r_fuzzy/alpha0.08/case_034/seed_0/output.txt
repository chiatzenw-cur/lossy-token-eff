### 2. Quick Physical Exam Focus Points (walk‑through)  
- **Inspection**: distension, scars, skin changes, weight loss, pallor.  
- **Auscultation**: hyperactive vs. hypoactive bowel sounds.  
- **Palpation**: tenderness (location, rebound), guarding, mass.  
- **Percuss**: tympany (gas) or dullness (fluid, mass).  
- **Abdominal auscultation during palpation & percussion**: “pain banding.”  
- **Rectal exam** (if indicated): occult bleeding, masses, fecal impaction.  
- **Other** (for women): pelvic exam for gynecologic causes.  

Cross‑reference exam questions with history to prune possibilities.

### 3. Differential Diagnosis (Intense Abdominal Pain)

| Category | Common Culprit | Typical Red/Blue Flag(s) | Quick Work‑up |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Appendic (appendicitis)** | RLQ, migratory pain, nausea, low‑grade fever | Peritonitis, elevated WBC | CBC, CRP, abdominal ultrasound or CT |
| **Diverticulitis** | LLQ, fever  >38°C, bowel habit change | RLQ pain, weight loss, anemia | CT abdomen w/ contrast |
| **Gastric/Pyloric ulcer** | Epigastric, burning, relieved by food | Hematemesis / melaena, melena or coffee‑ground vomiting | UGI endoscopy (when indicated) |
| **Cholecystitis / Gallstones** | RUQ pain, after fatty meals, Murphy’s sign | Fever | RUQ ultrasound |
| **Pancreatitis** | Epigastric radiating to back | Elevated AST/ALT, lipase >3× ULN | Serum lipase, amylase |
| **Intestinal obstruction** | Cramping, vomiting, last stool  >24 h | Abdominal distension, high‑set white‑neonatal | X‑ray series or CT abdomen |
| **IBD flare (Crohn’s/UC)** | RLQ/LLQ pain, diarrhea, weight loss | Blood in stool, fever | Colonoscopy, fecal calprotectin |
| **Gynecologic (e.g., ovarian torsion, ectopic)** | Pelvic pain, adnexal mass | Dysmenorrhea, amenorrhea, ectopic bleeding | Pelvic ultrasound |
| **Mesenteric ischemia** | Sudden severe pain, “pain out of proportion” | Acute onset, ileal pain, serious comorbidities | CTA/MRA abdomen |
| **Peptic/duodenal ulcer perforation** | Sudden, sharp pain-left shoulder | Rigidity, rebound, peritoneal signs (severe shock) | Immediate imaging → emergency |
| **Functional (IBS)** | Cramping, relief with defecation | No alarm signs, normal labs | Consider when others excluded |
| **Travelers / Foodborne** | Nausea, diarrhea, cramping | Recent travel, food changes | Stool cultures if needed |

### 4. “First‑Aid” Home / Outpatient Options While Awaiting Definitive Care

> **Make sure to follow up promptly or seek emergent care.** Below are general self‑care measures illustrating common, low‑risk interventions you can recommend *in parallel*.

| Scenario | Pharmacologic (OTC‑Rx) | Herbs / Natural | Lifestyle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Acute gastritis / reflux** | Alginates (Gaviscon), H₂ blockers (Famotidine 10 mg) | **Ginger** (1 g capsules) for nausea, **Chamomile tea** for soothing. | Small meals, avoid late eating, elevate bed head. |
| **Early mild abdominal cramping / IBS‑like** | Antispasmodics / antidiarrheals (Imodium, Pepto‑C) | Watercress, peppermint oil capsules (for IBS‑D) | Increase soluble fiber, stress-management stretching, hydration. |
| **Residual mild ‘stomach flu’** | Electrolyte solutions, Tums | **Chamomile** or **Peppermint** tea | Rest, fluid maintenance, bland diet. |
| **Minor gallstone pain** | NSAIDs (if no renal issues) | **Milk thistle** 200 mg (Lecithin & silymarin) | Low‑fat diet (avoid large oily dishes). |
| **Early pancreatitis pain** *(if pain mild & no suspicion of obstruction)* | Acetaminophen 650 mg 4x daily (avoid opioids until review) | Certain herbs (e.g., **Turmeric** for anti‑inflammatory, careful dosing with NSAIDs) | NPO (nothing by mouth) until next assessment. |
| **Post‑operative/road‑rash pain** | OTC pain meds (PARACETAMOL, Ibuprofen naproxen) | **Aloe vera gel** externally for skin irritation | Keep incision clean, maintain oral hydration if tolerated. |

> **When these methods could be detrimental (contingency)**  
> *If vomiting >24 h, pain “in the worst way,” or if there is abdominal rigidity → Use immediate medical assessment.*

### 5. Integration of the Patient Profile

When you receive details:

1. **Age**:  
   - **<5 y**: infectious causes (viral gastroenteritis), intussusception.  
   - **5–18 y**: appendicitis, constipation, hemorrhoids.  
   - **19–64 y**: typical GI pathologies, gallstone, ulcer.  
   - **≥65 y**: high suspicion for malignancy, diverticulitis, perforation, mesenteric ischemia.  

2. **Lifestyle**:  
   - **Diet**: High‑fat → gallstone risk; low fiber → constipation.  
   - **Alcohol**: Can contribute to gastritis, pancreatic inflammation.  
   - **Smoking**: Increases ulcer risk, gastric cancer risk.  
   - **Weight**: Obesity adds gallstone and reflux risk.  

3. **Medical Background**:  
   - **RA/IBD** → consider flares or medication side‐effects.  
   - **Diabetes** → possibility of gastropenic ulcer or autonomic neuropathy causing eating patterns.  
   - **Long‑term NSAIDs** → gastric erosions or perforation.  
   - **Immunosuppressants** → increased infection risk, enteric candidiasis.  

4. **Medications**:  
   - **Beta‑blockers** & **ACE‑I** → can mask pain.  
   - **Opioids** → can cause constipation & pain masking.  
   - **Antibiotics** (especially fluoroquinolones) → predisposition to *C. difficile* colitis.

### 6. What to Do Next – Step‑by‑Step

1. **Clarify details**: Through a phone‑in-filled questionnaire or early triage.  
2. **Prioritize red flags**: Ask for vital signs if possible.  
3. **Recommend urgent care**: If any acute red flag exists, direct to ER or urgent clinic.  
4. **If safe to manage outpatient**:  
   - **Order basic labs**: CBC, CMP, lipase >3× ULN, stool occult blood.  
   - **Order imaging** specifically indicated by history/physical: ultrasound (RUQ or abdomen), CT abdomen.  
   - **Start symptomatic therapy**: As above, tailored to likely differential.  
5. **Document**: Chief complaint, HPI, ROS, PGx, all medications, allergies, physical exam findings.

### 7. A Sample “Scenario to Illustrate the Process”

**Patient:** 38‑year‑old female, occasional beer, likes spicy and fried foods.  
**Chief Complaint:** Severe epigastric pain started 12 h ago, sharp, radiating to shoulder.  
**Associated:** Nausea, two emesis events, no fever.  
**History:** Hx. 2 years of uncomplicated functional dyspepsia. No medication allergies.

> **Interpretation:** 
> • Sudden, sharp, shoulder‑radiating pain points to possible pancreatitis or perforated ulcer or severe gallstone episode.  
> • After alcohol,; high‑fat meals can precipitate gallstones.  
> • Past dyspepsia → risk of ulcer.  
> • **Action**: Immediate gastric and pancreatic labs (lipase, amylase), abdominal ultrasound, keep NPO, consider empiric H₂ blocker, and advise urgent imaging/urgent care.

### 8. Final, Bottom‑Line Advice

| When to “Call the Doctor” | When to “Call 911” |
|---|---|
| Persistent pain >24 h despite OTC relief; new or worsening tenderness; abdominal distension; fever or chills | Sudden severe pain radiating to the back or shoulder; you feel faint or dizzy; blood pressure drop; foul odor in vomit; you hear or feel they’re bleeding; inability to retain fluids |

> **Remember:** Better to triage to a clinician promptly if uncertain.
