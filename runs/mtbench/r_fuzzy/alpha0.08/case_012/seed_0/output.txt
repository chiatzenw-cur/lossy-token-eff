|---------------|------------------|------------|-----------------------|
| • Identify the main causes, key events, and outcomes of the First and Second Opium Wars. | • Explain the economic and imperial motives of Britain and China. | • Create scripted dialogue that reflects historical authenticity. | • Develop empathy & respect for differing perspectives. |
| • Recall the Treaty of Nanking's terms. | • Describe how the Opium Wars altered Chinese society and global trade. | • Use mime and physicality to communicate non‑verbal messages. | • Recognize cultural sensitivity in representation. |

## 2. Day‑by‑Day Breakdown

### Day 1 – The Narrative Fabrics

1. **Hook (5 min)** – Quick video ~30 s plus “What do you think?”  
2. **Mini‑Lecture & Story‑Circle** – Use a pre‑drawn story‑circle graphic for 10 min: trade → conflict → warfare → treaty.  
3. **Perspective Pair Construction** – Students are divided into four “pair teams.” Each pair is assigned a viewpoint and creates a one‑page player card.  
4. **Warm‑Up (5 min)** – “Body Scan & Emotional Posture.”  
5. **Drafting & Mime Intro** – Students write a 3‑minute script.  They also stage a 30‑second “dry run” where they encode a gesture (e.g., a “holding a rope” gesture for smugglers).  
6. **Assignment** – Pairs complete the script and bring to Day 2 for rehearsal.  

**Differentiated Tasks:**  
- Language‑support: Provide a glossary of key terms.  
- Advanced: Encourage students to incorporate primary source quotes.  

---

### Day 2 – From Text to Body

1. **Mirror Warm‑Up (2 min)** – Student X mirrors Y; switch.  
2. **Reading & Immediate Feedback (10 min)** – Read script aloud; note historical accuracy.  
3. **Stage Planning (8 min)** – Mark personal space and blocking on classroom floor.  
4. **Emotion Map Workshop (5–7 min)** – Using a “feather” metaphor: comfort → performance.  
5. **Peer Feedback (10 min)** – Use 3‑question structure: *What worked?* *How was the body language?* *What can be improved?*  
6. **Action Sequences** – Rehearse for 5 min (e.g., the 1842 attack).  

**Mini‑Performance Focus**: “What shift in narrative voice will we maintain from negotiation?” Students practice moving from closed posture to open stance.  

---

### Day 3 – Showcasing History

1. **Silence & Focus (5 min)** – Students generate a “pause for thought” ritual.  
2. **Final Rehearsal (10 min)** – In‑group run-through, teacher circulates for pacing and clarity.  
3. **Performances (15–20 min)** – Each group showcases (limited audience).  
4. **Debrief & Reflection (5 min)** – Each student writes a reflection page via the guided template.  
5. **Synthesis & Next Steps (5–6 min)** – Teacher ties drama outcomes to historical facts.  

**Post‑Performance Assignment (Homework)** – Reflective essay linking performance to core historical concepts and personal learning.

## 4. Drama / Mime Techniques Explained

1. **Kinetic Gestures** – Simple reusable motions to quickly indicate profession.  
2. **Spatial Dynamics** – Using “grid” to create distinct “zones” for Chinese vs. British characters.  
3. **Emotion‑Muscle Mapping** – Visual mapping of tension in specific muscles to align with feelings.  
4. **Freeze‑Stage** – Students freeze at “critical moment” scenes & each other can comment.  
5. **Silence & Soundscapes** – Use silent drama for interior monologues; layered soundnotes for realism.

## 6. Reflection Prompt Ideas for Students

After the performance, provide prompts:

1. **Which body gesture communicated your character’s main motivation? Why.**  
2. **Did you observe any schemas that were not historically accurate? How would you fix it?**  
3. **What was the most challenging aspect of keeping your fictional character true to a real historical scene?**  
4. **How did collaboration alter your understanding of the Opium Wars?**

## 7. Teacher’s Notes & After‑Action Debrief

- **Warm‑up Importance**: The “Body Scan” routine reduces cognitive load and allows students to focus on *present* performance.  
- **Safety of Representation**: Avoid ad‑libbing “excessive” gestures that could misrepresent Chinese culture.  
- **Timing**: Keep groups short—control pacing by pausing after a 3‑minute limit.  
- **Scaffold Feedback**: Use the 3‑question set to encourage constructive criticism.  
- **Wrap‑up**: Create a shared PDF “Opium War Drama Quick‑Guide” for future classmates.
