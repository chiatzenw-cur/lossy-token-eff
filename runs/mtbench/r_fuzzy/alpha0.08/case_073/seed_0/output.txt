- **Basic statement:**  Genetic information flows *one‑way* from **DNA → RNA → Protein**.  
- **What it means:**  
  1. **DNA** (deoxyribonucleic acid) stores the master copy of an organism’s hereditary information.  
  2. **Transcription**: a segment of DNA is copied into messenger RNA (mRNA).  
  3. **Translation**: the mRNA is read by ribosomes and transfer RNAs (tRNAs) to synthesize a polypeptide chain, which folds into a functional protein.  
- **Why “one‑way”?:** In the classic view, once information has been used to make RNA or protein, it cannot be “re‑written” back to DNA. (Of course, later discoveries—retroviruses and reverse transcriptase—show there are exceptions.)

---

### Processes that constitute the central dogma

| Step | Process | Purpose | Example |
|------|---------|---------|---------|
| **Replication** | Data duplication of DNA | Cell division, preservation of genome | Semiconservative replication (Watson‑Crick branch model) |
| **Transcription** | DNA → RNA | Generates RNA copies that carry the message out of the nucleus | RNA polymerase II transcribes mRNA |
| **RNA processing (eukaryotes)** | capping, splicing, polyadenylation | Renders the RNA functional and exportable | Removal of introns, addition of 5’ cap |
| **Translation** | RNA → Protein | Builds polypeptide chains that become functional proteins | Ribosomal decoding via codons and tRNAs |
| **Post‑translation modifications** | Phosphorylation, glycosylation, etc. | Activates/targets proteins | Glycogen synthase activation |
| **Other related pathways** | Reverse transcription, DNA repair, recombination | Varied, some “cross‑barriers” | HIV reverse transcriptase, restriction‑modification systems |

*(Note: Many of these processes are inter‑related and involve additional proteins, enzymes, and co‑factors.)*

### Who coined the term “central dogma”

- **Francis P. Crick** – a British biophysicist and co‑discoverer of the DNA double‑helix structure.  
- **Citation:** *Crick, F. (1958). “On Protein Synthesis.” Nature 172, 251–252.*  
- In this and subsequent communications, Crick formulated a concise principle: “DNA → RNA → Protein.” He later elaborated the concept in a 1970 review in *Journal of Molecular Biology*.

---

### Quick Sidebar – Why “dogma?”

Crick used “dogma” deliberately to emphasize that, as far as he could see, the rule was a **redundant hypothesis** – once you had the mechanism (transcription + translation), it automatically implied the irreversibility of the flow. It became part of the scientific lexicon and helped standardise the training of biologists for decades.

---

**Bottom line:**  

- The **central dogma** is a conceptual framework that describes the unidirectional flow of genetic information from genes (DNA) → expressed genes (RNA) → functional products (protein).  
- It is underpinned by the well‑worked processes of DNA replication, transcription, RNA processing, translation, and subsequent protein maturation.  
- **Francis Crick** pioneered and popularised this principle in 1958.
