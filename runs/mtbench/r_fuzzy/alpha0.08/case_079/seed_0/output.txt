## Detailed Dos & Don’ts

### 1. **Punctuality**

| Do | Don’t |
|----|-------|
| Arrive 10–15 min early for every meeting. | Arrive late or “on time”; even a short delay can be interpreted as a sign of disrespect. |
| Call or send an email if you’re suddenly delayed. | Wait until the meeting starts to apologize. |

### 2. **Business‑Card Etiquette**

| Example | Explanation |
|---------|--------------|
| **Receive:** Hold the card with both hands, flip it over to view the name side section, read it slowly, and note any honorifics. | Shows respect for the person’s value. |
| **Giver:** Offer your card with both hands, ensure it’s facing the recipient. | Clear, professional & respectful. |
| **Handling:** Don’t write on a card immediately; keep it in a clean holder. | The card is a showing of you. |

**Common Mistake:** Writing on the card in your own handwriting (unless the other party asks it).  
**Fix:** Keep a business‑card holder.  

### 3. **Bowing & Greeting**

| When | How |
|------|-----|
| Initial greeting | A courteous “usual bow” (10–15°).|
| Handshake | Usually follows the bow; only after establishing rapport politely.|
| Thank/ Apology | Slight bow combined with a formal phrase: 「ありがとう (arigatou)」or 「申し訳ありません (moushiwake arimasen)."|

### 4. **Hierarchy & Titles**

| Rank | Title to be used | How it appears |
|------|------------------|----------------|
| Executive/President | 名・Company名 野村株式会社 (○○ 会社名 ○○ 社長) | Recognized; use `sama` if extremely senior. |
| Mid‑level manager/engineer | 名前 + 様 (san) | Standard. |
| Junior staff | 名前 | Use `san`. |

**NOTE:** In general, avoid using first names unless invited.

### 5. **Gift‑Giving (Omiyage)**

- **When**: at the end of a business trip or when visiting a company for the first time.  
- **What**: small items like local sweets, specialty teas, or travel‑size office supplies (no alcohol if no indication).  
- **How**: Offer the gift with both hands or receive it politely, showing a small bow.  

### 6. **Silence & Indirectness**

- **“No”** is rarely said directly; people often speak of “not suitable” or “might be not possible”.  
- **If uncertain**: Use “Just to confirm…?” or “I’m wondering how that might work.”  
- **Reading partners**: Body language and level of attention can confirm agreement.

### 7. **Clothing & Appearance**

- **Men**: Dark suit, white shirt, conservative tie.  
- **Applies to ‘barista’ conversations but corporate meetings still call for the norm.**  
- **Women**: Conservative business suits or skirts; avoid high heels if expected.  

### 8. **Hand Gestures (& Meeting Structure)**

| Setting | What’s accepted | What’s not |
|--------|-----------------|------------|
| Business meeting | Silent observation, minimal gesturing, waiting for the leader to speak before interjecting | Frequent hand gestures, talking way loud, direct confrontation |
| Public spaces | “Silent” voice (voice is used typically for the environment) | Loud conversation or shouting. |

### 9. **Email Etiquette**

| Key Feature | Need |
|-------------|------|
| Subject line | Foo | Thailand is very short, an 8‑hour ISO timing. |
| Opening 1 & 2 | Good format: “Dear e.g., Mr. Yamada”; “Thank you for meeting.” |
| Body | 3–4 concise lines; bullet points if needed. |
| Closing | `敬具` (敬具 `Keigu`) or `Yours sincerely` in English, with contact details. |

### 10. **Dining Etiquette**

- **Ordering**: Wait before ordering.  
- **Drinks**: Do not pour your own drink; wait for the person of higher rank to initiate.  
- **Toasting**: Gently raise your glass; “乾杯 (Kanpai)” with an angled tilt of the cup toward each person (not directly).  
- **Serving order**: Wait for the senior to start; often the host, then the guest.  
- **Finish**: Do not leave food on the plate—it’s a sign of generosity.  
- **Packets**: Leave the dish empty after finishing.

### 11. **Handling Refusal or Disagreement**

- **Phrase**: “I understand your concern. Perhaps we consider…”  
- **Tone**: Polite, calm, and respectful.

## Bottom Line & Practical Tips

| Habit | Why it matters | Quick Action |
|-------|----------------|--------------|
| **Plan & Confirm** | Japanese schedule is tight | Send a calendar invite 1–2 weeks ahead, confirm it a week before. |
| **Bring a Business‑Card Holder** | Paper cards have a professional life cycle | Keep them in a clean box; ask if you’re allowed to store them. |
| **Observe & Adapt** | Every office has unique subtleties | Ask a colleague for subtle cues before any first meeting. |
| **Small‑scale Gifting** | Show you value a partnership | Choose a tasteful souvenir, put it in the envelope, and say “Omiyage harimashita” (“I brought you something”). |
| **Use Formal language** | Keep communication courteous | Avoid slang, keep pronouns in formal form. |
| **Listen before answering** | Bureaucratic environment expects careful reading | Give full “ゆっくり高（yukkuri taka)” responses. |
