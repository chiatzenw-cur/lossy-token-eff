## 1. What Is Probability?

**Probability = “Chance of an event happening.”**  
It’s a number between **0 and 1** (or `0%` to `100%`).  
- `0` → impossible event  
- `1` → guaranteed event  
- `0.5` or `50%` → just as likely as not

**Notation:**  
- Event = `A` (say, “rolling a 3”)  
- Probability of `A` = `P(A)`

---

## 2. The Basics – Counting and Sample Space

### 2.1 **Sample Space (S)**  
All the possible outcomes of an experiment.

| Experiment | Sample Space (S) |
|------------|-----------------| 
| Rolling 1 die | `{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}` |
| Flipping coin | `{H, T}` |
| Drawing one card from a deck | `{2♩ of hearts, …, A♠ of spades}` (52 cards) |

### 2.2 **Counting Outcomes**  
If all outcomes are equally likely:

```
P(A) = (# of outcomes in A) ÷ (# of outcomes in S)
```

---

## 3. Simple Examples

### Example 1: Flipping a Coin  
- **Event A:** Heads  
- **Sample Space S:** `{H, T}`  
- Result: `P(A) = 1/2 = 0.5 = 50%`

### Example 2: Rolling a Die  
- **Event B:** Rolling an even number  
- **S:** `{1,2,3,4,5,6}`  
- Even numbers: `{2,4,6}` → 3 favorable outcomes  
- Result: `P(B) = 3/6 = 0.5 = 50%`

---

## 4. When Outcomes Aren’t Equally Likely

Sometimes you can’t simply “divide counts.” For *non‑uniform* experiments you weight the outcomes.

### Example 3: Rolling a Physical Die**
If a die is lopsided and lands on 6 20% of the time, but on other faces 14% each:

```
P(6) = 0.20
P(A) for any other number = 0.14
```

### Example 4: Drawing a Card from a Shuffled Deck  
Each card is equally likely **if the deck is fair**.  
If you’re doing a real world experiment (e.g., a deck slightly weighted), you might estimate probabilities with relative frequencies from many trials.

---

## 5. Adding Events – **Union**

If you want the probability that *either* event happens:

```
P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) – P(A and B)
```

*Why subtract?* Because when you add `P(A)` and `P(B)` you double‑count the overlap.

### Example: Rolling a Die — “Even or 4”
- `P(even) = 3/6 = 0.5`
- `P(4)=1/6≈0.17`
- `P(even AND 4)` = 4 is even → `1/6≈0.17`

```
P(even or 4) = 0.5 + 0.17 – 0.17 = 0.5
```

So the chance of getting an even number *or* a 4 is still 50%!

---

## 6. Disjoint (Mutually Exclusive) Events

If two events cannot happen at the same time:

- `P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B)`

No overlap, so no subtraction needed.

### Example: You pick a spice from a basket  
**A:** Salt  
**B:** Pepper  
You can’t pick both at once → just add the probabilities.

---

## 7. Complementary Events

The complement of an event `A` means “not A.”

```
P(not A) = 1 – P(A)
```

### Example: Probability of *NOT* rolling a 6
`P(6) = 1/6 ≈ 0.17`  
`P(not 6) = 1 - 0.17 = 5/6 ≈ 0.83`

So 83% chance you won’t roll a 6.

---

## 8. Conditional Probability (When One Event Affects Another)

If you know one event happened, the probability of another may change.

```
P(B | A) = P(A and B) ÷ P(A)  (A is given/known)
```

### Example: Picking Cards  
You know you picked a heart. What’s the chance it is the Ace of hearts?

- `P(heart) = 13/52 = 0.25`
- `P(heart and Ace) = 🂡♣ / 52 = 1/52 ≈ 0.0192`

```
P(Ace of hearts | heart) = (1/52) ÷ (1/4) = 4/52 = 1/13 ≈ 0.0769
```

So, under the assumption that it’s a heart, the odds bump to about 7.7%.

---

## 9. Independence

Two events are independent if one does not affect the other:

```
P(A and B) ≈ P(A) × P(B)
```

**Example:** Rolling a die *and* flipping a coin.  
Single roll: `P(5) = 1/6`.  
Single flip: `P(H) = 1/2`.  

joint: `P(5 and H) = 1/6 × 1/2 = 1/12`.

---

## 10. A Real‑World “Chance” Example

**Scenario:** You’re in a grocery store. The clerk says “There are about 30 people in the line, and each has 20 % chance of being an instant winner for a free pizza.”

- **Probability an individual wins**: `0.20`  
- **Probability *no one* wins**: `0.80^30 ≈ 0.003` (≈0.3%)  
- **Open for surprises:** at least one winner ≈ `1 - 0.003 = 0.997` (99.7% chance at least one will win)

That’s how you can comfort the group—they almost *guaranteed* a pizza marathon!

## 11. Quick Flashcard: “Power Rules”

| Rule | Formula | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| **Addition (disjoint)** | `P(A ∪ B)` | Rolling a 1 or a 2: `0.1667 + 0.1667 = 0.3333` |
| **Addition (not disjoint)** | `P(A) + P(B) – P(A ∩ B)` | Rolling a 1 or 3 or again a 1: `0.1667 + 0.1667 – 0.1667 = 0.1667` |
| **Multiplication (independent)** | `P(A ∩ B)` | Coin + die: `0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25` |
| **Complement** | `P(¬A)` | Not rolling 6 on die: `1 - 1/6 = 5/6` |

## 13. Quick Recap With Cheat Sheet

1. **Sample Space (S)** – all possible outcomes.  
2. **Event (A)** – a subset you care about.  
3. **Probability** – `|E| / |S|` (if all outcomes are equal).  
4. **Complement** – `1 – P(A)`.  
5. **Union (A or B)** – `|A| + |B| - |A∩B|`.  
6. **Intersection (A and B)** – `|A ∩ B| / |S|`.  
7. **Conditional** – `P(B|A) = (P(A∧B))/(P(A))`.  
8. **Independent** – `P(A∧B) = P(A) * P(B)`.
