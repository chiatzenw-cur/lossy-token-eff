## 1. Fiscal Policy: What Drives Output & Inflation?

Fiscal policy—government’s budgetary and tax policy—directly cuts into or fuels aggregate demand.  Its *multipliers* can be large for **USD** 150‑million USD, for the U.S. in the 2008–2010 stimulus.

### Key Effects of Fiscal Policy

1. **Fiscal “boost”** when the economy falls below potential:  *stimulus* + deficit → huge “stimulus multiplier” (price‑level).  We saw *triple amplification in the 2009 and 2020 stimulus packages* in an 850+ point GDP bump.

3. **Fiscal “bargaining”**: In late‑2000s, private fiscal terms are negotiated after *tax‑recovery* measures; in low‑growth chambers, *monetary tightening* can keep a 0.1 % inflation target.

## 4. How All The Pieces Connect – A Macro‑Policy‑Map

1. **Fiscal policy** (government buys, transfers, budget) **direct** – 
   - increases *aggregate demand* → *grows GDP* (if economic capacity is under‑utilised)  
   - may *increase inflation* if the economy is near its *capacity* or *add* a *debt‑crowding* effect.  

2. **Monetary policy** (central bank adjusts rates, supply) **indirect**: *influences Wealth, Investment, Consumer Debt*—thus lowering real demand, but sometimes raises rates to mop up *price chases* on certain goods.  

3. **Both** and **autocorrelation** interplay with *inflation expectations* and *policy credibility*.
