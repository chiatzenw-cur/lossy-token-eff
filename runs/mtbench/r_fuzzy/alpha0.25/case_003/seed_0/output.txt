> **Step 1. Gather an All‑Clear, “I’ll Talk again” Safety Net**  
>  A good start is to create a short, neutral environment where both of you can speak without interruption.  *Technique:* Warm‑up with a quick, word‑less check‑in: “What’s one thing you’d like us to focus on today?” This signals that you’re open to conversation, not the other way around.

#### A. Listen, Don’t Just Respond  
1. **Active Listening** – Repeat eye‑contact, a neutral “I hear you.”  
2. **Validate** – Even if you disagree about the detail, say “I see how hard that was to you.”  
3. **Ask When You Need Clarification** – “Can you tell me more about that?”  

*Why it helps:* Validation turns the speaker from “I’m wrong” into “I’m heard,” and the partner instinctively feels less defensive.

#### B. Express Your Own Needs, Not Accusations  
1. **Use “I” Statements** – “I feel overwhelmed when I feel unheard,” rather than “You never listen.”  
2. **Stay Specific** – “When I asked for cooking help and you no‑did, I felt unsupported.”  
3. **Keep It Present‑Moment** – Focus on “what’s happening now” rather than a lifetime history.

*Why it helps:* “I” statements help the partner feel that you’re sharing a feeling—not throwing blame on them.

#### C. Invite Them to Share Their Perspective  
| Prompt | Sample Strong‑Verbal Question | Sample Non‑Verbal Cue |
|--------|------------------------------|-----------------------|
| “I want to understand how the whole thing feels.” | “What part of this situation hurt you the most?” | Gentle nod, open palm |
| “How does that sound for you?” | “What’s been going for you?” | Tilting head, brief smile |

*Why it helps:* Letting people explain their experience brings a fresh angle in the drama that you might not have realized.

#### D. Set a “Right‑Now” Repair Plan  
1. Choose one problem you’ll fix first: “I’ll cook dinner; you can read.”  
2. Agree on the outcome.  
3. Commit to reviewing it in the next month.  

*Why it helps:* Small wins reduce the emotional fallout; primes confidence for tackling larger challenges.

#### E. Use a “Check‑In” Routine  
> **E.g.,** 1‑minute nightly “energy check” before, during, or after a family activity—“I liked the part where we played board together; I’m a little anxious about how we handle chores.”  

*Why it helps:* Prevents conflict from building at the “big‑moment” and keeps both parties in line.
