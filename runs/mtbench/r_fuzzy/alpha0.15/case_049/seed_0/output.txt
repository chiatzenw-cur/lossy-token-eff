## 1. Starry Night – Vincent van Gogh  
*Core Themes: swirling motion, feelings, perception of light.*

**“Spin‑and‑Search” AR Adventure**

1. **Setup**  
   * Use a simple AR icon (or QR‑coded card) to bring the painting onto new screens (tablet, projector).  
   * Kids wear a lightweight computer‑vision “mask” (e.g., a cardboard eye‑band) or just use cards on a table.  

2. **Game Flow**  
   * The painted sky now “spins” when the child turns the card/board.  
   * Hidden objects (little stars, a crescent moon, a grumpy cat) appear inside the swirling cloud.  
   * Kids must rotate the glass/card until the object becomes visible.  

3. **Learning Hooks**  
   * **Motion Studies**: teens learn how a simple rotation creates rotating patterns.  
   * **Emotion Matching**: each object correlates to a colour‑emotion card (blue = calm, orange = excitement).  
   * **Narrative**: the child becomes the “Night Navigator” who must return the lost star hearts to the sky.  

4. **Extension**  
   * Small stamps or stickers of the warped objects to paste on an artistic “night‑cape.”

## 2. The Last Supper – The Sunny‑Settled (copy) (any 16th‑century “Meal‑Gathering”)  
- *Core Themes: Family, sharing, teamwork.*

**“Mystery‑Treasure Scavenger Hunt”**

1. **Setup**  
   * Create a large print‑out of the painting or a wall‑mounted mural.  
   * Hide bright‑colored “treat” tokens (e.g., small paper “egg” shaped items) around the room.  

2. **Gamification**  
   * Each token has a clue referencing the position in the painting (e.g., “I sit beside your right hand in your painting of a table”).  
   * Children solve *creatively*—they often look at different angles of a child’s drawing or a big poster that shows the painting in full.  

3. **Co‑op Element**  
   * Kids must trade clues at a “congregational table” so they all help each other get the full treasure chest.  
   * Builds sharing, dialogue, and listening skills.

4. **Learning Hooks**  
   * **Spatial Awareness**: children locate objects based on orientation references.  
   * **Art‑Observation Tips**: identify details (e.g., the porcupine), discuss why companion animals might appear.  
   * **Cooking Theme**: use simple snack time to discuss how each “ingredient” contributes to the dish.  

5. **Extension**  
   * Kids create a short story in which the retrieved objects magically enliven the painting – a “story map” on paper.

## 3. The Persistence of Memory – Salvador Dalí  
- *Core Themes: Time, fluidity, imagination.*

**“Memory‑Scape VR Game”**

1. **Setup**  
   * Use a cheap VR headset or tablet‑based VR (Google Cardboard, EasyVR for tablets).  
   * Create a 3‑D model (SketchUp or Blender) of the famous melted clocks and the desert horizon.  

2. **Game Loop**  
   * Exploration: children can click on each melting clock to **“freeze”** or **“stretch”** them.  
   * Puzzles: to “kick” them back in normal shape, kids must answer trivia about the concept of time (time‑zones, saying “good morning”).  

3. **Educational Goals**  
   * How objects change shape under different forces (physics basics).  
   * Memories are flexible. Children can write “memories” on a paper and attach the clip‑on analog version.  

4. **Dialog & Storytelling**  
   * A voice‑over guide (the child's own recording) explains where each clock came from.  
   * A “time‑clock” timer tracks how long the children “protect” the clocks from melting.  

5. **Extension**  
   * Kids draw their own “meeting clocks” in a way that alphabeted on a paper sheet.

## 4. The Creation of Adam – Michelangelo  
- *Core Themes: Connection, creativity, human‑divine touch.*

**“Puzzle‑World 'Touch' Tablet Game”**

1. **Setup**  
   * Build a large cross‑screen puzzle (two halves down the table).  
   * Each half contains matching shapes of the human hand‑spread.  
   * Uses a tablet or interactive whiteboard at the corner where the faces *touch* (audible beep, color change).  

2. **Game Flow**  
   * Each child or pair “blinks” a shape, and the other half lights up with a matching color.  
   * The children then take the real hands and finally feel the “real” Émulation of the biblical hand (soft foam).  
   * The baby (a rubber ball) is placed amid the chalk‑white wall, and kids touch the ball with it on the surface.  

3. **Learning Objectives**  
   * **Spatial Reasoning**: match shapes in pairs.  
   * **Hands‑On Biology**: see how the hand evolves.  
   * **Story & Discussion**: talk about the concept of “gift” to connect art and emotions.  

4. **Extension**  
   * Create a mini‑book where the children animate the “on‑website” hand‑touch, learning a simple coding loop with blocks (MIT Scratch or Tynker).

## 5. Water Lilies – Claude Monet  
- *Core Themes: Light, water, colors, nature.*

**“DIY Sensory Painting Workshop”**

1. **Setup**  
   * Provide each child with crayons, watercolor paints, a bright canvas (or a thick paper).  
   * Set up a multi‑sensory station: Yellow/green sunlight (LED), “water” sound (water‑bowl effect) and even a high‑contrast scent like mint.  

2. **Activity Loop**  
   * Start with “draw a water lily” in crayon and entire base of the sketch.  
   * Then pop “water lily dyes” into small water droplets – each droplet has a tiny mark of a key color from Monet’s palette.  
   * The bath – they re‑apply the paint in a rippling effect – letting the paint flow.  

3. **Interactive Themes**  
   * **Tactile**: a “heavily textured” background of sand or glitter to feel the lily’s pound.  
   * **Audio**: as they paint, a tiny stream plays.  
   * **Color Matching**: kids match to another painting or a color picker that they can swipe to create a 3‑color wheel.  

4. **Learning Hooks**  
   * How light changes with depth; snapshot each step by taking a photo.  
   * Discuss “opacity” (mixing two colour paints create a “new” color now).  

5. **Extension**  
   * Kids create a small “own garden” book that compiles pricing for “lily prints” to display in a gallery.
