**Grade Level:** 9‑10  
**Topic:** The Opium Wars (1839‑1842 & 1856‑1860) – China & Britain  
**Duration:** 3 consecutive class periods (45 min each)  

---

### 1.  Learning Objectives  
By the end of the 3‑day unit, students will be able to:

| Knowledge | Skills | Attitudes |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Explain key facts about the Opium War(s) (causes, parties, trade ends, unequal treaties). | Use drama & mime techniques to represent historical events (e.g., “The Opium Factory,” “The Treaty of Nankingham”). | Recognize the impact of colonialism and trade imbalance on modern China’s development. |
| Identify & describe the consequences of the wars for China, Britain, and international trade. | Work collaboratively as a “trading company” or “parliamentary council” to negotiate a fictional representation of treaty terms. | Develop empathy for people in cross‑cultural conflict, fostering respectful dialogue. |

---

### 2.  Materials & Resources  

| Item | Source | Justification |
|------|--------|---------------|
| **Primary PowerPoint** (concise timeline, images, maps) | Teacher’s own creation | Quick overview; visual cues for drama |
| **Role‑character sheet** (Briton, Chinese imperial official, opium dealer, merchant, sea‑captain) | Custom worksheet | Clarifies identity and motivations |
| **Script prompts** (5‑min scenes) | Teacher’s script fragments | Offers structure for improvisation |
| **Projected “Treaty of Nanking” handshake clip** | Khan‑UK 2022 documentary | Modern visual anchor |
| **Simplified “Shooting a Rope” mime guide** – from *Teatro Estratto* (online) | Free online tutorial | Quick mime foundation |
| **Clothing / props** (paper hats, scarves, maps, small boxes labelled “Opium” or “Tea” | In‑class, minimal cost | Enhances immersion |
| **Digital Collaboration tool** (e.g., Padlet / Google Forms) | Google Workspace | Collects collective treaty ideas |
| **Rubric** (role‑play, content accuracy, creativity) | Teacher | Assessment clarity |
| **Reflection journal** (handout) | Teacher | Post‑performance reflection |

---

### 3.  Pre‑Presentation Preparation  

1. **Brief Warm‑Up (15 min)** – “Opposing Forces” physical exercise – 2‑person works in parallel (one quietly “chasing” the other with slower breath).   
   *Purpose:* Build kinesthetic energy, introduce theme of “balance of power.”

2. **Context Notes** – Students skim a 1‑page “Opium Context” handout (trade figures, dates, and a quick glossary).  

3. **Group Formation (1 hr)** – Randomly assign 5‑man groups: 2 non‑British roles (imperial official, general, an “ordinary Chinese” or “merchant”) and 3 British (merchant, admiral, or trade officer).

## Day‑by‑Day Breakdown

| Time | Activity | Drama / Theatre Techniques | Learning Focus |
|------|----------|-----------------------------|----------------|
| **Day 1 (45 min)** | 1. **Mini‑Lecture** (10 min) – Overview of pre‑-war trade, British colonial interest, Chinese resettlement | *Teacher uses a “timeline” projected as a playbill.*  | Facts and framing of the story |
| | 2. **Character Mapping Check‑In** (5 min) – Students label protagonist traits, personal stakes | *Students sketch in front of a mirror, then present.* | Personalization & empathy |
| | 3. **Movement Sketch** (15 min) – “Trade Route Cartography” (students move in line, showing routes on the floor) | *Use of *lambda dance* to depict goods moving.* | Geography & logistics |
| | 4. **Intro to Mime** (10 min) – Teacher demonstration of “opium scene” (no words, using hand gestures, stoops). | *Train of thought: “when the tea is hijacked we’re under pressure”* | Non‑verbal storytelling |
| | 5. **Exit Ticket & Reflection Prompt** (5 min) | *Students write 1 sentence about how the role description changed their understanding.* | Introspective connection |

| **Day 2 (45 min)** | 1. **Scene Rig** (10 min) – Assign a short scene: (a) “Entrance by the Fleet”, (b) “Negotiated Opium Trade”, (c) “The Battle of Palau” | *Use of a stage ladder (tableau) for each scene.* | Preparation of drama |
| | 2. **Improvisational Role‑Play** (25 min) – 4‑minute scripts, no set time limit, with “signal” when next group starts. | *Ground rules: one “action word” per sentence; “freeze” & “unfreeze” re‑enacts.* | Practicing argument & negotiation |
| | 3. **Group Reflection & Peer Feedback (5 min)** – Quick rubric exchange. | *Guided questions: Express what you felt; why did you choose this gesture?* | Metacognition |
| | 4. **Wrap‑Up & Homework Preview** (5 min) – Students prepare “Draft Treaties” in Padlet, reflecting 2‑3 signed terms. | *Encourages research, collaborative editing* | Ongoing learning |

| **Day 3 (45 min)** | 1. **Polishing Stage** (10 min) – Students rehearse, consider costumes/props. | *“Rehearse in pairs” technique, review “key moments.”* | Confidence building |
| | 2. **Final Performance (30 min)** – Groups perform sequentially (3 scenes each). Stage outlines on floor, audience sits “in front of the trade port.” | *Full production: entrance music, sound effect (sea‑waves), debate style.* | Masterclasses | 
| | 3. **Audience Voting & Awards (3 min)** – Vote on “Best Empathy”, “Creative Use of Mime”, “Accurate Representation.” | *Class vote & teacher comment.* | Recognition |
| | 4. **Reflection Journals (2 min)** – Students write what they learned and how drama helped. | *Prompt: “Describe a moment when you felt your role shift.”* | Deep reflection |
| | 5. **Class Debrief** (5 min) – Quick discussion: “What did the Opium Wars teach us about global trade today? How did drama help?” | *Teacher connects to global citizenship skills.* | Synthesis |

| **Collective Treaty Draft** | • Collaborative argument & negotiation <br>• Inclusion of modeled treaty terms (e.g., “fix tariffs on tea”). |
| **Reflection Journal** | • Depth of reflection (3 pts) <br>• Connection to drama techniques (2 pts). |
| **Peer‑Feedback** | • Thoughtfulness & constructive critique (1 pt). |
| **Participation Rubric** | • Attendance, punctuality, cooperation (0‑2 pts). |

## Differentiation & Inclusion  

| Age/GLB/Tips student | Strategy | Rationale |
|-----------------------|----------|-----------|
| **EAL** | Provide a bilingual glossary, pair with bilingual buddy. | Language scaffolding. |
| **STEM‑favoring** | Offer to simulate “alea dans” (chance games) to model economic factors. | Engages logical minds. |
| **Creative Arts** | Let them design their own production posters. | Extra creative outlet. |
| **Physical Limitations** | Provide stationary props (e.g., board blocks) to represent characters & avoid large movement. | Accessibility. |
| **Split‑group Approach** | Allow one teacher‑assistant or teaching assistant to monitor a mini‑group of 4 or 5 students for better supervision. | Manage class size. |

## Lesson Reflection (Teacher)

- **What Worked?**  
  - Students were visibly engaged during rehearsals.  
  - The “Stationary Negotiation” scene showed heightened empathy.  
- **What Needed Tuning?**  
  - Some groups over‑sampled “dialogue” vs. mime; a 10‑minute “warm‑up” could improve staying in the character key.  
- **Next Lesson Focus:**  
  - Connect to modern trade relations (e.g., Belt‑and‑Road Initiative) using modern media exposures.  
  - Expand the role‑play to include a *Diplomatic Summit* where they both present the treaty and defend it.
