## 8.  Conclusion  

The U.S. and Chinese antitrust agencies operate under a combination of legal traditions, cultural, political, and economic frameworks that are shaping the competitive landscape in different ways.  

- **US** has carved its niche in *“rule‑based, market‑share”* enforcement – there is a unique public trust for legal certainty.  
- **China** harnesses *wide‑scope powers* to intervene and promote “social‐public interest” – their administration appears to be leaning towards an **“industry‑specific” regulation** that can shape the market architecture for the next decade.

**For practitioners, entrepreneurs, and business analysts**:  
- Antitrust implications matter from product design to supply chain collaboration.  
- In both cases, counsel should evaluate independent IP, data flows, and supply‑chain *market‑share* metrics that might pave the way for regulators.  
- The *globalization of digital ecosystems* demands that you consider *dual‑jurisdiction* compliance—pursuing ans *co‑operative* negotiations across the WTO or the EU’s Digital Economy Cooperation.
