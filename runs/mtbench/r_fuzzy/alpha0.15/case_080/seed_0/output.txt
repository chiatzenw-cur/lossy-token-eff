### Take‑Away Recommendations for Aspiring Documentarians

1. **Every “Factual” Film is a Story** – Whether you’re using reenactments (*The Thin Blue Line*) or staged memories (*The Act of Killing*), treat your source material as *narrative threads*. Cliffhangers, beats, emotional arcs are all *designed with intention*.

2. **Documentary Style Is a Toolset, Not a Restriction** – Invent your own blend: Martin Scorsese’s *Raging Bull*‑style confession, McCarthy’s improvisational “edutainment” of *An Inconvenient Truth*. Choosing a voice (first‑person, omniscient, or ensemble) defines your film’s legitimacy with cinematographic style.

3. ***Credibility is Collaborative*** – When you invite your subjects to intervene (*The Act of Killing*), produce transparency guidelines on editing, and allow them to “review” scenes. Keep a version of the script in the subject’s hands; let the film keep its factual currency while receding nuance.

4. **Sound Is a Story‑teller** – Anonymizing voices—como in *The Cove*—conceals power dynamics; using ambient sound can focus viewers to the scene entirely. Practice building with music vs. silence; they’re both inseparable narrative devices.

5. **Visual Language = Cultural & Ethical Compass** – The hyper‑real 3‑D visuals in *Cave of Forgotten Dreams* are *not* an excuse for anthropological shrink‑wrapping. When you capture eye‑catching shots without contextualizing semantics, you risk burying the story in technobabble.

### Quick Study Path
