## 1. Time as a Narrative Construct

**Narrative Psychology** tells us that *we* constitute time. Erik Erikson’s “Identity vs. Confusion” and “Integrity vs. Despair” stages describe a shift from *looking outward* to *looking inward* and back—post‑mortem narratives become more embodied.  
For a child, “the future” is a field of *possibilities.*  
For an elder, “I had those years” becomes a *chart* for how to spend the final stretch.

---

## 2. The Changing Sense of Future

### 2.1 Imperfect Forecasting

- *Mental Calendar:* In adolescence, the brain can anticipate days but struggles with decades.  
- *“Limited Future” theory:* When the future is perceived as “short handful” (mid‑life), people become more present‑directive.  
- *Quantitative model:* Smith & Biro’s “time‑consistency” theory shows that people discount future benefits more steeply as they age, but as age increases beyond 200 years, the discount rate stabilizes.

### 2.2 The “Time Dilation” Experience

- **Children & Adolescents:** A five‑minute nap can feel “long” or “short” depending on excitement.  
- **Early Adults:** Time feels “real” until the deadline.  
- **Mid‑Age:** The brain’s *semantic memory* hits 3–5 year “task chunks” that compress large durations.  
- **Older Age:** As self‑concept shrinks, the sense of “years left” becomes a *numerical countdown*—a nightly recalling of a calendar day.  

---

## 3. Mortality as an Anchoring Variable

### 3.1 The Medical‑Risk Lens

- *Life expectancy trends*: More comfort with mortality means the *psychological buffer* shrinks.  
- *Health anxiety* is low in youth, spikes during mid‑life where health markers start to show wear, and calms thereafter if managed.  

### 3.2 Cultural and Social Markers

- **Renaissance:** The 12‑year “apprentice age” and 30‑year “guild admittance.”  
- **Biblical Ages:** “Sages” are honoured at 70.  
- **Modern Trends:** *Quarter‑life crises* highlight a cultural shift toward a “no‑profit” model for adulthood.

### 3.3 Psychophysiological Shifts

1. **Neuro‑Decline**  
   - The prefrontal cortex used to future‑value planning deteriorates after 60, leading to *emotional salience* dominance, making present risk more pronounced.  
2. **Hormonal Oscillations**  
   - The hormone *serotonin* lapses during adolescence.  
   - Levels of *cortisol*, the stress hormone, spike during career fulcrums.
