**What happens in each stage?**

1. **Light‑dependent reactions**  
   - Each photon excites chlorophyll‑a in Photosystem II (Photosystem I uses photons it absorbs after Photosystem II).  
   - Excited electrons travel through a protein‑complex electron‑transport chain (ETC).  
   - Splitting water in Photosystem II releases O₂ and protons in the thylakoid lumen.  
   - As ATP synthase uses the proton gradient, ATP is produced (photophosphorylation).  
   - Electrons ultimately reduce NADP⁺ to NADPH, which carries high‑energy electron pairs to the stroma.

2. **Calvin cycle**  
   - CO₂ is required for carbon fixation; the enzyme Rubisco catalyzes the attachment of CO₂ to RuBP, yielding 3‑phosphoglycerate (3‑PGA).  
   - ATP and NADPH from the light reactions power the reduction of 3‑PGA to G3P.  
   - A portion of G3P leaves the cycle to become glucose and other carbohydrates, while the rest is used to regenerate RuBP, hence the cycle continues.  

**Net overall reaction**  
(Using the combined net result of both stages)  
```
6 CO₂ + 12 H₂O + 12 photons → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ + 12 H₂O
```
(The water on the left is consumed in the light reactions; the water on the right is a by‑product of the dark reactions; overall the reaction balances out.)

So, light‑dependent reactions generate the energy carriers (ATP & NADPH) and release oxygen, while the Calvin cycle uses those carriers to incorporate CO₂ into sugars that sustain plant growth and, ultimately, the wider biosystem.
