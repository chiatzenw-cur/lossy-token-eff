Machine learning (ML) is a subfield of artificial intelligence that focuses on building algorithms that we can **teach** to perform tasks by learning from data, rather than being explicitly programmed for every possible case.  
Broadly, an ML pipeline looks like this:

1. **Collect & clean data** – raw observations (images, text, sensor readings).

2. **Choose an objective** – what do we want to predict / discover / decide?

3. **Select a learning algorithm** – a mathematical model that maps inputs to outputs.

4. **Train / fit the model** – let the algorithm adjust its internal parameters to make the objective well satisfied on the training data.

5. **Validate & test** – evaluate performance on unseen data, adjust hyper‑parameters.

6. **Deploy / update** – put the model into production and keep it learning (if relevant).

The hallmark of “learning” is that the algorithm automatically adjusts its internal state (population of weights or rules, etc.) by trial‑and‑error or by following a formal optimization principle.

## 2. Big categories of learning

| Type | What you get | How learning occurs | Typical data | Common algorithms | Real‑world use |
|------|--------------|----------------------|--------------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Supervised** | Predict/label a new input | The algorithm is shown *pairs* (input, correct label). It learns a mapping that minimizes discrepancy. | Labeled examples (image + class, sensor + fault status). | Linear regression, Decision trees, SVMs, Neural nets. | Spam filtering, credit‑card fraud detection, disease diagnosis |
| **Unsupervised** | Discover hidden structure | Only *inputs* are fed. The algorithm uncovers patterns, groupings, or compressed representations. | Untagged features (customer purchases, MRI scans). | K‑means, hierarchical clustering, PCA, PCA‑based autoencoders, t‑SNE, DBSCAN. | Market segmentation, anomaly detection, dimensionality modeling |
| **Reinforcement** | Learn a policy that maximizes cumulative reward | An *agent* takes action, receives a *reward* from the environment, and then learns to pick better actions over time. | States, actions, delayed rewards (game board, robot sensors). | Q‑learning, Policy Gradient, Actor‑Critic, Deep Q‑Network (DQN). | Game playing (AlphaGo), robotic locomotion, recommendation systems |

## 3. In‑depth comparison

| Feature | Supervised | Unsupervised | Reinforcement |
|--------|------------|--------------|----------------|
| **Label requirement** | Yes – target variable must exist | No – only raw input features | No explicit labels, but must provide **reward** signal |
| **Feedback signal** | Immediate – loss = difference between prediction & ground truth | None – algorithm decides what is “interesting” | Delayed, sparse reward since outcome comes after a sequence of actions |
| **Typical problem** | Classification, regression | Clustering, dimensionality reduction, density estimation | Decision learning, control, sequential planning |
| **Evaluation criterion** | Accuracy, MSE, F1, ROC‑AUC | Silhouette, Davies‑Bouldin, explained variance, reconstruction error | Cumulative reward, success rate, stability |
| **Sample complexity** | Often higher – you need enough labeled examples | Lower – can use large corpora without labels | Depends on environment; often high to cover diverse states |
| **Common data sources** | Human‑labeled spreadsheets, camera + annotation | Raw sensor logs, user logs, unlabelled visual data | Simulated environments (game engines), real robots |

## 4. Real‑world examples

### 4.1 Supervised Learning – Email Spam Detection

- **Problem**: For each incoming email, decide *spam* vs *ham* or rank by spam probability.
- **Model**: A logistic network (or Naïve Bayes) trained on thousands labeled email samples.
- **Why supervised**: We already have the ground truth (the inbox filter or human flag). The model learns a mapping from features (word frequencies, header heuristics) to labels.
- **Evaluation**: Accuracy, ROC‑AUC, false‑positive rate.

### 4.2 Unsupervised Learning – Customer Segmentation

- **Problem**: Group A~Z thousand customers into clusters that reflect buying habits, even though we don’t know the “true” cluster assignments.
- **Model**: k‑means or Gaussian Mixture Models that partition the feature space (purchase amount, product categories, frequency).
- **Why unsupervised**: We want to discover natural patterns; there’s no predetermined label.
- **Evaluation**: Silhouette score, intra‑cluster distance, business interpretability.

### 4.3 Reinforcement Learning – Autonomous driving

- **Problem**: Drive the car through traffic with minimal accidents. The car (agent) gets delayed reward signals: +1 for staying on road, negative for crash or violation.
- **Model**: Deep RL policy network (e.g., Trust‑Region Policy Optimization with sensor images).
- **Why RL**: The task is sequential and each action affects the future state. There is no static label for labels, the only feedback is the reward from the environment.
- **Evaluation**: Road safety metrics, average speed, compliance rate.

## 5. A few extra points

| Category | Sub‑types | What’s common |
|----------|-----------|---------------|
| Supervised | *Regression* (predict a continuous score). *Classification* (categorical). | Objective: minimize loss w.r.t. ground‑truth. |
| Unsupervised | *Cluster* (group). *Feature extraction* (PCA, principal components). | Objective: self‑‑consistency (e.g., minimize reconstruction error). |
| Reinforcement | *Model‑based* (learn environment model). *Model‑free* (learn action‑value directly). | Objective: maximize expected cumulative reward. |

---

## 6. Take‑away

* **Supervised**: “Teach someone to label exactly what you want.”  
* **Unsupervised**: “Let the data discover patterns on itself.”  
* **Reinforcement**: “Let an agent try, get a score, and learn to get higher scores.”

In practice, many systems blend them, e.g., self‑supervised learning (a form of unsupervised pre‑training that creates pseudo‑labels) or RL with supervised pre‑training. So the boundaries of the categories are useful for cognitive framing but rarely hard cliffs when building real systems.
