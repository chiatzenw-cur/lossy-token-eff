## Why These Five Compose a “Golden Set” for Evaluation

1. **Clarity & Precision** is the first checkpoint: if the argument is garbled, no subsequent logical analysis is meaningful.
2. **Logical Structure** turns what’s clear into something that can be tested—you get the classic assertions “If A and B, then C’, plus a see‑through proof‑tree.
3. **Evidence** is the bridge from claim to reality; an argument that flows logically but sits on flimsy facts is still weak.
4. **Assumptions & Counter‑Arguments** surface the “hidden currency” in many debates; they usually spell the difference between a robust argument and a rhetorical flourish.
5. **Scope & Applicability** restricts perspective, making it easier to judge whether the claim is *generally* true or just a case‑specific anecdote.

By cycling through these lenses, you can spot a great argument, a good argument, fallacies, or simply building a defense that never stands up to scrutiny.

### Quick Evaluation Checklist (for Writers)

1. **Stated claim clearly?**  
2. **Premises listed and understood?**  
3. **Logical link between claim & premises?**  
4. **Each premise backed by evidence or authority?**  
5. **Assumptions & limits of the argument expressed?**  
6. **Potential objections confronted?**  

If the answer to each of these is “yes,” you’re usually dealing with a genuinely persuasive argument. If not, the piece is an opportunity to draft a stylistic rewrite that will survive criticism.
