If we change one digit to \(1\), the resulting number must be divisible by \(7\).

---

### 1.  Congruences

Using \(1000\equiv -1,\,100\equiv 2,\,10\equiv 3,\,1\equiv 1\pmod 7\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{(replace thousands)} &\; 1000+100b+10c+d \equiv 0 \pmod 7\\
\text{(replace hundreds)} &\; 1000a+100+10c+d \equiv 0 \pmod 7\\
\text{(replace tens)} &\; 1000a+100b+10+d \equiv 0 \pmod 7\\
\text{(replace units)} &\; 1000a+100b+10c+1 \equiv 0 \pmod 7
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence

\[
\begin{cases}
2b+3c+d \equiv 1\\[2pt]
-a+3c+d \equiv 5\\[2pt]
-a+2b+d \equiv 4\\[2pt]
-a+2b+3c \equiv 6
\end{cases}\pmod 7
\]

---

### 2.  Solving the system

Subtract the first equation from the others to eliminate \(d\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
a+2b &\equiv 3\\
a+3c &\equiv 4\\
a+d &\equiv 2
\end{aligned}
\]

From \(a+d \equiv 2\) we have \(d\equiv 2-a\).

Substituting \(d\) into the first equation gives  

\[
2b+3c- a \equiv 6 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 3c-2a\equiv 3 .
\]

Using \(a+3c\equiv 4\) we eliminate \(c\):

\[
3c\equiv 4-a \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 4-a-2a\equiv 3\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 3a\equiv 1.
\]

The inverse of \(3\) modulo \(7\) is \(5\), so  

\[
a\equiv 5\pmod 7.
\]

Now

\[
\begin{aligned}
2b &\equiv 3-a \equiv 3-5 \equiv 5 \pmod 7 \;\Longrightarrow\; b\equiv 6,\\
3c &\equiv 4-a \equiv 4-5 \equiv 6 \pmod 7 \;\Longrightarrow\; c\equiv 2,\\
d &\equiv 2-a \equiv 2-5 \equiv 4 \pmod 7 .
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus in \(\mathbb Z_7\)

\[
a\equiv5,\qquad b\equiv6,\qquad c\equiv2,\qquad d\equiv4 .
\]

- \(c\equiv2\pmod 7\Rightarrow c=2\) or \(9\).
