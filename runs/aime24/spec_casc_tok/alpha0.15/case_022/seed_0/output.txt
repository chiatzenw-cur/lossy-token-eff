Because the dodecagon is regular, every pair of opposite vertices is diametrically
opposite: \(V_i\) and \(V_{i+6}\) (indices modulo 12).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 1.  How can a rectangle be inscribed in the circle?

A rectangle inscribed in a circle has its vertices at angles  
\(\alpha ,\beta ,\alpha+\pi ,\beta+\pi\).  
Hence, inside the regular dodecagon we may choose any vertex \(V_s\) to be the
first vertex of a rectangle; let the second vertex be \(V_{s+k}\) for some
\(k\) with \(1\le k\le 5\) (distances \(2-5\) give diagonals; distance \(1\)
gives a side).  
The remaining two vertices are automatically the opposite ones:
\(V_{s+6}\) and \(V_{s+6+k}\).

Thus a rectangle is determined by a pair  
\((s,k)\) where \(s\in\{0,1,\dots ,11\}\) and \(k\in\{1,2,3,4,5\}\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.  Counting the distinct rectangles

For each choice of \(s\) and \(k\) we obtain the set of vertices  

\[
\{\,V_s,\;V_{s+k},\;V_{s+6},\;V_{s+6+k}\,\}.
\]

Changing \(s\) to \(s+6\) gives the same set of vertices, only written in a
different order.  Therefore each rectangle is counted twice in the
\(12\times5=60\) pairs \((s,k)\).

Hence the number of distinct rectangles is  

\[
\frac{60}{2}=30 .
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 3.  Verification that every side is a side or a diagonal

For any \(k\) with \(1\le k\le 5\):

* \(V_s\) to \(V_{s+k}\) is a chord of step \(k\) (diagonal when \(k\ge2\),
  side when \(k=1\)).
* \(V_{s+k}\) to \(V_{s+6}\) has step \(6-k\) (also between \(1\) and \(5\)).
* The other two sides are symmetric.

Thus every side of the rectangle lies on a side or a diagonal of the
dodecagon.
