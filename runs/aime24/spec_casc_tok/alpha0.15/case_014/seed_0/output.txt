--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 1.  The configuration of the circles

Because the two extreme circles are tangent to the two sides \(AB\) and \(BC\) of the triangle,
the center of a circle is **always** at a distance \(R\) from the side \(AC\).
Hence all centers lie on a line \(L\) that is parallel to \(AC\) and is at a
distance \(R\) from it.

*   Let \(P\) be the intersection of \(L\) with the line that is parallel to
    \(AB\) at a distance \(R\) from \(AB\).  
    The first circle is centered at \(P\).

*   Let \(Q\) be the intersection of \(L\) with the line that is parallel to
    \(BC\) at a distance \(R\) from \(BC\).  
    The last circle is centered at \(Q\).

The circles are sequentially tangent, so the distance between the
centers of two consecutive circles equals \(2R\).
Consequently, the distance between \(P\) and \(Q\) equals

\[
PQ = 2R(n-1)
\tag{1}
\]

where \(n\) is the number of circles.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.  A convenient coordinate system

Let the triangle be right‑angled at \(B\); the configuration of the circles
indeed forces \(\angle ABC=90^\circ\).
Place

\[
B=(0,0),\qquad A=(a,0),\qquad C=(0,b),
\]

so that \(AB=a,\; BC=b\) and \(AC=\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}\,=c\).

The line \(L\) is parallel to \(AC\) and at a distance \(R\) from it.
Its equation is  

\[
y = -\frac{b}{a}x + c-\frac{Rc}{a},
\qquad\text{(distance to \(AC\) is \; \(R\))}.
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 3.  Coordinates of the two extreme centers

1. **First circle** – tangent to \(AB\) and \(AC\).  
   The line parallel to \(AB\) at distance \(R\) is \(y=R\).
   Intersecting with \(L\) gives

   \[
   P\Bigl(\;x_{P}=a-\frac{R(a+c)}{b},\;\;y_{P}=R\Bigr).
   \]

2. **Last circle** – tangent to \(BC\) and \(AC\).  
   The line parallel to \(BC\) at distance \(R\) is \(x=R\).
   Intersecting with \(L\) gives

   \[
   Q\Bigl(\;x_{Q}=R,\;\;y_{Q}=b-\frac{R(b+c)}{a}\Bigr).
   \]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 4.  The distance \(PQ\)

The vector \( \overrightarrow{PQ}=Q-P\) is  

\[
\overrightarrow{PQ}=
\Bigl(\;R-a+\frac{R(a+c)}{b},\;
      b-R-\frac{R(b+c)}{a}\Bigr).
\]

The line \(L\) has direction vector \((a,-b)\); a unit vector along
\(L\) is \(\displaystyle \frac{(a,-b)}{c}\).
Hence

\[
|PQ|= 
\frac{(-a+R\frac{a+c}{b})\,a-(b-R\frac{b+c}{a})\,b}{c}
      =c\Bigl(\frac{RS}{ab}-1\Bigr),
\]

where  

\[
S=a+b+c .
\]

### 5.  Relation between \(r\), \(R\) and \(n\)

From (1) we have

\[
c\Bigl(\frac{RS}{ab}-1\Bigr)=2R(n-1).
\tag{2}
\]

Introduce  

\[
k=\frac{S}{ab}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{RS}{ab}=Rk .
\]

Equation (2) becomes

\[
c\bigl(Rk-1\bigr)=2R(n-1).
\tag{3}
\]

Because \(r\) is the inradius of a right triangle,

\[
r=\frac{a+b-c}{2}=\frac{S-c}{2}

\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{\,r=\dfrac{1}{k}\,}.
\tag{4}
