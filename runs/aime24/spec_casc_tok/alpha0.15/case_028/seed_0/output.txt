### 1.  Congruence conditions

Using \(1000\equiv 6,\;100\equiv 2,\;10\equiv 3,\;1\equiv 1\pmod 7\), the four required congruences are  

\[
\begin{aligned}
&1000+100b+10c+d \equiv 0 \pmod 7 &&\Rightarrow 2b+3c+d\equiv 1 \pmod 7 \tag{1}\\
&1000a+100+10c+d  \equiv 0 \pmod 7 &&\Rightarrow 6a+3c+d\equiv 5 \pmod 7 \tag{2}\\
&1000a+100b+10+ d  \equiv 0 \pmod 7 &&\Rightarrow 6a+2b+d\equiv 4 \pmod 7 \tag{3}\\
&1000a+100b+10c+1  \equiv 0 \pmod 7 &&\Rightarrow 6a+2b+3c\equiv 6 \pmod 7 \tag{4}
\end{aligned}
\]

Subtracting (1) from the other three gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
6a-2b &\equiv 4 \pmod 7 \;\; \Longrightarrow\; 3a-b\equiv 2\pmod 7,\\
6a-3c &\equiv 3 \pmod 7 \;\; \Longrightarrow\; 2a-c\equiv 1\pmod 7,\\
6a-d  &\equiv 5  \pmod 7 \;\; \Longrightarrow\; d\equiv 6a-5\pmod 7.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus  

\[
b\equiv 3a-2,\qquad c\equiv 2a-1,\qquad d\equiv 6a-5 \pmod 7 .
\]

### 2.  Determining \(a\)

Insert these expressions into (1):

\[
2(3a-2)+3(2a-1)+(6a-5)\equiv 1\pmod 7 .
\]

Simplifying,

\[
18a-12\equiv 1\pmod 7

4a-12\equiv 1\pmod 7

4a\equiv 6\pmod 7.

Because \(4^{-1}\equiv 2\pmod 7\), we obtain

\]

The only admissible digit is \(a=5\).

### 3.  Solving for \(b,c,d\)

\[
\begin{aligned}
b &\equiv 3\cdot5-2=13\equiv 6 \pmod 7 \;\Rightarrow\; b=6,\\[2pt]
