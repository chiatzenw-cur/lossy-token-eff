\[
\log_x(y^x)=10,\qquad \log_y(x^{4y})=10 .
\]

These equalities give two equations

\[
y^x=x^{10}\qquad\text{and}\qquad x^{4y}=y^{10}. \tag{1}
\]

---

### 1.  Convert to logarithms

Taking natural logarithms,

\[
x\ln y = 10\ln x ,\qquad 4y\ln x = 10\ln y. \tag{2}
\]

Let  

\[
a=\ln x,\qquad b=\ln y .
\]

Then \(x=e^{a}\) and \(y=e^{b}\).  
Equations (2) become

\[
e^{a}b = 10a,\qquad 4e^{b}a = 10b. \tag{3}
\]

---

### 2.  Multiply the equations

Multiply the two equations of (3):

\[
(e^{a}b)(4e^{b}a)=10a\cdot10b
\]

\[
4e^{a+b}ab = 100ab .
\]

Because \(a,b>0\) (since \(x,y>1\)), we divide by \(ab\):

\[
4e^{a+b}=100
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
e^{a+b}=25.
\]

---

### 3.  Interpret \(e^{a+b}\)

But \(a+b=\ln x + \ln y = \ln(xy)\).  
Therefore

\[
e^{\ln(xy)} = 25 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad xy = 25.
\]

---

\[
\boxed{25}
