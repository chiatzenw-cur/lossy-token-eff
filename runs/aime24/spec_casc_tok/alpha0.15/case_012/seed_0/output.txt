A path that changes direction exactly four times consists of \(4\) turns, therefore it is made of  
\(4+1=5\) blocks of consecutive moves.  
The blocks must alternate between horizontal and vertical moves.

---

### 1.  Start with a horizontal block

Then the block pattern is  

\[
R\,U\,R\,U\,R
\]

Let  

\[
r_1,r_3,r_5 \ge 1,\qquad u_2,u_4 \ge 1
\]

be the lengths of the \(R\)‑blocks and \(U\)‑blocks respectively.  
They must satisfy

\[
r_1+r_3+r_5 = 8,\qquad u_2+u_4 = 8 .
\]

The number of ordered triples \((r_1,r_3,r_5)\) of positive integers summing to \(8\) is

\[
\binom{8-1}{3-1}=\binom{7}{2}=21 .
\]

The number of ordered pairs \((u_2,u_4)\) of positive integers summing to \(8\) is

\[
\binom{8-1}{2-1}=\binom{7}{1}=7 .
\]

Thus, for paths starting with a horizontal block, we have

\[
21 \times 7 = 147 \text{ possibilities.}
\]

### 2.  Start with a vertical block

By symmetry the count is the same: \(147\) possibilities.
