The lottery draws a random 4‑element subset \(R\) from the 10 numbers.

Let \(X=|J\cap R|\) be the number of matches between her set and the drawn set.

---

### 1. Probability of a grand prize  
A grand prize occurs when \(X=4\).  
Only one 4‑subset matches \(J\), so

\[
P(X=4)=\frac{1}{\binom{10}{4}}=\frac{1}{210}.
\]

---

### 2. Probability of winning a prize  
Winning a prize requires \(X\ge 2\).  
Count the number of 4‑subsets that intersect \(J\) in exactly \(k\) elements:

\[
N_k=\binom{4}{k}\binom{6}{4-k}\qquad (k=0,1,2,3,4).
\]

Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
N_2 &= \binom{4}{2}\binom{6}{2}=6\cdot 15=90,\\
N_3 &= \binom{4}{3}\binom{6}{1}=4\cdot 6=24,\\
N_4 &= \binom{4}{4}\binom{6}{0}=1.
\end{aligned}
\]

The total number of prize‑winning draws is

\[
N_{\ge 2}=N_2+N_3+N_4=90+24+1=115.
\]

Thus

\[
P(X\ge 2)=\frac{115}{210}=\frac{23}{42}.
\]

---

### 3. Conditional probability  
\[
P(X=4\mid X\ge 2)=\frac{P(X=4)}{P(X\ge 2)}
 =\frac{\frac{1}{210}}{\frac{23}{42}}
 =\frac{1}{210}\cdot\frac{42}{23}
 =\frac{1}{115}.
\]

So the required probability is \(\frac{m}{n}=\frac{1}{115}\) with \(m=1,\; n=115\).
