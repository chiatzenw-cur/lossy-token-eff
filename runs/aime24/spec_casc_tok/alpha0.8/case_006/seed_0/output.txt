If the volume is \(V\) and the total surface area is \(A_{\text{tot}}\), then the distance from \(I\) to each face (the inradius) is  

\[
r=\frac{3V}{A_{\text{tot}}}.
\]

---

### 1. Coordinates of the tetrahedron

Take  

\[
A=(0,0,0),\qquad B=(\sqrt{41},0,0).
\]

Place \(C\) in the same plane \(z=0\):

\[
C=\Bigl(\frac{16}{\sqrt{41}},\,\frac{12\sqrt{21}}{\sqrt{41}},\,0\Bigr).
\]

The chosen coordinates satisfy  

\[
AB=\sqrt{41},\quad AC=\sqrt{80},\quad BC=\sqrt{89}.
\]

Let \(D=(u,v,w)\).  
Using the distances to \(A,B,C\) we find

\[
u=\frac{25}{\sqrt{41}},\qquad
v=\frac{556}{3\sqrt{861}},\qquad
w=\frac{80}{3\sqrt{21}}.
\]

(These are obtained by solving the three equations  
\(u^2+v^2+w^2=89,\; (u-\sqrt{41})^2+v^2+w^2=80,\; (u-\frac{16}{\sqrt{41}})^2+(v-\frac{12\sqrt{21}}{\sqrt{41}})^2+w^2=41.\))

---

### 2. Volume

The volume of a tetrahedron with vertices \(A,B,C,D\) is

\[
V=\frac{1}{6}\Bigl|\,\vec{AB}\times\vec{AC}\,\cdot\vec{AD}\Bigr|.
\]

Here  

\[
\vec{AB}\times\vec{AC}=(0,0,\sqrt{41}\,y),
\quad
y=\frac{12\sqrt{21}}{\sqrt{41}},
\]
so  

\[
\vec{AB}\times\vec{AC}=(0,0,12\sqrt{21}).
\]

Thus  

\[
V=\frac{1}{6}\,12\sqrt{21}\,w
      =2\sqrt{21}\,w
      =2\sqrt{21}\cdot\frac{80}{3\sqrt{21}}
      =\frac{160}{3}.
\]

---

### 3. Surface area

Each face has side lengths \(\sqrt{41},\sqrt{80},\sqrt{89}\), so all faces are congruent.  
The area of one face (say \(ABC\)) is

\[
\operatorname{Area}_{ABC}
    =\frac12\,\sqrt{41}\cdot\frac{12\sqrt{21}}{\sqrt{41}}
    =6\sqrt{21}.
\]

Hence

\[
A_{\text{tot}}=4\cdot 6\sqrt{21}=24\sqrt{21}.
\]

---

### 4. Inradius

\[
r = \frac{3V}{A_{\text{tot}}}
   = \frac{3\left(\frac{160}{3}\right)}{24\sqrt{21}}
   = \frac{160}{24\sqrt{21}}
   = \frac{20}{3\sqrt{21}}
   = \frac{20\sqrt{21}}{63}.
\]

Thus the distance from \(I\) to each face is \( \displaystyle \frac{20\sqrt{21}}{63}\).

Here \(m=20,\; n=21,\; p=63\) (with \(\gcd(20,63)=1\) and \(21\) square‑free).

\[
m+n+p = 20+21+63 = 104.
\]
