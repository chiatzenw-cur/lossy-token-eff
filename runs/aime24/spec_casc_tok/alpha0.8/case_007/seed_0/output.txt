Its radius is

\[
r=\frac12\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}}
\]

Hence \(r^{2}=\dfrac{a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}}{4}\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 1.  Conditions for the box

The box satisfies  

\[
\begin{cases}
ab+bc+ca = 27 & \text{(surface area)}\\[2mm]
abc = 23 & \text{(volume)}
\end{cases}
\]

Let \(S=a+b+c\).  Then  

\[
a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}=S^{2}-2(ab+bc+ca)=S^{2}-54 .
\]

Thus \(r^{2}=\dfrac{S^{2}-54}{4}\).  
To maximise \(r^{2}\) we need to maximise \(S\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.  Maximising \(S\)

Using the two constraints, the cubic with roots \(a,b,c\) is  

\[
x^{3}-Sx^{2}+27x-23=0 .
\]

There must exist positive real roots, so the discriminant must be
non‑negative.  
Instead of a discriminant calculation, we find the stationary points of
\(S\) via Lagrange multipliers:

\[
\begin{aligned}
2a &= \lambda_{1}(b+c)+\lambda_{2}bc,\\
2b &= \lambda_{1}(a+c)+\lambda_{2}ac,\\
2c &= \lambda_{1}(a+b)+\lambda_{2}ab .
\end{aligned}
\]

The symmetric solution \(a=b=c\) is impossible (\(a^{3}=23\) and
\(3a^{2}=27\) cannot hold simultaneously).  
The other possibilities are obtained by setting two variables equal:
\(a=b=x,\; c=y\).

From the constraints

\[
\begin{cases}
x^{2}+2xy=27,\\[1mm]
x^{2}y=23 ,
\end{cases}
\]
which gives \(x^{3}-27x+46=0\).

The positive roots are

\[
x=2 \quad\text{or}\quad x=-1+2\sqrt6 .
\]

For \(x=2\), \(y=\dfrac{23}{4}=5.75\).
For \(x=-1+2\sqrt6\), \(y=\dfrac{25+4\sqrt6}{23}\).

Compute the corresponding sums \(S\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
S_{1}&=2+2+\frac{23}{4}=9.75,\\[2mm]
S_{2}&=2(-1+2\sqrt6)+\frac{25+4\sqrt6}{23}\approx 9.31 .
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus \(S\) is maximised at \(a=b=2,\;c=\dfrac{23}{4}\).

### 3.  Largest radius
