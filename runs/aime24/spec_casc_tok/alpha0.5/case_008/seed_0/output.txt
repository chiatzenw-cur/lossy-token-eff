\[
\log _x(y^x)=\log _y(x^{4y})=10 .
\]

---

### 1.  Translate the logarithms into equations

\[
\log _x(y^x)=10 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y^x=x^{10}.
\]

\[
\log _y(x^{4y})=10 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x^{4y}=y^{10}.
\]

Taking natural logarithms of both equations gives

\[
x\ln y = 10\ln x ,      \qquad\qquad (1)
\]
\[
4y\ln x = 10\ln y .      \qquad\qquad (2)
\]

---

### 2.  Introduce \(a=\ln x,\;b=\ln y\)

Then \(x=e^a,\;y=e^b\).  
Equations (1)–(2) become

\[
e^a\,b = 10a,\qquad 4e^b\,a = 10b.
\]

Solve each for \(b\) and \(a\):

\[
b = \frac{10a}{e^a}, \qquad
a = \frac{5}{2}\,\frac{b}{e^b}.
\]

---

### 3.  Eliminate \(a\) and \(b\)

Insert the expression for \(b\) into the expression for \(a\):

\[
a = \frac{5}{2}\,\frac{\, 10a/e^a\,}{e^b}
   = \frac{25a}{e^a e^b}.
\]

Since \(a>0\) (because \(x>1\)), cancel \(a\):

\[
1 = \frac{25}{e^a e^b}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
e^a e^b = 25.
\]

But \(e^a=x\) and \(e^b=y\), so

\[
xy = 25.
\]

---

### 4.  Verification (optional)

Setting \(xy=25\) satisfies both
\[
x\ln y = 10\ln x ,\qquad 4y\ln x = 10\ln y,
\]
and indeed positive real solutions \(x>1,\;y>1\) exist (e.g. \(x\approx1.536,\;y\approx16.27\)).
