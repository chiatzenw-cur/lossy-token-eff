### 1.  Write the two conditions in terms of the sums  

**Row condition**

\[
abc + def = 999
\]

\[
100a+10b+c+100d+10e+f = 999
\]
\[
100(a+d)+10(b+e)+(c+f)=999\tag{1}
\]

Define  

\[
S_1=a+d,\; S_2=b+e,\; S_3=c+f.
\]

Then (1) becomes  

\[
100S_1+10S_2+S_3=999. \tag{2}
\]

**Column condition**

\[
ad+be+cf = 99
\]

\[
10a+d+10b+e+10c+f = 99
\]
\[
10(a+b+c)+(d+e+f)=99\tag{3}
\]

Define  

\[
T_1=a+b+c,\; T_2=d+e+f.
\]

Then (3) becomes  

\[
10T_1+T_2=99. \tag{4}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.  Solve the equations for the sums  

**From (2).**  
All \(S_i\) are between \(0\) and \(18\).

* Mod 10: \(S_3\equiv 999\pmod{10}\Rightarrow S_3=9\).
* Subtract \(S_3\): \(100S_1+10S_2=990\Rightarrow 10S_1+S_2=99\).
* Mod 10: \(S_2\equiv 99\pmod{10}\Rightarrow S_2=9\).
* Hence \(10S_1+9=99\Rightarrow S_1=9\).

Thus  

\[
S_1=S_2=S_3=9 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a+d=b+e=c+f=9. \tag{5}
\]

**From (4).**  
Because \(T_1\le 27\), \(10T_1\le 270\); the only viable values are

\[
T_1=8\;\text{or}\;9.
\]

*If \(T_1=9\):* \(T_2=9\).  
*If \(T_1=8\):* \(T_2=19\).

But adding the three equations in (5) gives  

\[
(a+d)+(b+e)+(c+f)=27.
\]

So \(T_1+T_2=27\).  
The pair \((T_1,T_2)=(9,9)\) gives \(T_1+T_2=18\neq27\).  
Hence the only possible pair is  

\[
T_1=8,\qquad T_2=19. \tag{6}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 3.  Reduce to a single free set of variables  

From (5) and (6):

\[
\begin{aligned}
a+d &= 9, & d &= 9-a,\\
b+e &= 9, & e &= 9-b,\\
c+f &= 9, & f &= 9-c,\\
a+b+c &= 8 .
\end{aligned}
\]

The last equation is the only independent condition on \((a,b,c)\);  
\(d,e,f\) are then determined by the first three equations.

### 4.  Count the admissible triples \((a,b,c)\)

We need non‑negative integers \(a,b,c\) with \(a+b+c=8\) and each \(\le 9\).  
Since \(8\le 9\), the upper bound never binds, so the number of solutions is

\[
\binom{8+3-1}{3-1}=\binom{10}{2}=45.
\]

Each such triple yields a unique choice of \(d,e,f\) (still digits \(0\)–\(9\)), so each triple produces a valid grid.
