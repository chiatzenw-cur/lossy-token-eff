### 1.  Geometry of a contact point of the two surfaces  

The torus is a surface of revolution around the \(z\)–axis.  
In the \(x\!-\!z\) plane one sees a circle

\[
(x-R)^{2}+z^{2}=a^{2}\;(1)
\]

(centre \((R,0)\)).
The sphere of radius \(B\) whose centre lies on the same \(z\)-axis has the
cross‑section

\[
x^{2}+(z-c)^{2}=B^{2}. \;(2)
\]

The two curves touch while the torus is   
*outside* the sphere (\(T\) “rests on outside of \(S\)”)  
or while the torus is
*inside* the sphere (\(T\) “rests on inside of \(S\)”).
In the first case the two circles are *externally* tangent; in the second
they are *internally* tangent.



--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.  Distance between the centres

\[
d=\sqrt{R^{2}+c^{2}}
\]

must satisfy

\[
\begin{cases}
d=a+B &\text{(external tangency on the outside)}\\[2mm]
d=B-a &\text{(internal tangency on the inside)} .
\end{cases}
\]

Consequently  

\[
c^{2}=(a+B)^{2}-R^{2}=14^{2}-6^{2}=160\quad
\quad\text{(outside case)}
\]
\[
c^{2}=(B-a)^{2}-R^{2}=8^{2}-6^{2}=28\quad
\quad\text{(inside case)} .
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 3.  The radius of the circle of contact on the torus  

For a point of contact the line joining the two centres passes through
the contact point.  
With \(d=a+B\) (outside case) the contact point \(P\) is

\[
\overline{C_{1}P}=a,\qquad
\overline{P\,C_{0}}=B ,
\]

hence, in the horizontal direction,

\[
x_{P}=R-\frac{aR}{d}=R-\frac{aR}{a+B}
    =R\Bigl(1-\frac{a}{a+B}\Bigr)=R\frac{B}{a+B}.
\]

The distance of this point from the \(z\)-axis is exactly the radius
\(r\) of the touching circle on the torus:

\[
r_{\text{outside}}=x_{P}=R\frac{B}{a+B}
          =6\left(\frac{11}{14}\right)=\frac{33}{7}.
\]

In the inside case \(d=B-a\) and the same reasoning yields

\[
x_{P}=R-\frac{aR}{d}
    =R\left(1-\frac{a}{B-a}\right)
