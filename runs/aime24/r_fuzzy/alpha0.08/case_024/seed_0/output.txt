### a.  Sum of the two rows

From the first equation

\[
100(a+d)+10(b+e)+(c+f)=999 \tag{2}
\]

Taking it modulo ten gives  

\[
c+f\equiv 9 \pmod{10}.
\]

Since each digit is at most \(9\), the only possibility  
\(c+f=9\).  
Let  

\[
x=a+d,\qquad y=b+e.
\]

Equation (2) becomes  

\[
100x+10y+9=999 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 10x+y=99. \tag{3}
\]

Hence

\[
x=9,\qquad y=9,,\qquad  
a+d=9,\;\;b+e=9,\;\;c+f=9. \tag{4}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### b.  Sum of the columns

Insert (4) into the second equation of (1):

\[
10(a+b+c)+(d+e+f)=99.
\]

Using \(d=9-a,\; e=9-b,\; f=9-c\),

\[
10(a+b+c)+(27-(a+b+c))=99 \Longrightarrow 9(a+b+c)=72 .
\]

Thus

\[
a+b+c=8. \tag{5}
\]

### c.  Counting the possibilities

The digits \(a,b,c\) are non‑negative integers with

\[
a+b+c=8,\qquad 0\le a,b,c \le 9.
\]

Because the sum is only \(8\), the upper bound \(9\) imposes no further
restriction. Thus we simply count the non‑negative solutions to
\(a+b+c=8\):

\[
\binom{8+3-1}{3-1}=
\binom{10}{2}=45 .
\]

determined by \(d=9-a,\;e=9-b,\;f=9-c\), and all of them are indeed
