### 1.  Convert the equalities to equations

\[
\log_x(y^x)=10 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad y^x = x^{10}\quad\text{(A)}
\]

\[
\log_y(x^{4y})=10 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad x^{4y}=y^{10}\quad\text{(B)}
\]

Take natural logs of each side:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{(A)}:\quad x\ln y = 10\ln x \;\; \Rightarrow\;\; \ln y = \frac{10\ln x}{x}\;\; (1)\\
&\text{(B)}:\quad 4y\ln x = 10\ln y \;\; \Rightarrow\;\; 4y = \frac{100}{x}\;\; (2)\\[4pt]
\end{aligned}
\]

Equation (2) gives a direct relation between \(x\) and \(y\):

\[
x(4y)=100 \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; y = \frac{25}{x}.
\]

Hence the product

\[
y\,x= x\cdot\frac{25}{x}=x\cdot\frac{25}{x}=25.
\]

\left(\frac{25}{x}\right)^x = x^{10}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
