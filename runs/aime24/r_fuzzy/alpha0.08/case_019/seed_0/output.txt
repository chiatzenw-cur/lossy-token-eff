### 1.  The point \(C\) on \(\overline{AB}\)

Let \(C=(X,Y)\) lie on \(\overline{AB}\).  
The end points of the segment are

\[
A=\left(\frac12,0\right)\qquad B=\left(0,\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right).
\]

\[

### 2.  Condition for uniqueness

For a segment of \(\mathcal F\) to contain \(C\) we need a pair
\((x,y)\in\mathcal F\) that satisfies (1).
If \(C\) were contained in another segment \(P'Q\) of the family,
then (1) would have a solution other than the one corresponding to
\(\overline{AB}\) (\(x=\tfrac12,\;y=\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\)).
Therefore, for the unique point \(C\) all solutions of (1) have to be
exactly this one.

Fix \(X,Y\) on \(\overline{AB}\) and define  

\[
g(r)=\frac{X}{r}+\frac{Y}{\sqrt{1-r^{2}}},\qquad 0<r<1 ,
\]

where \(r=x\) and \(y=\sqrt{1-r^{2}}\) (because \(x^{2}+y^{2}=1\)).
The equation gives a solution precisely when \(g(r)=1\).

The function \(g(x)\) tends to \(+\infty\) as \(x\to 0\) or
\(x\to1\).  
Hence if \(g(r)=1\) has only one solution,
that solution must occur at a global minimum of \(g\).
Consequently \(g'(x)=0\) at \(x=\tfrac12\).

\[

\[
-\,4X+\frac{4Y}{3\sqrt3}=0
