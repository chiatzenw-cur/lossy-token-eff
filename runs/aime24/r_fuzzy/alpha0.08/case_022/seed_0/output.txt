A segment of the **dodecagon** lies on a side or a diagonal, so every
side of a rectangle we are looking for is a chord joining two of the
vertices of the dodecagon.

---------------------------------------------------------------------

### 1.  Perpendicular chords

In a circle a quadrilateral is a rectangle iff each angle is a right
angle.  
For a cyclic quadrilateral this is equivalent to each pair of opposite
vertices being the endpoints of a diameter.  
Hence in a regular dodecagon a rectangle whose sides are chords must have
**two pairs of opposite vertices**; those pairs are diameters of the
circumcircle.  

The dodecagon has exactly \(6\) pairs of diametrically opposite vertices:

\[
(0,6),\;(1,7),\;(2,8),\;(3,9),\;(4,10),\;(5,11).
\]

---------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2.  Any two such pairs form a rectangle

Choose any two of these six diametric pairs.
Let the chosen pair’s vertices be \(A\) and \(A+6\), and let the second
pair’s vertices be \(B\) and \(B+6\), where the indices are taken
modulo \(12\).

The four points \(A,B,A+6,B+6\) lie on the circle and are connected
([\(A,B\)], \([B,B+6]\), \([B+6,A+6]\), \([A+6,A]\)).
Each of the four points sees the opposite one with a straight line
through the centre, thus its opposite angles are right angles.
Consequently the quadrilateral is a rectangle.

Conversely, any rectangle whose sides are sides/diagonals of the
dodecagon must have this form, because its opposite vertices must be
diametrically opposite.  

Hence every rectangle corresponds to a choice of **exactly two distinct
diametric pairs**.

---------------------------------------------------------------------

### 3.  Counting

We just need to choose two of the six diametric pairs:
\[
\binom{6}{2}=15 .
\]

Therefore, the number of rectangles that can be drawn inside the given
regular dodecagon, with each side lying on either a side or a
diagonal, is

\[
\boxed{15}.
