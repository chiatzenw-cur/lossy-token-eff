### 1. Count the draws that give at least a prize  
A prize is won if \(|A\cap B|\ge 2\).

For a fixed \(k\) (number of matches) we have  
\[
\#\{\text{draw}(B): |A\cap B|=k\}
    = \binom{4}{k}\binom{6}{4-k}
\]
because we must choose \(k\) of Jen’s 4 numbers and the remaining \(4-k\) of the 6 numbers not on her ticket.

Compute for \(k=2,3,4\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
k=2:&\quad\binom{4}{2}\binom{6}{2}=6\cdot15=90\\[2pt]
k=3:&\quad\binom{4}{3}\binom{6}{1}=4\cdot6=24\\[2pt]
k=4:&\quad\binom{4}{4}\binom{6}{0}=1\cdot1=1 .
\end{aligned}
\]

So total draws giving a prize:
\[
90+24+1=115 .
\]

---

### 2. Count the draws that give a grand prize  
A grand prize requires all four numbers to match, i.e. \(k=4\).  
Only one such draw exists (Jen’s exact set).

---

### 3. Probability of grand prize given a prize  
The conditional probability is

\[
P(\text{grand} \mid \text{prize})
= \frac{ \frac{1}{\binom{10}{4}} }}{ \frac{115}{\binom{10}{4}} }
= \frac{1}{115}.
\]

Thus the probability can be expressed as \(\frac{m}{n}\) with
\(m=1,n=115\) (already in lowest terms).
