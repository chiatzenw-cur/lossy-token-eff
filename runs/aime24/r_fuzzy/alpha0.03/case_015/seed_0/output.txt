\[
A=(x_1,y_1),\qquad B=(x_2,y_2)
\]

be two adjacent vertices of the rhombus whose diagonals meet at the
origin.  
Since the diagonals bisect one another, the opposite vertices are

\[
C=(-x_i,-y_i),\qquad D=(-x_2,-y_2).
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 1.  Condition for a rhombus

All vertices lie on  

\[
\frac{x^2}{20}-\frac{y^2}{24}=1, \qquad\text{and}\qquad AB=BC=CD=DA .
\]

The condition \(AB=BC\) is equivalent to

\[
(x_1-x_2)^2+(y_1-y_2)^2=(x_1+x_2)^2+(y_1+y_2)^2 .
\]

After simplifying we obtain  

\[
x_1x_2+y_1y_2=0.
\]

Hence the two radius‑vectors \(\overrightarrow{OA}\) and \(\overrightarrow{OB}\)
are orthogonal:

\[
A\cdot B=0. \tag{1}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.  Existence of a point \(A\) orthogonal to a given \(B\)

Let  

\[
B=(p,q), \qquad  \frac{p^{2}}{20}-\frac{q^{2}}{24}=1. \tag{2}
\]

The line through the origin perpendicular to \(B\) has equation
\(y=-\frac{p}{q}x\) (for \(q\neq0\)).  
It intersects the hyperbola iff the intersection conditions give
a real \(x\), i.e. iff  

\[
\frac1{20}-\frac{p^{2}}{24q^{2}}>0
   \;\Longleftrightarrow\;\frac{p^{2}}{q^{2}}<\frac65 ,
   \;\; \text{or}\;\; \frac{q^{2}}{p^{2}}>\frac56 . \tag{3}
\]

Thus a vertex \(B\) can serve as a rhombus vertex provided that
(2) and (3) hold.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 3.  Minimising \(BD^{2}\)

Because \(D=-B\),

\[
BD^{2}=|(B)-(-B)|^{2} = |2B|^{2}=4(p^{2}+q^{2}). \tag{4}
\]

Let  

\[
p^{2}=t,\quad q^{2}=u .
\]

From (2):

\[
t =20+\frac{5}{6}u. \tag{5}
\]

From (3):

\[
\frac{u}{t}>\frac56 \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;
u>\frac56 t=\frac56\!\left(20+\frac{5}{6}u\right)
       \;\Longrightarrow\; u> \frac{600}{11}. \tag{6}
\]

The sum \(t+u\) (and thus \(BD^{2}\)) becomes

\[
t+u = 20+\frac{11}{6}u. \tag{7}
\]

Equation (7) is strictly increasing in \(u\); therefore its minimum is
obtained when \(u\) approaches the lower bound of (6):

\[
u_{\min}\;=\;\frac{600}{11},
\qquad
t_{\min}\;=\;20+\frac56\,u_{\min}
          \;=\;20+\frac{500}{11}
          \;=\;\frac{720}{11}.
\]

Hence

\[
(t+u)_{\min}=20+\frac{11}{6}\!\cdot\!\frac{600}{11}
              =20+100=120 .
\]

Finally, using (4),

\[
BD^{2}_{\min}=4(t+u)_{\min}=4\cdot120=480.
\]

The inequality (3) is strict, so \(u\) cannot equal \(600/11\); the
value \(BD^{2}=480\) is unattainable but can be approached arbitrarily
close from above.  Therefore

\[
\boxed{480}
\]

is the greatest real number that is less than \(BD^{2}\) for every such
rhombus.
