### 1.  The line \(D,E,C,F\)

Since \(D\) and \(E\) are both on the bottom side of the first rectangle,
they have the same \(y\)-coordinate \(16\).
The same is true for \(C\) and \(F\).
Hence all four points lie on the horizontal line \(y=16\).

Thus

\[
E=(p,16),\qquad F=(p+184,16),\qquad D=(0,16),\qquad C=(107,16)
\]

and the unknown \(p\) is the \(x\)-coordinate of \(E\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.  The circle through \(A,D,H,G\)

The quadrilateral \(A,D,H,G\) is concyclic.
Because \(A\) and \(D\) are vertically aligned, the perpendicular bisector
of \(AD\) is the horizontal line \(y=8\); hence the centre of the circle is
\((h,8)\) for some real \(h\).

Distances to the centre must be equal:

\[
(h^{2}+8^{2})=\bigl(p-h\bigr)^{2}+25^{2}
          =\bigl(p+184-h\bigr)^{2}+25^{2}
\tag{1}
\]

Subtracting the last two equalities gives

\[
(p-h)^{2}=(p+184-h)^{2}\Longrightarrow p-\,h=-92
\]
so \(h=p+92\).

Insert this into \(h^{2}+8^{2}\) and into one of the other expressions:

\[
h^{2}+64=(p-h)^{2}+625
      =( -92)^{2}+625
      =8464+625
      =9089 .
\]

Thus

\[
h^{2}=9089-64=9025 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad h=\pm95 .
\]

Consequently

\[
p=h-92=\begin{cases}
95-92=3,\\[2mm]
-95-92=-187 .
\end{cases}
\]
