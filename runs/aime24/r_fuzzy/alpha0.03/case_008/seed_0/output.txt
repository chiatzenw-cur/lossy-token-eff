### 1.  Translate the logarithmic equations into algebraic equalities  

\[
\log_x y^x = 10 \iff y^x = x^{10}\tag{1}
\]
\[
\log_y x^{4y} = 10 \iff x^{4y} = y^{10}\tag{2}
\]

---

### 2.  Take natural logarithms  

From (1):  

\[
x\ln y =10 \ln x \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{\ln y}{\ln x}=\frac{10}{x}\tag{3}
\]

From (2):  

\[
4y\ln x =10 \ln y \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{\ln y}{\ln x}=\frac{4y}{10}\;=\;\frac{2y}{5}\tag{4}
\]

---

### 3.  Equate the two expressions for \(\dfrac{\ln y}{\ln x}\)

\[
\frac{10}{x}=\frac{2y}{5}\;\Longrightarrow\;
5 \cdot 10 = 2xy \;\Longrightarrow\; xy = 25.
\]

Thus the product of every pair \((x,y)\) that satisfies the given conditions equals \(25\).
