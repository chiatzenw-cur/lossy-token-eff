\text{Let } a,b,c>0\text{ be the side lengths of a rectangular box.}
\]

\[
\begin{aligned}
2(ab+bc+ca)&=54\quad\Longrightarrow\quad ab+bc+ca=27,\\
abc&=23 .
\end{aligned}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 1.  Sphere that contains a box

The smallest sphere that can contain a rectangular box is the one that
circumscribes the box.  
Its radius is half the space diagonal

\[
r=\frac12\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad r^2=\frac14\,(a^2+b^2+c^2).
\]

For the whole set \(\mathcal B\) we need the radius of a sphere that can
contain **every** box in the set, so

\[
r^2=\frac14\max_{\mathcal B}\!(a^2+b^2+c^2).
\]

Thus we must **maximize** \(a^2+b^2+c^2\) under the two constraints
above.

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2.  Using symmetric sums

Let  

\[
\begin{aligned}
s_1&=a+b+c,\\
s_2&=ab+bc+ca=27,\\
s_3&=abc=23 .
\end{aligned}
\]

Then

\[
a^2+b^2+c^2=s_1^2-2s_2
          =s_1^2-54 .
\]

Hence maximizing \(a^2+b^2+c^2\) is equivalent to maximizing \(s_1\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 3.  Maximal sum of the sides  
Set two sides equal – this gives the extremal boxes.

Let \(a=b=x\), \(c=y\).  
From the volume and surface‑area conditions

\[
\begin{cases}
x^2y &=23,\\
x^2+2xy &=27 .
\end{cases}
\]

Eliminate \(y\): \(y=\dfrac{23}{x^{2}}\).  
Insert into the second equation:

\[
x^2+\frac{46}{x}=27
\]
\[
\Longrightarrow\;x^3-27x+46=0
\]

The cubic factors:

\[
(x-2)(x^2+2x-23)=0
\]

\[
x=2\quad\text{or}\quad x=-1+2\sqrt6 .
\]

#### •  The solution \(x=2\)

\[
y=\frac{23}{4}=5.75,\qquad
s_1=2+2+5.75=9.75 .
\]

#### •  The solution \(x=-1+2\sqrt6\)

\[
y=\frac{23}{\,25-4\sqrt6\,}\approx1.514,\qquad
s_1\approx9.31 .
\]

Thus the **largest** possible \(s_1\) is \(9.75\), obtained for the
box with side lengths \((2,\,2,\,5.75)\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 4.  Compute \(r^2\)

The maximal sum of the squares of the sides is

\[
a^2+b^2+c^2=2^2+2^2+5.75^2
           =4+4+33.0625
           =\frac{657}{16}.
\]

Therefore

\[
r^2=\frac14\left(\frac{657}{16}\right)
    =\frac{657}{64}.
\]

The fraction \(\dfrac{657}{64}\) is already in lowest common terms
(\(\gcd(657,64)=1\)).  
Let

\[
r^2=\frac{p}{q}\qquad\text{with}\qquad p=657,\;q=64 .
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 5.  Final answer

\[
p+q = 657+64 = \boxed{721}.
\]
