### 1.  Exactly four direction changes

Changing direction four times means the path consists of  
\(4+1+1 = 5\) monotone segments, alternating between horizontal and vertical moves.

There are two possible patterns of directions:

* \(R\,U\,R\,U\,R\) (start with \(R\))
* \(U\,R\,U\,R\,U\) (start with \(U\))

In each pattern the 5 segments have their own lengths that sum to \(16\).  
Because 8 of the moves are \(R\) and 8 are \(U\), the segments with the same direction must
together contain exactly 8 moves.

---

### 2.  Counting the lengths

**Pattern \(R\,U\,R\,U\,R\).**  

* 3 \(R\)-segments: positive integers \(r_1,r_2,r_3\) with
  \[
  r_1+r_2+r_3 = 8.
  \]
  Number of ordered solutions = \(\binom{8-1}{3-1}=\binom{7,2}=21\).

* 2 \(U\)-segments: positive integers \(u_1,u_2\) with
  \[
  u_1+u_2 = 8,
  \]
  giving \(\binom{8-1}{2-1}=\binom{7,1}=7\) possibilities.

Total for this pattern: \(21\times7=147\).

**Pattern \(U\,R\,U\,R\,U\).**  
Exactly the same calculation (now 3 \(U\)-segments and 2 \(R\)-segments) gives another \(147\).

---

### 3.  Total number of paths

\[
147 + 147 = 294 .
\]

Therefore, the number of distinct paths of length \(16\) that change direction exactly four times is  

\[
\boxed{294}.
