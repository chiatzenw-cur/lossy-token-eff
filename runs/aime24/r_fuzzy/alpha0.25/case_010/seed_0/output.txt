For a fixed set \(J\) of the \(4\) distinct numbers chosen by Jen, the number of drawn sets that contain exactly \(k\) of her numbers is

\[
\binom{JK}{k}\binom{10-4}{4-k}= \binom{4}{k}\binom{6}{4-k}.
\]

The probability of exactly \(k\) matches is this number divided by the total number of
possible draws \(\binom{10}{4}=210\).

* \(k=2:\) \(\binom{4}{2}\binom{6}{2}=6\cdot15=90\)
* \(k=3:\) \(\binom{4}{3}\binom{6}{1}=4\cdot 6=24\)
* \(k=4:\) \(\binom{4}{4}\binom{6}{0}=1\)

So the probability that Jen wins a prize (at least two matches) is

\[
P(\text{prize})=\frac{90+24+1}{210}=\frac{115}{210}=\frac{23}{42}.
\]

The probability of the grand prize (all four with matches) is

\[
P(\text{grand prize})=\frac{1}{210}.
\]

Hence

\[
P(\text{grand prize}\mid\text{prize})=
\frac{P(\text{grand prize})}{P(\text{prize})}
=\frac{1/210}{115/210}= \frac{1}{115}.
\]

This is already in lowest terms.

Therefore \(m=1\) and \(n=115\), giving

\[
m+n = \$\boxed{116}.   \]
