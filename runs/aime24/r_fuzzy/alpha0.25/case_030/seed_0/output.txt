Let the \(5\times5\) board be identified by its rows \(R_1,\dots ,R_5\) and columns  
\(C_1,\dots ,C_5\).  
A *configuration* of chips is the arrangement of white or black chips in some
of the cells.

We have to count the number of configurations that satisfy

*   at most one piece in a cell,
*   in every row all pieces containing a chip have the same colour.
    The same holds for every column,
*   it cannot be made *smaller* by adding something at a free cell
    (i.e. no free cell is addable without violating one of the two
    previous linear conditions).

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 1.  The structure of a maximal arrangement

Assume a configuration does *not* fill the entire board.  
Suppose there is a row \(R\) that is not occupied by a chip,
and a column \(C\) that does not contain a chip.
The cell \((R,C)\) is empty.  
The configuration does **not** violate condition 2, hence the row
\(R\) and column \(C\) have no restriction on colours.

Place an additional chip of **any** colour on \((R,C)\).
After row \(R\) and column \(C\) have one chip, condition 2 is still
satisfied: a row (or column) that contains a single chip is, by
definition, monochrome.  
Thus a configuration that is not whole–board can be expanded
while keeping conditions 1,2 satisfied – contradicting the
maximality requirement.

Hence a maximal arrangement must occupy *every* cell of a
\(25\)‑cell board.  Hence all \(25\) cells are filled by a
chip colour.

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2.  Consistency of colours for a fully filled board

Let \(W\) be the set of white cells that must appear in the board.
If the board contains a white cell in column \(C_j\) and a black cell
in the same column \(C_j\), then column \(j\) would contain two
different colours – impossible by condition&nbsp;2.  
Hence **every column contains chips of only one colour**.

The same reasoning with the rows gives the same statement:
every row consists entirely of chips of one colour.  
If some row were white, every column that has a chip in that row
would need to be black (because all cells in that row must also be black).
But the column already contains a white chip, which can not be both
white and black – contradiction.  Thus the body of every row having a
chip must be of the exact same colour as the body of every column
having a chip.

Consequently the **only** monochromatic board that fills the whole
\(25\) cells holds **either** all white chips, **or** all black chips.

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 3.  Use of the available chips

The problem supplies 25 indistinguishable white chips and 25
indistinguishable black chips.  
The board to be filled uses exactly 25 chips, one per cell, thus
never exceeds the available supply.  
Both colour‑uniform fillings satisfy both colours‑conditions.

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 4.  Counting the selections

Number of maximal arrangements =
# of colour‑fulfilments that fill every cell
= 2 (all white, all black).
