### 1.  Condition that \(C\) lies on the family only once

Let \(C=(x_c,y_c)\) lie on the line \(AB\).  
Let \((t_0,u_0)\) denote the intercepts of the line \(AB\), so

\[
t_0=\frac12,\qquad u_0=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}.
\]

The point \(C\) occurs on a segment with intercepts \((t,u)\) iff

\[
x_c u + y_c t = t u \qquad\text{and}\qquad t^2+u^2=1.
\]

The set \(\{(t,u):x_c u + y_c t = t u\}\) is a hyperbola in the \((t,u)\)-plane.  
It intersects the circle \(t^2+u^2=1\) at the points of \(\mathcal{F}\) that contain \(C\).

For \(C\) to lie on **exactly one** segment of \(\mathcal{F}\) (namely \(AB\)), the hyperbola must be tangent to the circle at \((t_0,u_0)\) – otherwise there would be two distinct intersection points.

### 2.  Tangency condition

Let  

\[
F_1(t,u)=t^2+u^2-1,\qquad F_2(t,u)=x_c u + y_c t - t u .
\]

Tangency at \((t_0,u_0)\) requires the gradients to be parallel:

\[
(2t_0, 2u_0)=\lambda\,\bigl(y_c-u_0,\;x_c-t_0\bigr)
\]

for some scalar \(\lambda\).
Eliminating \(\lambda\) gives

\[
t_0(x_c-t_0)=u_0(y_c-u_0).
\tag{1}
\]

Together with the equation of the line through \(C\),

\[
x_c u_0 + y_c t_0 = t_0 u_0
\tag{2}
\]

and the circle \(t_0^2+u_0^2=1\),
