### 1.  Convert the logarithm equations to exponent form  

\[
\log_x(y^x)=10\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y^x=x^{10}\qquad\text{(1)}
\]

\[
\log_y(x^{4y})=10\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x^{4y}=y^{10}\qquad\text{(2)}
\]

---

### 2.  Take natural logarithms  

From (1):  

\[
x\ln y = 10\ln x\qquad\text{(A)}
\]

From (2):  

\[
4y\ln x = 10\ln y\qquad\text{(B)}
\]

---

### 3.  Equate the two expressions for \(\ln y\)

From (A): \(\displaystyle \ln y=\frac{10\ln x}{x}\)

From (B): \(\displaystyle \ln y=\frac{4y}{10}\ln x=\frac{2y}{5}\ln x\)

Because \(x>1\), \(\ln x>0\), so we may cancel \(\ln x\):

\[
\frac{10}{x}=\frac{2y}{5}
\]

Multiply both sides by \(5x\):

\[
50 = 2xy\quad\Longrightarrow\quad xy = 25 .
\]

---

### 4.  No further information needed  

The two original equations are satisfied for some \(x>1,\;y>1\) with \(xy=25\).
