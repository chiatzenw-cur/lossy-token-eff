(a,b,c)\in \mathbb Z_{\ge 0},\qquad a+b+c=300,\qquad

### 1.  Re‑write the equation

Because \(b+c=300-a,\; a+b=300-c,\; a+c=300-b\),

\[
a^{2}b+a^{2}c+b^{2}a+b^{2}c+c^{2}a+c^{2}b=
a^{2}(300-a)+b^{2}(300-b)+c^{2}(300-c).
\]

Let  

\[
S_{1}=a+b+c=300,\qquad 
S_{2}=a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2},\qquad
S_{3}=a^{3}+b^{3}+c^{3}.
\]

Using \(S_{1}=300\),

\[
a^{2}(300-a)+\dots = 300S_{2}-S_{3}=6\,000\,000. \tag{1}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.  Express \(S_{2}\) and \(S_{3}\) via the elementary symmetric sums

\[
\begin{aligned}
S_{2}
&=S_{1}^{2}-2(ab+bc+ca)=300^{2}-2P,\\
S_{3}
&=S_{1}^{3}-3S_{1}P+3Q
    =27\,000\,000-900P+3Q,
\end{aligned}
\]

where 

\[
P=ab+bc+ca,\qquad Q=abc .
\]

Insert them into (1):

\[
300(90\,000-2P)-(27\,000\,000-900P+3Q)=6\,000\,000 .
\]

After simplification

\[
300P-3Q=6\,000\,000 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 
100P-Q=2\,000\,000. \tag{2}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 3.  Express the third symmetric sum in terms of \(a\)

Fix \(a\) (\(0\le a\le 300\)), let \(b+c=300-a=:s\) and \(p=bc\).

Then  

\[
P=ab+ac+bc=a\,s+p,\qquad Q=a\,p .
\]

Using (2),

\[
a\,p=100(a\,s+p)-2\,000\,000
\;\Longrightarrow\;
p(a-100)=100a\,s-2\,000\,000 .
\]

With \(s=300-a\) the right–hand side factorises:

\[
\begin{aligned}
100a(300-a)-2\,000\,000
&=30\,000a-100a^{2}-2\,000\,000\\
&=(a-100)(20\,000-100a).
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence for \(a\neq 100\),

\[
p=20\,000-100a . \tag{3}
\]

(The same formula also holds for \(a=100\) because then the left side is
\(0\) and the right hand side is \(0\).)

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 4.  Recover \(b\) and \(c\)

We know \(s=b+c=300-a\) and \(p=bc=20\,000-100a\).
The quadratic equation

\[
x^{2}-s\,x+p=0
\]

has discriminant  

\[
\Delta=s^{2}-4p=(300-a)^{2}-4(20\,000-100a)=a^{2}-200a+10\,000
=(a-100)^{2}.
\]

Thus \(\Delta\) is a perfect square for every integer \(a\); consequently
\(b,c\) are integers.  In fact

\[
b,c=\frac{\,s\pm\sqrt{\Delta}\,}{2}
      =\frac{300-a\pm|a-100|}{2}.
\]

Two possibilities occur:

* \(a\le 100\): then \(|a-100|=100-a\),
  \[
  b=200-a,\qquad c=100 .
  \]
* \(a\ge 100\): then \(|a-100|=a-100\),
  \[
  b=100,\qquad c=200-a .
  \]

Thus for every integer \(a\) with \(0\le a\le 200\) we obtain

\[
(a,b,c)=(a,\,100,\,200-a),
\]

and every such triple satisfies the original equation.  
Conversely, if \((a,b,c)\) satisfies the conditions, this form is
forced: putting \(a\) as the first coordinate gives the same relation.

### 5.  Counting the ordered triples

All unordered triples in the form \((a,100,200-a)\) are distinct for
different \(a\) except when \(a=100\) (which gives the triple
\((100,100,100)\)).
For \(a\neq 100\) all three numbers are distinct, so each such triple
has \(6\) permutations.  
For \(a=100\) the triple is identical in all permutations, giving
\(1\) ordered triple.

There are \(201\) values of \(a\) (\(0,1,2,\dots ,200\)).  
Hence

\[
\text{number of ordered triples}
=6\,(201-1)+1
=1206-5
=1201 .
\]
