### 1.  Count the draws that give a grand prize

### 2.  Count the draws that give at least a prize  
Jen wins a prize whenever the draw contains at least two of her numbers.  
We count the draws that contain exactly \(k\) of Jen’s \(4\) chosen numbers, for \(k=2,3,4\).

\[
\begin{aligned}
k=2 &: \binom{4}{2}\binom{6}{2}=6\cdot 15=90,\\[4pt]
k=3 &: \binom{4}{3}\binom{6}{1}=4\cdot 6=24,\\[4pt]
k=4 &: \binom{4}{4}\binom{6}{0}=1\cdot 1=1.
\end{aligned}
\]

Total draws that give a prize:

\[
N_{\text{prize}} = 90+24+1 = 115 .
\]

### 3.  Compute the conditional probability  
The probability of a grand prize (over all \( \binom{10}{4}=210 \) possible draws) is  

\[
P(\text{grand}) = \frac{1}{210}.
\]

\[
P(\text{prize}) = \frac{115}{210}.
\]

\[
P(\text{grand}\mid \text{prize}) = 
\frac{P(\text{grand})}{P(\text{prize})}
