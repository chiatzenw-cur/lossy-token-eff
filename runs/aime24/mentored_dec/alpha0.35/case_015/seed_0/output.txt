Since the diagonals intersect at the origin, the centre of the rhombus is \((0,0)\).  
Hence  

\[
C=-A,\qquad D=-B .
\]

Therefore \(BD\) is the segment joining \(B\) to \(-B\), so  

\[
BD=2|B| \quad\Longrightarrow\quad BD^{2}=4\,(x_{B}^{2}+y_{B}^{2}).
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 1.  Condition for a rhombus

All sides are equal: \(AB=BC\).
With \(A=(x_A,y_A),\,B=(x_B,y_B)\),

\[
|A-B|^{2}=|A+B|^{2}
\]

gives

\[
(x_Ax_B+y_Ay_B)=0.
\tag{1}
\]

Thus \(A\) and \(B\) are perpendicular vectors.

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2.  Coordinates of the vertices

Let \(B=(x,y)\) satisfy the hyperbola  

\[
\frac{x^{2}}{20}-\frac{y^{2}}{24}=1,
\qquad   x\neq 0 ,\; y\neq 0 .
\tag{2}
\]

From (1) a suitable \(A\) must satisfy

\[
\begin{cases}
u\,x+v\,y=0 ,\\[2mm]
\dfrac{u^{2}}{20}-\dfrac{v^{2}}{24}=1 ,
\end{cases}
\]

where \(A=(u,v)\).

Solve \(v=-\dfrac{ux}{y}\) from the first equation and substitute:

\[
u^{2}\!\left(\frac1{20}-\frac{x^{2}}{24y^{2}}\right)=1 .
\]

Thus a real \(u\) exists iff

\[
\frac1{20}-\frac{x^{2}}{24y^{2}}>0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
6y^{2}>5x^{2}.
\tag{3}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 3.  Relation between \(x\) and \(y\)

From (2),

\[
x^{2}=20+ \frac{5}{6}y^{2}.
\]

Insert this into (3):

\[
6y^{2}>5\!\left(20+\frac{5}{6}y^{2}\right)
\;\Longrightarrow\;
11y^{2}>600
\;\Longrightarrow\;
y^{2}>\frac{600}{11}.
\tag{4}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 4.  Minimum of \(BD^{2}\)

Using \(BD^{2}=4(x^{2}+y^{2})\) and (2),

\[
BD^{2}=4\!\left(20+ \frac{5}{6}y^{2}+y^{2}\right)
      =80+\frac{22}{3}y^{2}.
\]

Because \(y^{2}\) is bounded below by \(\dfrac{600}{11}\), the smallest possible value of \(BD^{2}\) is attained when \(y^{2}\) approaches this lower bound:

\[
BD^{2}_{\min}=80+\frac{22}{3}\!\left(\frac{600}{11}\right)
               =80+\frac{22\cdot 600}{33}
               =80+400=480.
\]

The inequality (4) is strict, so \(BD^{2}\) is actually **greater than** \(480\) for every admissible rhombus, but values can be made arbitrarily close to \(480\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 5.  Conclusion

The greatest real number that is less than \(BD^{2}\) for all such rhombi is  

\[
