### 1.  The two 3‑digit row numbers

\[
R_{1}=100a+10b+c,\qquad R_{2}=100d+10e+f .
\]

The condition  
\[
R_{1}+R_{2}=999\tag{1}
\]
is equivalent to  
\[
100(a+d)+10(b+e)+(c+f)=999 .
\]

Let  

\[
s_{1}=a+d ,\qquad s_{2}=b+e ,\qquad s_{3}=c+f ,
\qquad 0\le s_{i}\le 18 .
\]
Then (1) gives  

\[
100s_{1}+10s_{2}+s_{3}=999 .
\]

Because \(s_{1}\ge 0\) and \(s_{1}\le 18\), the term \(100s_{1}\) must be \(900\).
Hence  

\[
s_{1}=9,\quad
10s_{2}+s_{3}=99 .
\]

Likewise \(s_{2}=9,\; s_{3}=9\).  
Thus

\[
a+d=9,\quad b+e=9,\quad c+f=9. \tag{2}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.  The three 2‑digit column numbers

\[
C_{1}=10a+d,\quad C_{2}=10b+e,\quad C_{3}=10c+f.
\]

Their sum is  

\[
C_{1}+C_{2}+C_{3}=99. \tag{3}
\]

Using (2) for each column,

\[
\begin{aligned}
10a+d &=10a+(9-a)=9a+9 ,\\
10b+e &=9b+9 ,\\
10c+f &=9c+9 .
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence

\[
C_{1}+C_{2}+C_{3}=9(a+b+c)+27 .
\]

From (3) we obtain

\[
9(a+b+c)+27=99\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a+b+c=8. \tag{4}
\]

### 3.  Counting the solutions

The complementary digits are forced by (2):

\[
d=9-a,\qquad e=9-b,\qquad f=9-c .
\]

So a choice of \(a,b,c\) uniquely determines the entire grid.
