--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 1. The length of the list must be even

If the list had an odd length \(n\), its median would be the middle value
\(a_{(n+1)/2}\,,\) a value that is already in the list.  
Because the problem states the median is an integer **not** in the list,
\(n\) cannot be odd. Therefore \(n\) is even.

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2. The mode \(9\) must appear at least twice

If \(9\) were the only value that appears more than once, \(\#9\ge 2\).
If \(\#9=1\) then the list would have only one repeated value; thus no
unique mode would exist.  
Hence let \(k = \#9\ge 2\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 3. Possibilities for the number of 9’s

Let the total number of other elements be \(m=n-k\).  
The remaining sum is

\[
S=30-9k .
\]

We have to choose \(m\) positive integers (none of them can be \(9\)
and no other number can repeat more than \(k-1\) times, otherwise 9
would not be the unique mode).  
Also, a **unique** mode means the other numbers must each appear at most
once. Thus all \(m\) other numbers must be distinct.

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 4. Trying \(k=3\)

If \(k=3\) then \(9k=27\) and the remaining sum is \(3\).
With \(m=3\) distinct positive integers summing to 3 we would need
\(1+2+3=6\), impossible.  

Hence \(k\neq 3\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 5. Considering \(k=4\) and larger

For \(k=4\) we need \(9\cdot4=36>30\), impossible.  
Any larger \(k\) would also exceed the total sum.

Thus the only remaining possibility is  

\[
k=2\;\;(\text{two 9’s}) .
\]

### 6. Determining the remaining \(m\)

With \(m=2\) the two numbers must be distinct and summing to 12.
