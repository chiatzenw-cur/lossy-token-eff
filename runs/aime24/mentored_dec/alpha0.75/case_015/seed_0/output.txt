\[
A=(x_1,y_1),\quad B=(x_2,y_2),\qquad C=(-x_1,-y_1),\quad D=(-x_2,-y_2)
\]

be the vertices of the rhombus whose diagonals intersect at the origin.
The hyperbola condition gives  

\[
\frac{x_1^2}{20}-\frac{y_1^2}{24}=1,\qquad 
\frac{x_2^2}{20}-\frac{y_2^2}{24}=1 .
\]

The side lengths of the rhombus are equal, so

\[
\begin{aligned}
AB^2 &= (x_1-x_2)^2+(y_1-y_2)^2,\\
BC^2 &= (x_2+x_1)^2+(y_2+y_1)^2 .
\end{aligned}
\]

Setting \(AB^2 = BC^2\) gives  

\[
x_1x_2 + y_1y_2 = 0 \quad\text{(the dot product of the position vectors is zero)}. \tag{1}
\]

---

### Condition on the existence of an \(A\) for a given \(B\)

Equation (1) means that \(A\) lies on the perpendicular line to \(B\):

\[
x_1 x_2 + y_1 y_2 =0 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; x_1 = -\frac{y_2}{x_2} y_1 .
\]

Substituting in the hyperbola equation for \(A\),

\[
\frac{(-\tfrac{y_2}{x_2}y)^2}{20}-\frac{y^2}{24}=1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
y^2\!\left(\frac{y_2^2}{20x_2^2}-\frac1{24}\right)=1 .
\]

Thus a real solution exists iff

\[
\frac{y_2^2}{20x_2^2}-\frac1{24} \;>\;0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
y_2^2>\frac{5}{6}x_2^2 .
\tag{2}
\]

---

### Expressing \(y_2^2\) in terms of \(x_2^2\)

Let  

\[
w = x_2^2 .
\]

Using the hyperbola for \(B\),

\[
\frac{w}{20}-\frac{y_2^2}{24}=1
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
y_2^2 =  \frac{6w-120}{5} .
\]

Substituting this into the inequality (2):

\[
\frac{6w-120}{5}>\frac{5}{6}w
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
6w-120 >\frac{25}{6}w
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
11w>720
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
w>\frac{720}{11}.
\]

Hence  

\[
x_2^2 \;>\; \frac{720}{11}\,, \qquad
y_2^2 \;=\; \frac{600}{11}\quad\text{(when } w=\frac{720}{11}\text{)} .
\]

Since \(w\) cannot equal the bound, these inequalities are strict and
\(\frac{720}{11}\) is the *infimum* of all possible \(x_2^2\).

---

### Minimising \(BD^2\)

With \(B\) and \(D\) opposite each other,

\[
BD^2=(x_2-(-x_2))^2+(y_2-(-y_2))^2
       =4(x_2^2+y_2^2).
\]

Using the limiting values,

\[
\begin{aligned}
BD^2_{\min} &=
4\bigl(\tfrac{720}{11}+\tfrac{600}{11}\bigr)
      =4\bigl(\tfrac{1320}{11}\bigr)
      =\frac{5280}{11}
      =480 .
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus every possible rhombus has \(BD^2>480\), and \(480\) is the
greatest real number that is less than \(BD^2\) for all such rhombi.
