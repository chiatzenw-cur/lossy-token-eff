Changing direction once means that the same direction steps are grouped into “runs”.  
If a path has **exactly 4 changes** of direction, it has  

\[
\text{changes}=4\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \text{runs}=5
\]

The 5 runs must alternate between \(R\) and \(U\).  
There are two possible alternations:

* \(R,U,R,U,R\) – starts with \(R\) and ends with \(R\) (3 \(R\)-runs, 2 \(U\)-runs).
* \(U,R,U,R,U\) – starts with \(U\) and ends with \(U\) (3 \(U\)-runs, 2 \(R\)-runs).

For each alternation we must allocate the lengths of the runs so that the total number of \(R\) steps is \(8\) and the total number of \(U\) steps is \(8\).

---

### 1.  Paths starting with \(R\)  (\(R,U,R,U,R\))

* 3 \(R\)-runs whose lengths sum to \(8\)  
  \(\#\text{positive solutions}= \binom{8-1}{3-1} = \binom{7}{2}=21.\)

* 2 \(U\)-runs whose lengths sum to \(8\)  
  \(\#\text{positive solutions}= \binom{8-1}{2-1} = \binom{7}{1}=7.\)

Number of paths for this pattern: \(21\times 7 = 147.\)

---

### 2.  Paths starting with \(U\)  (\(U,R,U,R,U\))

Analogously, the 3 \(U\)-runs sum to \(8\) and the 2 \(R\)-runs sum to \(8\), giving the same counts:

\(21\times 7 = 147.\)

---

### Total

\[
147 + 147 = 294.
\]

Hence, there are  

\[
\boxed{294}
\]

paths of length \(16\) from the lower‑left to the upper‑right corner of an \(8\times8\) grid that change direction exactly four times.
