### 1.  Express the product as a resultant  

The polynomial whose roots are the 13th roots of unity is

\[
\Phi_{13}(x)=\frac{x^{13}-1}{x-1}=x^{12}+x^{11}+\cdots +x+1.
\]

For a monic polynomial \(g(x)\) of degree \(n\) and \(f(x)\) of degree \(m\),

\[
\operatorname{Res}(g,f)=\prod_{i=1}^{n} f(\alpha_i)
   =\prod_{j=1}^{m} g(\beta_j),
\]
where \(\alpha_i\) and \(\beta_j\) are the roots of \(g,f\) respectively.

Here \(g(x)=\Phi_{13}(x)\), \(n=12\) and  
\(f(x)=x^2-2x+2\), \(m=2\).  
Hence

\[
P=\operatorname{Res}(\Phi_{13},\,x^2-2x+2)
    =\prod_{j=1}^{2}\Phi_{13}(\beta_j)
\]
with \(\beta_{1,2}\) the roots of \(f(x)\).

---

### 2.  Find the roots of \(f(x)\)

\[
x^2-2x+2=0 \Longrightarrow x=1\pm i.
\]

Let \(\alpha=1+i,\;\beta=1-i\).

---

### 3.  Evaluate \(\Phi_{13}\) at \(1\pm i\)

\[
\Phi_{13}(z)=\frac{z^{13}-1}{z-1}.
\]

\[
\Phi_{13}(\alpha)=\frac{(1+i)^{13}-1}{(1+i)-1}
                 =\frac{(1+i)^{13}-1}{i},
\]
\[
\Phi_{13}(\beta)=\frac{(1-i)^{13}-1}{(1-i)-1}
                 =\frac{(1-i)^{13}-1}{-i}.
\]

Multiply them:

\[
P=\Phi_{13}(\alpha)\Phi_{13}(\beta)
  =\frac{\big((1+i)^{13}-1\big)\big((1-i)^{13}-1\big)}{i(-i)} 
  =\big((1+i)^{13}-1\big)\big((1-i)^{13}-1\big).
\]

---

### 4.  Compute \((1\pm i)^{13}\)

Using the binomial expansion or the polar form \((1+i)=\sqrt2\,e^{i\pi/4}\),

\[
(1+i)^{13}=\big(\sqrt2\big)^{13}e^{i13\pi/4}
          =2^{6.5}e^{i13\pi/4}
          =-64(1+i),
\]
\[
(1-i)^{13}=\overline{(1+i)^{13}}=-64(1-i).
\]

Thus

\[
(1+i)^{13}-1=-64-64i-1=-65-64i,
\]
\[
(1-i)^{13}-1=-64+64i-1=-65+64i.
\]

---

### 5.  Final product

\[
P =(-65-64i)(-65+64i)=65^2+64^2=4225+4096=8321.
\]

\[
8321 \equiv 321 \pmod{1000}.
