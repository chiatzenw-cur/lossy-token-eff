The lottery draws a random \(4\)-subset of \(S\).

For a fixed set, the number of draws that match his set in exactly \(k\) elements is  

\[
\binom{4}{k}\binom{6}{4-k}\qquad (k=0,1,2,3,4)
\]

since he must choose \(k\) of his 4 numbers and \(4-k\) of the remaining \(6\) numbers.

Compute the numbers:

\[
\begin{aligned}
k=0 &: \binom{4}{0}\binom{6}{4}=1\cdot 15=15,\\
k=1 &: \binom{4}{1}\binom{6}{3}=4\cdot 20=80,\\
k=2 &: \binom{4}{2}\binom{6}{2}=6\cdot 15=90,\\
k=3 &: \binom{4}{3}\binom{6}{1}=4\cdot 6=24,\\
k=4 &: \binom{4}{4}\binom{6}{0}=1\cdot 1=1.
\end{aligned}
\]

The total number of possible draws is \(\binom{10}{4}=210\).

---

### Probability of winning a prize

A prize is won when at least \(2\) numbers match.  
So favorable draws:  

\[
90+24+1=115
\]

Thus

\[
P(\text{at least 2 matches})=\frac{115}{210}=\frac{23}{42}.
\]

### Probability of the grand prize

All four numbers match:

\[
P(\text{4 matches})=\frac{1}{210}.
\]

---

### Conditional probability

\[
P(\text{grand prize}\mid \text{prize})
= \frac{P(\text{4 matches})}{P(\text{at least 2 matches})}
= \frac{1/210}{115/210}
= \frac{1}{115}.
\]

The fraction \(\frac{1}{115}\) is already in lowest terms.  
Hence \(m=1,\; n=115\) and

\[
m+n = 1+115 = 116.
\]
