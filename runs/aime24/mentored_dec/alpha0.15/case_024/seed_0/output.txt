--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 1.  Equations from the row numbers

Reading horizontally we get two 3‑digit integers

\[
\overline{abc}=100a+10b+c ,\qquad  
\overline{def}=100d+10e+f .
\]

The sum is \(999\):

\[
100a+10b+c+100d+10e+f = 999 .
\tag{1}
\]

Define  

\[
X=a+d,\qquad Y=b+e,\qquad Z=c+f .
\]

Equation (1) becomes  

\[
100X+10Y+Z = 999,          \tag{2}
\]
with \(0\le X,Y,Z\le 18\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.  Equations from the column numbers

Reading vertically each column gives a two‑digit number

\[
\overline{ad}=10a+d,\quad
\overline{be}=10b+e,\quad
\overline{cf}=10c+f .
\]

Their sum is 99:

\[
10a+d+10b+e+10c+f = 99,
\]
or, in terms of \(t=a+b+c,\; u=d+e+f\),

\[
10t+u = 99.          \tag{3}
\]

Also

\[
t+u = (a+d)+(b+e)+(c+f)=X+Y+Z. \tag{4}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 3.  Determining \(X,Y,Z\)

From (2) we solve for \(X,Y,Z\) with
\(0\le X,Y,Z\le18\).

\(X\) cannot exceed 9 because for \(X=10\) we would have negative
\(Z\).  
For \(X=9\):  

\[
100X = 900,\qquad 999-900=99=10Y+Z.
\]

The only way \(10Y+Z\) can be 99 with \(0\le Y,Z\le18\) is 

\[
Y=9,\; Z=9 .
\]

For \(X\le8\) the remainder \(999-100X\) would be \(>198\) and cannot be
written as \(10Y+Z\) with \(Y,Z\le18\).  
Hence the unique solution is

\[
X=Y=Z=9 .
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 4.  Determining the row sums

With \(X+Y+Z=27\), equations (3)–(4) give

\[
9t + X+Y+Z = 99 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
t = \frac{99-27}{9}=8,
\]
and therefore

\[
u = X+Y+Z-t = 27-8=19 .
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 5.  Counting the digit assignments

The only remaining constraints are

\[
a+d=9,\qquad b+e=9,\qquad c+f=9,\qquad 
a+b+c=8 .
\]

Fix \(a,b,c\) with \(0\le a,b,c\le9\) and \(a+b+c=8\).  
Then automatically

\[
d=9-a,\; e=9-b,\; f=9-c,
\]

and each of \(d,e,f\) is also a digit \(0\!-\!9\).  
Hence the problem reduces to counting the solutions of \(a+b+c=8\) with
non‑negative integers not exceeding 9. Since \(8<9\), the upper bounds
impose no restriction.

Number of solutions:

\[
\binom{8+3-1}{3-1}= \binom{10}{2}=45 .
\]

Each such triple \((a,b,c)\) yields a unique complete grid, giving
45 total arrangements.
