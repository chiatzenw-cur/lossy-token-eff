- \(D\) = residents who own a bag of candy hearts  

Every resident owns a bag of candy hearts, so \(D\) is the whole population of \(900\).

---

### 1.  Numbers of residents by how many of the four items they have  

Let  

\[
\begin{aligned}
x_1 &= \text{# who own exactly 1 of the 4 items}  \\
x_2 &= \text{# who own exactly 2 of the 4 items}  \\
x_3 &= \text{# who own exactly 3 of the 4 items}  \\
x_4 &= \text{# who own all 4 items}  
\end{aligned}
\]

We are given  
\[
x_2 = 437,\qquad x_3 = 234 .
\]

Since every resident owns a bag of candy hearts, “exactly 2 items” means  
heart + exactly one of the other three commodities, “exactly 3 items” means  
heart + exactly two of the other three commodities, etc.

Because the total population is \(900\),

\[
x_1 + x_2 + x_3 + x_4 = 900 .
\]

Hence   

\[
x_1 = 900 - (437+234+x_4)= 229- x_4 .
\]

---

### 2.  Use the given sizes of the sets \(A,B,C\)

The total number of possessions of the ring, golf clubs, and spade is  

\[
|A|+|B|+|C| = 195+367+562 = 1124 .
\]

For a resident, each possession contributes at most one to this sum.  
If a resident has \(k\) of the three items \(A,B,C\), they contribute \(k\) to the sum.

Thus  

\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{Sum of |A|+|B|+|C|} &=
(1)\cdot x_2 + (2)\cdot x_3 + (3)\cdot x_4  \\[4pt]
&= 1(437) + 2(234) + 3x_4  \\[4pt]
&= 437 + 468 + 3x_4 = 905 + 3x_4 .
\end{aligned}
\]

Setting this equal to the known total \(1124\),

\[
905 + 3x_4 = 1124
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
3x_4 = 219
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
x_4 = 73 .
\]

---

### 3.  Verification

The numbers by category become

\[
\begin{aligned}
x_4 &= 73,\\
x_3 &= 234,\\
x_2 &= 437,\\
x_1 &= 229-73 = 156 .
\end{aligned}
\]

\[
x_1+x_2+x_3+x_4 = 156+437+234+73 = 900 .
\]

All values are non‑negative and satisfy the set sizes, confirming the consistency.
