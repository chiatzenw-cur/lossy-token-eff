### 1.  Geometry of the chain of equal circles

Put the triangle in a convenient coordinate system.

\[
B=(0,0),\qquad A=(a,0),\qquad C=(0,b)\qquad (a,b>0)
\]

Thus \(AB=a,\;BC=b\) and \(AC=\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}\).

Let the radius of every circle in the chain be \(r\) and let  
\(P_{1},P_{2},\dots ,P_{n}\) be the centres of the \(n\) circles.
All circles are tangent to the base \(AC\), therefore every centre
lies on the line parallel to \(AC\) at a distance \(r\) from it.
Denote this line by \(\ell\) :

\[
\ell:\; (b)x+(a)y-a b=-r\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}\tag{1}
\]

The first centre \(P_{1}\) is tangent to the side \(AB\) (the \(x\)–axis),  
hence \(P_{1}=(x_{1},r)\) with \(x_{1}\) obtained from (1).  
The last centre \(P_{n}\) is tangent to the side \(BC\) (the \(y\)–axis);  
hence \(P_{n}=(r,y_{n})\) with \(y_{n}\) obtained from (1).

Solving (1) for the stated points gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
x_{1}&=  \frac{ab-r(a+b+\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}})}{b},\\[2mm]
y_{n}&=  \frac{ab-r(a+b+\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}})}{a}.   \tag{2}
\end{aligned}
\]

The distance between the first and the last centres is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{dist}(P_{1}P_{n})
 &=\sqrt{(x_{1}-r)^{2}+(y_{n}-r)^{2}}     \\
 &=\frac{ab-r(a+b+\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}})}{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}}. \tag{3}
\end{aligned}
\]

Because consecutive centres are \(2r\) apart,
\[
\operatorname{dist}(P_{1}P_{n})=(n-1)2r. \tag{4}
\]
