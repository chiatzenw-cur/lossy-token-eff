Let  

\[
p(x)=x^{13}-1, \qquad q(x)=x^{2}-2x+2.
\]

Both polynomials are monic.  
The **resultant** of \(p\) and \(q\) is

\[
R(p,q)=\prod_{p(\alpha)=0}q(\alpha)
      =\prod_{q(\beta)=0}p(\beta).
\]

Hence  

\[
P=R(p,q).
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 1.  The roots of \(q(x)\)

\[
x^{2}-2x+2=0 \Longrightarrow x=1\pm i.
\]

Call these roots \(\beta_1=1+i\) and \(\beta_2=1-i\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.  Evaluate \(p(x)=x^{13}-1\) at the roots of \(q\)

\[
\beta_1=1+i=\sqrt2\,e^{i\pi/4}, \qquad
\beta_2=1-i=\sqrt2\,e^{-i\pi/4}.
\]

Because \((\sqrt2)^ {13}=2^{13/2}=64\sqrt2\),

\[
\beta_1^{13}=64\sqrt2\,e^{\,i\frac{13\pi}{4}}
            =64\sqrt2\,e^{\,i\left(3\pi+\frac{\pi}{4}\right)}
            =-\,64\sqrt2\,e^{\,i\pi/4}
            =-64(1+i).
\]

Thus  

\[
p(\beta_1) =\beta_1^{13}-1 =-64(1+i)-1 =-65-64i.
\]

Similarly,

\[
p(\beta_2)=\beta_2^{13}-1 =-64(1-i)-1 =-65+64i.
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 3.  The resultant

\[
R(p,q)=p(\beta_1)\,p(\beta_2)
