---

### 1.  Rewrite the logarithmic equations

\[
\log_x(y^x)=10 \;\Longrightarrow\; y^x = x^{10}
\]
\[
\log_y(x^{4y})=10 \;\Longrightarrow\; x^{4y} = y^{10}
\]

Take natural logarithms of each:

\[
x\ln y = 10\ln x \tag{1}
\]
\[
4y\ln x = 10\ln y \tag{2}
\]

---

### 2.  Relate the logarithm ratios  

From (1):

\[
\frac{\ln y}{\ln x}=\frac{10}{x} \tag{3}
\]

From (2):

\[
\frac{\ln x}{\ln y}=\frac{10}{4y}= \frac{5}{2y}
\]
Inverting gives

\[
\frac{\ln y}{\ln x}= \frac{2y}{5} \tag{4}
\]

---

### 3.  Equate the two expressions for \(\displaystyle\frac{\ln y}{\ln x}\)

\[
\frac{10}{x}= \frac{2y}{5}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 50 = 2xy \quad\Longrightarrow\quad xy = 25
\]

The product \(xy\) is therefore \(25\).

---

Using \(xy=25\) and \(y=25/x\), the original equations are satisfied by real numbers \(x>1,\; y>1\) (e.g., \(x\approx1.53,\; y\approx16.33\)), confirming the consistency of the solution.
