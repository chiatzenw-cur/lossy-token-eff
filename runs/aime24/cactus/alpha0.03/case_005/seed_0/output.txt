### 2.  When is \(-1\) a fourth power modulo \(p\)?

Let \(g\) be a primitive root of the prime field \(\mathbb F_{p}^{\times}\).
The element \(-1\) is \(g^{(p-1)/2}\).  
We need \(x^4=g^{(p-1)/2}\), i.e. \(g^{4k} = g^{(p-1)/2}\), which is solvable iff

4k \equiv \frac{p-1}{2}\pmod{p-1}.
\]

### 5.  Lifting each base root

For each such \(r_{0}\) write the lift as \(r=r_{0}+17t\; (0\le t\le 16)\).

Using the linear approximation  

\[
f(r)\equiv f(r_{0})+f'(r_{0})\,17t\pmod{289}
\]

and solving \(f(r)\equiv0\pmod{289}\) gives the smallest admissible \(t\).

| \(r_{0}\) | \(f(r_{0})\) | \(f'(r_{0})\) | equation for \(t\) | minimal \(t\) | \(r=r_{0}+17t\) |
|-----------|--------------|---------------|-------------------|---------------|----------------|
| 2         | 17           | 32            | \(17(1+32t)\equiv0\) (mod 289) | 9 | 155 |
| 8         | 51           | 104 (≡ 26)    | \(51+66t\equiv0\) | 16 | 280 |
| 9         | 204          | 401 (≡ 26)    | \(204+42t\equiv0\) | 10 | 179 |
| 15        | 51           | 515 (≡ 106)   | \(51+71t\equiv0\) | 7  | 134 |

Thus the smallest lift is \(r=134\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 6.  Verification

\[
\begin{aligned}
134^4+1 &= 322\,417\,937,\\[1mm]
322\,417\,937\,/\,289 &= 1\,115\,633,
\end{aligned}
\]
so \(289\mid 134^4+1\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 7.  Conclusion

The least prime with the required property is \(p=17\).  
The smallest positive integer \(m\) such that \(m^{4}+1\) is divisible by
\(p^{2}=289\) is

\[
\boxed{134}.
