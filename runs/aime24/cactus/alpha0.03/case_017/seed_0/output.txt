### 3.  Using the \(r\)-\(R\) relation

The well–known identity

\[
r=\!4R\sin \frac{A}{2}\sin \frac{B}{2}\sin \frac{C}{2}
\]

comes to light.  Thus

\[
\sin \frac{A}{2}\sin \frac{B}{2}\sin \frac{C}{2}
       =\frac{r}{4R}= \frac{6}{52}
       =\frac{3}{26}. \tag{4}
\]

Taking (2) for \(\sin u=\sin \frac{A}{2}\) gives

\[
\sin \frac{B}{2}\sin \frac{C}{2}
       =\frac{3/26}{\,3/\sqrt{39}}
       =\frac{\sqrt{39}}{26}. \tag{5}
\]

---

### 4.  Computing \(\sin B\sin C\)

From identities involving half‑angles:

\[
\sin\frac{B}{2}\sin\frac{C}{2}
   =\frac{\cos\frac{B-C}{2}-\cos\frac{B+C}{2}}{2},\qquad
\cos\frac{B}{2}\cos\frac{C}{2}
   =\frac{\cos\frac{B-C}{2}+\cos\frac{B+C}{2}}{2}.
\]

Hence  

\[
\sin B\sin C=
4\sin\frac{B}{2}\sin\frac{C}{2}
      \cos\frac{B}{2}\cos\frac{C}{2}
= \cos ^2\!\frac{B-C}{2}-\cos ^2\!\frac{B+C}{2}. \tag{6}
\]

From (3) and \(\frac{(B+C)}{2}= \frac{180^\circ-A}{2}=90^\circ-\frac{A}{2}\),

\[
\cos \frac{B+C}{2}
= \sin \frac{A}{2}=\frac{3}{\sqrt{39}}.
\]

Using (5) we find

\[
\cos \frac{B-C}{2}
   = \frac{3}{\sqrt{39}}+\frac{\sqrt{39}}{13}
   =\frac{2\sqrt{39}}{13}.
\]

Thus

\[
\cos ^2\!\frac{B-C}{2}
   =\frac{4\cdot39}{169}= \frac{156}{169},
\qquad
\cos ^2\!\frac{B+C}{2}
   =\frac{9}{39}= \frac{3}{13} =\frac{39}{169}.
\]

Insert them into (6):

\[
\sin B\sin C
= \frac{156}{169}-\frac{39}{169}
=\frac{117}{169}. \tag{7}
\]

---

### 5.  Product of the two sides

\(b=CA,\;c=AB\) satisfy

\[
b=2R\sin\frac{B}{1},\qquad
c=2R\sin\frac{C}{1}.
\]

Hence

\[
bc=4R^2\sin B\sin C
   =4(13)^2\left(\frac{117}{169}\right).
\]

The factor \(4(13)^2/169\) simplifies to \(4\).  Therefore

\[
bc=4\cdot117=468.
\]

Since \(bc = AB\cdot AC\), we obtain

\[
\boxed{468}.
