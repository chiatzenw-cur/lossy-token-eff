- \(J\) be the 4‑number set John chooses.  
- \(T\) be the random 4‑number set chosen by the lottery.  

The jackpot \(k=|J\cap T|.\)  
If the jackpot is at least 2 the player has a *prize*, if it is 4 the player wins the *grand prize*.

---

### Counting the ways

For a fixed J, the number of ways that the lottery chooses choosing exactly \(k\) of John’s remaining numbers maximally and assuming \(k\) matches with J is  

\[
\binom{4}{k}\;\binom{6}{4-k}\qquad (k=0,1,2,3,4).
\]

Total number of ways to pick 4 numbers from 10:

\[
\binom{10}{4}=210 .
\]

---

### Probability of winning the grand prize

One of the 210 ways is the same set as J’s set, so  

\[
P(K=4)=\frac{1}{210}.
\]

---

### Probability of winning a prize

At least 2 of the drawn numbers must match J’s choice:

\[
P(K\ge 2)=\frac{\binom{4}{2}\binom{6}{2}+ \binom{4}{3}\binom{6}{1}+ \binom{4}{4}\binom{6}{0}}{\binom{10}{4}}.
\]

\[
\begin{aligned}
P(K\ge 2) &=\frac{6\cdot15+4\cdot6+1}{210}  \\
          &=\frac{90+24+1}{210} = \frac{115}{210}= \frac{23}{42}.
\end{aligned}
\]

---

### Probability of a grand prize given a reward

\[
\begin{aligned}
P(\text{grand}\mid\text{flag}) &=\frac{P(K=4)}{P(K\ge 2)}\\
 &= \frac{1/210}{23/42} \\
 &= \frac{1}{210}\times\frac{42}{23} \\
 &= \frac{42}{4830} \\
 &= \frac{1}{115}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus \( \displaystyle \frac{m}{n} = \frac{1}{115}\).  
The integers \(m\) and \(n\) are relatively prime, so 

\[
m+n = 1+115 = 116.
