\log_{x}\!\left(y^{x}\right)=10,\qquad 
\log_{y}\!\left(x^{4y}\right)=10
\]

First write each equation in ordinary logarithms.

---

### 1.  Convert to natural logarithms  

\[
\log_{x}\!\left(y^{x}\right)=\frac{\ln (y^{x})}{\ln x}=10 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 
\frac{x\ln y}{\ln x}=10 .
\]

Thus

\[
x\ln y = 10 \ln x   \tag{A}
\]

Similarly

\[
\log_{y}\!\left(x^{4y}\right)=\frac{\ln (x^{4y})}{\ln y}=10
\quad\Rightarrow\quad 
\frac{4y\ln x}{\ln y}=10 .
\]

Hence

\[
4y\ln x = 10 \ln y   \tag{B}
\]

---

### 2.  Express \(\ln y\) from each equation  

From (A):

\[
\ln y = \frac{10(\ln x)}{x}. \tag{1}
\]

From (B):

\[
\ln y = \frac{4\,y\,(\ln x)}{10}
      = \frac{2y(\ln x)}{5}. \tag{2}
\]

Because \(x>1\) and \(y>1\), \(\ln x>0\); hence we can cancel \(\ln x\) from (1) and (2).

---

### 3.  Equate the two expressions for \(\ln y\)

\[
\frac{10}{x}
  = \frac{2y}{5}
  \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;
  10 = \frac{2xy}{5}
  \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;
  xy = 25.
\]

Thus the product of the two numbers is \(25\).

\boxed{25}
