The total number of moves needed is  

\[

The path has **exactly four** changes of direction, i.e. we have \(5\) monotone segments.

For this to happen the segments must alternate, so the only possible orderings are

* \(H\;V\;H\;V\;H\)  (3 horizontal segments, 2 vertical segments)  
* \(V\;H\;V\;H\;V\)  (3 vertical segments, 2 horizontal segments)

---

### 1.  Horizontal‑vertical layout \(H\,V\,H\,V\,H\)

*The horizontal segments:* the total horizontal length is \(8\).  
We have to distribute this length among \(3\) contiguous horizontal segments,  
each of which must be at least 1 unit long (since a zero‑length segment would not be a turn).  
The number of ways to do this is

\[
\binom{8-1}{3-1}=\binom{7}{2}=21 .
\]

*The vertical segments:* the total vertical length is \(8\).  
It must be split into \(2\) positive integers.  
The number of ways is

\[
\binom{8-1}{2-1}=\binom{7}{1} = 7 .
\]

Any choice of the horizontal and vertical lengths gives a unique path,
hence the number of paths that start with a horizontal segment is

\[
21 \times 7 = 147 .
\]

---

### 2.  Vertical‑horizontal layout \(V\,H\,V\,H\,V\)

By the same logic we now have

* 3 vertical segments, the_vertical part of 8 units:

\[
\binom{7}{2}=21
\]

* 2 horizontal segments, the thickness part of 8 units:

\[
\binom{7}{1}=7 .
\]

Thus there are \(21 \times 7 = 147\) paths starting with a vertical segment.

---

Adding the two possible starting orientations gives

\[
147+147 = 294 .
