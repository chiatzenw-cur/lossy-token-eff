\[
\log_{x}(y^x)=10 ,\qquad \log_{y}(x^{4y})=10 .
\]

---

### 1.  Convert to ordinary logarithms  

\[
\log_{x}(y^x)=\frac{\ln(y^x)}{\ln x}=10
      \stackrel{\text{exp}}{\Longrightarrow} x\ln y = 10\ln x .
\]

\[
\log_{y}(x^{4y})=\frac{\ln(x^{4y})}{\ln y}=10
      \stackrel{\text{exp}}{\Longrightarrow} 4y\ln x = 10\ln y .
\]

---

### 2.  Express \(x\) and \(y\) in terms of the logarithms  

Define \(L_x=\ln x,\;L_y=\ln y\;(>0)\).

Then  

\[
x\;L_y = 10L_x, \qquad 4y\,L_x = 10L_y .
\]

From the first equation  

\[
x=\frac{10L_x}{L_y}. \tag{1}
\]

From the second equation  

\[
y=\frac{10L_y}{4L_x}. \tag{2}
\]

---

### 3.  Product \(xy\)

\[
xy=\left(\frac{10L_x}{L_y}\right)\left(\frac{10L_y}{4L_x}\right)
   =\frac{10\cdot10}{4}=\frac{100}{4}=25 .
\]

Thus the value of \(xy\) is independent of the particular values of \(x,y\).
