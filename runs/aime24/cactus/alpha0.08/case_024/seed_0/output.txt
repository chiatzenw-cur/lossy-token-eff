### 1.  The condition on the two numbers that read left‑to‑right  

The first row gives the three‑digit number \(100a+10b+c\);  
the second row gives \(100d+10e+f\).  
Their sum must equal \(999\):

\[
(100a+10b+c)+(100d+10e+f)=999 .
\tag{1}
\]

Add the two numbers columnwise.  
Let \(k_1,k_2\) be the carries from the units and the tens column.

\[
\begin{array}{cccc}
\text{units:}& a_3&f:&c+f = 9 +10k_1\\llines
\text{top:}& b+e +k_1 &=9+10k_2\\
\text{top:}& a+d +k_2 &=9
\end{array}
\]

Since each digit is at most \(9\) the sums \(c+f,\;b+e\) are at most \(18\).  
Thus \(k_1=0\) and \(k_2=0\), giving

\[
c+f=9,\qquad b+e=9,\qquad a+d=9. \tag{2}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.  The condition on the three numbers that read top‑to‑bottom  

The three columns form the numbers

\[
10a+d,\qquad 10b+e,\qquad 10c+f .
\]

Their sum must equal \(99\):

\[
(10a+d)+(10b+e)+(10c+f)=99 .
\tag{3}
\]

Using (2) write \(d=9-a,\;e=9-b,\;f=9-c\):

\[
10(a+b+c) + (d+e+f)=99 .
\]

With \(d+e+f = (9-a)+(9-b)+(9-c)=27-(a+b+c)\),

\[
10(a+b+c)+27-(a+b+c)=99 .
\]

Hence  

\[
9(a+b+c)=72 \;\Longrightarrow\; a+b+c=8. \tag{4}
\]

### 3.  Counting the possibilities

\binom{8+3-1}{3-1}= \binom{10}{2}=45 .
