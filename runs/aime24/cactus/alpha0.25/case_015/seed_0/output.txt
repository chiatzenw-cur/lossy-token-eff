The condition that all sides are equal is equivalent to  

\[
AB = BC \Longleftrightarrow x_1x_2+y_1y_2 =0.
\]

Thus the symmetric vectors \( \mathbf{A}=(x_1,y_1)\) and  
\(\mathbf{B}=(x_2,y_2)\) are orthogonal.

### 1.  The hyperbola condition  

Both points must lie on  

\[
\frac{x^2}{20}-\frac{y^2}{24}=1
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
6x^2-5y^2=120.
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.  Existence of a product \(A\) orthogonal to a given \(B\)

Suppose \(B=(x,y)\) with \(y\neq 0\).  
For an orthogonal \(\mathbf{A}=(a,b)\) we have \(ax+by=0\), so \(b\approx
-\frac{ax}{y}\).

Insert this into \(a^2/20-b^2/24=1\):

\[
\frac{a^2}{20}-\frac{a^2x^2}{24y^2}=1,
\]

\[
a^2=\frac{1}{\frac1{20}-\frac{x^2}{24y^2}}\; .
\]

For a real solutions, the denominator must be positive:

\[
\frac1{20}>\frac{x^2}{24y^2}\;\Longrightarrow\;
6y^2>5x^2.
\tag{1}
\]

### 3.  Radial distance from \((x,y)\) must satisfy (1)

From the hyperbola condition \(6x^2-5y^2=120\) we express

\[
y^2=\frac{6x^2-120}{5}.
\]

Insert into (1):

\[
6\!\left(\frac{6x^2-120}{5}\right)>5x^2
  \;\Longrightarrow\; 11x^2>720
  \;\Longrightarrow\; x^2> \frac{720}{11}.
\]

Hence

\[
y^2=\frac{6x^2-120}{5}\; \ge\; \frac{6(720/11)-120}{5}= \frac{720}{11}.
\]

Thus the minimal squared length of the vector \(B\) from the origin is
\[
x^2+y^2=\frac{720}{11}+ \frac{720}{11}=120.
\]

### 4.  Length of the diagonal \(BD\)

The diagonal \(BD\) joins \(B(x,\;y)\) and \(D(-x,\;-y)\).

\[
BD^2=(x-(-x))^2+(y-(-y))^2=(2x)^2+(2y)^2=4(x^2+y^2).
