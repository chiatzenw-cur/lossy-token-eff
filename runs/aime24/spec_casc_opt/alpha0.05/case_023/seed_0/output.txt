### 1.  How many times can \(m\) be?

* If \(m=5\) then \(9m=45>30\).  Impossible.  
* If \(m=4\) then \(9m=36>30\).  Impossible.  
* If \(m=3\) then \(9m=27\) and the remaining sum is \(3\).  
  The four numbers in the list are \(9,9,9,3\).  
* If \(m=2\) the remaining sum is \(12\).  The remaining
  numbers must be distinct (otherwise another number would be
  a mode of frequency \(2\)).  The smallest possible sum of
  \(k\) distinct positive integers is \(1+2+\cdots+ k\).  
  Taking \(k=4\) gives \(1+2+3+6=12\).  The list would be
  \(1,2,3,6,9,9\); here the median is \((3+6)/2=4.5\), not an
  integer.  Hence \(m=2\) fails.

\boxed{\,9,\;9,\;9,\;3\,}.
