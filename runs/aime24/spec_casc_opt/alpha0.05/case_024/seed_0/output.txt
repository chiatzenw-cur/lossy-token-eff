\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
a & b & c\\
d & e & f\\
\end{array}
\end{array}
\]

where \(a,b,c,d,e,f\) are digits \(0\leq\le 9\).

---

### 1.  Sum of the rows

\[
a+b+c = 100a+10b+c,\qquad d+e+f = 100b+10e+f
\]

but the added condition is

\[
(100a+10b+c)+(100d+10e+f)=999 .
\]

---

### 2.  Sum of the columns

Reading top to bottom in each column gives a two‑digit number whose
sum can be written as

\[
(10a+d)+(10b+e)+(10c+f)=99 .
\]

Forming two equations:

\[
\begin{cases}
100a+10b+c+100d+10e+f=999,\\[2mm]
10a+d+10b+e+10c+f=99 .
\end{cases}
\]

---

### 3.  Subtracting the equations

Subtract (2) from (1):

\[
100a+10b+c+100d+10e+f-((10a+d)+(10b+e)+(10c+f))=899 .
\]

Simplifying:

\[
10a+d+10b+e+10c+f = 99 .
\]

From the subtraction we get

\[
10a+d+10b+e+10c+f=99. \tag{1}
\]

The extra two equations involving carries give

\[
a+d=\!9,\qquad b+e=\!9,\qquad c+f=\!9 .
\]

Let \(d=9-a,\; e=9-b,\; f=9-c\).

---

### 4.  Express the column sum as a single digit

Using (1):

\[
10a+d+10b+e+10c+f
 = 10a+(9-a)+10b+(9-b)+10c+(9-c)
 = 9(a+b+c)+27 .
\]

Hence

\[
9(a+b+c)+27=99   \;\Longrightarrow\; a+b+c=8 .
\]

---

### 5.  Count the admissible triples

The solutions of \(a+b+c=8\) with \(a,b,c\) digits \(0\le\le9\) are
non‑negative integer solutions of \(\;a+b+c=8\).
Because \(8 < 9\), the upper bound \(9\) does not restrict any variable.

The number of non‑negative integers solutions to \(a+b+c=8\) is

\[
\binom{8+3-1}{3-1} = \binom{10}{2}=45.
\]

For each such triple \((a,b,c)\) the values \(d=9-a,\; e=9-b,\; f=9-c\)
are uniquely determined and are also valid digits \((0\le d,e,f\le 9)\).

Thus every admissible triple \((a,b,c)\) gives a distinct grid, and all
\(45\) grids satisfy this problem.
