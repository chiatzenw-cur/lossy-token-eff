Hence the vertices are of the form  

\[
A=(x_1,y_1),\qquad B=(x_2,y_2),\qquad C=(-x_1,-y_1),\qquad D=(-x_2,-y_2) .
\]

All four points lie on the hyperbola           
\[
\frac{x^{2}}{20}-\frac{y^{2}}{24}=1 .
\tag{1}

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 1.  Condition for a rhombus

Let  
\[
\vec{AB}=B-A=(x_2-x_1,\; y_2-y_1),\qquad
\vec{BC}=C-B=(-x_1-x_2,\; -y_1-y_2).
\]

The squared side lengths are

\[
\begin{aligned}
|AB|^{2}&=(x_2-x_1)^{2}+(y_2-y_1)^{2},\\
|BC|^{2}&=(x_1+x_2)^{2}+(y_1+y_2)^{2}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For a rhombus to have all sides equal we need \(|AB|^{2}=|BC|^{2}\).

A simple algebraic manipulation gives

\[
|AB|^{2}=|BC|^{2}\iff x_1x_2+y_1y_2=0,
\tag{2}
\]

i.e. the two position vectors \((x_1,y_1)\) and \((x_2,y_2)\) are orthogonal.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.  Parametrisation of the hyperbola

Write the hyperbola in hyperbolic form

\[
x=\sqrt{20}\cosh t,\qquad
y=\sqrt{24}\sinh t ,\qquad t\in\mathbb R .
\]

Let  

\[
(x_1,y_1)=\bigl(\sqrt{20}\cosh a,\;\sqrt{24}\sinh a\bigr),\qquad
(x_2,y_2)=\bigl(\sqrt{20}\cosh b,\;\sqrt{24}\sinh b\bigr).
\]

Condition (2) becomes

\[
20\cosh a\cosh b+24\sinh a\sinh b = 0 .
\]

Using \( \cosh\alpha\cosh\beta
  +\sinh\alpha\sinh\beta =\cosh(\alpha+\beta)\) and  
\( \cosh\alpha\cosh\beta
  -\sinh\alpha\sinh\beta =\cosh(\alpha-\beta)\),

\[
-2\cosh(a+b)+22\cosh(a-b)=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\cosh(a+b)=11\cosh(a-b).
\]

Re‑expressing with hyperbolic tangents yields the much simpler condition

\[
\tanh a\;\tanh b=\frac{5}{6}.                \tag{3}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 3.  Expression for the diagonal \(BD\)

The second diagonal is \(BD\).  
Its vector is simply

\[
\vec{BD}=D-B=(-2x_2,\,-2y_2)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
BD^{2}=4(x_2^{2}+y_2^{2}).
\]

Because \(x_2=\sqrt{20}\cosh b,\;y_2=\sqrt{24}\sinh b\),

\[
x_2^{2}+y_2^{2}=20\cosh^{2}b+24\sinh^{2}b
                =44\cosh^{2}b-24
\]

and therefore

\[
BD^{2}=176\cosh^{2}b-96.          \tag{4}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 4.  Minimising \(BD^{2}\)

From (3) we have \( |\tanh b|>5/6\) (otherwise \(|\tanh a|>1\) which is impossible).  
Thus \(b\) must satisfy

\[
|b|> \operatorname{artanh}\!\left(\frac{5}{6}\right)
= \tfrac12\ln\!\left(\frac{1+5/6}{1-5/6}\right)
= \tfrac12\ln 11 .
\]

Since \(\cosh b\) is an increasing function of \(|b|\), the
smallest possible value of \(\cosh^{2}b\) is reached when
\(|b|=\operatorname{artanh}(5/6)\).  
Using \(\cosh^{2}b=\frac{1}{1-\tanh^{2}b}\),

\[
\cosh^{2}b_{\min}
=\frac{1}{1-(5/6)^{2}}
=\frac{1}{1-\frac{25}{36}}
=\frac{36}{11}.
\]

Insert this into (4):

\[
BD^{2}_{\min}=176\cdot\frac{36}{11}-96

\]

Thus

\[
BD^{2}>480\quad\text{for every admissible rhombus},
\]

and \(480\) is the greatest real number that is less than \(BD^{2}\) for all such rhombi.
