Because the total sum is \(30\),

\[
9m \le 30 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad m \le 3 .
\]

The mode is uniquely \(9\), so no other number may appear the same number of times as \(9\).

---

### 1.  \(m=3\)

Then \(9\cdot 3 = 27\) and the remaining sum is \(3\).  
The other numbers must each occur at most \(2\) times.  
Possible choices are \(\{3\}\) or \(\{1,2\}\).

* \( \{3\}\) gives the list \([3,9,9,9]\) – its median is \(9\), which **does** appear in the list.
* \( \{1,2\}\) gives \([1,2,9,9,9]\) – its median is \(9\), again in the list.

Thus \(m=3\) fails.

---

### 2.  \(m=2\)

Now \(9\cdot 2 = 18\) and the remaining sum is \(12\).  
The other numbers must each occur at most once, so they must be distinct.

Let \(k\) be the number of other numbers.  
The total number of elements is \(n=2+k\).  
Since the median must be an integer not in the list, \(n\) must be even, so \(k\) is even.

---

#### (a)  \(k=2\)

We need two distinct positive integers that sum to \(12\):
\[
\{4,8\},\quad \{5,7\}
\]

* \(\{4,8\}\) gives the list \([4,8,9,9]\).  
  The two middle numbers are \(8\) and \(9\), so the median is \(\frac{8+9}{2}=8.5\) – not an integer.

* \(\{5,7\}\) gives the list \([5,7,9,9]\).  
  The two middle numbers are \(7\) and \(9\), so the median is \(\frac{7+9}{2}=8\).  
  The median \(8\) is a positive integer and does **not** appear in the list.  
  The mode is \(9\) (occurring twice), and it is unique.

Thus \([5,7,9,9]\) satisfies all conditions.

---

#### (b)  \(k=4\)

We need four distinct positive integers summing to \(12\).  
The possibilities are

\[
\{1,2,3,6\},\qquad \{1,2,4,5\}.
\]

In each case the sorted list is

\[
1,2,3,6,9,9 \quad\text{or}\quad 1,2,4,5,9,9 .
\]

The two middle numbers are \(3,6\) or \(4,5\), giving medians  
\(\frac{3+6}{2}=4.5\) and \(\frac{4+5}{2}=4.5\)—neither an integer.
