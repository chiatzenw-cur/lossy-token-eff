\[
\begin{aligned}
N_1 &= 1000(1)+100b+10c+d,\\
N_2 &= 1000a+100(1)+10c+d,\\
N_3 &= 1000a+100b+10(1)+d,\\
N_4 &= 1000a+100b+10c+1 .
\end{aligned}
\]

All four numbers must be divisible by \(7\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 1.  Congruences for the digits

Because  

\[
1000\equiv 6,\quad 100\equiv 2,\quad 10\equiv 3,\quad 1\equiv 1\pmod 7,
\]

we have  

\[
N\equiv 6a+2b+3c+d\pmod 7 .
\]

The divisibility conditions become

\[
\begin{cases}
6+2b+3c+d\equiv 0,\\
6a+2+3c+d\equiv 0,\\
6a+2b+3+d\equiv 0,\\
6a+2b+3c+1\equiv 0\pmod 7 .
\end{cases}
\]

Subtracting the first congruence from the other three gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
6a-2b &\equiv 4,\\
6a-3c &\equiv 3,\\
6a-d &\equiv 5,\\
2b-d &\equiv 1,\\
3c-d &\equiv 2\pmod 7 .
\end{aligned}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2.  Expressing \(b,c,d\) in terms of \(a\)

From the last two equations

\[
d\equiv 2-a,\qquad

b\equiv 5+3a\pmod 7 .
\]

From \(3c-d\equiv 2\):

\[

c\equiv 6+2a\pmod 7 .
\]

\boxed{\,b\equiv 5+3a,\; c\equiv 6+2a,\; d\equiv 2-a\pmod 7\,}.
