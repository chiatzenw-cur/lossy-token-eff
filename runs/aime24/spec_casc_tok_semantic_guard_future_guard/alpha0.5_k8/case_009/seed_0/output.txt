Let \(n\) be the number of tokens on the stack.  
\(n\) is a **P‑position** iff every legal move from \(n\) leads to an **N‑position**.

The legal moves are to remove \(1\) or \(4\) tokens, so

\[
n\text{ is a P‑position }\iff \{n-1,\,n-4\}\subseteq \text{N‑positions}.
\]

Checking successive values gives

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
n & \text{Outcome} \\
\hline
0 & P\\
1 & N\\
2 & P\\
3 & N\\
4 & N\\
5 & P\\
6 & N\\
7 & P\\
8 & N\\
9 & N\\
10 & P\\
\end{array}
\]

Continuing this pattern we find that the P‑positions are exactly the numbers congruent to \(0\) or \(2\pmod 5\):

- If \(n\equiv 0\text{ or }2\pmod 5\) then \(n-1\) and \(n-4\) are \(\equiv 4,1\) or \(1,3\pmod 5\), which are all N‑positions, so \(n\) is a P‑position.
- If \(n\equiv 1,3,4\pmod 5\) then one of \(n-1\) or \(n-4\) is \(\equiv 0\) or \(2\pmod 5\), hence an N‑position, so \(n\) is an N‑position.

Thus, when Alice starts from a pile of size \(n\), Bob (the second player) wins precisely when \(n\equiv 0\) or \(2\pmod 5\).

Now count positive integers \(n\le 2024\) with \(n\equiv 0\) or \(2\pmod 5\).

\[
\begin{aligned}
\#\{n\le2024 : n\equiv 0\pmod5\} &= \left\lfloor\frac{2024}{5}\right\rfloor = 402,\\[4pt]
\#\{n\le2024 : n\equiv 2\pmod5\} &= \left\lfloor\frac{2024-2}{5}\right\rfloor +1 = 404+1 = 405.
