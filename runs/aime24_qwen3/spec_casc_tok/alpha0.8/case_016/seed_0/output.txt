### Step 1: Understanding the Items

The four items are:
1. Diamond ring
2. Golf clubs
3. Garden spade
4. Candy hearts (everyone owns this, so it's a universal set)

So, when we say "own exactly two things," it means **exactly two** among the four items — since everyone has candy hearts, this is equivalent to **exactly one of the other three items**.

Similarly:
- "Exactly three things" means **exactly two of the other three items** (since one is already included as candy hearts).
- Therefore, "exactly three of these things" means **owning exactly two among the other three items**.
- And "exactly four of the things" means **owning all three of the other items** (diamond ring, golf clubs, garden spade), since candy hearts are mandatory.

---

### Step 2: Define Variables

Let:
- $ a $ = number of people who own exactly **one** of the other three items (i.e., exactly two of the four total things — including candy hearts)  
    → Given: $ a = 437 $

- $ b $ = number of people who own exactly **two** of the other three items (i.e., exactly three of the four total things)  
    → Given: $ b = 234 $

- $ c $ = number of people who own **all three** of the other three items (i.e., all four things, including candy hearts)  
    → This is the value we're solving for, and we call it $ x $

- $ y $ = number of people who own **none** of the other three items (i.e., own only candy hearts)  

Since all 900 residents own candy hearts, we can say:

$$
a + b + c + y = 900  
\Rightarrow 437 + 234 + x + y = 900  
\Rightarrow x + y = 900 - (437 + 234) = 900 - 671 = 229
$$

### Step 3: Apply Inclusion-Exclusion Principle

Now consider the total number of people who **own at least one** of the other three items (D, G, S), i.e., those who have **at least one** of diamond ring, golf clubs, or garden spade—excluding candy hearts. This can also be calculated as:

$$
|D \cup G \cup S| = |D| + |G| + |S| - (|D \cap G| + |D \cap S| + |G \cap S|) + |D \cap G \cap S|
$$
