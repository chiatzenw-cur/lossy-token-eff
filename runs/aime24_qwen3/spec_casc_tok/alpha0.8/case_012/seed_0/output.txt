To determine the number of paths of length 16 on an 8×8 grid from the lower-left corner to the upper-right corner that change direction exactly four times, we must analyze the structure of such paths carefully.

---

### Step 1: Understanding the Grid and Path

An **8×8 grid** has a width of 8 units and a height of 8 units. Moving from (0,0) to (8,8) involves:

- **8 right (R)** moves
- **8 up (U)** moves

In total, a path has **16 steps**, composed of **8 R** and **8 U**. So, any valid path must consist of **8 R's and 8 U's** in some order, and the total number of such unordered sequences is:
$$
\binom{16}{8}
$$
But we are not looking for all paths, only those that **change direction exactly four times**.

---

### Step 2: Direction Changes and Segments

Every time a path switches from R to U or U to R, it's considered a **direction change**. 

Thus, if a path has **exactly 4 direction changes**, it must be composed of **5 segments** of consecutive moves in the same direction. 

- Each such **segment** must have at least 1 move (you can't have a zero-length segment).
- The segments must alternate direction (R, U, R, U, R or U, R, U, R, U) depending on the starting direction.

Because the path ends at the same point it started (upper-right corner), and there are **an even number of direction changes (4)**, the path must **start and end with the same direction**. So, the starting direction can either be R or U.

---

### Step 3: Case Analysis Based on Start Direction

#### Case 1: Path starts with R

- The segments are: R, U, R, U, R
- There are 3 R segments and 2 U segments
- Since the total number of R steps is 8, we need to divide these 8 R's into **3 positive integer parts**, and the 8 U's into **2 positive integer parts**

Number of ways to split 8 R's into 3 parts: 
$$
\binom{8 - 1}{3 - 1} = \binom{7}{2} = 21
$$

Number of ways to split 8 U's into 2 parts: 
$$
\binom{8 - 1}{2 - 1} = \binom{7}{1} = 7
$$

So, for this case: $21 \times 7 = 147$ possible paths starting with R

#### Case 2: Path starts with U

This is symmetric. The segments are: U, R, U, R, U
- 3 U segments and 2 R segments

Similarly:
- Ways to split 8 U's into 3 parts: $\binom{7}{2} = 21$
- Ways to split 8 R's into 2 parts: $\binom{7}{1} = 7$

So, for this case: $21 \times 7 = 147$ possible paths starting with U

---

### Step 4: Total Number of Valid Paths

The total number of such paths is the sum of the two cases:
$$
147 \text{ (start with R)} + 147 \text{ (start with U)} = 294
$$
