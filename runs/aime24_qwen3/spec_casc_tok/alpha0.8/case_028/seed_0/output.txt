To solve this problem, we want to find the **greatest** four-digit integer $ N $ such that **changing any one of its digits to 1 results in a number divisible by 7**. Then, we are to find $ Q + R $, where $ Q $ and $ R $ are the quotient and remainder when $ N $ is divided by 1000.

### **Step 1: Understanding the Problem**

Let $ N = 1000A + 100B + 10C + D $, where $ A, B, C, D \in \{0,1,...,9\} $ and $ A \ne 0 $ (since $ N $ is a four-digit number).

We are required to check the four transformations:

1. Change digit A to 1 ⇒ $ N_1 = 1000 + 100B + 10C + D $
2. Change digit B to 1 ⇒ $ N_2 = 1000A + 100 + 10C + D $
3. Change digit C to 1 ⇒ $ N_3 = 1000A + 100B + 10 + D $
4. Change digit D to 1 ⇒ $ N_4 = 1000A + 100B + 10C + 1 $

All these four numbers must be divisible by 7.

---

### **Step 2: Modular Analysis**

Let’s consider the congruences modulo 7:

- $ 1000 \equiv 6 \mod 7 $
- $ 100 \equiv 2 \mod 7 $
- $ 10 \equiv 3 \mod 7 $
- $ D \equiv D \mod 7 $

Using these, we can reduce the expressions:

1. $ N_1 = 1000 + 100B + 10C + D \equiv 6 + 2B + 3C + D \mod 7 $
2. $ N_2 = 1000A + 100 + 10C + D \equiv 6A + 2 + 3C + D \mod 7 $
3. $ N_3 = 1000A + 100B + 10 + D \equiv 6A + 2B + 3 + D \mod 7 $
4. $ N_4 = 1000A + 100B + 10C + 1 \equiv 6A + 2B + 3C + 1 \mod 7 $

To satisfy the condition that all four expressions are $ \equiv 0 \mod 7 $, we set all equal to 0 modulo 7 and solve the system:

- $ 6 + 2B + 3C + D \equiv 0 \mod 7 $  → Equation (1)
- $ 6A + 2 + 3C + D \equiv 0 \mod 7 $  → Equation (2)
- $ 6A + 2B + 3 + D \equiv 0 \mod 7 $  → Equation (3)
- $ 6A + 2B + 3C + 1 \equiv 0 \mod 7 $  → Equation (4)

Subtracting equation (2) from (1), we get:
$$
(6 + 2B + 3C + D) - (6A + 2 + 3C + D) = 0 - 0 \mod 7 \\
\Rightarrow 2B - 6A + 4 \equiv 0 \mod 7 \\
\Rightarrow 2B \equiv 6A - 4 \mod 7 \\
\Rightarrow B \equiv 3A - 2 \mod 7 \quad \text{[Multiplying both sides by inverse of 2 mod 7, which is 4]}
$$

Similarly, subtracting (3) from (2):
$$
(6A + 2 + 3C + D) - (6A + 2B + 3 + D) = 0 - 0 \mod 7 \\
\Rightarrow 2 + 3C - 2B - 3 = 0 \mod 7 \\
\Rightarrow 2C - 2B - 1 = 0 \mod 7 \\
\Rightarrow 2C \equiv 2B + 1 \mod 7 \\
\Rightarrow C \equiv B + \frac{1}{2} \mod 7 \Rightarrow C \equiv B + 4 \mod 7 \quad \text{(since 2^{-1} mod 7 = 4)}
$$

From the first transformed value, $ N_1 \equiv 6 + 2B + 3C + D \equiv 0 \mod 7 $, and substituting the expressions for B and C in terms of A, we derive that:

- $ B \equiv 3A - 2 \mod 7 $
- $ C \equiv 2B + 4 \mod 7 \Rightarrow C \equiv 6A - 6 + 4 \mod 7 = 6A - 2 \mod 7 $
