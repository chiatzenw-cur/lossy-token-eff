To solve this problem, we consider a $5 \times 5$ grid with 25 indistinguishable white chips and 25 indistinguishable black chips. The task is to find the number of ways to **place some of these chips** on the grid under the given constraints:

1. **Each cell contains at most one chip**.
2. **All chips in the same row and all chips in the same column must have the same color**.
3. **Any additional chip placed on the grid would violate one or more of the previous two conditions**.

---

### Understanding the Maximal Condition

This is the **crucial part** of the problem. The third condition tells us that the placement is **maximal** — meaning that *no chip can be added* to the grid without violating the row and column consistency rules. This has a significant implication:

> **Any empty cell in the grid must be such that placing a chip there would force a contradiction in its row or column.**

In other words, every empty cell must be adjacent (in a row or column) to a **contradictory** assignment of color. Otherwise, we wouldn’t consider the placement maximal.

---

### Breaking Down the Constraints

Let’s rephrase the implications of the constraints mathematically:

- If a row contains **any chips**, they must be of **the same color**, say white.
- Similarly, if a column contains **any chips**, they must be of the same color.
- Thus, the color of a row or column is determined by whether it contains any chips, and the color is either white or black.

Now, if a **row is white**, then all cells in that row **must be white** (since you cannot add a black chip without violating the condition that all chips in the row are the same color).

Similarly, if a **column is black**, then all cells in that column **must be black**.

But this immediately gives a contradiction:

> Suppose we have a row that is white and a column that is black. Since they intersect at one cell, that cell must be both white and black — which is **not allowed**.

This tells us that **white and black rows/columns cannot intersect**. That is, they must be **non-overlapping** in terms of their placement in the grid.

So, the only possible maximal configurations are:

1. **All white chips**: Make every cell contain a white chip. Since all cells are covered and every row and every column is **entirely white**, this is a consistent and maximal configuration.
2. **All black chips**: Analogous to the above — every cell is black, rows and columns consistent, and maximal.
