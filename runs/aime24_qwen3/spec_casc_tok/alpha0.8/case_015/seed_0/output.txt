### Step 1: Understanding the Geometry

- The diagonals of a rhombus intersect at the origin and divide each other perpendicularly.
- Since the diagonals intersect at the origin, this implies that:
  - If $A = (a, b)$, then $C = (-a, -b)$.
  - Similarly, if $B = (c, d)$, then $D = (-c, -d)$.

- The length of diagonal $BD$ is the distance between points $B$ and $D$, i.e.,  
  $$
  BD^2 = (c - (-c))^2 + (d - (-d))^2 = 4c^2 + 4d^2 = 4(c^2 + d^2)
  $$
  So the expression we're interested in is $4(c^2 + d^2)$.

- A crucial condition from the geometry is that diagonal $AC$ and $BD$ are perpendicular. The vector $AC = C - A = (-2a, -2b)$ and vector $BD = D - B = (-2c, -2d)$ are perpendicular. The dot product being zero gives:
  $$
  (-2a)(-2c) + (-2b)(-2d) = 4(ac + bd) = 0 \Rightarrow ac + bd = 0
  $$
