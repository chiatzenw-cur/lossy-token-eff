### Step 2: Parametrize Line $ AD $

We parametrize the line segment $ AD $ with parameter $ t \in [0,1] $, such that:

- $ A $ corresponds to $ t = 0 $
- $ D $ corresponds to $ t = 1 $

So, the parametric equations for the segment are:

- $ x(t) = \frac{1}{3} + \frac{25}{6}t $
- $ y(t) = \frac{4\sqrt{14}}{3} - \frac{125\sqrt{14}}{33}t $

We plug these into the equation of the circumcircle $ \omega $, which is found as:

$$
x^2 + y^2 - 9x - \frac{33\sqrt{14}}{28}y = 0
$$

Substituting the parametric form into the circle equation and simplifying, we get a quadratic in $ t $. Solving this equation (note that $ t = 0 $ is a solution, corresponding to point $ A $), we find the second solution for $ t $, which is:

$$
t = \frac{88}{169}
$$

This $ t $ gives the parameter value for point $ P $, which is the second intersection of line $ AD $ with the circle.

---

### Step 3: Compute $ AP $ in Terms of $ t $

Since the parametric form of the line uses $ t $ as a linear scaling, the distance from $ A $ to $ P $ is given by the product of the parameter $ t $ and the length of $ AD $:

$$
AP = t \cdot AD
$$

We compute $ AD $:

- $ \Delta x = \frac{9}{2} - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{25}{6} $
- $ \Delta y = -\frac{27\sqrt{14}}{11} - \frac{4\sqrt{14}}{3} = -\frac{125\sqrt{14}}{33} $

Then:

$$
AD = \sqrt{ \left( \frac{25}{6} \right)^2 + \left( \frac{125\sqrt{14}}{33} \right)^2 }
= \frac{325}{22}
$$

So:

$$
AP = \frac{88}{169} \cdot \frac{325}{22} = \frac{100}{13}
$$

---

### Step 4: Final Answer

We are given that $ AP = \frac{m}{n} $, with $ m $ and $ n $ relatively prime. Here, $ m = 100 $, $ n = 13 $, and both are coprime.

Thus,

$$
m + n = 100 + 13 = \boxed{113}
$$
