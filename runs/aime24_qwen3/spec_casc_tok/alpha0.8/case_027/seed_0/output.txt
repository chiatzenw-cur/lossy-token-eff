### Step 1: Understanding the Structure of the Sets

Bob lists **all finite nonempty sets** $ B $ of **positive integers** such that **the maximum element of $ B $** belongs to the set $ A $.

Let $ A = \{a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n\} $, where $ a_1 < a_2 < \dots < a_n $ are distinct positive integers.

For each $ a_i \in A $, the number of sets $ B $ with **maximum element $ a_i $** is:

$$
2^{a_i - 1}
$$

Why?  
Because any such subset $ B $ with maximum element $ a_i $ must contain $ a_i $, and can include **any subset** of the integers less than $ a_i $ (i.e., $ \{1, 2, \dots, a_i - 1\} $).  
There are $ 2^{a_i - 1} $ such subsets, since we are freely selecting which of the $ a_i - 1 $ smaller elements to include.

Thus, **the total number of such sets B** is:

$$
\sum_{a \in A} 2^{a - 1}
$$

---

### Step 2: Interpreting the Problem

We are told this sum equals **2024**:

$$
\sum_{a \in A} 2^{a - 1} = 2024
$$

This means the sum of the numbers $ 2^{a_i - 1} $ (which are all powers of 2) equals **2024**.

Because 2024 is a sum of **distinct powers of 2**, we can use its **binary representation** to identify the individual powers of 2 that sum up to 2024.
