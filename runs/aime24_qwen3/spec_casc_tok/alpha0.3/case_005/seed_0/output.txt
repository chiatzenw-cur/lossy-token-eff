### Step 1: Determine the Least Prime $ p $ such that $ p^2 \mid n^4 + 1 $

We consider the congruence:

$$
n^4 \equiv -1 \pmod{p^2}
$$

This implies that $ n^4 \equiv -1 \pmod{p} $, and hence $ -1 $ is a **quartic residue** modulo $ p $. 

The multiplicative group $ \mathbb{Z}_p^{\times} $ has order $ p-1 $, and for $ -1 $ to be a quartic residue modulo $ p $, there must exist an integer $ k $ with:

$$
k^4 \equiv -1 \pmod{p}
\Rightarrow k^8 \equiv 1 \pmod{p}
$$

This forces the **order** of $ k $ modulo $ p $ to be exactly 8 (since the order can't divide 4). Thus, the order 8 must divide $ p - 1 $, which means:

$$
p \equiv 1 \pmod{8}
$$

Therefore, the candidate primes are those congruent to 1 modulo 8. The first few such primes are:
