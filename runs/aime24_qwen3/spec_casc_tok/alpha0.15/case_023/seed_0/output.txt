2. The **unique mode** of the list is 9 — meaning 9 appears **more times** than any other number, and **only** 9 occurs that many times.
3. The **median** is a **positive integer not present in the list** itself.

Our goal is to find the **sum of the squares of all the items** in the list.

### Step 1: Determine Possible Number of Elements (n)

- Since the **median is not in the list**, the **mode cannot be the median**, nor can the median be an element of the list.
- If the list has an **odd number of elements**, then the **median is the middle element**, which **must appear in the list**. So this would **contradict** the stipulation that the median is not in the list. Hence, **n must be even**.

So we focus our efforts on even values of $ n $ such that the sum is 30 and the conditions above are satisfied.

- $ a + b = 30 - 9 - 9 = 12 $

Let’s explore all possible integer pairs $ (a, b) $ where $ a \leq b \leq 9 $, $ a + b = 12 $, and $ b $ is odd:

- $ b = 9 \Rightarrow a = 3 $: List = [3, 9, 9, 9], mode = 9 (okay), median = 9 → **Invalid** because median is in the list.
- $ b = 7 \Rightarrow a = 5 $: List = [5, 7, 9, 9]  
  - **Sum**: 5 + 7 + 9 + 9 = 30  
  - **Mode**: 9 appears twice (others appear once) → **Unique mode**  
  - **Median**: $ \frac{7 + 9}{2} = 8 $  
  - **Check**: 8 is **not in the list** → ✅

This configuration satisfies all the conditions!

---

### Step 3: Compute the Sum of the Squares

The list is:  
$$
[5, 7, 9, 9]
$$

- $ 5^2 = 25 $
- $ 7^2 = 49 $
- $ 9^2 = 81 $ (appearing twice)

So the **sum of the squares** is:  
$$
25 + 49 + 81 + 81 = \boxed{236}
$$

### Step 4: Exhaust Other Possibilities

We checked other values of $ n $ (like 6, 8, etc.) and found that:
- For $ n = 4 $, [5, 7, 9, 9] is the **only valid configuration**.
- For $ n > 4 $, the total required sum for the other elements (to make the total sum 30) becomes either too small, or the constraints on the mode and the requirement that the median be **not in the list** cannot be satisfied simultaneously.
