---

### Understanding the Game

- Players take turns removing **1 or 4** tokens from a stack starting with $ n $ tokens.
- The player who **removes the last token** **wins**.
- Alice goes **first**.

We define:
- A **losing position** as one where the **current player cannot force a win** regardless of their move.
- A **winning position** as one where the **current player has at least one move** that leads to a **losing position** for the opponent.

Our goal is to find how many **positive integers $ n \leq 2024 $** are **losing positions**, since **if the game starts at such a number**, **Bob has a guaranteed winning strategy**, no matter how Alice plays.

---

### Strategy: Identifying Losing Positions

Through recursive thinking and pattern analysis, we find that **all losing positions** are those for which:

$$
n \equiv 0 \pmod{5} \quad \text{or} \quad n \equiv 2 \pmod{5}
$$

Let’s verify this pattern:
- For small values of $ n $, we determine the losing positions manually and observe that they indeed follow this pattern.
- We then generalize the recurrence: if a player is faced with such a count (i.e., $ n \equiv 0 $ or $ 2 \pmod{5} $), all possible moves (removing 1 or 4 tokens) result in positions where the opponent can win.
- Conversely, if $ n \not\equiv 0 $ or $ 2 \pmod{5} $, the current player can always make a move leading to a losing position for the opponent.

Therefore, the set of **losing positions** (and hence the positions where **Bob has a forced win**) corresponds to all natural numbers $ n \leq 2024 $ such that:

$$
n \equiv 0 \pmod{5} \quad \text{or} \quad n \equiv 2 \pmod{5}
$$
