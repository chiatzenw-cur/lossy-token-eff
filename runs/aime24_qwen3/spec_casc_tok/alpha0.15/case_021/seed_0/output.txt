To solve the problem, we consider the concept of a **$ b $-beautiful number**, which is defined as a positive integer $ n $ that:

1. Has **exactly two digits** when expressed in base $ b $, and
2. The **sum of the digits equals the square root of $ n $**.

---

### Step-by-Step Breakdown:

#### 1. **Form of a Two-Digit Number in Base $ b $**

A number $ n $ that has exactly two digits in base $ b $ takes the form:

$$
n = a \cdot b + c
$$

where $ a $ and $ c $ are digits in base $ b $, i.e., $ 1 \leq a \leq b - 1 $ and $ 0 \leq c \leq b - 1 $.

---

#### 2. **Given Condition: $ \text{Sum of Digits} = \sqrt{n} $**

This gives the condition:

$$
a + c = \sqrt{a \cdot b + c}
$$

Squaring both sides, we get:

$$
(a + c)^2 = a \cdot b + c
$$

Rewriting:

$$
k^2 = a \cdot b + c \quad \text{where } k = a + c
$$

Rewriting for $ a $, we have:

$$
a = \frac{k^2 - k}{b - 1}
$$

Since $ a $ must be an integer, and lie in the valid digit range $ 1 \leq a \leq b - 1 $, we require that:

$$
k^2 - k \equiv 0 \pmod{b - 1}
$$

---

#### 3. **Reformulating with a New Variable**

Let $ m = b - 1 $. Then $ b = m + 1 $, and for $ b \geq 2 $, we have $ m \geq 1 $. The problem reduces to finding the **minimum** $ m \geq 1 $ such that the number of integers $ k \in [ \lceil \sqrt{m + 1} \rceil, m ] $ satisfying:

$$
k(k - 1) \equiv 0 \pmod{m}
$$

i.e., $ k(k - 1) $ is divisible by $ m $, is **greater than 10**. This is because for each such $ k $, there is a corresponding unique $ b $-beautiful number $ n = k^2 $, and more than 10 such $ n $'s correspond to more than 10 values of $ k $.

---

#### 4. **Key Observation: Number of Solutions to $ k(k - 1) \equiv 0 \pmod{m} $**

This is equivalent to finding the number of **idempotent elements modulo $ m $** that satisfy $ x^2 \equiv x \pmod{m} $, or equivalently $ k(k - 1) \equiv 0 \pmod{m} $.

A known result in number theory tells us that the number of **modular solutions** to $ x^2 \equiv x \pmod{m} $ is:

$$
2^r \quad \text{where } r \text{ is the number of distinct prime factors of } m
$$

Therefore, the number of such values $ k $ in the range $ [ \lceil \sqrt{m + 1} \rceil, m ] $ is $ 2^r - t $, where $ t $ is the number of such solutions less than $ \sqrt{m + 1} $.

---

#### 5. **Finding the Minimal $ m $ with More Than 10 Such $ k $**

We want $ 2^r - t > 10 $. Let’s explore small values of $ r $:

- $ r = 3 \Rightarrow 2^3 = 8 $ → at most 8 values.
- $ r = 4 \Rightarrow 2^4 = 16 $

Thus, we need $ m $ to have **at least 4 distinct prime factors**, to potentially reach a total of 16 solutions.

The smallest number with 4 distinct prime factors is:

$$

$$
