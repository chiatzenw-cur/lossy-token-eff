### Understanding the Structure

A key property of the hexagon is that all its sides are of equal length (equilateral), and **all pairs of opposite sides are parallel**. This symmetry leads to a geometric configuration where the hexagon is surrounded by a triangle formed by the **extensions** of the three **non-adjacent** sides $ AB $, $ CD $, and $ EF $. 

This is a known result in geometry: for a convex equilateral hexagon with opposite sides parallel, the triangle formed by extending such sides is directly related to the hexagon’s side length. In such cases, the triangle is formed externally to the hexagon, and the hexagon is situated between these extended sides.

---

### Known Geometric Theorem

There is a useful geometric theorem which provides the relationship between the side of the hexagon and the side lengths of the triangle it defines:

> **If the triangle formed by extending the non-adjacent sides of an equilateral hexagon has side lengths $ a $, $ b $, $ c $, then the side length $ s $ of the hexagon is given by**
$$
\frac{1}{s} = \frac{1}{a} + \frac{1}{b} + \frac{1}{c}
$$

This is derived from the proportionality relations in the triangle-leaving mechanisms of such hexagons.

### Applying the Formula

Substitute the given side lengths of the triangle:

- $ a = 200 $
- $ b = 240 $
- $ c = 300 $

Compute the sum of reciprocals:

$$
\frac{1}{s} = \frac{1}{200} + \frac{1}{240} + \frac{1}{300}
$$

To perform the addition, find the least common denominator. The LCM of 200, 240, and 300 is **1200**.

$$
\frac{1}{200} = \frac{6}{1200}, \quad \frac{1}{240} = \frac{5}{1200}, \quad \frac{1}{300} = \frac{4}{1200}
$$

$$
\frac{1}{s} = \frac{6 + 5 + 4}{1200} = \frac{15}{1200} = \frac{1}{80}
