We are given two logarithmic equations involving real numbers $ x $ and $ y $, both greater than 1:

$$
\log_x(y^x) = 10 \quad \text{and} \quad \log_y(x^{4y}) = 10
$$

Our goal is to find the value of $ xy $ for which such numbers $ x $ and $ y $ exist.

---

### Step 1: Use Logarithmic Identities

Recall the logarithmic identity:

$$
\log_b(a^c) = c \log_b(a)
$$

Apply this to both equations:

- From $ \log_x(y^x) = 10 $, we get:
  $$
  x \log_x(y) = 10 \quad \text{(1)}
  $$

- From $ \log_y(x^{4y}) = 10 $, we get:
  $$
  4y \log_y(x) = 10 \quad \text{(2)}
  $$

---

### Step 2: Express Logarithms in Relation

There is a well-known identity:

$$
\log_x(y) = \frac{1}{\log_y(x)}
$$

Let us denote:

$$
\log_x(y) = a \Rightarrow \log_y(x) = \frac{1}{a}
$$

Substitute into equations (1) and (2):

- From (1):
  $$
  x \cdot a = 10 \Rightarrow x = \frac{10}{a}
  $$

- From (2):
  $$
  4y \cdot \frac{1}{a} = 10 \Rightarrow y = \frac{5a}{2}
  $$

---

### Step 3: Compute the Product $ xy $

Now that we have expressions for $ x $ and $ y $ in terms of $ a $, we compute:

$$
xy = \left( \frac{10}{a} \right) \left( \frac{5a}{2} \right) = \frac{10 \cdot 5a}{2a} = \frac{50a}{2a} = 25
$$

So regardless of the value of $ a $, the product $ xy = 25 $.

### Step 4: Validate the Existence of Such $ x $ and $ y $

We now need to confirm that there exist real numbers $ x, y > 1 $ satisfying the original equations and such that $ xy = 25 $.

Returning to the original logarithmic identities:

$$
\log_x(y^x) = x \cdot \log_x(y) = 10
\Rightarrow \log_x(y) = \frac{10}{x}
$$

Similarly:
$$
\log_y(x^{4y}) = 4y \cdot \log_y(x) = 10
\Rightarrow \log_y(x) = \frac{10}{4y} = \frac{5}{2y}
$$

But since $ \log_x(y) = \frac{1}{\log_y(x)} $, we have:

$$
\frac{10}{x} = \frac{1}{\frac{5}{2y}} = \frac{2y}{5}
\Rightarrow \frac{10}{x} = \frac{2y}{5}
$$

Multiply both sides by $ x $ and $ 5 $:

$$
50 = 2xy \Rightarrow xy = 25
$$

This again confirms that:

$$
\boxed{xy = 25}
$$
