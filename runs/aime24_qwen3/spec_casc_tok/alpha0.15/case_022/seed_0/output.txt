A **rectangle** must have four sides that are all **equal in pairs** and **perpendicular to each other**. When we inscribe a **rectangle inside a regular polygon**, the **diagonals** of the rectangle must be **equal and intersect at 90°**, which is only guaranteed when both diagonals are **diameters of the circumscribed circle**. This is because **only diameters pass through the center of the circle**, and in a rectangle inscribed in a circle (and hence a dodecagon), the diagonals must be **equal in length and intersect at the center**.

### **Key Insight: Rectangle Formation via Diameters**

Thus, any rectangle that can be formed using sides **only from the sides or diagonals** of a regular dodecagon must be formed by choosing **two distinct diameters** — these diameters serve as the **diagonals** of the rectangle.
