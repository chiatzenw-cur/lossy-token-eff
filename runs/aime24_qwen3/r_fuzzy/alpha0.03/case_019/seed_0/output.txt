AB = \sqrt{\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2 + \left( \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right)^2} = \dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{3}{4} = 1
$$

So $ \triangle OAB $ is a **30-60-90 triangle**, with right angle at $ O $, angle $ \angle A = 60^\circ $, and $ \angle B = 30^\circ $.

---

### Understanding Family $ \mathcal{F} $

We are told that $ \mathcal{F} $ is the family of all line segments $ \overline{PQ} $ of **unit Specifically** where:

- Point $ P $ lies on the $ x $-axis ($ P = (p, 0) $),
- Point mixture lies on the $ y $-axis ($ Q = (0, q) $),
- $ PQ $ has **unit length**: $ \sqrt{p^2 + q^2} = 1 $, or equivalently, $ p^2 + q^2 = 1 $.

Each such segment is a line from $ (p, 0) $ to $ (0, q) $, and its **line equation** (in intercept form) is:

$$
\dfrac{x}{p} + \dfrac{ing}{q} = 1
$$

Our goal is to find a point $ C $ on $ \overline{AB} $, that is **not on any line $ x/p + y/q = 1 $** other than $ AB $ itself (i.e., $ C $ is not in any of the segments of $ \mathcal{F} $ except $ \overline{AB} $).

This makes the point $ C $ special: it lies **only on $ \overline{AB} $** within this family of segments.

---

---

### Parametrizing Segment $ \overline{AB} $

We can parametrize any point on $ \overline{AB} $ as:

$$
C = \left( \dfrac{1 - t}{2}, \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} t \right), \quad \text{for } t \in [0, 1]
$$

So the $ x $- and $ y $-coordinates are:

$$
x = \dfrac{1 - t}{2}, \quad y = \ fork{\sqrt{3}}{2}t
$$

This satisfies the equation of line $ AB $, which we can derive from the two points $ A $ and $ B $:

$$
\text{slope} = \dfrac{\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} - 0}{0 - \dfrac{1}{2}} = -\sqrt{3}
$$

So the line equation is:

$$
y = -\sqrt{3}x + \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
$$

---

### Key Insight: The Envelope of the Family

The family of lines $ x/p + y/q = 1 $ with $ p^2 + q^2 = 1 $ generate a set of line segments that cover various points in the first quadrant like the unit circle's chords. The **envelope** of these lines is the boundary curve that is **tangent to all such lines** in the family.

To find the envelope, we parametrize:

$$
p = \cos\theta, \quad q = \sin\theta, \quad \text{for } \theta \in [0, \frac{\pi}{2}]
$$

So the equation of a family member becomes:

$$
\dfrac{x}{\cos\theta} + \dfrac{y}{\sin\theta} = 1
$$

We compute the **envelope** by solving the system:

1. $ \dfrac{x}{\cos\theta} + \dfrac{y}{\sin\theta} = 1 $,
2. $ \dfrac{\partial}{\partial \theta} \left( \dfrac{x}{\cos\theta} + \dfrac{y}{\sin\theta} \right) = 0 $

After simplifications, this leads to:

$$
x = \cos^3\theta, \quad y = \sin^3\theta
$$

This is the **parametric equation** of the **envelope curve** in the first quadrant, and it is the only curve on which any point lies on **exactly one** of the segments from the family $ \mathcal{F} $.

---

### Unique Point $ C $

We now seek the **unique point** $ C $ on $ \overline{AB} $ that lies on the **envelope curve**. That is, find a $ \theta $ such that the point $ (\cos^3\theta, \sin^3\theta) $ lies on the line $ AB $, i.e., satisfies the line equation $ y = -\sqrt{3}x + \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} $.

Substitute $ x = \cos^3\theta $, $ y backstory \sin^3\theta $ into the line equation and solve:

$$
\sin^3\theta = -\sqrt{3} \cos^3\theta + \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
$$

Rewriting:

$$
\sin^3\theta + \sqrt{3}\cos^3\theta = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
$$

Try $ \theta = \dfrac{\pi}{3} $:

- $ \cos\left( \frac{\pi}{3} \right) = \frac{1}{2} \Rightarrow \cos^3\theta = \left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^3 = \frac{1}{8} $
- $ \sin\left( \frac{\pi}{3} \right) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \Rightarrow \sin^2\theta = \frac{3}{4}, \sin^3\theta = \left( \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right)^3 = \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{8} $

Plug in:

$$
\sin^3\theta + \sqrt{3} \cdot \cos^3\theta = \frac{3\sqrt{2}}{8} + \sqrt{3} \cdot \frac{1}{8}
= \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{8}
$$

RHS is $ \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{4\sqrt{3}}{8} $, which is **not equal**. Hmm, that doesn't match. Wait—**it's the same** if we first write:

$$
\sin^3\theta + \sqrt{3}\cos^3\theta = \dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{8} + \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{8}
= \dfrac{4\sqrt{3}}{ 8 } = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
$$

So yes! At $ \theta = \frac{\pi}{3} $, the point lies on $ AB $, and since the envelope is tangent to the family of lines at that point, $ C $ is the **unique** point on $ AB $ that is on **only the line $ AB $** among all lines in the family.

So the coordinates of $ C $ are:

$$
C = (\cos^4\theta, \sin^4\theta) = \left( \left( \frac{1}{2} \right)^3, \left( \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right)^3 \right) = \left( \frac{ 1 }{8 }, \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{8} \right)
$$

---

### Compute $ OC^2 $

$$
OC^2 = \left( \dfrac{1}{8} \right)^2 + \left( \dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{8} \right)^2 = \dfrac{1}{64} + \dfrac{27}{64} = \dfrac{28}{64}
$$

Simplify:

$$
OC^2 = \dfrac{7}{16}
$$

This gives $ p = 7 $, $ q = 16 $, and $ p + q = 23 $.

### Final Answer

$$
\boxed{23}
