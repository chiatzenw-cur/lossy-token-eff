$$
P(\text{Grand Prize} \mid \text{Winnable Prize})
= \frac{P(\text{Grand Prize and Winnramer Prize})}{P(\text{Winnable Prize})}
$$

But since **winning the grand prize automatically includes winning the prize**, the event "Grand Prize and Winnable Prize" simplifies to just "Grand Prize". Thus, we compute:

$$
P(\text{Grand Prize} \ mid \text{Winnable Prize}) = \frac{P(\text{Grand Prize})}{P(\text{Winnable Prize})}
$$

---

### Step 1: Total Number of Lottery Outcomes

The lottery involves choosing **4 distinct numbers** from the set  
$$
S = \{1, 2, 3, ..., 10\}.
$$  
The total number of such outcomes is:
$$
\binom{10}{4} = 210
$$

---

### Step 2: Probability of Winning the Grand Prize

For **Jen to win the grand prize**, all four of her chosen numbers must match exactly the four drawn. Since there is only one specific 4-number combination that matches hers out of all the possible 210 combinations, the probability is:
$$
P(\text{Grand Prize}) = \frac{1}{210}
$$

---

### Step 3: Probability of Winning a Prize (At Least Two Matches)

We count how many lottery combinations match **at least two** of the four numbers Jen chose.

We consider cases with exactly **2 matches**, **3 matches**, and **4 matches**:

- **Exactly 2 matches:**  
  Choose 2 of Jen's 4 numbers and 2 of the remaining 6 (those not in her set):  
  $$
  \binom{4}{2} \cdot \binom{6}{2} = 6 \cdot 15 = 90
  $$

- **Exactly 3 matches:**  
  Choose 3 of Jen's 4 numbers and 1 of the remaining 6:  
  $$
  \binom{4}{3} \cdot \binom{6}{1} = 4 \cdot 6 = 24
  $$

- **Exactly 4 matches:**  
  All 4 of Jen's numbers match:  
  $$
  \binom{4}{4} \cdot \binom{6}{0} = 1 \cdot 1 = 1
  $$

Adding these, the number of favorable outcomes for at least two matches is:
$$
90 + 24 + 1 = 115
$$

Therefore:
$$
P(\text{Winnable Prize}) = \frac{115}{210}
$$

---

### Step 4: Conditional Probability

Now compute:
$$
P(\text{Grand Prize} \mid \text{Winnable Prize}) = \frac{\frac{1}{210}}{\frac{115}{210}} = \frac{1}{115}
$$

So, the probability of winning the grand prize **given** that she won a prize is $ \frac{1}{115} $.
