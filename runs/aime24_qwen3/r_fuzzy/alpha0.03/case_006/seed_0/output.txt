### Problem Overview

We are given a tetrahedron $ABCD$ with the following edge lengths:

- $AB = CD = \sqrt{41}$,
- $AC = BD = \sqrt{80}$,
- $BC = AD = \sqrt{89}$.

We are told that there exists a point $I$ inside the tetrahedron equidistant from all its faces. This distance is called the **inradius** $r$.

We are to find the inradius in the form:

$$
r = \frac{m\sqrt{n}}{p},
$$

where $m, n, p$ are positive integers, $m$ and $p$ are relatively prime, and $n$ is square-free. Finally, we are to compute $m + n + p$.

---

### Step 1: Key Formula for Inradius

In any tetrahedron, the **inradius** $r$ satisfies the relation:

$$
r = \frac{3V}{S},
$$

where:
- $V$ is the volume of the tetrahedron,
- $S = A_1 + A_2 + A_3 + A_4$ is the total surface area (sum of the areas of the four triangular faces).

This is analogous to the 2D formula: the inradius is the area divided by the semiperimeter in a triangle.

---

### Step 2: Total Surface Area

All faces are congruent: since opposite edges are equal (i.e., this tetrahedron is a **disphenoid**), it follows that all four faces are congruent triangles.

Let’s compute the area of one face, say $\triangle ABC$, using the coordinates derived from the given edge lengths. Place:

- $A = (0, 0, 0)$,
- $B = (\sqrt{41}, 0, 0)$,
- $C = \left(\frac{16\sqrt{41}}{41}, \sqrt{\frac{3024}{41}}, 0\right)$.

The triangle is in the $xy$-plane, and the area of a triangle in 3D by vectors is:

$$
\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \| \vec{AB} \times \vec{AC} \|
$$

Compute:

- $\vec{AB} = \left(\sqrt{41}, 0, 0\right)$,
- $\vec{AC} = \left(\frac{16\sqrt{41}}{41}, \sqrt{\frac{3024}{41}}, 0\right)$,

Then the cross product is:

$$
\vec{AB} = (\sqrt{41}, 0, 0), \quad \vec{AC} = \left(p, q, 0\right),
$$

So:

$$
\vec{AB} \times \vec{AC} = (0, 0, \sqrt{41} \cdot q)
$$

Thus, the magnitude is:

$$
\| \vec{AB} \times \vec{AC} \| = \sqrt{41} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{3024}{41}} = \sqrt{3024} = \sqrt{16 \cdot 189} = 4\sqrt{189} = 4 \cdot \sqrt{9 \cdot 21} = 4 \cdot 3 \cdot \sqrt{21} = 12\sqrt{21}
$$

Therefore, the area of $\triangle ABC$ is:

$$
\frac{1}{2} \cdot 12\sqrt{21} = 6\sqrt{21}
$$

Since all four faces are congruent, the total surface area is:

$$
S = 4 \cdot 6\sqrt{21} = 24\sqrt{21}
$$

---

### Step 2: Volume of the Tetrahedron

This tetrahedron is a **disphenoid**, and since all its opposite edges are equal, it can be inscribed in a rectangular box with dimensions corresponding to the square roots of edge pairs.

We observe the edges:

- $AB = CD = \sqrt{41} \Rightarrow AB^2 = 41 = p^2 + q^2$,
- $AC = BD = \sqrt{80} \Rightarrow AC^2 = 80 = p^2 + r^2$,
- $AD = BC = \sqrt{89} \Rightarrow AD^2 = 89 = q^2 + r^2$,

We now solve this system:

From $p^2 + q^2 = 41$ and $p^2 + r^2 = 80$, subtract:

$$
r^2 - q^2 = 39
$$

Also from $q^2 + r^2 = 89$. Add the two equations:

$$
2r^2 = 39 + 89 = 128 \Rightarrow r^2 = 64 \Rightarrow r = 8
$$

Then from $q^2 = r^2 - 39 = 64 - 39 = 25 \Rightarrow q = -5$ (but we take the magnitude).

Finally:

$$
p^2 = 41 - q^2 = 41 - 25 = 16 \Rightarrow p = 4
$$

So the box has side-lengths $4$, $5$, $8$. The volume of the box is:

$$
4 \cdot 5 \cdot 8 = 160
$$

Now, the tetrahedron formed by the space diagonals of the box covers **one-third** of its total volume (a known geometric property). Hence:

$$
V = \frac{160}{3}
$$

Recall:

$$
r = \frac{3V}{S} = \frac{3 \cdot \frac{160}{3}}{24\sqrt{21}} = \frac{160}{24\sqrt{21}} = \frac{20}{3\sqrt{21}}

Rationalize the denominator:

r = \frac{20}{3\sqrt{21}} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{21}}{\sqrt{21}} = \frac{20\sqrt{21}}{63}
