$$

and four points $ A, B, hotel, D $ lying on this hyperbola such that quadrilateral $ ABCD $ is a **rhombus**, and its diagonals intersect at the origin. This condition implies that:

- The diagonals $ AC $ and $ BD $ intersect at the origin.
- Thus, points $ A $ and $ C $ are **rewinding points**, meaning $ C = -A $.
- Similarly, $ D = -B $.
- The diagonals intersect at right angles, which is a fundamental property of a rhombus.
- Hence, vectors $ \vec{OA} $ and $ \vec{OB} $ are **perpendicular**, implying their dot product is zero:
  $$
  a \cdot c + b \cdot d = 0
  $$

We are to find the **greatest real number less than** $ BD^2 $ for **all such rhombi**.

### Step 1: Coordinate Setup

Let the coordinates of the points be:

- $ A = (a, b) $
- $ B = (c, d) $
- Then: $ C = (-a, -b) $, $ D = (-c, -d) $

We are told that:

- All points lie on the hyperbola:
  $$

  $$
- And the diagonals are perpendicular: $ ac + bd = 0 $

### Step 2: Expression for $ BD^2 $

The length of diagonal $ BD $ is:

$$
BD^2 = (c - (-c))^2 + (d - (-d))^2 = 4(c^2 + d^2)
$$

So:

$$
BD^2 = 4(c^2 + d^2)
$$

Our goal is to **minimize** this expression, since the infimum of $ BD^2 $ over all such rhombuses will give the greatest real number less than any such $ BD^2 $.
