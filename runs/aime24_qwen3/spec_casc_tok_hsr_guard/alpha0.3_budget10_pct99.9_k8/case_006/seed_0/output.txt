This set of equal opposite edges is characteristic of a **disphenoid**, a special type of tetrahedron where all opposite edges are equal and it has four congruent triangular faces. This symmetry implies that the tetrahedron has an insphere—i.e., a sphere that is tangent to all four faces—whose center is the **incenter** of the tetrahedron. The distance from the incenter to each face is the **inradius**.

### Step 1: Understanding the Inradius Formula

For a general tetrahedron with volume $V$ and total surface area $A$, the **inradius** $d$ is given by:

$$
d = \frac{3V}{A}

- $V$, the volume of the tetrahedron
- $A$, the total surface area of the tetrahedron
