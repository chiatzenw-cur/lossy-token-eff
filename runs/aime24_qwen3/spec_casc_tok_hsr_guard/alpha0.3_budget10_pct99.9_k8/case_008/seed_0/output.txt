We are given two logarithmic equations involving real numbers $ x $ and $ y $, both greater than 1:

$$
\log_x(y^x) = 10 \quad \text{and} \quad \log_y(x^{4y}) = 10
$$

Our goal is to find the value of $ xy $.

---

### Step 1: Use Logarithmic Identities

Recall the logarithmic identity:
$$
\log_b(a^c) = c \cdot \log_b(a)
$$
Apply this to simplify both equations.

**First equation:**
$$
\log_x(y^x) = x \cdot \log_x(y) = 10
$$
Let’s denote:
$$
\log_x(y) = a \Rightarrow x \cdot a = 10 \Rightarrow a = \frac{10}{x}
$$
Thus:
$$
\log_x(y) = \frac{10}{x} \Rightarrow y = x^{\frac{10}{x}}
$$

**Second equation:**
$$
\log_y(x^{4y}) = 4y \cdot \log_y(x) = 10
$$
And since:
$$
\log_y(x) = \frac{1}{\log_x(y)} = \frac{x}{10}
$$
Substitute into the second equation:
$$
4y \cdot \frac{x}{10} = 10 \Rightarrow \frac{4xy}{10} = 10 \Rightarrow \frac{2xy}{5} = 10
$$
Solving for $ xy $,
$$
xy = 25
$$

---

### Step 2: Verification by Multiplying the Two Equations

Alternatively, consider multiplying the two original simplified equations:

$$
\left(x \cdot \frac{\ln y}{\ln x}\right) \cdot \left(4y \cdot \frac{\ln x}{\ln y}\right) = 10 \cdot 10
$$

Simplifying the multiplication:
$$
x \cdot 4y \cdot \frac{\ln y}{\ln x} \cdot \frac{\ln x}{\ln y} = 100
\Rightarrow 4xy = 100 \Rightarrow xy = 25
$$

This confirms our earlier result.
