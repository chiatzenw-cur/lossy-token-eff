To solve this problem, we are asked to find the conditional probability that Jen **wins the grand prize** given that she **wins a prize** in a lottery where:

- Jen picks 4 distinct numbers from $ S = \{1, 2, \dots, 10\} $.
- A lottery draws 4 numbers from $ S $.
- **Winning a prize**: if at least 2 of her numbers are among the 4 drawn.
- **Winning the grand prize**: if all her numbers match the 4 drawn.

We are to compute $ \Pr(\text{Grand Prize} \mid \text{Prize}) $, and express this as $ \frac{m}{n} $, where $ m $ and $ n $ are relatively prime. Finally, we must compute $ m + n $.

---

### Step 1: Total Possible Combinations

From the set $ S = \{1, 2, \dots, 10\} $, there are $ \binom{10}{4} = 210 $ possible ways to draw 4 numbers.

---

### Step 2: Compute the Probability of Winning a Prize

This is the probability that **at least 2** of Jen's chosen numbers are in the 4 drawn numbers. To compute this, we instead count the number of combinations where **fewer than 2** (i.e., 0 or 1) of her numbers match the drawn numbers and subtract that from the total.

- **Number of combinations with 0 matches**:
  - Choose all 4 numbers from the 6 that **do not** match Jen's chosen numbers:
  $$
  \binom{6}{4} = 15
  $$

- **Number of combinations with 1 match**:
  - Choose 1 number from Jen's 4, and the remaining 3 from the 6 not matched:
  $$
  \binom{4}{1} \cdot \binom{6}{3} = 4 \cdot 20 = 80
  $$

So, the number of combinations where **fewer than 2** numbers match is:
$$
15 + 80 = 95
$$

Thus, the number of combinations where **at least 2** match is:
$$
210 - 95 = 115
$$

Therefore, the **probability of winning a prize** is:
$$
P(B) = \frac{115}{210} = \frac{23}{42}
$$

---

### Step 3: Compute the Probability of Winning the Grand Prize

This is the probability that **all 4 numbers** match, which is:

- Only **one** specific combination meets this: the one containing all 4 of Jen's chosen numbers.
- Hence:
$$
P(A) = \frac{1}{210}
$$

---

### Step 4: Apply Conditional Probability Formula

Since the event of winning the **grand prize** implies winning the **prize**, we use the conditional probability formula:
$$
P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(A)}{P(B)} = \frac{1/210}{23/42}
$$

Simplify the fraction:
$$
\frac{1/210}{23/42} = \frac{1}{210} \cdot \frac{42}{23} = \frac{42}{210 \cdot 23} = \frac{1}{5 \cdot 23} = \frac{1}{115}
$$

---

### Step 5: Final Answer

We are told that the probability is $ \frac{m}{n} $, where $ m $ and $ n $ are relatively prime. From the above, $ m = 1 $ and $ n = 115 $, so:
$$
m + n = 1 + 115 = \boxed{116}
