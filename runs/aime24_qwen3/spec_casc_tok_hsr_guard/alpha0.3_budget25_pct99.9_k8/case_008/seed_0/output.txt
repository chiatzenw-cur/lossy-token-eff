We are given two equations involving logarithms with variable bases:

$$
\log_x(y^x) = 10 \quad \text{and} \quad \log_y(x^{4y}) = 10
$$

We are also told that $ x > 1 $ and $ y > 1 $, and are asked to find the value of $ xy $.

---

### Step 1: Apply Logarithmic Identity

Recall the logarithmic identity:
$$
\log_b(a^c) = c \cdot \log_b(a)
$$

Apply this to both equations:

- First equation:
$$
\log_x(y^x) = x \cdot \log_x(y) = 10 \tag{1}
$$

- Second equation:
$$
\log_y(x^{4y}) = 4y \cdot \log_y(x) = 10 \tag{2}
$$

---

### Step 2: Use the Reciprocal Logarithmic Identity

We also know:
$$
\log_x(y) = \frac{1}{\log_y(x)} \tag{3}
$$

Let’s denote $ \log_x(y) = a $, then from equation (1):

$$
x \cdot a = 10 \quad \Rightarrow \quad a = \frac{10}{x} \tag{4}
$$

From equation (2), since $ \log_y(x) = \frac{1}{a} $, we have:

$$
4y \cdot \frac{1}{a} = 10
$$

Substitute $ a = \frac{10}{x} $ into this:

$$
4y \cdot \frac{x}{10} = 10 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \frac{4xy}{10} = 10
$$

Simplify:

$$
\frac{2xy}{5} = 10 \quad \Rightarrow \quad 2xy = 50 \quad \Rightarrow \quad xy = 25
$$

---

### Step 3: Verify Consistency

We can confirm this result using different approaches:

- If we write $ \log_x(y^x) = 10 $, then from the identity $ \log_x(y^x) = x \log_x(y) $, we confirm:
  $$
  x \log_x(y) = 10 \Rightarrow \log_x(y) = \frac{10}{x}
  $$
- Similarly, from $ \log_y(x^{4y}) = 10 $, we get:
  $$
  4y \log_y(x) = 10 \Rightarrow \log_y(x) = \frac{10}{4y} = \frac{5}{2y}
  $$

Since $ \log_x(y) = \frac{1}{\log_y(x)} $, we verify:

$$
\frac{10}{x} = \frac{2y}{10} \Rightarrow \frac{10}{x} = \frac{y}{5} \Rightarrow 50 = xy
$$

Thus, the result $ xy = 25 $ holds under the given assumptions.
