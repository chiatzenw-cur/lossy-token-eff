- An $8 \times 8$ grid means we need to make **8 moves to the right (R)** and **8 moves up (U)** to reach the upper-right corner from the lower-left. So the total number of steps is:

  $$
  8 + 8 = 16
  $$

- Each move is along one of the grid lines. A path is defined by the order of these 16 moves.

- A **direction change** occurs when a path switches from `R` to `U` or `U` to `R`.
- If a path **changes direction exactly four times**, it consists of:

  $$
  4 + 1 = 5 \text{ segments}
  $$

    $$
    R, U, R, U, R

Thus, the number of direction changes (4) received by these 5 segments is consistent.

### Counting the Possible Paths

We now count the number of valid such paths under these conditions.

#### Step 1: Splitting the Moves into Segments

- For **Case 1 (Starting with R)**:
  - The 8 R's are split into **3 segments**, each of length at least 1. The number of ways to do this is:

    $$
    \binom{8 - 1}{3 - 1} = \binom{7}{2} = 21
    $$

  - The 8 U's are split into **2 segments**, each of length at least 1:

    $$
    \binom{8 - 1}{2 - 1} = \binom{7}{1} = 7
    $$

  - Total for this case:  
    $$
    21 \times 7 = 147
    $$

- For **Case 2 (Starting with U)**:
  - The 8 U's are split into **3 segments**:

    $$
    \binom{7}{2} = 21
    $$

  - The 8 R's are split into **2 segments**:

    $$
    \binom{7}{1} = 7
    $$

  - Total for this case:  
    $$
    21 \times 7 = 147
    $$

#### Step 2: Adding Both Cases

Since **case 1 and case 2 are disjoint** (one starts with R and ends with R, the other starts with U and ends with U), the total number of such paths is:

$$
147 + 147 = \boxed{294}
$$
