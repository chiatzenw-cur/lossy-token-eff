To solve the problem, we are given a family of rectangular boxes with fixed surface area and volume, and we are to find the radius $ r $ of the smallest sphere that can enclose all such boxes. This requires identifying the **maximum** possible space diagonal of a box in the family, since the sphere must enclose the largest diagonal.

---

### Step 1: Expressing the Constraints
Let the dimensions of a box be $ a, b, c $.  
Then:

- **Surface Area**: $ 2(ab + bc + ca) = 54 \Rightarrow ab + bc + ca = 27 $  
- **Volume**: $ abc = 23 $

We want to **maximize** the space diagonal, which is:

$$
\text{Diagonal} = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}
$$

Therefore, to maximize the radius $ r $, we need to maximize:

$$
a^2 + b^2 + c^2
$$

---

### Step 2: Strategy to Maximize $ a^2 + b^2 + c^2 $
We use the identity:

$$
(a + b + c)^2 = a^2 + b^2 + c^2 + 2(ab + bc + ca)
$$

So:

$$
a^2 + b^2 + c^2 = (a + b + c)^2 - 2 \cdot (ab + bc + ca)
$$

Given that $ ab + bc + ca = 27 $, we get:

$$
a^2 + b^2 + c^2 = (a + b + c)^2 - 54
$$

Thus, maximizing $ a^2 + b^2 + c^2 $ is equivalent to maximizing $ a + b + c $, subject to:

- $ ab + bc + ca = 27 $
- $ abc = 23 $

This suggests we should **maximize the sum** $ a + b + c $ under the given constraints.

### Step 3: Using Symmetry to Simplify
Because of symmetry, the maximum of the sum $ a + b + c $ under the given constraints is expected to occur when **two variables are equal**. Assume $ a = b $, then:

From $ abc = 23 $, we get $ a^2c = 23 \Rightarrow c = \frac{23}{a^2} $

From $ ab + bc + ca = 27 $, we get:

$$
a^2 + 3ac = 27
$$

Substitute $ c = \frac{23}{a^2} $:

$$
a^2 + 3a \cdot \frac{23}{a^2} = a^2 + \frac{69}{a} = 27
$$

Multiply both sides by $ a $:

$$
a^3 + 69 = 27a \Rightarrow a^3 - 27a + 69 = 0
$$

This cubic has a rational root:

$$
a = 2 \quad \text{(since } 2^3 - 27 \cdot 2 + 69 = 8 - 54 + 69 = 23 \ne 0 \text{ (not a root))}
$$

Wait, try $ a = 2 $ again, then $ c = \frac{23}{4} \approx 5.75 $

Let's verify:

$$
a = 2, b = 2, c = \frac{23}{4}
$$

Compute $ ab + bc + ca = 2 \cdot 2 + 2 \cdot \frac{23}{4} + 2 \cdot \frac{23}{4} = 4 + \frac{23}{2} + \frac{23}{2} = 4 + 23 = 27 $ ✔

Compute $ abc = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot \frac{23}{4} = \frac{92}{4} = 23 $ ✔

### Step 4: Compute Maximum $ a^2 + b^2 + c^2 $

$$
a^2 + b^2 + c^2 = 2^2 + 2^2 + \left( \frac{23}{4} \right)^2 = 4 + 4 + \frac{529}{16} = 8 + \frac{529}{16} = \frac{657}{16}
$$

---

### Step 5: Find Radius $ r $ of the Sphere

The radius $ r $ of the smallest sphere that can enclose the box is half the space diagonal:

$$
r = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}
$$

$$
r^2 = \frac{1}{4} \cdot (a^2 + b^2 + c^2) = \frac{657}{16 \cdot 4} = \frac{657}{64}
$$

### Step 6: Final Answer

We are told $ r^2 = \frac{p}{q} $, where $ p $ and $ q $ are relatively prime positive integers. So:

$$
p = 657, \quad q = 64
$$

Check if $ \gcd(657, 64) = 1 $:

- Prime factorization of 657: $ 3^2 \cdot 73 $
- Prime factorization of 64: $ 2^6 $

No common factors, so they are coprime.

Now:
