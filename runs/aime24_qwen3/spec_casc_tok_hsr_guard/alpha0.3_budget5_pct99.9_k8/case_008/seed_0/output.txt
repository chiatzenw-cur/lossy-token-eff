### Step 1: Use Logarithmic Properties

Apply the logarithmic identity $ \log_b(a^c) = c \cdot \log_b(a) $.

- From $ \log_x(y^x) = 10 $, we get:
  $$
  x \log_x y = 10
  $$
  
- From $ \log_y(x^{4y}) = 10 $, we get:
  $$
  4y \log_y x = 10
  $$

---

### Step 2: Use Reciprocal Logarithmic Relationship

Recall that $ \log_x y = \frac{1}{\log_y x} $. Let’s denote $ \log_y x = k $. Then:

- $ \log_x y = \frac{1}{k} $

So the first equation becomes:
$$
x \cdot \frac{1}{k} = 10 \Rightarrow \frac{x}{k} = 10 \Rightarrow k = \frac{x}{10}
$$

Substituting into the second equation:
$$
4y \cdot k = 10 \Rightarrow 4y \cdot \frac{x}{10} = 10
$$

Simplify:
$$
\frac{4xy}{10} = 10 \Rightarrow 4xy = 100 \Rightarrow xy = 25
$$
