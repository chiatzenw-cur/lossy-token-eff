To determine the length $ AP $ in triangle $ ABC $ inscribed in a circle $ \omega $, where the tangents at $ B $ and $ C $ intersect at point $ D $, and line $ AD $ intersects the circle again at point $ P $, we are given the side lengths:

- $ AB = 5 $
- $ BC = 9 $
- $ AC = 10 $

We are to compute $ AP $ in the form $ \frac{m}{n} $ with $ m, n $ coprime and find $ m + n $.

---

### Step 1: Coordinate Setup

We place triangle $ ABC $ on the coordinate plane as follows:

- Let $ B = (0, 0) $
- Let $ C = (9, 0) $ (since $ BC = 9 $)
- Let $ A = (x, y) $ satisfying:
  $$
  AB = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = 5 \quad \text{and} \quad AC = \sqrt{(x - 9)^2 + y^2} = 10
  $$
Solving these equations, we get:
$$
x = \frac{1}{3}, \quad y = \frac{4\sqrt{14}}{3}
\Rightarrow A = \left( \frac{1}{3}, \frac{4\sqrt{14}}{3} \right)
$$

---

### Step 2: Find Tangent Lines at $ B $ and $ C $

Using the fact that tangents at points on a circle are perpendicular to radii at those points, and finding the center $ O $ of the circumcircle, we derive the slope of the tangent at $ B $ to be $ \frac{-6\sqrt{14}}{11} $, and at $ C $ to be $ \frac{6\sqrt{14}}{11} $.

Thus, the equations of the tangents are:

- At $ B $: $ y = \frac{-6\sqrt{14}}{11}x $
- At $ C $: $ y = \frac{6\sqrt{14}}{11}(x - 9) $

Solving these equations gives the coordinates of point $ D $ as:
$$
D = \left( \frac{9}{2}, \frac{-27\sqrt{14}}{11} \right)

$$
\left( \frac{9}{2} - \frac{1}{3}, \frac{-27\sqrt{14}}{11} - \frac{4\sqrt{14}}{3} \right) = \left( \frac{25}{6}, \frac{-125\sqrt{14}}{33} \right)
$$

So the parametric form is:
$$
x(t) = \frac{1}{3} + \frac{25}{6}t,\quad y(t) = \frac{4\sqrt{14}}{3} - \frac{125\sqrt{14}}{33}t

### Step 4: Find Intersection Point $ P $
