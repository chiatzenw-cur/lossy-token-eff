To solve this problem, we are given a classic conditional probability scenario involving two events:

- **Event A**: Jen wins the **grand prize**, which means all **4 of her selected numbers match exactly** the 4 randomly chosen numbers.
- **Event B**: Jen **wins a prize**, which means that **at least two** of her selected numbers match the 4 randomly chosen numbers.

---

### Step 1: Total Number of Possibilities

Jen chooses 4 distinct numbers from the set $ S = \{1, 2, \dots, 10\} $, and the lottery picks 4 distinct numbers from the same set. Since the order doesn't matter, we are dealing with **combinations**.

$$
\text{Total combinations} = \binom{10}{4} = 210
$$

---

### Step 2: P(A) – Probability of Winning the Grand Prize

For the grand prize, all 4 of Jen’s numbers must match exactly the 4 chosen numbers. Since only one specific combination of 4 numbers (her selected numbers) would do that, the probability is:

$$
P(A) = \frac{1}{210}
$$

---

### Step 3: P(B) – Probability of Winning a Prize

Winning the prize means **at least two** of her numbers match the 4 randomly chosen numbers. This includes those with **exactly 2, 3, or 4 matches**. To compute this, we again use combinations.

- **Exactly 2 matches**:
  $$
  \binom{4}{2} \times \binom{6}{2} = 6 \times 15 = 90
  $$
- **Exactly 3 matches**:
  $$
  \binom{4}{3} \times \binom{6}{1} = 4 \times 6 = 24
  $$
- **Exactly 4 matches**:
  $$
  \binom{4}{4} \times \binom{6}{0} = 1 \times 1 = 1
  $$

Adding these up gives the number of **favorable cases** for winning a prize:

$$
90 + 24 + 1 = 115
$$

So, the probability of winning the prize is:

$$
P(B) = \frac{115}{210}
$$

This fraction reduces to:

$$
P(B) = \frac{23}{42}

### Step 4: Compute Conditional Probability

We now compute the conditional probability $ P(A|B) $, which is the probability that Jen wins the **grand prize** **given** that she has **won a prize**. This is computed as:

$$
P(A|B) = \frac{P(A)}{P(B)} = \frac{\frac{1}{210}}{\frac{23}{42}} = \frac{1}{210} \times \frac{42}{23} = \frac{42}{210 \times 23} = \frac{1}{5 \times 23} = \frac{1}{115}
