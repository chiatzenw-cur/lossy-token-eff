- Triangle $ ABC $ is inscribed in a circle $ \omega $.
- The tangents to $ \omega $ at $ B $ and $ C $ intersect at a point $ D $.
- Line $ AD $ intersects the circle again at point $ P $.
- Given: $ AB = 5 $, $ BC = 9 $, $ AC = 10 $.

We are to find the value of $ AP $ as a reduced fraction $ \frac{m}{n} $ and compute $ m + n $.

---

### Step 1: Use Coordinate Geometry

Assign coordinates to the triangle:

- Let $ B = (0, 0) $, $ C = (9, 0) $.
- Let point $ A = (x, y) $. Using the distance formula:
  - $ AB = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} = 5 $
  - $ AC = \sqrt{(x - 9)^2 + y^2} = 10 $

Solving this system gives:
$$
x = \frac{1}{3}, \quad y = \frac{4\sqrt{14}}{3}
$$
So, $ A = \left( \frac{1}{3}, \frac{4\sqrt{14}}{3} \right) $

---

### Step 2: Find the Tangent Points and Coordinates of $ D $

From geometry, the tangents at $ B $ and $ C $ intersect at point $ D $. Using the properties of the circle and its tangent lines, we find the **equations** of the two tangents and solve for their intersection point $ D $. The process involves:

- Finding the **center of the circle** by completing the square on the general circle equation passing through $ A, B, C $.
- Deriving the **slope of the tangents** at $ B $ and $ C $, using the fact that the tangent is perpendicular to the line from the center to the point.

After tedious but accurate derivation, we find:
$$
D = \left( \frac{9}{2}, -\frac{27\sqrt{14}}{11} \right)
$$

### Step 3: Find the Equation of Line $ AD $

Using the coordinates of $ A $ and $ D $, the slope of line $ AD $ is:
$$
m_{AD} = \frac{y_D - y_A}{x_D - x_A} = -\frac{10\sqrt{14}}{11}
