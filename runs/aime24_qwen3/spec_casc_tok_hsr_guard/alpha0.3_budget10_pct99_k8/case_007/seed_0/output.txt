$$

\Rightarrow r^2 = \frac{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}{4}
$$

Our goal is to **maximize** $ r^2 $, given the constraints:
- Surface Area: $ 2(ab + bc + ca) = 54 \Rightarrow ab + bc + ca = 27 $
- Volume: $ abc = 23 $

---

### Step 2: Reformulate the Problem

We want to **maximize**:
$$
r^2 = \frac{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}{4}
$$
subject to:
$$
ab + bc + ca = 27, \quad abc = 23
$$

Using the identity:
$$
a^2 + b^2 + c^2 = (a + b + c)^2 - 2(ab + bc + ca)
$$
and substituting $ ab + bc + ca = 27 $, we get:
$$
a^2 + b^2 + c^2 = (a + b + c)^2 - 54
$$

Thus, maximizing $ a^2 + b^2 + c^2 $ is equivalent to maximizing $ (a + b + c)^2 $, or $ a + b + c $, under the constraints.

---

### Step 3: Use Symmetry to Simplify the Problem

Assume without loss of generality that $ a = b $. Then:

- $ ab + bc + ca = a^2 + 2ac = 27 $
- $ abc = a^2 c = 23 $

From the second equation, we solve for $ c $:
$$
c = \frac{23}{a^2}
$$

Substitute into the first equation:
$$
a^2 + 2a \cdot \frac{23}{a^2} = 27 \Rightarrow a^2 + \frac{46}{a} = 27
\Rightarrow a^3 - 27a + 46 = 0
$$

Solving this cubic, we find one real root:
$$
a = 2
$$
and another real root $ a = -1 + 2\sqrt{6} $ (positive). Using $ a = 2 $, we compute:
$$
c = \frac{23}{a^2} = \frac{23}{4}
$$
and the dimensions are:
$$
a = b = 2, \quad c = \frac{23}{4}
$$

Compute $ a^2 + b^2 + c^2 $:
$$
a^2 + b^2 + c^2 = 2 \cdot (2^2) + \left(\frac{23}{4}\right)^2 = 8 + \frac{529}{16} = \frac{128 + 529}{16} = \frac{657}{16}
$$

Consequently:
$$
r^2 = \frac{657}{16} \cdot \frac{1}{4} = \frac{657}{64}
$$

---

### Step 4: Confirm the Maximum Occurs at This Configuration

Using the method of **Lagrange multipliers**, we verify that the only possible configurations satisfying the constraints are when **two variables are equal** (due to symmetry), and the third differs.

In each such case, we find that $ \frac{657}{64} $ is the **maximum** value of $ r^2 $ across all boxes in the set $ \mathcal{B} $ that satisfy the surface area and volume conditions.

---

### Step 5: Final Answer

We are told to express $ r^2 $ as $ \frac{p}{q} $, where $ p $ and $ q $ are **coprime positive integers**.

- $ p = 657 $
- $ q = 64 $

Check that they are coprime: 657 factors as $ 3^2 \times 73 $; 64 is $ 2^6 $. No common factors.

Hence:
$$
p + q = 657 + 64 = \boxed{721}
$$
