To determine the probability that Jen wins the **grand prize** given that she **won a prize**, we apply the principles of **conditional probability**.

---

### Step 1: Understanding the Problem

Jen selects 4 distinct numbers from the set  
$$
S = \{1, 2, 3, \ldots, 10\}
$$  
and the lottery randomly chooses 4 numbers from the same set. She **wins a prize** if **at least two** of her numbers match the lottery’s numbers. She **wins the grand prize** if **all four** of her numbers match the lottery’s numbers.

We are asked to compute:
$$
P(\text{Grand Prize} \mid \text{Won a Prize})
$$

This is a standard **conditional probability** question. By the definition of conditional probability:
$$
P(\text{Grand Prize} \mid \text{Won a Prize}) = \frac{P(\text{Grand Prize} \cap \text{Won a Prize})}{P(\text{Won a Prize})}
$$

But since **winning the grand prize implies winning a prize**, we have:
$$
P(\text{Grand Prize} \cap \text{Won a Prize}) = P(\text{Grand Prize})
$$

Thus,
$$
P(\text{Grand Prize} \mid \text{Won a Prize}) = \frac{P(\text{Grand Prize})}{P(\text{Won a Prize})}
$$

---

### Step 2: Total Number of Possible Outcomes

The total number of ways to choose 4 distinct numbers from 10 is:
$$
\binom{10}{4} = 210
$$

So, the total number of possible lottery results is **210**.

---

### Step 3: Compute $ P(\text{Grand Prize}) $

Jen wins the grand prize only when the lottery draws **exactly the same 4 numbers** she chose. There is exactly **one** such combination (her chosen 4 numbers), so:
$$
P(\text{Grand Prize}) = \frac{1}{210}
$$

---

### Step 4: Compute $ P(\text{Won a Prize}) $

To win a prize, at least two of Jen’s numbers must be among the four lottery numbers. We compute the number of such combinations using the **hypergeometric** principle:

- **Exactly 2 matches**:  
  Select 2 numbers from her 4, and 2 from the other 6:  
  $$
  \binom{4}{2} \cdot \binom{6}{2} = 6 \cdot 15 = 90
  $$

- **Exactly 3 matches**:  
  Select 3 from her 4, and 1 from the other 6:  
  $$
  \binom{4}{3} \cdot \binom{6}{1} = 4 \cdot 6 = 24
  $$

- **Exactly 4 matches (grand prize)**:  
  Select all 4 from her numbers:  
  $$
  \binom{4}{4} \cdot \binom{6}{0} = 1
  $$

So, total number of *winning* combinations:
$$
\text{Total Wins} = 90 + 24 + 1 = 115
$$

Thus:
$$
P(\text{Won a Prize}) = \frac{115}{210}
$$

### Step 5: Compute Conditional Probability

Now, we can compute:
$$
P(\text{Grand Prize} \mid \text{Won a Prize}) = \frac{1/210}{115/210} = \frac{1}{115}
