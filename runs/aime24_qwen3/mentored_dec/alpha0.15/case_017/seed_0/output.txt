To solve the problem, we are given the following about triangle $ \triangle ABC $:

- Inradius $ r = 6 $

We are to find the product $ AB \cdot AC $.

### Step 1: Use the condition $ IA \perp OI $

In triangle $ \triangle ABC $, with $ O $ the circumcenter and $ I $ the incenter, the condition $ IA \perp OI $ implies that triangle $ AIO $ is a right triangle with the right angle at $ I $. This means:

$$
AI^2 + OI^2 = OA^2
$$

We know:

$$
OA = R = 13, \quad OI^2 = R^2 - 2Rr = 13^2 - 2 \cdot 13 \cdot 6 = 169 - 156 = 13
$$

So:

$$
AI^2 = OA^2 - OI^2 = 169 - 13 = 156 \quad \Rightarrow \quad AI = \sqrt{156} = 2\sqrt{39}
$$

---

### Step 2: Use the formula for $ IA $

The distance from the incenter $ I $ to a vertex, such as $ A $, can also be written as:

$$
IA = \sqrt{r^2 + (s - a)^2}
$$

Where:
- $ r = 6 $
- $ s $ is the semi-perimeter
- $ a = BC $, the side opposite vertex $ A $

Plugging in the known values:

$$
\sqrt{6^2 + (s - a)^2} = \sqrt{156} \quad \Rightarrow \quad 36 + (s - a)^2 = 156
$$

$$
(s - a)^2 = 120 \quad \Rightarrow \quad s - a = 2\sqrt{30}
$$

### Step 3: Express the area and semi-perimeter

We know the area $ A $ of triangle $ ABC $ can be expressed in two ways:

$$
A = r \cdot s = 6s

$$

$$

Equating the two gives:

$$

Now, since $ AB \cdot AC = b \cdot c $, then:

$$
abc = a \cdot b \cdot c = a \cdot (AB \cdot AC)

So:
