To solve the problem, we analyze the arrangement of two sets of circles within triangle $ ABC $, where both sets are placed in the same geometric configuration and their respective numbers and radii differ.

---

### **Understanding the Configuration**

The essential configuration is a **chain of circles** along the **angle bisector of angle $ B $** in triangle $ ABC $. Each circle is:

- **Tangent to sides $ AB $ and $ BC $** of the triangle.
- **Tangent to the previous circle in the chain**, so their centers lie along the same line (the angle bisector).

This setup implies the circles are similar to **Apollonius circles**—circles tangent to two sides of a triangle and a previous circle.

---

### **Geometric Insight with Trigonometry**

Let’s denote:
- $ r $ as the radius of a circle in the chain.
- $ \theta = \angle B $ in triangle $ ABC $.
- $ n $ as the number of such circles in a configuration.

Each center of these circles lies along the angle bisector of $ \angle B $. Consider the distance from vertex $ B $ to the center of the first circle to be:
$$
d_1 = \frac{r}{\sin(\theta/2)}
$$

The distance between the centers of two adjacent circles is $ 2r $, and for $ n $ circles, the total distance from $ B $ to the center of the last circle becomes:
$$
d_n = \frac{r}{\sin(\theta/2)} + 2r(n - 1)
$$

We **assume** that the **last circle in the chain is tangent to the incircle of triangle $ ABC $**. The incircle also lies on the angle bisector, at a distance:
$$
d_{\text{in}} = \frac{r_{\text{in}}}{\sin(\theta/2)}
$$
where $ r_{\text{in}} $ is the inradius of triangle $ ABC $.

Since the last circle is tangent to the incircle, the distance between their centers is $ r_{\text{in}} + r $. Therefore:
$$
d_{\text{in}} - d_n = r_{\text{in}} + r
$$
Substitute the expressions:
$$
\frac{r_{\text{in}}}{\sin(\theta/2)} - \left( \frac{r}{\sin(\theta/2)} + 2r(n - 1) \right) = r_{\text{in}} + r
$$
Simplify:
$$
\frac{r_{\text{in}} - r}{\sin(\theta/2)} - 2r(n - 1) = r_{\text{in}} + r
$$
Rearranging:
$$
r_{\text{in}} - r = (r_{\text{in}} + r)(\sin(\theta/2)) + 2r(n - 1)\sin(\theta/2)
$$
$$
r_{\text{in}} - r = r_{\text{in}} \sin(\theta/2) + r\sin(\theta/2) + 2r(n - 1)\sin(\theta/2)
$$
$$
r_{\text{in}} (1 - \sin(\theta/2)) = r [1 + (2n - 1)\sin(\theta/2)]
$$
$$
r_{\text{in}} = \frac{r [1 + (2n - 1)\sin(\theta/2)]}{1 - \sin(\theta/2)}
$$

We are given:

- **Scenario 2:** 2024 circles of radius $ 1 $

In both cases, the **same triangle** $ ABC $ allows such arrangements. Therefore, the inradius $ r_{\text{in}} $ used in the formula must be the **inradius of triangle $ ABC $**. Equating the two expressions for $ r_{\text{in}} $:
