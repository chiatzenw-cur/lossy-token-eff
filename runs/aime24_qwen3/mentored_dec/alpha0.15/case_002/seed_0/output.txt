### **Step 1: Use Heron's Formula to Find the Area and Circumradius**

We begin by computing the semiperimeter $ s $ of triangle $ ABC $:

$$
s = \frac{AB + BC + AC}{2} = \frac{5 + 9 + 10}{2} = 12
$$

Now use Heron’s formula to compute the area $ K $ of triangle $ ABC $:

$$
K = \sqrt{s(s - AB)(s - BC)(s - AC)} = \sqrt{12 \cdot 7 \cdot 3 \cdot 2} = \sqrt{504} = 6\sqrt{14}
$$

Then, use the formula for the circumradius $ R $ of a triangle:

$$
R = \frac{abc}{4K} = \frac{5 \cdot 9 \cdot 10}{4 \cdot 6\sqrt{14}} = \frac{450}{24\sqrt{14}} = \frac{75\sqrt{14}}{56}
$$

### **Step 2: Use the Tangent Length from Point $ D $**

Point $ D $ is the intersection of the tangents at $ B $ and $ C $ to the circle. A known property in geometry is that the length of a tangent from point $ D $ to the circle is given by:

$$
DB = \frac{BC}{2 \cos A}
$$

We first compute $ \cos A $ using the Law of Cosines in triangle $ ABC $:

$$
\cos A = \frac{b^2 + c^2 - a^2}{2bc} = \frac{10^2 + 5^2 - 9^2}{2 \cdot 10 \cdot 5} = \frac{100 + 25 - 81}{100} = \frac{44}{100} = \frac{11}{25}

### **Step 3: Use Power of a Point and Relation with $ DA $ and $ DP $**

Since line $ AD $ is a secant that intersects the circle at $ A $ and $ P $, the **Power of a Point Theorem** tells us:

$$
DB^2 = DP \cdot DA
$$

We are given that the triangle is inscribed in a circle and $ D $ lies outside it, so $ P $ lies on segment $ AD $, meaning:

$$
DA = DP + AP \quad \Rightarrow \quad DP = DA - AP

DB^2 = DP \cdot DA = (DA - AP) \cdot DA

DB^2 = DA^2 - AP \cdot DA
