### Understanding the Problem

We are told:

The question asks for the number of residents who own **all four** of these items.

### Key Insight

Since **every resident owns a bag of candy hearts**, we can eliminate C from the analysis as a separate category. Thus, owning **all four** of the listed things is equivalent to owning **D, G, S, and C** — which is the same as owning **D, G, and S**, because C is a universal set.

Therefore, the problem reduces to finding how many residents own **all three** of D, G, and S. We call this number **x**.

But how do we find **x**?

### Step-by-Step Breakdown

Let’s define:

- **a = number of people who own exactly one of D, G, S**  
- **b = number of people who own exactly two of D, G, S**

From the problem:

- **a = 437** → These are the people who own **exactly two** of the four items (D, G, S, C). Since C is universal, this corresponds to **exactly one of D, G, S** and C.

$$
\text{Total D, G, and S ownerships} = |D \cup G \cup S| = |D| + |G| + |S| - (|D \cap G| + |D \cap S| + |G \cap S|) + |D \cap G \cap S|
