### Step 1: Use Power of a Point

One key geometric relationship is the **Power of a Point Theorem**. Since $ D $ lies outside the circle and $ AD $ intersects the circle at two points $ A $ and $ P $, we apply the formula:

$$

Here, $ DB = DC $ because both are tangent segments from $ D $ to the circle, so the formula becomes:

$$
DA \cdot DP = DB^2
$$

If $ P $ lies between $ D $ and $ A $, then $ DP = DA - AP $ and we can write:

$$
DA \cdot (DA - AP) = DB^2 \Rightarrow DA^2 - DA \cdot AP = DB^2 \Rightarrow AP = \frac{DA^2 - DB^2}{DA}
$$

Thus, we will compute $ DA $, $ DB^2 $, and then use this expression to find $ AP $.
