We are given the following two equations involving logarithms with the same base:

$$
\log_x(y^x) = 10 \quad \text{and} \quad \log_y(x^{4y}) = 10
$$

Here, both $ x $ and $ y $ are real numbers greater than 1. The task is to find the product $ xy $.

---

### Step 1: Apply Logarithmic Identities

We use the identity:

$$
\log_b(a^c) = c \log_b(a)
$$

Apply this to each equation:

- From $ \log_x(y^x) = 10 $, we get:
  $$
  x \log_x(y) = 10
  $$

- From $ \log_y(x^{4y}) = 10 $, we get:
  $$
  4y \log_y(x) = 10
  $$

---

### Step 2: Use the Reciprocal Relationship between Logarithms

Recall the key identity:

$$
\log_b(a) = \frac{1}{\log_a(b)}
$$

Thus,
$$
\log_x(y) = \frac{1}{\log_y(x)}
$$

Let’s define:
- $ a = \log_x(y) $

Then from the first equation:
$$
x a = 10 \quad \Rightarrow \quad a = \frac{10}{x}
$$

And from the second equation:
$$
4y \cdot \frac{1}{a} = 10
$$

Substituting $ a = \frac{10}{x} $:
$$
4y \cdot \frac{x}{10} = 10
\Rightarrow \frac{4xy}{10} = 10
\Rightarrow \frac{2xy}{5} = 10
\Rightarrow xy = 25
$$
