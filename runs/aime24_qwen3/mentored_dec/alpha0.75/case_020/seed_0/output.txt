To evaluate the product  
$$
\prod_{k=0}^{12} \left(2 - 2\omega^k + \omega^{2k}\right)
$$  
where $\omega$ is a 13th root of unity, we must take advantage of the fact that the set $\omega^k$ for $k = 0, 1, \dots, 12$ are all 13th roots of unity (including $1 = \omega^0$).

---

### Step 1: Observe Key Properties of $\omega$

Since $\omega \ne 1$ is a 13th root of unity, we know that:

- $\omega^k$ as $k$ ranges from 0 to 12 gives all 13 roots of the cyclotomic polynomial:
  $$
  x^{13} - 1 = (x - 1)\prod_{k=1}^{12} (x - \omega^k)
  $$
- All 13th roots satisfy $\omega^{13} = 1$.

So, our goal is to compute the product:
$$
\prod_{k=0}^{12} \left(2 - 2\omega^k + \omega^{2k}\right)
$$

This simplifies to:
$$
\prod_{k=0}^{12} \left(\omega^{2k} - 2\omega^k + 2\right)
$$

Now, let’s consider the general quadratic term:
$$
\omega^{2k} - 2\omega^k + 2 = (\omega^k)^2 - 2\omega^k + 2 = (x - 1 - i)(x - 1 + i), \quad \text{where } x = \omega^k
$$

Thus, the entire product becomes:
$$
\prod_{k=0}^{12} (x - 1 - i)(x - 1 + i) = \prod_{k=0}^{12} (x - 1 - i) \cdot \prod_{k=0}^{12} (x - 1 + i)
$$

---

### Step 2: Apply Polynomial Root Theorem

From the identity:
$$
x^{13} - 1 = \prod_{k=0}^{12} (x - \omega^k)
$$
we deduce that:
$$
\prod_{k=0}^{12} (\omega^k - a) = (-1)^{13} \cdot \frac{a^{13} - 1}{a - 1} = -(a^{13} - 1) = 1 - a^{13}
$$

Let us apply this to both terms.

#### First term:
$$
\prod_{k=0}^{12} (\omega^k - 1 - i) = 1 - (1 + i)^{13}
$$

#### Second term:
$$
\prod_{k=0}^{12} (\omega^k - 1 + i) = 1 - (1 - i)^{13}
$$

So the entire product becomes:
$$
\left(1 - (1 + i)^{13} \right)\left(1 - (1 - i)^{13} \right)
$$

---

### Step 3: Compute $(1 + i)^{13}$ and $(1 - i)^{13}$

We use Euler's formula: $1 + i = \sqrt{2}e^{i\pi/4}$. Then:

$$
(1 + i)^{13} = \left(2^{1/2} \cdot e^{i\pi/4} \right)^{13} = 2^{13/2} \cdot e^{i13\pi/4}
$$

Reduce the angle modulo $2\pi$:  
$$
13\pi/4 \equiv 5\pi/4 \mod 2\pi \Rightarrow (1 + i)^{13} = 64\sqrt{2} \cdot e^{i5\pi/4}
= 64\sqrt{2} \cdot \left( -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} - i\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right) = -64 -64i
$$

Similarly:
$$
(1 - i)^{13} = -64 + 64i
$$

$$
\left(1 - (-64 -64i)\right)\left(1 - (-64 +64i)\right) = (1 + 64 +64i)(1 + 64 -64i)

$$

This is a product of complex conjugates:
$$
(65 + 64i)(65 - 64i) = 65^2 + 64^2 = 4225 + 4096 = 8321
