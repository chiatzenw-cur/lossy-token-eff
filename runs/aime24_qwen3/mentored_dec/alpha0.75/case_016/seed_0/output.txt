To find how many residents own all four items (including candy hearts), we use the total number of residents who own **diamond ring, golf clubs, garden spade**, and how they are distributed across overlaps (since candy hearts are universal).

Let:

- $ x = $ number of residents who own all **four items** (candy hearts, D, G, S)

  - $ A $ = number of residents with exactly three items (C, D, G)

  - $ C $ = number of residents with exactly three items (C, G, S)

Let’s denote:
- $ P $ = C & D only

- $ R $ = C & S only

P + Q + R = 437
$$

Now, we can compute the **total number of D, G, S** ownerships. Note that the given counts are:

- $ D = $ 195

- $ S = $ 562

Each resident with exactly **two items** (i.e., C and one of D, G, S) contributes 1 ownership to that item.

Each resident with exactly **three items** (C, D, G, etc.) contributes 2 to the total of that item's ownership.

Each resident with exactly **four items** (C, D, G, S) contributes 3 to the total of that item’s ownership.

Thus, the total ownership of all three (D, G, S) is:

$$
D + G + S = 195 + 367 + 562 = 1124
$$

Let’s also express that in terms of the variables we have:

So:

$$
D + G + S = 1 \cdot (P + Q + R) + 2 \cdot (A + B + C) + 3 \cdot x
$$

Substituting the known values:

1124 = 1 \cdot 437 + 2 \cdot 234 + 3x
