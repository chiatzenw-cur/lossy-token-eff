We are asked to compute the product  
$$
\prod_{k=0}^{12}(2 - 2\omega^k + \omega^{2k})
$$  
where $\omega$ is a 13th root of unity ($\omega \neq 1$), and then find the remainder when the result is divided by 1000.

---

### Step 1: Recognize the Structure of the Product

Note that $\omega$ being a 13th root of unity implies:
$$
\omega^{13} = 1, \quad \text{and} \quad \omega^k \text{ for } k=0,1,\dots,12 \text{ are all 13th roots of unity.}
$$

So the entire product is:
$$
\prod_{k=0}^{12} (2 - 2\omega^k + \omega^{2k})
$$  
This can be rewritten as:
$$
\prod_{\substack{x^{13}=1 \\ x\in \mathbb{C}}} (x^2 - 2x + 2)
$$

Note that the expression inside the product is:
$$
x^2 - 2x + 2 = (x - (1 + i))(x - (1 - i))
$$

This simplifies the problem: we are to compute:
$$
\prod_{x^{13}=1} (x - (1 + i))(x - (1 - i)) 
= \left[ \prod_{x^{13}=1} (x - (1 + i)) \right] \cdot \left[ \prod_{x^{13}=1} (x - (1 - i)) \right]
$$

---

### Step 2: Use the Polynomial Evaluation at Roots

For a polynomial $f(x) = x^{13} - 1$, the product:
$$
\prod_{x^{13}=1} (x - a)
$$  
is equal to $f(a)$, because this is the value of the polynomial at $x = a$.

Thus,
$$
\prod_{x^{13}=1} (x - (1 + i)) = (1 + i)^{13} - 1, \quad \text{and} \quad
\prod_{x^{13}=1} (x - (1 - i)) = (1 - i)^{13} - 1
$$

Therefore, the total product becomes:
$$
[(1 + i)^{13} - 1][(1 - i)^{13} - 1]
$$

---

### Step 3: Compute $(1 + i)^{13}$ and $(1 - i)^{13}$

We use polar form:
$$
1 + i = \sqrt{2} \left( \cos \frac{\pi}{4} + i \sin \frac{\pi}{4} \right) = \sqrt{2} e^{i\pi/4}
$$
$$
(1 + i)^{13} = (\sqrt{2})^{13} \cdot e^{i \cdot \frac{13\pi}{4}} 
= 2^{13/2} \cdot e^{i \cdot \left( \frac{13\pi}{4} \mod 2\pi \right)}

= 64 \sqrt{2} \cdot \left( -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} - i \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right)
