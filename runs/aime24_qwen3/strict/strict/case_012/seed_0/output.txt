To determine the number of paths of length $16$ on an $8 \times 8$ grid from the lower-left corner to the upper-right corner that change direction *exactly four times*, we proceed with a structured analysis of the problem.

---

### Understanding the Path

In an $8 \times 8$ grid, a path from the lower-left to the upper-right must consist of **8 right (R)** and **8 up (U)** steps, for a total of $16$ steps. Each step goes either right or up, and the direction can only change when a move from R to U or U to R occurs.

---

### Interpreting "Change Direction Exactly Four Times"

If a path changes direction exactly **four times**, this means it alternates directions four times, resulting in **5 segments** in total. Each segment consists of only one type of move (either all R or all U), but the segments alternate between R and U.

Since the path starts at the lower-left corner, and direction must alternate, there are **two possible configurations**:

1. **Starts with Right** → Segments: R, U, R, U, R → 3 R segments and 2 U segments
2. **Starts with Up** → Segments: U, R, U, R, U → 3 U segments and 2 R segments

In either case, the number of R (or U) steps is fixed at 8, and the number of segments in that direction is fixed at 3 or 2, corresponding to the number of direction changes.

---

### Applying the Combinatorial Model

We now count the number of such paths.

#### Starting with a Right Segment

- We need to split the **8 R steps** into **3 segments**, with each segment containing at least one move.
- Number of ways to split 8 R's into 3 non-empty segments is the number of **compositions of 8 into 3 parts**, which is:
  $$
  \binom{8-1}{3-1} = \binom{7}{2} = 21
  $$

- Similarly, we split the **8 U steps** into **2 segments**, each with at least one move:
  $$
  \binom{8-1}{2-1} = \binom{7}{1} = 7
  $$

- So, the number of paths that **start with R** and have exactly 4 direction changes is:
  $$
  21 \times 7 = 147
  $$

#### Starting with an Up Segment

- Now, split the **8 U steps** into **3 segments**, and the **8 R steps** into **2 segments**, giving:
  $$
  \binom{7}{2} \times \binom{7}{1} = 21 \times 7 = 147
  $$

---

### Final Count

Adding both cases (starting with R and starting with U) gives the total number of such paths:

$$
147 + 147 = \boxed{294}
$$
