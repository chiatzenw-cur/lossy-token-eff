To solve the problem, we analyze the geometry of triangle $ ABC $ inscribed in a circle $ \omega $, with tangents at $ B $ and $ C $ intersecting at point $ D $, and line $ AD $ intersecting the circle again at point $ P $.

We are given:
- $ AB = 5 $
- $ BC = 9 $
- $ AC = 10 $

We are to compute $ AP $ and express it as $ \frac{m}{n} $ with $ m $ and $ n $ coprime, then find $ m + n $.

---

### Step 1: Use the Power of a Point

Point $ D $ lies outside the circle $ \omega $, and lines $ DB $ and $ DC $ are tangents to the circle. Therefore, we can apply the **Power of a Point Theorem** at $ D $, which gives:

$$
DB^2 = DA \cdot DP
$$

Here, $ P $ is the other intersection of line $ AD $ with the circle $ \omega $. The goal is to find $ AP $. Let $ AP = x $, and note that from the geometry:

$$
DA = DP + PA = DP + x
$$

Substitute into the power of a point equation:

$$
DB^2 = DA \cdot DP = (DP + x) \cdot DP
$$

Expand:

$$
DB^2 = DP^2 + DP \cdot x
$$

Solving for $ x $:

$$
DP \cdot x = DB^2 - DP^2 \Rightarrow x = \frac{DB^2 - DP^2}{DP}
$$

But since we are seeking $ AP = x $, we can also rewrite the above as:

$$
x = \frac{DA^2 - DB^2}{DA}
$$

This is because:

$$
x = \frac{DB^2 - DP^2}{DP}, \quad DA = DP + x \Rightarrow DP = DA - x
$$

Substitute into the expression:

$$
x = \frac{DB^2 - (DA - x)^2}{DA - x}
$$

This confirms the cleaner expression:
$$
AP = \frac{DA^2 - DB^2}{DA}
$$

---

### Step 2: Compute $ DA $ and $ DB $

We compute these lengths using triangle side lengths and the **congruent tangent segments** property and **coordinates**.

Let’s place triangle $ ABC $ in the coordinate plane:

- Let $ B = (0,0) $
- Let $ C = (9,0) $
- Let $ A = \left(\frac{1}{3}, \frac{4\sqrt{14}}{3} \right) $

$$

$$
and similarly,
$$
AC^2 = \left( \frac{1}{3} - 9 \right)^2 + \left( \frac{4\sqrt{14}}{3} \right)^2 = \left(-\frac{26}{3} \right)^2 + \frac{224}{9} = \frac{676}{9} + \frac{224}{9} = \frac{900}{9} = 100 \Rightarrow AC = 10
$$

So the coordinates are consistent.
