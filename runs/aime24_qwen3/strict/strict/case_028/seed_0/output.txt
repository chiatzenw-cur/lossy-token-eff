$$

We require that changing **any one** of the digits to **1** results in a number divisible by 7.

The four types of modifications give us the following conditions based on the place values:

1. Change thousands digit: $ 1000 + 100b + 10c + d \equiv 0 \mod 7 $
2. Change hundreds digit: $ 1000a + 100 + 10c + d \equiv 0 \mod 7 $
3. Change tens digit: $ 1000a + 100b + 10 + d \equiv 0 \mod 7 $
4. Change units digit: $ 1000a + 100b + 10c + 1 \equiv 0 \mod 7 $

We reduce the coefficients modulo 7 using the facts:
- $ 1000 \equiv 6 \mod 7 $
- $ 100 \equiv 2 \mod 7 $
- $ 10 \equiv 3 \mod 7 $

Rewriting the congruences modulo 7:

- $ 2b + 3c + d \equiv 1 \mod 7 $  (Equation 1)
- $ 6a + 3c + d \equiv 5 \mod 7 $  (Equation 2)
- $ 6a + 2b + d \equiv 4 \mod 7 $  (Equation 3)
- $ 6a + 2b + 3c \equiv 6 \mod 7 $  (Equation 4)

---

### Step 2: Solve the System of Congruences

By subtracting and combining these equations, we derive:

- $ 6a - 2b \equiv 4 \mod 7 $  (Equation A)
- $ 2b - 3c \equiv 6 \mod 7 $  (Equation B)
- $ 3c - d \equiv 2 \mod 7 $  (Equation C)

Solve these step-by-step:

From Equation B:
$$
2b \equiv 3c + 6 \mod 7 \Rightarrow b \equiv (3c + 6)\cdot4 \mod 7
$$
Multiply both sides by the modular inverse of 2 (which is 4 in mod 7):

$$
b \equiv 4(3c + 6) \equiv 12c + 24 \equiv 5c + 3 \mod 7
$$

From Equation A:
$$
6a - 2b \equiv 4 \mod 7 \Rightarrow 6a \equiv 2b + 4 \mod 7
$$
Substitute $ b \equiv 5c + 3 $:
$$
6a \equiv 2(5c + 3) + 4 = 10c + 6 + 4 = 10c + 10 \equiv 3c + 3 \mod 7
$$
$$
\Rightarrow 2a \equiv c + 1 \mod 7 \Rightarrow c \equiv 2a - 1 \mod 7
$$

Now from Equation C:
$$
3c - d \equiv 2 \mod 7 \Rightarrow d \equiv 3c - 2 \mod 7
$$

We now have all variables expressed in terms of $ a $.

---

### Step 3: Determine the Largest Valid $ a $

To maximize $ N $, we want the **greatest possible** value of $ a $. However, we must **satisfy Equation 3**, derived from manipulations:

$$
6a + 2b + d \equiv 4 \mod 7
$$

Substitute all expressions in terms of $ a $, and after simplification, it leads to:
$$
2a \equiv 3 \mod 7 \Rightarrow a \equiv 5 \mod 7
$$

So the **only possible value for $ a $** is **5** (since $ a $ is a digit, and $ a=5 $ is the only digit in the range 1–9 that satisfies this).

---

### Step 4: Compute Remaining Digits

Given $ a = 5 $, compute all other digits:

- $ c \equiv 2a - 1 \equiv 2\cdot5 - 1 = 9 \mod 7 \Rightarrow c = 9 $ (since it's a digit)
- $ b \equiv 5c + 3 = 5\cdot9 + 3 = 48 \equiv 6 \mod 7 \Rightarrow b = 6 $
- $ d \equiv 3c - 2 = 3\cdot9 - 2 = 25 \equiv 4 \mod 7 \Rightarrow d = 4 $

So the number is:
$$
N = 1000a + 100b + 10c + d = 5694
$$

---

### Step 5: Verify Conditions

We'll briefly check that changing any digit to 1 results in a number divisible by 7:

- Change thousands digit to 1: $ 1694 \Rightarrow 1694 \div 7 = 242 $ ✅
- Change hundreds digit to 1: $ 5194 \Rightarrow 5194 \div 7 = 742 $ ✅
- Change tens digit to 1: $ 5614 \Rightarrow 5614 \div 7 = 802 $ ✅
- Change units digit to 1: $ 5691 \Rightarrow 5691 \div 7 = 813 $ ✅

All four conditions are satisfied.

---

### Step 6: Compute $ Q + R $

We are told to divide $ N $ by 1000:

$$
N = 5694 \Rightarrow \text{Quotient} = \left\lfloor \frac{5694}{1000} \right\rfloor = 5, \quad \text{Remainder} = 5694 - 5 \cdot 1000 = 694
$$

$$
Q + R = 5 + 694 = \boxed{699}
$$

### Final Answer

$$
\boxed{699}
