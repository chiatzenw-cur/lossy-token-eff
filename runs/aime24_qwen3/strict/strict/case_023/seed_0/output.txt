To solve this problem, we are given a list of **positive integers** with three specific conditions:

1. **The sum of the items in the list is 30.**
2. **The unique mode of the list is 9.**
3. **The median of the list is a positive integer that does not appear in the list itself.**

Our goal is to find the **sum of the squares of all the items** in such a list.

---

### Step 1: Understanding the Constraints

Let’s analyze the implications of each condition:

- The **sum is 30**. The list can be of any length as long as the total sum is 30.
- The **mode is unique and is 9**, so 9 must appear **more frequently than any other number**.
- The **median is not in the list**, which puts restrictions on how the numbers are arranged.

---

### Step 2: Implications of the Median Condition

The median is the **middle value of a sorted list**, but since the median is **not** in the list, the number of elements **must be even**. Why?

- If the number of elements is odd, the **median is the middle element**, which must be in the list. This **violates** the third condition.
- Therefore, the number of elements **must be even**.

So, we can eliminate any odd lengths of the list. Let’s proceed assuming **the list has an even number of elements**.

---

### Step 3: Searching for a Valid List

Let’s test small even-numbered lists to see if we can satisfy all the conditions.

#### Trying with $ n = 4 $

Suppose the list has 4 elements: $[a, b, c, d]$, sorted in increasing order.

- The median is $ (b + c)/2 $, which must be an **integer not present** in the list.
- The **mode is 9**, so it must appear more times than any other number.
- The **total sum is 30**, and all numbers are positive integers.

$$
[a, b, c, d] = [5, 7, 9, 9]
$$
