To find the **greatest real number that is less than $ BD^2 $ for all rhombuses $ ABCD $ with diagonals intersecting at the origin and all vertices lying on the hyperbola**  
$$
\frac{x^2}{20} - \frac{y^2}{24} = 1,
$$
we first need to understand the geometric conditions of the problem and reduce it to an algebraic minimization problem.

---

### Key Geometric Properties

- **Rhombus** with diagonals intersecting at the origin implies:
  - The diagonals are perpendicular and bisect each other.
  - The midpoint of both diagonals is the origin. So if $ A = (a, b) $, then $ C = (-a, -b) $, and if $ B = (c, d) $, then $ D = (-c, -d) $.
  - The diagonals $ AC $ and $ BD $ intersect perpendicularly at the origin.
  
- **Vectors**:
  - Since $ AC = \langle -2a, -2b \rangle $ and $ BD = \langle -2c, -2d \rangle $, the dot product $ \vec{AC} \cdot \vec{BD} = 0 $ gives:
    $$
    ( -2a)(-2c) + ( -2b)(-2d) = 4(ac + bd) = 0 \Rightarrow a c + b d = 0.
    $$
    Thus, **vectors $ \vec{OA} $ and $ \vec{OB} $ are perpendicular**.

---

### Reformulating the Problem

We are to **minimize $ BD^2 = 4(c^2 + d^2) $** under the condition:
- $ A = (a, b) $ and $ B = (c, d) $ lie on the hyperbola:  
  $$
  \frac{a^2}{20} - \frac{b^2}{24} = 1, \quad \frac{c^2}{20} - \frac{d^2}{24} = 1,
  $$
- and $ a c + b d = 0 $.

The **goal** is to find the **infimum** (greatest lower bound) of $ BD^2 $, since the problem asks for the **greatest real number that is less than $ BD^2 $ for all such rhombi**.

So we want to:
- Find the **smallest possible value** of $ c^2 + d^2 $,
- And from that, determine $ BD^2 = 4(c^2 + d^2) $.

---

### Minimizing $ c^2 + d^2 $

Let us define:
- $ S = c^2 + d^2 $, our target expression.
- From the hyperbola equation:
  $$
  \frac{c^2}{20} - \frac{d^2}{24} = 1 \Rightarrow c^2 = 20\left(1 + \frac{d^2}{24}\right).
  $$
- Substituting into $ S $:
  $$
  S = c^2 + d^2 = 20\left(1 + \frac{d^2}{24}\right) + d^2 = 20 + \frac{20}{24}d^2 + d^2 = 20 + \left(\frac{5}{6} + 1\right)d^2 = 20 + \frac{11}{6}d^2.
  $$

So,
$$
S = 20 + \frac{11}{6}d^2 \Rightarrow BD^2 = 4S = 80 + \frac{44}{6}d^2 = 80 + \frac{22}{3}d^2.
$$

Our next step is to **find the minimum value of $ BD^2 $** such that the perpendicularity condition $ a c + b d = 0 $ is satisfied, with both $ A $ and $ B $ lying on the hyperbola.

### Using Perpendicularity Condition

Suppose we consider point $ B = (c, d) $, and define point $ A = (d, -c) $. Then:
- Check if $ (a, b) = (d, -c) $ lies on the hyperbola:
  $$
  \frac{d^2}{20} - \frac{c^2}{24} = 1.

$$
\frac{c^2}{20} - \frac{d^2}{24} = 1.

We now have:
$$
\frac{d^2}{20} - \frac{c^2}{24} = \text{unknown}, \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{c^2}{20} - \frac{d^2}{24} = 1.
$$

Let’s subtract the two:
$$
\left(\frac{d^2}{20} - \frac{c^2}{24}\right) - \left(\frac{c^2}{20} - \frac{d^2}{24}\right) = 0.
$$

Simplifying:
$$
\frac{d^2}{20} - \frac{c^2}{24} - \frac{c^2}{20} + \frac{d^2}{24} = 0 \\
\Rightarrow d^2\left(\frac{1}{20} + \frac{1}{24}\right) - c^2\left(\frac{1}{20} + \frac{1}{24}\right) = 0.

This gives:
$$
d^2 = c^2.
$$

Substituting into the equation for $ B $:
$$
\frac{c^2}{20} - \frac{c^2}{24} = 1 \\
\Rightarrow c^2\left(\frac{1}{20} - \frac{1}{24}\right) = 1 \\
\Rightarrow c^2 = \frac{1}{\frac{1}{20} - \frac{1}{24}} = \frac{1}{\frac{6 - 5}{120}} = \frac{1}{\frac{1}{120}} = 120.
$$

So $ c^2 = d^2 = 120 $, and hence:
$$
BD^2 = 4(c^2 + d^2) = 4(120 + 120) = 4 \times 240 = \boxed{960}.
$$

This shows that **there exists a rhombus** with $ BD^2 = 960 $, and it’s achieved as long as both $ A $ and $ B $ are on the hyperbola and perpendicular.
