To solve the problem, we analyze the situation using **conditional probability**, because we are asked to compute the probability that Jen wins the **grand prize** *given* that she **won a prize**.

---

### Step 1: Total Number of Lottery Combinations

Jen chooses **4 distinct numbers** from the set $ S = \{1, 2, 3, \dots, 10\} $, and the lottery draws **4 distinct numbers** randomly from this same set. Therefore, the total number of possible lottery outcomes — that is, the set of all 4-number combinations — is:

$$
\binom{10}{4} = 210
$$

---

### Step 2: Understanding Events A and B

Let:
- **Event A** be "Jen wins the grand prize", i.e., all **4 of her numbers** match the **4 randomly chosen numbers**.
- **Event B** be "Jen wins a prize", i.e., **at least two** of her numbers match.

Because winning the grand prize **implies** winning the prize (since a grand prize outcome has 4 matches, which are certainly at least two), we have **A ⊆ B**, so:

$$
P(A \cap B) = P(A)
$$

The conditional probability is then:

$$
P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(A)}{P(B)}
$$

---

### Step 3: Calculating $ P(A) $

The only lottery draw that matches all of Jen’s 4 numbers exactly is the specific combination she chose. Since there are 210 possible draws, the probability that she wins the grand prize is:

$$
P(A) = \frac{1}{210}
$$

---

### Step 4: Calculating $ P(B) $

We want the probability that **at least two** of her numbers match. Instead of computing this directly, we calculate the complement — the probability of having **fewer than two** matches, i.e., **zero** or **exactly one** match.

- **Zero matches**: All 4 numbers are from the **remaining 6 numbers** not picked by Jen. So:

$$
\binom{6}{4} = 15
$$

- **Exactly one match**: Choose **1** number from her 4, and **3** numbers from the remaining 6. So:

$$
\binom{4}{1} \cdot \binom{6}{3} = 4 \cdot 20 = 80
$$

Total number of unfavorable combinations (zero or one match):

$$
15 + 80 = 95
$$

Thus, the number of favorable combinations for B (at least two matches):

$$
210 - 95 = 115
$$

So:

$$
P(B) = \frac{115}{210}
$$

Now compute:

P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(A)}{P(B)} = \frac{1/210}{115/210} = \frac{1}{115}
