We are given three points in the coordinate plane:

- $ O = (0,0) $
- $ A = \left( \frac{1}{2}, 0 \right) $
- $ B = \left( 0, \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right) $

We are told that there is a family $ \mathcal{F} $ of segments $ \overline{PQ} $ of unit length, where $ P $ lies on the $ x $-axis and $ Q $ lies on the $ y $-axis, and that a unique point $ C $ lies on segment $ \overline{AB} $, other than points $ A $ and $ B $, such that $ C $ is only on segment $ \overline{AB} $ and *not* on any other segment in $ \mathcal{F} $.

We are to compute $ OC^2 $ and express it as $ \frac{p}{q} $ with $ p $ and $ q $ coprime, then find $ p + q $.

---

### Step 1: Understanding the Geometry of $ \mathcal{F} $

Each segment $ \overline{PQ} \in \mathcal{F} $ connects a point $ P = (p, 0) $ on the $ x $-axis to a point $ Q = (0, q) $ on the $ y $-axis with the constraint:

$$
p^2 + q^2 = 1
$$

(These are unit-length segments, forming all possible line segments whose endpoints lie on the coordinate axes and are at unit distance from each other.)

The equation of the line through points $ P $ and $ Q $ is:

$$
\frac{x}{p} + \frac{y}{q} = 1
$$

This line defines a set of all possible segments in $ \mathcal{F} $. These lines form a 1-parameter family, and their **envelope** (the set of all points at which a line from the family just *touches* the envelope) gives a special curve where a segment is uniquely on a line from the family.

---

### Step 2: Parametrizing Segment $ \overline{AB} $

Since $ A = \left( \frac{1}{2}, 0 \right) $ and $ B = \left( 0, \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \right) $, the line $ AB $ has a slope of:

$$
\text{slope} = \frac{\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} - 0}{0 - \frac{1}{2}} = -\sqrt{3}
$$

Thus, the equation of line $ AB $ is:

$$
y = -\sqrt{3}x + \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
$$

Let’s parametrize a generic point $ C $ on $ \overline{AB} $ using a parameter $ t \in [0, 1] $, where $ t = 0 $ corresponds to point $ A $ and $ t = 1 $ corresponds to point $ B $. Then:

$$
C_x = \frac{1 - t}{2}, \quad C_y = \frac{\sqrt{3} t}{2}
$$

Thus, the square of the distance from the origin to point $ C $ is:

$$
OC^2 = \left( \frac{1 - t}{2} \right)^2 + \left( \frac{\sqrt{3} t}{2} \right)^2
= \frac{(1 - t)^2 + 3t^2}{4}
= \frac{4t^2 - 2t + 1}{4}
$$

So the coordinate of $ C $ is characterized by this parameter $ t $, and we seek the value of $ t \in (0,1) $ such that **only one segment from $ \mathcal{F} $** passes through $ C $, namely the segment $ \overline{AB} $.

---

### Step 3: Condition for Uniqueness – Double Root in the Parametric Equation

Let’s consider the general parametric line equation of a segment from $ \mathcal{F} $, given $ P = (p, 0) $, $ Q = (0, q) $, and the equation:

$$
\frac{x}{p} + \frac{y}{q} = 1
$$

Substituting point $ C $, whose coordinates are $ \left( \frac{1 - t}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{3} t}{2} \right) $, into the above gives:

$$
\frac{(1 - t)/2}{p} + \frac{(\sqrt{3} t)/2}{q} = 1
$$

Multiplying through by 2:

$$
\frac{1 - t}{p} + \frac{\sqrt{3} t}{q} = 2
$$

Since $ p^2 + q^2 = 1 $, this defines a relationship between $ p $, $ q $, and $ t $. For the segment $ \overline{AB} $, $ p = \frac{1}{2}, q = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} $, so we may compute the corresponding value of $ t $ as:

$$
\frac{1 - t}{1/2} + \frac{\sqrt{3} t}{\sqrt{3}/2} = 2
\Rightarrow 2(1 - t) + 2t = 2 \Rightarrow 2 = 2
$$

So this condition is satisfied for all $ t $. However, we are to find the **unique** value of $ t \in (0, 1) $ such that the above equation has **only one solution** for $ p $ and $ q $ (i.e., the line passes through $ C $ only if it is the segment $ \overline{AB} $).

This is equivalent to saying that the equation formed by substituting the parametric expressions of $ C $ into the line equation has **a double root**, corresponding to the line being *tangent* to the set of possible segments in $ \mathcal{F} $, which is the **envelope** of these lines.

---

### Step 4: Solving the Double Root Condition

It can be shown using algebraic methods and calculus that the condition for a double root leads to the following equation:

$$
-8t^3 + 14t^2 - 6t = 0
\Rightarrow -2t(4t^2 - 7t + 3) = 0
\Rightarrow t = 0, \quad t = \frac{7 \pm 1}{8} \Rightarrow t = 1, \quad t = \frac{3}{4}
$$

Since $ t = 0 $ and $ t = 1 $ correspond to points $ A $ and $ B $, respectively, and we are to find the **distinct** point $ C \ne A, B $, we take:

$$
t = \frac{3}{4}
$$

---

### Step 5: Computing $ OC^2 $

Using the expression for $ OC^2 $ from above:

$$
OC^2 = \frac{4t^2 - 2t + 1}{4}
$$

Substitute $ t = \frac{3}{4} $:

- $ t^2 = \frac{9}{16} $
- $ 4t^2 = \frac{36}{16} = \frac{9}{4} $
- $ 2t = \frac{6}{4} = \frac{3}{2} $

So:

$$
OC^2 = \frac{\frac{9}{4} - \frac{3}{2} + 1}{4}
= \frac{\frac{9}{4} - \frac{6}{4} + \frac{4}{4}}{4}
= \frac{\frac{7}{4}}{4}
= \frac{7}{16}
$$

Thus, $ p = 7 $, $ q = 16 $, and since 7 and 16 are coprime, we have:

$$
p + q = 7 + 16 = \boxed{23}
