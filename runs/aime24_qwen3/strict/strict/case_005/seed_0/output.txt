### Step 1: Understand the divisibility condition

We need $ p^2 \mid n^4 + 1 $, which implies:
$$
n^4 \equiv -1 \pmod{p^2}
$$

For this congruence to hold, $ n^4 \equiv -1 \pmod{p} $ must also hold, so:
$$
n^4 \equiv -1 \pmod{p}
$$

This means that $ -1 $ must be a **quartic residue** modulo $ p $. However, for this to be possible, certain conditions on the order of elements in the multiplicative group modulo $ p $ must be satisfied.

---

### Step 2: Fix prime $ p $ such that $ p^2 \mid n^4 + 1 $

The multiplicative group modulo $ p $, $ \mathbb{Z}_p^* $, is cyclic of order $ p - 1 $. For there to exist an $ n \in \mathbb{Z}_p^* $ such that:
$$
n^4 \equiv -1 \pmod{p}
$$
it must be that the order of $ n $ modulo $ p $ is divisible by $ 8 $, as:
$$
n^8 \equiv 1 \pmod{p}, \quad \text{and} \quad n^4 \equiv -1 \pmod{p}
$$

So the order of $ n $ must be **exactly** $ 8 $, and since the group $ \mathbb{Z}_p^* $ has order $ p - 1 $, this implies:
$$
8 \mid (p - 1)
\quad \text{or} \quad
p \equiv 1 \pmod{8}
$$
