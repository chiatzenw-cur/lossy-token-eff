We're told that $ C $ is a March point that lies **on the segment $ \overline{AB} $** (but not at $ A $ or $ B $), and that **no other segment point of length 1 from the family $ \mathcal{F} $** (lines $ PQ $ of unit length with $ P $ on the x-axis and $ Q $ on the y-axis) **passes through $ C $**, except for the segment $ \overline{AB} $ itself.

---

### Step 1: Parametrize Segment $ \overline{AB} $

Let $ C $ be any point on $ \overline{AB} $. We can parametrize it as:

$$
C = \left( \tfrac{1}{2}(1 - t), \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}t \right), \quad \text{for some } t \in (0,1).
$$

We denote this parameter as $ t_{AB} $. So:

$$
x = \tfrac{1}{2}(1 - t_{AB}) , \quad y = \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}t_{AB}
$$

and the coordinates of $ C $ are:
$$
C = \left( \tfrac{1}{2}(1 - t_{AB}), \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}t_{AB} \right)
$$

---

### Step 2: Condition for Non-Other Segments Passing Through $ C $

Let $ P = (p, 0) $ and $ Q = (0, q) $ be endpoints of a unit length segment $ \overline{PQ} \in \mathcal{F} $. So:

$$
p^2 + q^2 = 1
$$

Any point on $ \overline{PQ} $ has coordinates:
$$
(p(1 - t), qt)
$$

If $ C $ lies on the segment $ \overline{PQ} $, then the same point must satisfy:

$$
x = p(1 - t) , \quad y = qt
$$

So from here, we can solve for $ p $ and $ q $:

$$
p = \frac{x}{1 - t}, \quad q = \frac{y}{t}
$$

Substituting into the constraint $ p^2 + q^2 = 1 $, we get:

$$
\frac{x^2}{(1 - t)^2} + \frac{y^2}{t^2} = 1
$$

This equation gives a condition on $ t $ for which a point $ (x, y) $ lies on the segment $ \overline{PQ} $, and so:

$$
\frac{x^2}{(1 - t)^2} + \frac{y^2}{t^2} = 1
$$

Now, since $ C = (x, y) $ lies on $ \overline{AB} $, $ x $ and $ y $ depend on $ t_{AB} $. Substituting:

$$
x = \tfrac{1}{2}(1 - t_{AB}) , \quad y = \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}t_{AB}
$$

$$
\frac{\left( \tfrac{1}{2}(1 - t_{AB}) \right)^2}{(1 - t)^2} + \frac{\left( \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}t_{AB} \right)^2}{t^2} = 1
$$

Multiply both sides by 4 to simplify:

$$
\frac{(1 - t_{AB})^2}{(1 - t)^2} + \frac{3 t_{AB}^2}{t^2} = 4
$$

### Step 3: Uniqueness Condition via Double Root

To ensure uniqueness, we impose the condition that $ t = t_{AB} $ is a **double root** of $ f(t) = 0 $. This happens when both the function and its derivative vanish at $ t = t_{AB} $.

So:

$$
f(t_{AB}) = 0 \quad \text{(already satisfied)}
$$
$$
f'(t_{AB}) = 0
$$

Compute the derivative:

$$

$$

Set $ f'(t_{AB}) = 0 $:

$$
\frac{2(1 - t_{AB})^2}{(1 - t_{AB})^3} - \frac{6 t_{AB}^2}{t_{AB}^3} = 0

$$

\Rightarrow \frac{2}{1 - t_{AB}} = \frac{6}{t_{AB}}
