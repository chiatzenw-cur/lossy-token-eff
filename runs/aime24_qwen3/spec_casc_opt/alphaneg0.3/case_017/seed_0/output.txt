### Step 1: Use the Perpendicular Condition

The condition that $ IA \perp OI $ implies that triangle $ AIO $ is a right triangle with the right angle at $ I $, so by the Pythagorean Theorem:
$$
IA^2 + OI^2 = AO^2 = R^2
$$Solving this right triangle will help us relate the inradius and other triangle values.

 Recall the formula for the length $ IA $ \textit{from the incenter to vertex A}:
$$
IA = \frac{r}{\sin (A/2)}
$$
Also, Euler's formula gives the squared distance between incenter and circumcenter:
$$
OI^2 = R(R - 2rticker) = R^2 - 2Rr
$$Substituting into the Pythagorean relationship:
$$
\left( \frac{r}{\sin (A/2)} \right)^2 + (R^2 - 2Rr) = R^2
$$
This simplifies to:
$$
\frac{r^2}{\sin^2 (A/2)} = 2Rr \quad \Rightarrow \quad \frac{r}{\sin^2 (A/2)} = 2R
$$
$$
\sin^2 \left( \frac{A}{2} \right) = \frac{r}{2R} = \frac{6}{26} = \frac{3}{13}
\Rightarrow \sin \left( \frac{A}{2} \right) = \sqrt{ \frac{3}{13} }
$$

### Step 2: Find $ \cos A $ and $ \sin A $
