We are given that Alice chooses a set $ A $ of positive integers, and Bob lists all finite nonempty sets $ B $ of positive integers such that the **maximum element** of $ B $ belongs to $ A $. Bob's list contains exactly **2024** sets. Our goal is to determine the **sum of the elements** in $ A $.

---

### **Understanding the Problem**

For a single element $ a \in A $, the number of sets $ B $ that **maximize** at $ a $ (i.e., have $ a $ as their maximum) is equal to the number of subsets of $ \{1, 2, \dots, a - 1\} $, since each such subset can be combined with $ a $ to form a set with maximum $ a $. Therefore, there are $ 2^{a - 1} $ such sets.

Hence, for a general set $ A = \{a_1, a_2, \dots, a_k\} $ (with elements sorted), the total number of sets Bob lists is:

$$
\sum_{a \in A} 2^{a - 1}
$$

We are told this total is **2024**.

---

### **Reduction to Binary Representation**

The expression $ \sum_{a \in A} 2^{a - 1} = 2024 $ means that **2024 is expressed as the sum of distinct powers of 2**, where each power corresponds to $ a - 1 $ for each element $ a \in A $. This implies that the **positions of the 1s in the binary representation of 2024** correspond to the exponents $ a - 1 $, and therefore each element of $ A $ is $ e + 1 $, where $ e $ is the position (i.e., the power of two) of a 1 in the binary expansion.

---

### **Step-by-Step Calculation**

Let’s compute the **binary representation of 2024**:

- $ 2024 = 2^{10} + 2^9 + 2^8 + 2^7 + 2^6 + 2^5 + 2^3 $
- This corresponds to the binary number: 11111101000

Now, identify the **positions of the 1s** (counting from the **right**, starting at 0):
- Positions with 1s: 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10

Then, the elements of $ A $ are:
$$
a_i = e_i + 1 \quad \text{for } e_i = \text{positions of 1s in binary}
$$
$$
\Rightarrow A = \{4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11\}
$$
