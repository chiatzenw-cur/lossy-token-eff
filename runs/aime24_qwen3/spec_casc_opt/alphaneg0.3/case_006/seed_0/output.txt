To solve this problem, we are given a tetrahedron $ABCD$ with the edge lengths:

We're told that there exists a point $I$ inside the tetrahedron equidistant to all four faces. This implies the existence of an incenter (the point equidistant to all faces), and hence the tetrahedron supports an inscribed sphere. The radius $r$ of such a sphere is equal to the *inradius* of the tetrahedron, which is defined by the formula:

$$
r = \frac{3V}{S}
$$

where:
- $V$ is the volume of the tetrahedron,
- $S$ is the total surface area of the tetrahedron.

### Step 1: Properties of the Tetrahedron

All faces of the tetrahedron are congruent triangles with side lengths $ \sqrt{41}, \sqrt{80}, \sqrt{89} $. This opens the possibility that the tetrahedron is a **disphenoid**, a tetrahedron with congruent faces and opposite edges equal. For such tetrahedrons, we can place the vertices in 3D space in a convenient form that respects these symmetry properties.

Using a known symmetric configuration, we assign coordinates:

- $A = (2, \frac{5}{2}, 4)$
- $B = (-2, -\frac{5}{2}, 4)$
- $C = (-2, \frac{5}{2}, -4)$

### Step 2: Compute the Volume (Using Triple Scalar Product)

We compute the volume using the scalar triple product:

$$

From the coordinates, we compute the vectors:

0 & -5 & -8
