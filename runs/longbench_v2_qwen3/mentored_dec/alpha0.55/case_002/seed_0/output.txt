### Key Analysis:

The **TRIPS Agreement**, particularly **Article 61**, imposes a **minimum standard** requiring Members to provide **criminal procedures and penalties for wilful trademark counterfeiting or copyright piracy on a commercial scale**. This obligation includes penalties such as imprisonment or monetary fines, and the threshold is tied to qualifying acts as "on a commercial scale."

The **Panel's findings** are crucial here:

- The **United States argued** that China’s criminal thresholds are too low and thus fail to criminalize acts that could clearly be deemed "on a commercial scale."

- The **Panel emphasized** that "commercial scale" is a **relative** and **context-dependent** concept—not an absolute standard. It requires evaluation based on the **nature of the act, the market, and the product**, rather than rigid numerical or quantitative benchmarks.
- The **Panel acknowledged** that the **TRIPS Agreement does not mandate uniform thresholds** or prescriptive enforcement mechanisms across all Members, which reinforces that China's **measures are in line with the minimum standards** required by the TRIPS Agreement.

The **Panel also concluded** (at **paragraph 7.669**) that the **U.S. had not made a prima facie case** that the thresholds violate Article 61. Thus, the **thresholds are not inconsistent** with the obligations of the TRIPS Agreement, even though they may not cover **every** act that could be considered "on a commercial scale."
