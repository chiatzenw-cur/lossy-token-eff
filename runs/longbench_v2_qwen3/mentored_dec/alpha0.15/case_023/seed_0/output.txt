While 3DGS models the full volumetric radiance field and suffers from **depth inconsistency** due to affine approximations in perspective projection (which introduces errors at the edges and in varying viewpoints), 2DGS **avoids** this by embedding **2D disks** directly into 3D space and using **explicit ray-splat intersections**. This explicit method ensures that the projected positions of the disks are **perspectively accurate**, and their interpolation along rays preserves consistent depth and surface alignment, avoiding the distortions inherent in the volumetric approach of 3DGS.

### 1. **3DGS and its Limitations**
In 3DGS, the **affine approximation** of the projective transformation is used to project 3D Gaussians onto the image plane. However, this method only ensures **local consistency** at the Gaussian centers, leading to **inconsistent depth** when viewed from different angles due to the **multi-view inconsistency of 3D Gaussians**. These inconsistencies make it difficult to model surfaces with thin structures or high curvature. The **volumetric nature** of 3DGS **conflicts** with the **planar and thin nature** of real-world object surfaces, degrading both **geometric accuracy** and **rendering quality**.

---

### 2. **2DGS Approach and Its Advantages**
In contrast, 2DGS **collapses the 3D volume** into a **set of 2D oriented elliptical disks** embedded in 3D space. These disks are parameterized by **tangent vectors** and a **scaling factor**, and their **normal vector** is computed as the **cross product of the tangent vectors**. This ensures that the **surface normal** is **inherently aligned** with the actual surface geometry.

Moreover, 2DGS **explicitly computes the intersection of rays with splats** using **homogeneous transformations** and **ray-splat intersection** in **world space**. This method avoids the inaccuracies introduced by affine projections in 3DGS. As a result, the **depth** of each splat is accurately determined at each pixel, ensuring **multi-view consistency** and **accurate depth mappings**. 

The key mathematical reasoning is that 2DGS **directly computes surface normals from the tangent space** (defined by the principal direction vectors), which aligns the splats with the **actual surface geometry**. This avoids the need for **inference from depth gradients**, which is a primary limitation of 3DGS.
