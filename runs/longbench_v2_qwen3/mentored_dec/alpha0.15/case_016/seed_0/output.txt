2. **Multiliteracies (New London Group, Cope & Kalantzis)**:
   - Expands the definition of literacy to **include multimodal forms** (visual, audio, spatial, etc.).
   - Focuses on **multiple ways of representing meaning** and the **changing social, economic, and technological environments**.
   - While it embraces **cultural and linguistic diversity**, it is increasingly associated with **digitally mediated forms** (e.g., internet, multimedia).
   - It also engages with **power** in the context of **globalization** and **post-Fordist economic shifts**, but its practical implications often revolve around **technical proficiency** in new communication modes.

3. **Critical Literacy (Freire, Brandt)**:
   - **Centers on power, social justice**, and **empowerment**.
   - Sees literacy as a **social practice** that can be **transformative** when used critically.
   - Identity is tied to **power dynamics**, and literacy is a **tool of resistance** or **agency** against oppressive systems.
   - Critically examines **how power relations** influence who has access to literacy resources and how these resources are used.
