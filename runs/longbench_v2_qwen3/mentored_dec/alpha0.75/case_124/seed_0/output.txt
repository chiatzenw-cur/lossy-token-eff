It clarified that although the **Bankruptcy Code** imposes distinct consequences based on this classification, the **characterization of the contract is governed by state law**.
- **Bright-Line Test**: The **bright-line test**, part of Section 1-201(37) of the UCC, was applied, which includes factors like (1) the lease term relative to the goods’ economic life, (2) renewal guarantees, (3) an option to purchase for nominal consideration, or (4) the lease owner’s obligation to pay taxes or other expenses. WorldCom argued that the purchase option was **nominal**, but failed to demonstrate that the **reasonably predictable cost of performance was greater than the nominal consideration**, due to the lack of evidence about the parties' **anticipated values or expectations** at the time of the agreement.
- **Contextual Analysis**: If the bright-line test is not satisfied, the court then considers a **contextual analysis**—primarily whether the **lessor retains a "meaningful reversionary interest"** in the goods, as outlined in **In re Addison** and **In re Zaleha**. The court stressed that the **key to distinguishing a security interest from a lease** lies in whether the **lessor has an economically meaningful residual interest**. This includes an analysis of whether the **lessee can effectively return the equipment** (reversionary right), the **fungibility of the goods**, and the **utilities of the lease terms**.
