The key to understanding the superior surface projection accuracy of 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) lies in how it directly addresses the limitations of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), particularly regarding surface normals and depth consistency. 

### Core Issue with 3DGS:
3DGS represents surfaces using full 3D Gaussians, which inherently model volumetric radiance. This can result in **inconsistent depth** and **inaccurate surface normal estimates** during rendering, especially when viewed from different angles. The use of affine transformations for projection introduces **perspective distortion**, while the lack of explicit surface normal modeling leads to **inaccurate surface alignment**.

### Key Innovation in 2DGS:
2DGS collapses the 3D volume into 2D elliptical disks embedded in 3D space. This approach allows **direct alignment of the surface normals** by explicitly defining them as the cross product of the tangent vectors that define the orientation of each disk. These tangent vectors are derived from the 2D Gaussian's principal directions, ensuring that the **surface normals are inherently consistent with the surface geometry**. This avoids the multi-view inconsistencies that arise in 3DGS due to its inability to directly model surface normals.

### Mathematical Reasoning:
- **3DGS** uses a covariance matrix $ \Sigma $ to define the orientation and scaling of each Gaussian, which must be factorized into a scaling matrix and rotation matrix. These matrices are then used with affine projections to transform into image space, but this approach is prone to **perspective inaccuracies** and **depth inconsistencies**.
- **2DGS** avoids this by expressing the surface normals in terms of the tangent vectors $ \mathbf{t}_u $ and $ \mathbf{t}_v $, which are part of the 2D Gaussian's intrinsic orientation. The normal $ \mathbf{N} $ is computed as their cross product $ \mathbf{t}_u \times \mathbf{t}_v $, ensuring **direct and consistent alignment with surface normals**. This approach inherently avoids the angular inconsistencies and distortions seen in 3DGS, as the normals are not derived from depth gradients, which vary with the intersection plane.
