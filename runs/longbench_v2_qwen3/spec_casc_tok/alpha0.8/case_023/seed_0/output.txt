### Key Differences Between 2DGS and 3DGS

1. **3DGS** represents scenes using 3D Gaussians, which are volumes defined by full 3D covariance matrices (`Σ`). This allows for modeling of all angular components of the radiance field, but leads to:
   - **Depth inconsistency**: The affine projection of Gaussians onto the image plane introduces perspective errors, especially when rendering from different viewpoints.
   - **Inherent lack of surface fidelity**: While the 3D Gaussians are volumetric and offer a continuous representation, they do not directly model the surface geometry or normals. This leads to the necessity of deriving normals from *depth gradients*, which introduces inaccuracies in surface reconstruction.

2. **2DGS**, by contrast, **collapses** the volumetric representation into 2D elliptical disks (splatting), each associated with:
   - A **tangent plane** and smoothing using surface elements (surfel-like properties).
   - **Direct alignment with surface normals** via the orientation of tangent vectors.
   - **Ray-splat intersection** that ensures the projection is **perspectively correct**, avoiding the distortion caused by affine approximations in 3DGS.

---

### Why 2DGS Achieves Superior Surface Projection Accuracy

The main advantage of 2DGS lies in how it **aligns the projected shape with the actual surface geometry** across multiple viewpoints. This is made possible through:

- **Explicit ray-splat intersection**: In 3DGS, the affine approximation of a 3D Gaussian leads to inaccurate projections across views. In 2DGS, each 2D disk is projected using the ray-splat intersection technique that matches the viewpoint directly, which ensures **consistent depth and normal alignment** across view changes.
- **Tangent space-based surface orientation**: The surface normals in 2DGS are derived directly from the orientation of the tangent vectors, ensuring that each splat **maintains alignment with the actual surface geometry**, not just a depth map or an approximation from gradients.

These differences are explicitly highlighted in the text:  
- The text states that **2DGS explicitly defines normals through 2D tangent planes**, which prevents the angular inaccuracies that occur due to perspective distortions in 3DGS.  
- The **affine projection** of 3D Gaussians in 3DGS is criticized for introducing errors, while **2DGS avoids this by aligning the splat normals with the actual surface orientation**.
