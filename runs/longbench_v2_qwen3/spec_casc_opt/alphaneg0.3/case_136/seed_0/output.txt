### **Understanding the Question and Key Differences**

The question focuses on **the differences in how the Panel and the Appellate Body addressed the issue of "double remedies"** in the context of the **United States imposing anti-dumping and countervailing duties simultaneously on Chinese goods**. The goal is to identify which option best reflects their divergent findings and reasoning.

---

### **Analyzing the Panel's Position (WT/DS379/R)**

The Panel **rejected China's claim** that the U.S. was imposing a "double remedy" in its application of anti-dumping duties calculated using the NME methodology **alongside countervailing duties**. Their key reasoning was:

- **Article 19., of the SCM Agreement** imposes a limit on countervailing duties (they cannot exceed the subsidy amount), but it **does not address double remedies** explicitly.
- The **U.S. method of calculating normal value using surrogate (market economy) data** was seen as a legitimate method to determine whether a subsidy existed.
- The Panel determined that **the countervailing duty did not exceed the subsidy amount**, and hence **did not violate Article 19.4**.
- They emphasized the **narrow focus of the SCM Agreement** on subsidies and countervailing duties and were **not bound to address double remedies** explicitly.

The **Panel found no inconsistency** between the U.S. actions and its obligations under the SCM Agreement or the GATT 1994.

---

### **Analyzing the Appellate Body's Position (WT/DS379/AB/R)**

The Appellate Body **reversed the Panel's interpretation** of Article 19.3 of the SCM Agreement. Key aspects of their reasoning included:

- ** Article 19.3** requires countervailing duties to be levied **in the "appropriate amounts"**, which the Appellate Body interpreted as **not being determined solely by the subsidy amount** but also considering the **effect of anti-dumping duties** on the same subsidy.
- They emphasized a **coherent interpretation** of the SCM Agreement and the Anti-Dumping Agreement, meaning **both remedies must be assessed together** for consistency with the goal of offsetting subsidies causing injury.
- Therefore, if an anti-dumping duty already offsets part of the subsidy, **a countervailing duty should not be applied to the same subsidy**, or the **amount should be adjusted** accordingly.
- The **Appellate Body concluded that applying both duties, and especially using the same surrogate values to offset the same subsidy, could lead to double remedies** and hence **inconsistency** with **Article 19.3** of the SCM Agreement.

This showed that **the Appellate Body found the U.S. action inconsistent with the SCM Agreement**, and emphasized the sense that **both remedies should be analyzed in a holistic, context-driven manner**, rather than mechanically or separately.
