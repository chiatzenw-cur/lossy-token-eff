### 2.  Length \(DB\)

The tangents at \(B\) and \(C\) meet at \(D\).  
The angle between two such tangents is

\[
\angle BDC=180^\circ-\angle BOC
          =180^\circ-2\angle BAC ,
\]

hence  

\[
\cos\angle BDC
    =\cos(180^\circ-2A)
    =-\cos(2A)
    =-\bigl(2\cos^{2}A-1\bigr)
    =-\bigl(2(\tfrac{11}{25})^{2}-1\bigr)
    =\frac{383}{625}.
\]

With \(BC=9\) and \(BD=DC\) equal (\(D\) is the intersection of the two tangents), the law of cosines in \(\triangle BDC\) gives

\[
9^{2}=2\,BD^{2}\!\left(1-\frac{383}{625}\right)

\]

so  

\[
BD^{2}=\frac{81\cdot625}{2\cdot242}
      =\frac{50625}{484},

### 3.  Length \(AD\)

In triangle \(ABD\) the angle \(\angle ABD\) is the angle between the tangent at \(B\) and the chord \(AB\).  
By the tangent–chord theorem,

\[
\angle ABD=\angle ACB=C\;,
\]
hence  

\[
\angle ABD=180^\circ-C\quad(\text{interior angle of } \triangle ABD),
\]

because the interior angle of the triangle uses the larger of the two intersecting angles.  
Thus  

\[
\cos\angle ABD=\cos(180^\circ-C)=-\cos C=-\frac{13}{15}.
\]

\[
\begin{aligned}
AD^{2}

### 4.  Length \(AP\)

Point \(D\) lies outside the circle, and the line \(AD\) meets the circle again at \(P\).  
For the external point \(D\) the power of the point gives

\[
DA\cdot DP = DB^{2},
\]

\[
DA\bigl(DA-AP\bigr)=DB^{2}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
DA\cdot AP = DA^{2}-DB^{2}.
\]

\[
AP=\frac{DA^{2}-DB^{2}}{DA}
