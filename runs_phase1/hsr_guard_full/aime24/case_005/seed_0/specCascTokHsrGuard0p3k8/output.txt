We need a prime \(p\) for which there exists \(n\) with  

\[
p^{2}\mid n^{4}+1 \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad n^{4}\equiv -1 \pmod{p^{2}} .
\]

For an odd prime \(p\) the multiplicative group \((\mathbb Z/p^{2}\mathbb Z)^{\times}\) is cyclic of order  
\(\varphi(p^{2})=p(p-1)\).  
If \(n^{4}\equiv -1\pmod{p^{2}}\) then \(x=n\) satisfies \(x^{8}\equiv1\); hence \(x\) has order \(8\).  
Thus the group must contain an element of order \(8\), i.e. \(8\mid p(p-1)\).

Because \(p\) is prime and \(p\neq 2\), the factor \(8\) must divide \(p-1\).  
Hence we need

\[
p\equiv 1 \pmod{8}.
\]

The smallest prime in this arithmetic progression is \(p=17\).

(Checking \(p=2,3,5,7,11,13\) shows that no \(p^{2}\) divides any \(n^{4}+1\).)
