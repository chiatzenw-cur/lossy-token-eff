The four numbers obtained by replacing one digit of \(N\) by \(1\) are  

\[
\begin{aligned}
N_1 &= 1000+100b+10c+d,\\
N_2 &= 1000a+100+10c+d,\\
N_3 &= 1000a+100b+10+d,\\

\end{aligned}
\]

Each must be divisible by \(7\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### Congruences modulo \(7\)

Because  

\[
1000\equiv 6,\qquad 100\equiv 2,\qquad 10\equiv 3,\qquad 1\equiv 1\pmod 7,
\]

the conditions become  

\[
\begin{cases}
6+2b+3c+d \equiv 0,\\
6a+2+3c+d \equiv 0,\\
6a+2b+3+d \equiv 0,\\
6a+2b+3c+1 \equiv 0.
\end{cases}
\]

Subtracting \(0\) from each yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
2b+3c+d &\equiv 1, \tag{1}\\
6a+3c+d &\equiv 5, \tag{2}\\
6a+2b+d &\equiv 4, \tag{3}\\
6a+2b+3c &\equiv 6. \tag{4}
\end{aligned}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### Solving the system

From (1)  

\[
d \equiv 1-2b-3c \pmod 7. \tag{5}
\]

Insert (5) into (2) and (3):

\[
\begin{aligned}
6a-2b &\equiv 4,\\
6a-3c &\equiv 3. \tag{6}
\end{aligned}
\]

Using (4) gives

\[
6a+2b+3c \equiv 6. \tag{7}
\]

Solve (6) and (7).

From \(6a-2b\equiv 4\) we get  
\(2b \equiv 6a-4\;\Rightarrow\; b \equiv 3a+5 \pmod 7.\)

From \(6a-3c \equiv 3\) we get  
\(3c \equiv 6a-3\;\Rightarrow\; c \equiv 2a+6 \pmod 7.\)

\[
6a+2(3a+5)+3(2a+6) \equiv 6
