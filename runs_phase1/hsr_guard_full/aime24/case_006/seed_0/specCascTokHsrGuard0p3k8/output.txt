inside the tetrahedron whose distances to the faces are equal must be  
\((1:1:1:1)\); that point is the centroid of the vertices.  
Let \(G\) be this centroid.  
The distance from \(G\) to any face is the radius \(r\) of the insphere.

---

### 1.  Coordinates of the vertices

Choose a coordinate system:

\[
A=(0,0,0),\quad B=(\sqrt{41},0,0).
\]

Let \(C=(x_C,y_C,0)\) with

\[
\begin{cases}
x_C^2+y_C^2=80,\\
(x_C-\sqrt{41})^2+y_C^2=89.
\end{cases}
\]

Solving gives  

\[
x_C=\frac{16}{\sqrt{41}},\qquad
y_C^2=\frac{3024}{41}\Longrightarrow
y_C=4\sqrt{\frac{189}{41}} .
\]

Let \(D=(x_D, y_D, z_D)\) with

\[
\begin{cases}
x_D^2+y_D^2+z_D^2=89,\\
(x_D-\sqrt{41})^2+y_D^2+z_D^2=80,\\
(x_D-x_C)^2+(y_D-y_C)^2+z_D^2=41 .
\end{cases}
\]

From the first two equations

\[
x_D=\frac{25}{\sqrt{41}} .
\]

Using the third equation and the value of \(x_D\) gives the relation

\[
x_Dx_C+y_Dy_C=64,
\]
hence

\[
y_D=\frac{2224}{41y_C}
      =\frac{556\sqrt{41}}{41\sqrt{189}} .
\]

Now

\[
z_D^2=89-x_D^2-y_D^2=89-\frac{625}{41}-\frac{309\,136}{7\,749}
     =\frac{6400}{189}= \frac{80^2}{189}.
\]

Thus

\[
z_D=\frac{80}{\sqrt{189}} .
\]

---

### 2.  Radius of the insphere

The centroid \(G\) has coordinates

\[
G=\frac{A+B+C+D}{4}
   =\left(\frac{0+\sqrt{41}+x_C +x_D}{4},\;
           \frac{0+0+y_C+y_D}{4},\;
           \frac{0+0+0+z_D}{4}\right).
\]

Since the face \(ABC\) lies in the plane \(z=0\), the distance from \(G\) to
this face is simply \(z_G=\dfrac{z_D}{4}\).
Hence

\[
r=\frac{z_D}{4}
   =\frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{80}{\sqrt{189}}
   =\frac{20}{3\sqrt{21}}
   =\frac{20\sqrt{21}}{63}.
\]

This is of the required form \(\dfrac{m\sqrt{n}}{p}\) with

\[
m=20,\qquad n=21,\qquad p=63,
\]
where \(m\) and \(p\) are coprime and \(n\) is square‑free.

---

\[
m+n+p = 20+21+63 = 104.
\]
