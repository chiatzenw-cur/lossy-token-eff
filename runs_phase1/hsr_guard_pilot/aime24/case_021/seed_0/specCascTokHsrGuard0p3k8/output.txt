\[
n=a\,b+c\qquad (a\ge 1,\;0\le c<b)
\]

be the two–digit representation of a \(b\)-beautiful integer \(n\).  
The definition requires

\[
a+c=\sqrt n .
\]

Since \(a+c\) is an integer, \(\sqrt n\) must be an integer; let  

\[
k=\sqrt n \quad(k\ge 2).
\]

Hence \(n=k^{2}\) and the conditions become  

\[
\begin{cases}
a+b+c = k,\\[2pt]
a\,b+c   = k^{2}.
\end{cases}
\]

From the first equation \(c=k-a\).  
Insert this into the second:

\[
a\,b+k-a=k^{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
a(b-1)=k(k-1).
\]

Set \(d=b-1\;(d\ge 1)\).  Then  

\[
a=\frac{k(k-1)}{d} \qquad\text{and}\qquad 
c=k-a .
\]

Thus a pair \((a,c)\) exists **iff** \(d\mid k(k-1)\) and the resulting
\(a,c\) satisfy \(1\le a\le d-1\) and \(0\le c\le d\).
When \(d\mid k(k-1)\) and \(k\le d\) (see below) both inequalities hold automatically
(because \(0\le k-a\le k\le d\)).  
Hence the only restriction is

\[
2\le k\le d,\qquad d\mid k(k-1).
\tag{1}
\]

### How many integers \(k\) satisfy (1)?

Let  

\[
d=p_{1}^{e_{1}}\!p_{2}^{e_{2}}\cdots p_{r}^{e_{r}},\qquad  
\omega(d)=r=\text{number of distinct primes of }d .
\]

For a given prime power \(p_i^{e_i}\) the condition \(d\mid k(k-1)\)
implies that \(k\equiv 0\pmod{p_i^{e_i}}\) **or** \(k\equiv 1\pmod{p_i^{e_i}}\).
Because the prime powers are pairwise coprime, the Chinese Rem–Theorem
gives one solution modulo \(d\) for each choice of “0 or 1” for every
prime power.  
Thus there are exactly \(2^{\,\omega(d)}\) residue classes modulo \(d\)
that satisfy the divisibility.  
Among these, the residue \(1\) corresponds to \(k=1\), which is not
allowed (the number must have two digits), whereas the residue \(0\)
corresponds to \(k=d\), which is allowed.  
Hence the number of admissible \(k\) is

\[
N(d)=2^{\,\omega(d)}-1 .
\tag{2}
\]

### The requirement “more than ten \(b\)-beautiful integers”

\[
2^{\,\omega(b-1)}-1>10

\]
