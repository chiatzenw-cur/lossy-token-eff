### 1.  A condition on \(\sqrt n\)

Write \(s=\sqrt n\;(s\in\mathbb Z^+)\).  
Then  

\[
s^2=a\,b+c \qquad\text{and}\qquad c=s-a .
\]

Substituting \(c\):

\[
s^2=a\,b+(s-a)=a(b-1)+s .
\]

Thus  

\[
s^2-s=a(b-1)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad  
a=\frac{s(s-1)}{\,b-1\,}. \tag{1}
\]

Hence \(b-1\) must divide \(s(s-1)\); write \(d=b-1\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2.  Admissible values of \(s\)

We need

* \(a\) to be an integer,
* \(1\le a\le d\),
* \(c=s-a\) to satisfy \(0\le c\le d\).

From \(a\le d\) and \(s^2=s^2\) we obtain  

\[
s^2-s\le d^2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad s\le d .
\]

From \(c\ge 0\) (i.e. \(a\le s\)) we get

\[
\frac{s(s-1)}{d}\le s\quad\Longrightarrow\quad s-1\le d\quad\Longrightarrow\quad s\le d+1.
\]

The former inequality is sharp, so the **only possible range** for \(s\) is  

\[
s\in\bigl[\;t,\;d\bigr]\qquad\text{with}\qquad 
t=\Big\lceil\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4d}}{2}\Big\rceil .

### 3.  How many \(s\) can satisfy (2)?
