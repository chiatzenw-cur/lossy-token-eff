---

### 1.  Translate to exponential equations  

\[
\log_x(y^x)=10 \Longrightarrow y^x=x^{10}\tag{1}
\]
and  
\[
\log_y(x^{4y})=10 \Longrightarrow x^{4y}=y^{10}\tag{2}
\]

---

### 2.  Work with logarithms  

Let  

\[
A=\ln x,\qqu ad B=\ln y, \qquad x>0,\;y>0 .
\]

From (1)

\[
x\,B=10A \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad B=\frac{10A}{x}\tag{1'}
\]

From (2)

\[
4y\,A=10B \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad B=\frac{4yA}{10}\tag{2'}
        \]

Equating the two expressions for \(B\):

\[
\frac{10A}{x}=\frac{4yA}{10}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
4yx=100 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad xy=25.
\]

So the product \(xy\) is already determined.

---

### 3. (Optional) Verify that such \(x,y>1\) exist  

With \(xy=25\) write \(y=\dfrac{25}{x}\) and substitute into (1'):

\[
\ln\!\left(\frac{25}{x}\right)=\frac{10\,\ln x}{x}
\]

which indeed has a solution \(x>1\) (e.g. \(x\approx1.53,\;y\approx16.3\)).  
Thus the equations are consistent, and \(xy=25\) is the required value.
