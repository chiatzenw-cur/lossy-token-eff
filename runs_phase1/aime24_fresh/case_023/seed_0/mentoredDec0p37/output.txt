### 1.  The length of the list

Let the list have \(n\) terms.

*If \(n\) were odd*, the median would equal the middle element of the sorted list, which would then **appear in the list** – contradicting the condition that the median is **not** in the list.  
Hence \(n\) must be even.

Now consider possible even lengths:

| \(n\) | Largest possible number of 9’s (\(k\)) | Feasible sum for the remaining terms |
|------|------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------|
| 2 | \(k=1\) (mode not unique) | Impossible |
| 4 | \(k=2\) | Works |
| 6 | \(k=2\) | No acceptable configuration |
| 8 | \(k\le3\) | Impossible |

Thus the only viable length is \(n=4\).

### 2.  Constructing the list

Let the two 9’s be the mode.  
Let the other two distinct integers be \(x\) and \(y\) (\(x<y\ne9\)).  
The sum condition gives  

\[
9+9+x+y=30\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x+y=12 .
\]

The list sorted must be \(\{x,\,y,\,9,\,9\}\).

The median is  

\[
M=\frac{y+9}{2}.
\]

We must have \(M\) an integer, and \(M\) must not equal any of the four numbers.

Because \(y\ne9\), \(y+9\) must be even, i.e. \(y\) is odd.  
The only distinct positive integers adding to \(12\) with \(y\) odd are

\[
(x,y)=(5,7).
\]

(Other pairs \((1,11),(2,10),(3,9),(4,8)\) involve a unit that is not positive, or repeat 9.)

Thus the only possible list is  

\[
\{5,\,7,\,9,\,9\}.
\]

- Sum \(=30\).
