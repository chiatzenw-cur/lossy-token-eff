--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 1.  The condition on the rows  

The numbers formed by reading left‑to‑right are  

\[
\underbrace{100a+10b+c}_{\text{row 1}} , \qquad 
\underbrace{100d+10e+f}_{\text{row 2}} .
\]

Their sum is \(999\):

\[
100a+10b+c+100d+10e+f=999 .
\]

Group the terms as  

\[
100(a+d)+10(b+e)+(c+f)=999 .
\]

Because \(a+d,\;b+e,\;c+f\) are each between \(0\) and \(18\), the only way to represent the
number \(999\) in this form is  

\[
a+d=9,\qquad b+e=9,\qquad c+f=9. \tag{1}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.  The condition on the columns  

The numbers formed by reading top‑to‑bottom are  

\[
10a+d,\qquad 10b+e,\qquad 10c+f .
\]

Their sum is \(99\):

\[
10a+d+10b+e+10c+f=99 .
\]

Using (1) we can write \(d=9-a,\;e=9-b,\;f=9-c\).  
Hence the column sum becomes

\[
10(a+b+c)+(9-a)+(9-b)+(9-c)=99 .
\]

Simplifying,

\[
10(a+b+c)+27-(a+b+c)=99
\Longrightarrow 9(a+b+c)=72
\Longrightarrow a+b+c=8. \tag{2}
\]

For completeness,

\[
d+e+f=(9-a)+(9-b)+(9-c)=27-(a+b+c)=27-8=19 .
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 3.  Counting the solutions  

Equation (2) says that the three top‑row digits are non‑negative integers
\(0\le a,b,c\le 9\) with sum \(8\).  
There is no further restriction, because
\(d=9-a,\;e=9-b,\;f=9-c\) automatically lie between \(0\) and \(9\).

The number of non‑negative integer solutions to \(a+b+c=8\) is obtained by
stars and bars:

\[
\binom{8+3-1}{3-1}=\binom{10}{2}=45 .
\]

Each solution \((a,b,c)\) yields a unique grid, so the total number of
acceptable grids is

\[
\boxed{45}.
