15,21,36,70,85,91,105,106,120,126,141,175,190,196,210 .
\]

For each of these \(k\) we have
\(a=\dfrac{k(k-1)}{210}\), \(c=k-a\), and \(n=k^{2}\) satisfies  

\[
a+b\,c = k^{2},\qquad a+c=k .
\]

Thus there are \(15>10\) assuredly \(b\)-beautiful numbers when
\(b=d+1=211\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### Minimality

For any \(d<210\) we have at most three distinct prime factors,
hence at most \(2^{3}=8\) residue classes and consequently at most
\(8\) admissible \(k\).  
Therefore no base \(b<211\) can produce more than ten beautiful
integers.
