* \(s\) = kilometers per hour when the walk reaches the coffee shop (in the first case).  
* \(t\) = minutes spent in the coffee shop (the same amount in both cases).  

The walk is 9 km long.  
For a given walking speed \(v\), the time spent walking per minute is

\[
\text{walking time}= \frac{9}{v}\;(\text{hours}) .
\]

The total time for the walk is the walking time plus the coffee‑ing time \(\tfrac{t}{60}\) hours.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 1.  From the first two conditions

\[
\begin{cases}
 \frac{9}{s}=4-\tfrac{t}{60}\quad &(1)\\[2pt]
 \frac{9}{s+2}=2.4-\tfrac{t}{60}\quad &(2)
\end{cases}
\]

---

From (1) and (2)

\[
4-\frac{9}{s}=2.4-\frac{9}{s+2}\;\Longrightarrow\;
\frac{9}{\!s+2}-\frac{9}{s}=-1.6 .
\]

Using the common denominator \(s(s+2)\),

\[
9\left(\frac{s- (s+2)}{s(s+2)}\right)
=-1.6
\Longrightarrow
-18\Big/ \bigl(s(s+2)\bigr)=-1.6.
\]

Hence

\[
\frac{18}{s(s+2)}=1.6
\Longrightarrow
s(s+2)=\frac{18}{1.6}=11.25 .
\]

So

\[
s^2+2s-11.25=0
\;\Longrightarrow\;
s= \frac{-2+\sqrt{4+45}}{2}= \frac{5}{2}=2.5\;\text{km/h}.
\]

(We ignore the negative root.)

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.  The coffee‑shop time \(t\)

From (1):

\[
\frac{9}{s}=4-\frac{t}{60}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\frac{9}{2.5}=4-\frac{t}{60}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
3.6=\tfrac{t}{60}=4-3.6=0.4
\]

\[
\Rightarrow \;\frac{t}{60}=0.4\;\text{h}=24\text{ min}.
\]

Thus \(t = 24\) minutes.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 3.  Working speed \(s+\tfrac12\)

The slower walk is \((s+\tfrac12)=2.5 + 0.5 = 3.0\) km/h.

Walking time becomes

\[
\frac{9}{3.0}=3\text{ hours}=180\text{ minutes}.
\]

The coffee‑shop time is now still \(t=24\) minutes, and we are told the
total number of minutes that includes the coffee‑shop time is sought.

\[
\text{Total time}=180+24 = 204\text{ minutes}
\]
