\[
\text{Surface area:}\quad 2(ab+bc+ca)=54 \Longrightarrow ab+bc+ca=27
\]
\[
\text{Volume:}\quad abc=23
\]

For a box with these dimensions the smallest sphere that can contain it is the
sphere centered at the centre of the box; its radius is half the distance from
the centre to any of the eight vertices:

\[
r_{\text{box}}=\frac12\sqrt{\left(\frac{a}{2}\right)^2+\left(\frac{b}{2}\right)^2+\left(\frac{c}{2}\right)^2}
            =\frac12\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}.
\]

Hence for a box
\[
r_{\text{box}}^2=\frac14\,(a^2+b^2+c^2).
\]

The sphere that can contain **every** box in \(\mathcal B\) must have radius at least
the largest of those radii, so

\[
r^2=\frac14\max_{\mathcal B}\bigl(a^2+b^2+c^2\bigr).
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### Maximising \(a^2+b^2+c^2\)

Denote the symmetric sums

\[
s_1=a+b+c,\qquad s_2=ab+bc+ca=27,\qquad s_3=abc=23 .
\]

Then

\[
a^2+b^2+c^2=s_1^2-2s_2=s_1^2-54 .
\]

Thus we must maximise \(s_1^2\), i.e. maximise \(s_1\).
For fixed \(s_2\) and \(s_3\) the maximum \(s_1\) occurs when the cubic
whose roots are \(a,b,c\)

\[
t^3-s_1t^2+27t-23=0
\]

has a double root (discriminant zero).  
Let two roots be equal: \(b=c=x\), \(a=y\).

\[
\begin{cases}
xy+x^2+xy=27 &\Longrightarrow 2xy+x^2=27\\[2pt]
y\,x^2=23    &\Longrightarrow y=\dfrac{23}{x^2}
\end{cases}
\]

Substituting \(y\) gives

\[
46/x+x^2=27\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x^3-27x+46=0.
\]

The cubic factors as \((x-2)(x^2+2x-23)=0\), giving possible \(x\)

\[
x=2,\qquad x=-1\pm2\sqrt6 .
\]

Only the positive roots are admissible:

1. \(x=2,\; y=\dfrac{23}{4}\)  
   \[
   s_1=y+2x=\frac{23}{4}+4=\frac{39}{4},\qquad
   a^2+b^2+c^2=s_1^2-54=\frac{657}{16}.
   \]

2. \(x=-1+2\sqrt6,\; y=\dfrac{23}{x^2}\)  
   gives a smaller \(s_1\) and thus a smaller \(a^2+b^2+c^2\).

Hence the maximum is attained for \(x=2,\; y=23/4\) and

\[
\max_{\mathcal B}\bigl(a^2+b^2+c^2\bigr)=\frac{657}{16}.
\]
