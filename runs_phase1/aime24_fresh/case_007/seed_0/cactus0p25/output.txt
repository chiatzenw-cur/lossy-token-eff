<|end|><|start|>assistant<|channel|>final<|message|>For a rectangular box with side lengths \(a\), \(b\), \(c\)

\[
\text{Volume}=abc=23,\qquad 
\text{Surface area}=2(ab+bc+ca)=54
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad 
ab+bc+ca=27 .
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 1.  Radius of the smallest sphere containing a box

If the sphere is centred at the centre of the box, its radius must be half the
distance from the centre to a corner of the box, i.e.

\[
r=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}.
\]

Thus for a given box the smaller \(r\) is the smaller the value of
\(a^2+b^2+c^2\).  
The largest \(r\) that is capable of packing all boxes of the set is therefore
the **maximum** of \(a^2+b^2+c^2\) for all admissible \(a,b,c\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.  Expressing \(a^2+b^2+c^2\) in terms of the elementary symmetric
sums

Let  

\[
p=a+b+c,\qquad q = ab+bc+ca=27,\qquad r=abc=23. 
\]

Then

\[
a^2+b^2+c^2 = p^2-2q = p^2-54 .
\]

Thus

\[
w \;:=\; a^2+b^2+c^2 = p^2-54 .
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 3.  Expressing the restriction \(oxy+ab+bc+ca=27\)

Let \(t=ab\) and the other side be \(c\).  
Because \(c=23/(100t)\) from the volume condition we get
\(c=23/(ab)\).  A simple computation of \(27\)=\(ab\)+\(bc\)+\(ca\) furnishes
a single variable equation:

\[
27= t + \frac{23}{a} + \frac{23}{b}
          =t+\frac{23}{ab}\,(a+b)
          =t+\frac{23\,p}{t}.
\]

Hence

\[
p= \frac{27t-t^2}{23}.       \tag{1}
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 4.  Maximising \(a^2+b^2+c^2\)

With (1) substituted in the latter expression for \(w\),

\[
w=p^2-54
  =\frac{(27t-t^2)^2}{529}-2t+\frac{529}{t^2}.
\]

Then

\[
w'(t)=\frac{2(27t-t^2)(27-2t)}{529}-2-\frac{1058}{t^{3}}.
\]

Set \(w'(t)=0\) to locate stationary points.  Solving

\[
\frac{2(27t-t^2)(27-2t)}{529}-2-\frac{1058}{t^{3}}
   =0
\]

gives exactly

\[
t=11\frac{1}{2}.      \tag{2}
\]

Hence \(t=ab=11.5\).  For \(t=11.5\) the cubic has
\(b=\!c=2\) and \(a=23/(2\cdot2)=23/4=5.75\).
Thus the triple that satisfies (1) is  
\((a,b,c)=\left(\frac{23}{4},\, 2,\;2\right)\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 5.  Determining \(r^{2}\)

Compute \(a^2+b^2+c^2\) for this triple:

\[
a^2+b^2+c^2=\left(\frac{23}{4}\right)^2+2^2+\;2^2
=\frac{289}{16}+20
=\frac{41.0625}{1}=41.0625
=\frac{656}{16}= \frac{657}{4}= \frac{1374}{64}.
\]

Thus the smallest radius capable of containing all boxes is

\[
r=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{A}
=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{41.0625}
=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{657}{16}}
=\sqrt{\frac{657}{64}}\;.
\]

Therefore

\[
r^{2}=\frac{657}{64}.
\]

The fraction is already in lowest terms
(\(\gcd(657 , 64)=1\)).  
Consequently

\[
p+q = 657+64 = 721 .
\]
