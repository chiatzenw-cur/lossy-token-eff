--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 1.   Radius of the circumscribing sphere

The smallest sphere that can contain the box is centred at the centre of the box;  
its radius is half the space‑diagonal:

\[
r=\frac{\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}}}{2}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad 
r^{2}= \frac{a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}}{4}.
\]

For the set \(\mathcal B\) we need a radius that works for **every** box in the
set, so we must maximise \(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}\) under the two constraints.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 2.   Express \(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}\) in terms of the elementary symmetric sums

Let  

\[
\sigma_1=a+b+c, \qquad \sigma_2=ab+bc+ca=27,\qquad \sigma_3=abc=23 .
\]

Then

\[
a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}= \sigma_1^{\,2}-2\sigma_2
                 = \sigma_1^{\,2}-54 .
\]

Hence to maximise \(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}\) we must maximise \(\sigma_1=a+b+c\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 3.   Maximising \(\sigma_1\)

The roots \(a,b,c\) are the positive roots of  

\[
t^{3}-\sigma_1 t^{2}+27t-23=0 .
\]

For fixed \(\sigma_2\) and \(\sigma_3\), the possible values of \(\sigma_1\)
are limited by the condition that this cubic has three positive real roots.
The boundary of this region occurs when the cubic has a double root, i.e.
when two of the side lengths are equal.

Assume \(b=c=x\) and \(a=y\).  
From the constraints,

\[
\begin{cases}
xy+x^2+xy=2xy+x^{2}=27,\\[2pt]
y x^{2}=23\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad y=\dfrac{23}{x^{2}} .
\end{cases}
\]

Substituting \(y\) into the first equation gives

\[
2 x\!\left(\frac{23}{x^{2}}\right)+x^{2}=27
\;\Longrightarrow\;
x^{3}-27x+46=0 .
\]

The cubic has the positive root \(x=2\) (indeed \(2^{3}-27\cdot2+46=0\)).
The corresponding \(y\) value is

\[
y=\frac{23}{2^{2}}=\frac{23}{4}=5.75 .
\]

Thus one admissible box is \( (a,b,c)=(5.75,\,2,\,2)\) up to permutation.

The sum of the sides is

\[
\sigma_1=a+b+c=5.75+2+2=9.75 .
\]

A second positive root of the cubic is approximately \(x\approx3.90\), which
gives a smaller sum (\(\sigma_1\approx9.32\)).  
Since the “double‑root” boundary gives the only extremal points,
the maximum of \(\sigma_1\) is \(9.75\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 4.   Compute the maximum value of \(r^{2}\)

\[
a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}
    = \sigma_1^{\,2}-54=(9.75)^2-54=95.0625-54=41.0625 .
\]

Hence

\[
r^{2}=\frac{a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}}{4}
     =\frac{41.0625}{4}
     =10.265625
     =10+\frac{17}{64}
     =\frac{657}{64}\, .
\]

The fraction is already in lowest terms (\(\gcd(657,64)=1\)).
Thus \(p=657,\; q=64\) and

\[
p+q=657+64=\boxed{721}.
\]
