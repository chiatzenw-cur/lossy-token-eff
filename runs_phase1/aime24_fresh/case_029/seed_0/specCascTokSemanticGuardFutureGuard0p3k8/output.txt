Hence the point lies on the line joining the two centres, so that

\[
\frac{z-h}{11}=\frac{z}{\sqrt{9-x^{2}-y^{2}}} \tag{3}
\]

(The right–hand side comes from the torus formula (1).)

Because the torus is a surface of revolution, the cross‑section by planes
\(y=0\) gives the equation of a circle of radius \(3\) centred at \((6,0)\).
Thus we can take \(y=0\) and write

\[
(x-6)^{2}+z^{2}=9 \tag{4}
\]

Let \(B=\sqrt{9-z^{2}}\) (so \(B\ge 0\)).  
Then the outer radius of the torus cross–section at height \(z\) is  
\(R_{\text{out}}=6+B\) and the inner radius is \(R_{\text{in}}=6-B\).

Let us treat the two tangency cases separately.

---

### 2.  Outer tangency

For the outer circle we have

\[
\sqrt{121-(z-h)^{2}}=6+B \tag{5}
\]

From (3) with \(z>0\) we obtain  

\[
h=-\,\frac{6z}{B}\tag{6}
\]

Insert (5) and (6) into the torus condition (4).  
After a little algebra we arrive at the quadratic equation

\[
28B^{2}-27B-81=0
\]

whose positive solution is  

\[
B_{\text{out}}=\frac{9}{4}.
\]

Hence  

\[
R_{\text{out}}=6+B_{\text{out}}=6+\frac{9}{4}= \frac{33}{4}.
\]

---

### 3.  Inner tangency

For the inner circle

\[
\sqrt{121-(z-h)^{2}}=6-B \tag{7}
\]

Equation (3) now gives  

\[
h= z\!\left(2-\frac{6}{B}\right)\tag{8}
\]

Proceeding as before yields

\[
28B^{2}+27B-81=0
\]

with the positive root  

\[
B_{\text{in}}= \frac{9}{7}.
\]

Thus

\[
R_{\text{in}}=6-B_{\text{in}}=6-\frac{9}{7}= \frac{33}{7}.
\]

---

### 4.  Difference of the radii

The required radii of the tangency circles are

\[
r_{o}=R_{\text{out}}=\frac{33}{4},\qquad
r_{i}=R_{\text{in}}=\frac{33}{7}.
\]

Hence

\[
r_{i}-r_{o}= \frac{33}{7}-\frac{33}{4}
           = \frac{33(4-7)}{28}
           = -\,\frac{99}{28}.
\]

Taking the absolute value (since the problem asks for \(m/n\) with \(m,n>0\)),

\[
|r_{i}-r_{o}|=\frac{99}{28}.
\]

Therefore \(m=99,\; n=28\) and

\[
m+n=99+28=127.
\]
