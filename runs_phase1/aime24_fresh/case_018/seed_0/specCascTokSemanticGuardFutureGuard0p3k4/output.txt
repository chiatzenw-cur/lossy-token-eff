\]

### 2.  Express \(S_{3}\) in terms of \(S_{2}\)

\[
S_{3}=100S_{2}-2\,000\,000 .
\]

Because \(S_{3}\ge 0\) and \(S_{2}\le \frac{S_{1}^{2}}{3}=30\,000\),  
\(S_{2}\) must satisfy

\[
20\,000\le S_{2}\le 30\,000 .
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 3.  Solve for \((a,b,c)\)

Let \(s=a+b\;(=S_{1}-c)\) and \(p=ab\).  
Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
S_{2}&=ab+bc+ca=p+300s-s^{2},\\
S_{3}&=abc=p(300-s).
\end{aligned}
\]

Insert these into \(S_{3}=100S_{2}-2\,000\,000\):

\[
p(300-s)=100\bigl(p+300s-s^{2}\bigr)-2\,000\,000 .
\]

Rearranging gives

\[
p(200-s)=30\,000s-100s^{2}-2\,000\,000 .
\]

Since \(s\neq 200\) (the case \(s=200\) will be handled separately), we obtain

\[
p=\frac{30\,000s-100s^{2}-2\,000\,000}{200-s}
   =100\frac{(s-200)(s-100)}{s-200}
   =100(s-100).
\]

Thus

\[
p=ab=100(s-100), \qquad s=a+b.
\]

The discriminant of the quadratic \(x^{2}-sx+p=0\) is

\[
\Delta = s^{2}-4p = s^{2}-400(s-100)=(s-200)^{2},
\]

which is a perfect square. Hence

\[
a,b=\frac{s\pm (s-200)}{2}=\{\,100,\;s-100\,\}.
\]

So for \(s\neq 200\) we have

\[
(a,b,c)=\bigl(100,\;s-100,\;300-s\bigr).
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 4.  Handle the special case \(s=200\)

If \(s=200\) then \(ab=100(s-100)=0\), which forces \(a=b=100\).  
Consequently \(c=300-200=100\).

Thus the triple \((100,100,100)\) is also a solution.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 5.  Characterization of all solutions

All solutions are exactly the ordered triples that contain a \(100\) and
the other two numbers sum to \(200\):

\[
(a,b,c) \text{ is a permutation of } (100,\;x,\;200-x),
\qquad 0\le x\le 200 .
\]

--------------------------------------------------------------------
### 6.  Count the ordered triples

For a fixed variable equal to \(100\) there are \(201\) ways to choose
the other two numbers (since \(x\) can be \(0,1,\dots ,200\)).
There are three choices for which variable is \(100\):

\[
3\times 201 = 603 .
\]

The triple \((100,100,100)\) has been counted three times, so we
subtract the two extra counts:

\[
603-2 = 601 .
\]
