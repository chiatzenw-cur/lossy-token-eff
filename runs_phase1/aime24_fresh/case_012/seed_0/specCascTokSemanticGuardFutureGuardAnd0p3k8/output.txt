A path that stays on the grid lines consists of

### 1.  What does “exactly four changes of direction” mean?

A change of direction occurs when a step to the right is followed by a step up, or a step up is followed by a step to the right.  
Let the path have \(c\) changes.  
Then the path is divided into \(c+1\) monotone segments, each segment being a block of consecutive R’s or consecutive U’s.

For \(c=4\) we have \(5\) segments, alternating between R and U.

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2.  Two possible patterns

The first segment can be either R or U.

| Pattern | Sequence of segments | # of R segments | # of U segments |
|---------|----------------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| R‑U‑R‑U‑R | starts with R | 3 | 2 |
| U‑R‑U‑R‑U | starts with U | 2 | 3 |

--------------------------------------------------------------------

### 3.  Distributing the 8 R’s and 8 U’s among the segments

Each segment must contain at least one step.

*If the path starts with R*:  
- 3 R‑segments must sum to 8 → \(\binom{8-1}{3-1}=\binom{7}{2}=21\) ways.  
- 2 U‑segments must sum to 8 → \(\binom{8-1}{2-1}=\binom{7}{1}=7\) ways.

Total for this pattern: \(21 \times 7 = 147\).

*If the path starts with U*:  
- 2 R‑segments: \(\binom{7}{1}=7\) ways.  
- 3 U‑segments: \(\binom{7}{2}=21\) ways.

Total for this pattern: \(7 \times 21 = 147\).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
