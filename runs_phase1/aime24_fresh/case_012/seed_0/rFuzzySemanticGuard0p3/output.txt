A change‑direction count of \(4\) corresponds to **five runs** of consecutive

Let

* \(R_1,R_2,R_3\) be the lengths of the \(R\)’s in the three R‑segments
  (in the pattern \(R,U,R,U,R\) – start with R);
* \(U_1,U_2\) be the lengths of the two U‑segments (in the same pattern).

Different kinds of strings must therefore satisfy

\[
R_1+R_2+R_3=8,\qquad
U_1+U_2=8,\qquad
R_i>0,\;U_i>0\ .
\]

These choices are independent.  
The number of ways to distribute 8 identical R’s into 3 *positive* parts is  

\[
\binom{8-1}{3-1}= \binom{7}{2}=21 .
\]

\binom{8-1}{2-1}= \binom{7}{1}=7 .

\[
21\cdot 7=147
