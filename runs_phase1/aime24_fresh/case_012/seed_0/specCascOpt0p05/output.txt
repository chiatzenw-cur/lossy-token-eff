### 1.  What does “change direction exactly four times’’ mean?

If we change direction \(4\) times (i.e. an \(R\) is followed by a \(U\) or vice‑versa),
the sequence of moves is a **sequence of 5 runs**.  
Runs alternate between \(R\) and \(U\).

There are two possible ways to begin:

* **Start with \(R\)** – then the run pattern is  
  \(R, U, R, U, R\) (3 R–runs and 2 U–runs).

* **Start with \(U\)** – then the run pattern is  
  \(U, R, U, R, U\) (2 R–runs and 2 U–runs).

---------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2.  Count the possible numbers of steps in each run

Let \(r_1,r_2,r_3\) be the lengths of the \(R\)-runs and \(u_1,u_2\) the lengths of the \(U\)-runs when we start with \(R\).

The conditions are  
\[
r_1+r_2+r_3 = 8,\qquad u_1+u_2 = 8,\qquad r_i,u_i \ge 1 .
\]

Number of positive integer solutions to \(r_1+r_2+r_3 = 8\) (stars‑and‑bars):
\[
\binom{8-1}{3-1}=\binom{7}{2}=21 .
\]

Number of positive integer solutions to \(u_1+u_2 = 8\):
\[
\binom{8-1}{2-1}=7 .
\]

Thus, for each start direction \(R\),  
\[
\#\text{paths}= 21 \times 7 = 147 .
\]

By the same reasoning, the case with \(U\) first gives another \(21\) choices for the \(U\)-runs and \(7\) for the \(R\)-runs, again giving \(147\) paths.

\text{Total paths}=147+147 = 294 .
