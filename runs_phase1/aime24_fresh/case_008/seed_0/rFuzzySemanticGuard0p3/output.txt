\[
\log_{x} (y^{x})=10 \;\Longrightarrow\; x^{10}=y^{x}

\[
\log_{y} (x^{4y})=10 \;\Longrightarrow\; y^{10}=x^{4y}
\]

**2.** Take natural logarithms of each side.

Let  

\[
\ln ^{x} =\ln x,\qquad \ln ^{y} =\ln y .
\]

From the first equation  
\[
x\ln y = 10\ln x\;\Longrightarrow\; \ln x = \frac{x\ln y}{10}.
\]

From the second equation  
\[
4 y\ln x = 10\ln y \;\Longrightarrow\; \ln y = \frac{4y\ln x}{10}. 
\]

**3.** Eliminate the logarithms.  
Multiply both logarithmic equations:

\[
\Big[
x\ln y\Big]\,\Big(\;4y\ln x\Big)
   = 10\ln x\;\; \cdot\;10\ln y .
\]

The left–hand side simplifies to  

\[
4xy\,(\ln x)(\ln y),
\]

and the right–hand side to  

\[
100(\ln x)(\ln y).
\]

\[
4xy = 100 \;\Longrightarrow\; x y = 25.
