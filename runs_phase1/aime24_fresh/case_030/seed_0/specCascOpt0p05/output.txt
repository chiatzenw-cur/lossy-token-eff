### 1.  Consequences of maximality

For a row \(i\) to contain a chip it must have at least one column of the same
colour.  
Similarly for a column \(j\) to contain a chip it must have
at least one row of the same colour.

So for every colour present in the set of rows,
there must be a row of the *same* colour present in the set of columns, and vice versa.

The number of ways to choose \(m\) black rows from 5 rows is \(\binom{5}{m}\) and to
choose \(n\) black columns from 5 columns is \(\binom{5}{n}\).
Summing over all admissible sequences of \(m\) and \(n\):

\[
\sum_{m=1}^{4}\binom{5}{m}\;\sum_{n=1}^{4}\binom{5}{n}
   = 30 \times 30 = 900 .
\]

------------------------------------------------------------------

### 4.  Total number of ways

\[
\boxed{902}
\]

Thus there are \(902\) different ways to place chips on the \(5\times5\) grid
so that the two constraints hold and the arrangement is maximal.
