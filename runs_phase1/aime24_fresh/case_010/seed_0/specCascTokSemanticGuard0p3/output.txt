### Total number of possible draws

The lottery draws \(4\) distinct numbers from the 10‑element set \(S\).

\[
\binom{10}{4}=210
\]

So there are \(210\) equally likely draws.

---

### Draws that give a prize

A prize is won when at least two of Jen’s four numbers are among the drawn numbers.  
Let \(k\) be the number of matches between Jen’s set and the draw.

\[
k\in\{2,3,4\}
\]

- **Exactly \(k=2\) matches**  
  \[
  \binom{4}{2}\binom{6}{2}=6\cdot15=90
  \]

- **Exactly \(k=3\) matches**  
  \[
  \binom{4}{3}\binom{6}{1}=4\cdot6=24
  \]

- **Exactly \(k=4\) matches** (grand prize)  
  \[
  \binom{4}{4}\binom{6}{0}=1\cdot1=1
  \]

Adding them gives the number of draws that give a prize:

\[
90+24+1=115
\]

---

### Conditional probability of the grand prize

The grand prize occurs only when all four numbers match, which is a single draw out of the 115 prize‑giving draws.

\[
P(\text{grand prize}\mid\text{prize})=\frac{1}{115}
\]

This fraction is already in lowest terms, so let \(m=1,\;n=115\).

\[
m+n = 1+115 = 116
