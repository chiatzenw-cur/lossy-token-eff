\[

Jen first chooses a fixed set of 4 numbers.

---

### 1.  Total number of possible draws

\[
\binom{10}{4}=210.
\]

---

### 2.  Probability that Jen wins the grand prize  

The grand prize is awarded when the drawn set equals Jen’s set.  
Only one of the 210 possible draws does this:

\[
P(\text{grand prize})=\frac{1}{210}.
\]

---

### 3.  Probability that Jen wins a prize (at least two hits)

Let \(k\) be the number of matched numbers.  
For a fixed Jen’s set, the number of drawn sets that share exactly \(k\) elements with it is

\[
\binom{4}{k}\binom{6}{\,4-k\,},
\]

since \(k\) are chosen from her 4 numbers and the remaining \(4-k\) are chosen from the other 6 numbers in \(S\).

Compute for \(k=2,3,4\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
k=2:&\quad \binom{4}{2}\binom{6}{2}=6\cdot15=90,\\[2pt]
k=3:&\quad \binom{4}{3}\binom{6}{1}=4\cdot6=24,\\[2pt]
k=4:&\quad \binom{4}{4}\binom{6}{0}=1\cdot1=1.
\end{aligned}
\]

Total favorable draws for “at least two matches”:

\[
90+24+1=115.
\]

Thus

\[
P(\text{at least two matches})=\frac{115}{210}=\frac{23}{42}.
\]

---

### 4.  Conditional probability of the grand prize given a prize

\[
P(\text{grand prize}\mid\text{at least two matches})
=\frac{P(\text{grand prize})}{P(\text{at least two matches})}
=\frac{\frac{1}{210}}{\frac{23}{42}}
=\frac{1}{210}\cdot\frac{42}{23}
