S=\{1,2,\dots ,10\},

all \( \binom{10}{4}=210 \) subsets being equally likely.

---

### 1.  Number of draws that give \(k\) matches

Let \(k\) be the number of her numbers that also appear in the draw.  
To obtain exactly \(k\) matches:

* choose \(k\) of her \(4\) numbers: \( \binom{4}{k}\);
* choose the remaining \(4-k\) numbers from the \(10-4=6\) numbers she did **not** pick: \( \binom{6}{\,4-k\,}\).

Hence the number of such draws is  
\[
N_k=\binom{4}{k}\binom{6}{4-k}.
\]

Compute for the relevant values of \(k\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
k=4 &: \binom{4}{4}\binom{6}{0}=1,\\[2pt]
k=3 &: \binom{4}{3}\binom{6}{1}=4\cdot 6=24,\\[2pt]
k=2 &: \binom{4}{2}\binom{6}{2}=6\cdot 15=90.
\end{aligned}
\]

The remaining cases \(k=1,0\) give \(80\) and \(15\) draws respectively, and the total
\(1+24+90+80+15=210\).

---

### 2.  Probabilities

* Grand‑prize (\(k=4\)) probability  
  \[
  P(k=4)=\frac{1}{210}.
  \]

* Prize (at least two matches, \(k\ge 2\)) probability  
  \[
  P(k\ge 2)=\frac{1+24+90}{210}=\frac{115}{210}.
  \]

---

### 3.  Conditional probability

\[
P(\text{grand prize}\mid \text{prize})
=\frac{P(k=4)}{P(k\ge 2)}
=\frac{\frac{1}{210}}{\frac{115}{210}}
=\frac{1}{115}.
\]

Thus \(m=1,\; n=115\) and they are coprime.
