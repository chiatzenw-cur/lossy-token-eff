For a random draw of four numbers from \(S\), let  
\(K\) be the number of Jen’s numbers that appear in the draw.

The number of draws that contain exactly \(k\) of Jen’s numbers is  

\[
\binom{4}{k}\binom{6}{\,4-k\,}\qquad(k=0,1,2,3,4)
\]

because we choose \(k\) of her numbers and \(4-k\) of the other \(6\) numbers.

Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
p_0 &= \binom{4}{0}\binom{6}{4}=15,\\[2pt]
p_1 &= \binom{4}{1}\binom{6}{3}=80,\\[2pt]
p_2 &= \binom{4}{2}\binom{6}{2}=90,\\[2pt]
p_3 &= \binom{4}{3}\binom{6}{1}=24,\\[2pt]
p_4 &= \binom{4}{4}\binom{6}{0}=1.
\end{aligned}
\]

The total number of possible draws is \(\binom{10}{4}=210\).

* The probability of having all four numbers correct (grand prize) is  
  \[
  P(K=4)=\frac{p_4}{210}=\frac{1}{210}.
  \]

* The probability of winning a prize (at least two of Jen’s numbers) is  
  \[
  P(K\ge 2)=\frac{p_2+p_3+p_4}{210}= \frac{115}{210}.
  \]

Thus the required conditional probability is

\[
P(K=4\mid K\ge 2)=\frac{P(K=4)}{P(K\ge 2)}
   =\frac{1}{210}\Big/\frac{115}{210}
   =\frac{1}{115}.
\]

In lowest terms this is \(\tfrac{m}{n}=(1\! / 115)\).  
Hence \(m+n=1+115=116\).
