The lottery draws a set \(D\) of four distinct numbers from \(\{1,2,\dots ,10\}\).

For a fixed choice of \(J\), the probability that exactly \(k\) of Jen’s numbers are in the drawn set is a hypergeometric probability

\[
P(k)=\frac{\binom{4}{k}\binom{6}{4-k}}{\binom{10}{4}}, \qquad k=0,1,2,3,4.
\]

Indeed, we must choose \(k\) of Jen’s 4 numbers (\(\binom{4}{k}\)) and \(4-k\) of the 6 numbers that are not in \(J\) (\(\binom{6}{4-k}\)).  
The total number of possible draws is \(\binom{10}{4}=210\).

Compute the probabilities:

\[
\begin{aligned}
P(0)&=\frac{\binom{4}{0}\binom{6}{4}}{210}= \frac{15}{210}= \frac{1}{14},\\[2mm]
P(1)&=\frac{\binom{4}{1}\binom{6}{3}}{210}= \frac{80}{210}= \frac{8}{21},\\[2mm]
P(2)&=\frac{\binom{4}{2}\binom{6}{2}}{210}= \frac{90}{210}= \frac{3}{7},\\[2mm]
P(3)&=\frac{\binom{4}{3}\binom{6}{1}}{210}= \frac{24}{210}= \frac{4}{35},\\[2mm]
P(4)&=\frac{\binom{4}{4}\binom{6}{0}}{210}= \frac{1}{210}.
\end{aligned}
\]

---

### Probability that Jen wins a prize

\[
P(\text{prize}) = P(2)+P(3)+P(4)= \frac{90+24+1}{210}= \frac{115}{210}= \frac{23}{42}.
\]

### Probability that Jen wins the grand prize

\[
P(\text{grand}) = P(4)=\frac{1}{210}.

### Conditional probability

\[
