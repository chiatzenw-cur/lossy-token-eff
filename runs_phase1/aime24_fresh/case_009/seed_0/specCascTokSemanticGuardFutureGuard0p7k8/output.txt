**Proof by induction**

Assume that for all \(k<n\) the statement holds.  
If \(n\equiv 0\pmod 5\) then \(n-1\equiv 4\pmod 5\) and \(n-4\equiv 1\pmod 5\); both are winning positions, so \(n\) is losing.  
If \(n\equiv 2\pmod 5\) then \(n-1\equiv 1\pmod 5\) and \(n-4\equiv 3\pmod 5\); both are winning, so \(n\) is losing.  

Conversely, if \(n\equiv 1,3,4\pmod 5\) then either \(n-1\) or \(n-4\) is congruent to \(0\) or \(2\pmod 5\), giving a move to a losing position, so \(n\) is winning.  
Thus the characterization is complete.
